The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972: Complete Indian Employee Guide

Gratuity is a statutory monetary benefit provided by employers in India under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 to recognize an employee's continuous long-term service. It applies to all establishments, factories, mines, ports, oilfields, and commercial shops employing 10 or more persons on any day of the preceding 12 months.

Once the Act applies to an employer, it continues to apply indefinitely even if the headcount later drops below 10.

The 15/26 Statutory Calculation Formula

The law considers 26 working days in a month (excluding four Sundays) and awards 15 days of wages for every completed year of service:

Gratuity = (Last Drawn Monthly Basic + DA) × (15 / 26) × Completed Years of Service

Key Tenet: If an employee has worked for more than 6 months in their final year of tenure (e.g., 7 years and 7 months), the duration is statutorily rounded up to the next full year (8 years). If less than or equal to 6 months (e.g., 7 years and 4 months), it is rounded down (7 years).

Frequently Asked Questions About Gratuity

Is 5 years of service strictly mandatory to receive gratuity?

For permanent salaried employees resigning or switching jobs, 5 years of continuous service is mandatory under Section 4(1). However, the 5-year condition is entirely waived if employment terminates due to death or permanent disablement. Furthermore, under recent labour reforms, Fixed-Term Employment (FTE) contracts are eligible for pro-rata gratuity upon completing 1 year.

Can an employer pay more than the ₹20 Lakh statutory gratuity limit?

Yes. The ₹20 Lakh limit is a statutory minimum guarantee and tax exemption ceiling under Section 10(10). Employers are legally free to pay higher ex-gratia sums under corporate contractual policy. However, any amount exceeding ₹20,00,000 is added to your taxable income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates.

What components of salary are included in "Wages" for gratuity?

Under Section 2(s) of the Act, "wages" strictly means Basic Pay and Dearness Allowance (DA). House Rent Allowance (HRA), Special Allowance, conveyance, medical reimbursements, annual performance bonuses, and overtime pay are strictly excluded from the calculation.

Strategic Considerations Before Resigning

Keep these practical points in mind before submitting your resignation:

  1. The 4 Years and 240 Days Rule: In several landmark judicial rulings (e.g., Madras High Court in M. Ramasamy vs Management), working 240 days in the 5th year has been recognized as fulfilling the continuous 5-year requirement under Section 2A. However, to avoid litigation, completing 5 calendar years is safest.
  2. The 6 Months Rounding Advantage: Resigning at 7 years and 7 months yields 8 full years of gratuity calculation, earning you almost a full month's basic salary extra compared to resigning at 7 years and 5 months.
  3. Tax Exemption is Cumulative: The ₹20 Lakh tax exemption under Section 10(10) is a lifetime limit across all employers combined throughout your career.
