Gratuity Calculator India (15/26 Rule)

Written by Tejas Waghmare • Last updated: November 2026
Educational content only: This calculator computes statutory gratuity entitlement under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. Tax exemptions are governed by Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act.

Direct Answer: Gratuity is a statutory retirement benefit paid by Indian employers to employees who complete at least 5 continuous years of service. It is calculated using the official formula: (15 × Last Drawn Basic + DA × Years of Service) / 26, tax-exempt up to ₹20,00,000.

Service & Wage Parameters

Gratuity Entitlement

Statutory Gratuity Payable ₹2,42,308
Tax-Exempt Portion (Sec 10(10)) ₹2,42,308
Taxable Portion ₹0
Based on standard 26-working-day divisor mandated by law.

The 15/26 Statutory Calculation Formula

Under Section 4(2) of the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, for every completed year of service or part thereof exceeding six months, the employer pays gratuity at the rate of 15 days wages based on the rate of wages last drawn:

Gratuity = (15 * Last Drawn Basic Salary & DA * Completed Tenure) / 26

Why 26? The law assumes 26 working days in a month after excluding 4 Sundays.

2 Worked Mathematical Examples in Rupees

Example 1: Software Engineer Resigning After 6 Years

A software developer resigns with 6 years of service. Their last drawn Basic salary is ₹50,000/month.

  • Calculation: (15 * ₹50,000 * 6) / 26
  • Numerator: ₹45,00,000 / 26 = ₹1,73,077
  • Because ₹1,73,077 is well below the ₹20 Lakh statutory cap, the full amount is 100% tax-free.

Example 2: Senior Executive Retiring After 24 Years

A vice president retires after 24 years with a last drawn Basic salary of ₹2,00,000/month.

  • Calculation: (15 * ₹2,00,000 * 24) / 26
  • Numerator: ₹7,20,00,000 / 26 = ₹27,69,231
  • Tax-Free Limit: ₹20,00,000 under Section 10(10).
  • Taxable Gratuity: ₹27,69,231 - ₹20,00,000 = ₹7,69,231 (taxed at slab rates in year of receipt).

3 Common Mistakes in Gratuity Calculations

1. Using Total CTC Instead of Basic + DA

Gratuity relies only on Basic Salary plus Dearness Allowance. That is the legal rule. Allowances like HRA, special allowance, and performance bonuses are excluded.

2. Resigning at 4 Years and 10 Months

You need 5 full years of continuous service to qualify. That means at least 4 years and 240 working days. Resigning even two weeks early forfeits the entire benefit.

3. Forgetting the 6-Month Rounding Rule

Once past 5 years, any fraction over 6 months rounds up to the next full year. If you serve 7 years and 7 months, your tenure counts as 8 years. But 7 years and 5 months stays 7 years.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the maximum tax-free gratuity in India?

Under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act, the lifetime tax exemption limit on gratuity received by private-sector employees covered by the Act is ₹20,00,000 (₹20 Lakh).

Does the 5-year rule apply in case of death or disability?

No. The continuous 5-year service requirement is completely waived if employment terminates due to the death or permanent disablement of the employee.

Can an employer pay more than ₹20 Lakh gratuity?

Yes. An employer is free to pay any amount of gratuity exceeding statutory limits. However, any sum in excess of ₹20,00,000 will be treated as taxable income.
