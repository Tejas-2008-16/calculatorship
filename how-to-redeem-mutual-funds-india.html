How Mutual Fund Redemption Works in India: Exit Loads, Cut-Off Timings, and Tax Rules Explained

Hi, I'm Tejas. I'm an engineering student exploring Indian financial markets. This guide summarizes what I have learned about how mutual fund redemptions, exit loads, SEBI cut-off rules, and capital gains tax brackets operate under current regulations. This is strictly educational research and arithmetic—it is not financial advice, tax counsel, or a recommendation to redeem or stay invested. For personal financial planning, consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) or a Chartered Accountant.

Much of the mutual fund literature online focuses primarily on accumulating units: starting an SIP, comparing category expense ratios, or projecting compounding returns. However, understanding the redemption mechanics is equally critical: how fund units are redeemed, how exit load periods are calculated, and how capital gains taxes and settlement schedules work in practice.

Consider an illustration: An investor who has accumulated units across several years decides to redeem ₹25 lakh for a scheduled expenditure. If an investor submits an order after 3:00 PM on a Tuesday, the order is stamped with Wednesday's NAV. Under a T+2 equity settlement cycle, the payout arrives on Friday. Furthermore, if fresh units were purchased within the previous 12 months, those specific units may attract an exit load and short-term capital gains tax under standard FIFO (First-In, First-Out) matching rules.

This guide explains the operational framework governing mutual fund redemptions in India, including FIFO unit accounting, SEBI cut-off schedules, exit load mechanics, 2026 tax provisions, and the standard order process across RTA portals, AMC websites, and depository participants.

1. How Redemption Works: FIFO, Cut-Off Timings & T+2 Settlement

When you click "Redeem" on your investment platform, you are instructing the Asset Management Company (AMC) to buy back a specific number of units (or a fixed rupee value) at the prevailing Net Asset Value (NAV). However, the price you receive and the time it takes for cash to arrive depend on strict SEBI operational rules.

First-In, First-Out (FIFO) Unit Allocation Rule

In a monthly Systematic Investment Plan (SIP), you purchase a fresh batch of units every month. Each batch carries its own distinct purchase date and NAV price. When you place a partial redemption order, SEBI mandates that AMCs process your units on a First-In, First-Out (FIFO) basis. This means the units you purchased earliest in your investment journey are redeemed first!

Did You Know?
Because of the FIFO rule, if you started an SIP 4 years ago and place a partial redemption today equal to 50% of your portfolio, the units being sold are the oldest ones—which are completely exempt from exit load fees and qualify for Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) treatment!

SEBI NAV Cut-Off Timings for Redemptions

The NAV price applied to your redemption depends on when your order hits the AMC servers:

  • Before 3:00 PM (Business Day): If your redemption request is registered before the official SEBI cut-off time of 3:00 PM, you receive the closing NAV calculated at the end of that same business day.
  • After 3:00 PM (or Non-Business Day): If you submit your request after 3:00 PM on a weekday, or anytime on a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday, your order is processed using the closing NAV of the next working business day.

Payout Settlement Timelines (T+1 vs T+2)

Once your order is processed, SEBI's settlement cycle governs when the money is credited directly to your registered bank account via NEFT/RTGS:

💡 Expected Bank Credit Timelines in India:
  • Equity & Hybrid Funds (T+2 Days): If you redeem an equity fund before 3:00 PM on Monday (T), the money will be credited to your bank account by Wednesday evening (T+2).
  • Debt Mutual Funds (T+1 Day): Most liquid, ultra-short, and corporate bond funds settle within 1 working business day.
  • International / Overseas Funds (T+3 to T+5 Days): Funds investing in foreign equities take longer due to global currency conversion and overseas stock exchange settlement windows.

2. Exit Loads & Capital Gains Tax: Calculating the Net Payout

Before initiating a redemption, you must calculate two friction costs that directly reduce your net bank credit: Exit Load penalties and Capital Gains Tax liabilities.

Understanding Exit Load Windows

An exit load is a fee charged by the AMC to discourage short-term trading and protect long-term investors. For most Indian equity mutual fund schemes (Flexi-Cap, Large-Cap, Mid-Cap, Small-Cap):

  • Exit within 365 days (1 Year): A 1.0% exit load is deducted from your NAV price. For example, if the current NAV is ₹100, the AMC redeems your units at an effective NAV of ₹99.
  • Exit after 365 days (1 Year): 0% exit load (Zero penalty).
  • ELSS Funds (Tax-Saving): ELSS units carry a mandatory 3-year statutory lock-in. You cannot redeem them under any condition until 3 full years have elapsed from the purchase date of each specific SIP installment.

Capital Gains Tax Rules in 2026

When you redeem mutual fund units for a profit, the gains are taxed based on your holding period under Section 112A and Section 111A of the Income Tax Act:

Some investors evaluate harvesting gains up to this exemption limit within a financial year; consult a Chartered Accountant to assess how this applies to your specific tax situation.

3. Step-by-Step Redemption Routes by Channel

Redeeming mutual fund units can be initiated through multiple regulatory-compliant channels depending on how the folios are held (SoA physical folios vs demat accounts):

Method 1: Direct via Registrar & Transfer Agent (RTA) Portals

For Statement of Account (SoA) folios, RTAs such as CAMS and KFintech provide direct investor service portals (e.g., myCAMS, KFinKart, and MF Central):

  1. Log into the RTA investor portal or mobile application using verified PAN credentials and OTP.
  2. Navigate to Transact → Redeem.
  3. Select the relevant AMC folio and scheme.
  4. Specify redemption by Amount (₹) or by Units (or select full folio redemption).
  5. Confirm the registered bank account details displayed on screen and authorize the request via mobile/email OTP.

Method 2: Via Direct AMC Portals

  1. Log into the specific Asset Management Company (AMC) official website using your folio number or PAN.
  2. Select the fund scheme from your holdings dashboard.
  3. Click Redeem / Transact.
  4. Enter the desired redemption value or unit count.
  5. Authenticate via one-time password. Proceeds are transferred directly to the verified bank account on record.

Method 3: Via SEBI-Registered Depository Participants (Demat Units)

  1. Log into your depository participant (broker) investment interface on web or mobile.
  2. Navigate to your Mutual Fund Holdings / Portfolio.
  3. Select the specific scheme and click Redeem.
  4. Enter unit quantity and authorize the debit through CDSL / NSDL T-PIN and electronic verification.
  5. Redemption proceeds are credited either to your linked primary bank account or your intermediary ledger according to depository guidelines.

4. Factors to Consider When Evaluating a Redemption

Investors evaluate redemptions based on their personal financial timeline, asset allocation targets, and scheme fundamentals:

Common Reasons Investors Plan Redemptions

  • Milestone Goal Horizon: When a scheduled financial goal (such as higher education fees or down payment) approaches, investors often transition capital from volatile equities into stable debt or liquid funds well ahead of time to safeguard capital.
  • Asset Allocation Rebalancing: If market appreciation causes equities to expand beyond an investor's planned asset mix, redeeming a portion of equity gains to restore the target ratio is a disciplined risk management practice.
  • Fundamental Scheme Changes: Significant mandate changes, persistent benchmark underperformance across multiple market cycles, or changes in fund management may prompt investors to review their holdings.

Risks and Costs Associated with Hasty Exits

  • Reacting to Market Corrections: Redeeming during sharp market downturns locks in temporary paper declines into realized capital losses.
  • Tax and Exit Load Drag: Exiting before the completion of 365 days triggers short-term capital gains tax (20%) and applicable exit loads (typically 1%).
  • Compounding Interruption: Withdrawing funds prematurely removes capital from the compounding cycle, making long-term wealth accumulation more difficult.

5. Key Operational Checklist Before Placing a Redemption Order

  1. Check the Exit Load Window: Review your Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) or scheme factsheet to verify which unit tranches have crossed the exit load duration.
  2. Review Capital Gains Tax Slabs: Account for the distinction between STCG (holding period ≤ 12 months for equity) and LTCG (> 12 months) under current income tax regulations.
  3. Consider Gradual De-risking (STP): For upcoming financial commitments, some investors use a Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) over 6 to 18 months rather than redeeming an entire corpus on a single day.
  4. Verify Bank Account and KYC Status: Confirm that your registered bank details and IFSC code are active in the folio to prevent payment failures or physical cheque issuance.
  5. Maintain Tax Records: Download capital gains statements at the end of the financial year for accurate reporting in ITR-2 or ITR-3.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I cancel a redemption request after placing it?

If you placed your order before 3:00 PM on a business day, most platforms allow you to cancel the request online up until the 3:00 PM cut-off deadline. Once the 3:00 PM cut-off passes, the order is locked, transmitted to the AMC, and cannot be revoked.

What is the minimum redemption amount in mutual funds?

For most equity mutual funds in India, the minimum partial redemption amount is ₹500 or 50 units. If your remaining balance falls below the scheme's minimum account balance, the AMC may redeem the entire folio balance.

Does the AMC deduct TDS during mutual fund redemption for Indian resident individuals?

No. AMCs do not deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on mutual fund redemptions for resident Indian individuals. You are responsible for calculating your STCG/LTCG gains and paying applicable taxes via Advance Tax or during your annual ITR filing. (Note: TDS of 20% + surcharge applies to Non-Resident Indians / NRIs).

What happens if my registered bank account is closed when I redeem?

If the bank transfer fails due to a closed or invalid account, the AMC reverses the credit and issues a physical demand draft or banker's cheque to your registered postal address. To avoid this delay, update your bank account details via CAMS/KFintech before initiating redemption.

How to Think About This Yourself

Mutual fund redemption is an operational process, not an emotional reaction to market swings. Before submitting an order, inspect your unit statement to verify holding durations, potential exit loads, and tax categories. Calculate your net requirement and ensure that your banking credentials in the folio are current.

Where to Get Personalised Help

If you are planning significant withdrawals, rebalancing a complex multi-asset portfolio, or optimizing capital gains tax liabilities, consider consulting a fee-only SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) listed on the SEBI Intermediary Directory or a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA).
