Portfolio Rebalancing: Understanding Asset Allocation Drift & Mechanics

Key Conceptual Takeaways

  • Different asset classes grow at differing rates over time, causing the portfolio's actual asset allocation to diverge from its initial target weights.
  • Portfolio rebalancing is primarily a risk management mechanism designed to maintain consistent risk tolerance, rather than an active strategy to maximize returns.
  • Selling appreciated mutual fund units to rebalance triggers capital gains tax (LTCG / STCG) and potential exit loads.
  • Directing fresh periodic cash flows (SIPs or bonus inflows) toward underweight assets provides a mathematical way to adjust asset ratios without triggering redemption taxation.

1. Why Asset Allocation Drifts

When an investor constructs a portfolio across multiple asset classes (such as domestic equity and fixed-income debt), each asset class experiences different returns and volatility over time. During prolonged equity appreciation, equities grow faster than debt, causing equity to occupy an increasingly larger proportion of total portfolio value. This shifts the overall portfolio's risk profile to a higher volatility band than originally targeted.

2. Rebalancing Frameworks: Calendar vs. Threshold

In portfolio management theory, two common rebalancing approaches are recognized:

  • Calendar Rebalancing: Reviewing and adjusting portfolio weights at fixed time intervals (such as annually or semi-annually), regardless of the magnitude of market movements.
  • Threshold Rebalancing (e.g., 5/25 rule): Rebalancing only when an asset class deviates from its target allocation by a predetermined margin—for example, by 5 percentage points in absolute terms (e.g., a 60% equity target drifting past 65% or below 55%) or 25% in relative terms.

3. Execution Methods and Tax Considerations

Investors can execute rebalancing through two primary mechanics, each carrying distinct tax implications in India:

Execution Method Operational Mechanism Tax Consequence
Redemption & Reinvestment Sell overweight units; purchase underweight assets with proceeds Triggers capital gains tax (LTCG / STCG) on redeemed units and potential exit loads
Inflow Redirection Direct new periodic SIP installments or lumpsum surpluses to underweight asset Zero tax triggered (No sales or redemptions occur)
Multi-Asset / Hybrid Funds Fund manager dynamically adjusts equity/debt allocations within the scheme No tax liability for individual unit holders on internal trades

4. Hypothetical Rebalancing Walkthrough

Consider a hypothetical portfolio starting with ₹10,00,000 divided into an intended target ratio of 70% Equity (₹7,00,000) and 30% Debt (₹3,00,000). Over a simulated 2-year market cycle where equity grows 30% and debt yields 7%:

  • Equity value becomes: ₹7,00,000 × 1.30 = ₹9,10,000
  • Debt value becomes: ₹3,00,000 × 1.07 = ₹3,21,000
  • Total portfolio value: ₹12,31,000
  • Actual equity weight: ₹9,10,000 ÷ ₹12,31,000 = 73.9% (vs 70% target)
  • Actual debt weight: ₹3,21,000 ÷ ₹12,31,000 = 26.1% (vs 30% target)

To restore the original 70:30 allocation without liquidating equity, future new savings of approximately ₹1,00,000 can be directed toward the debt portion to bring its total value to approximately ₹4,21,000, realigning proportions without generating capital gains events.

5. How to Think About This Yourself

When reviewing whether your portfolio requires rebalancing, consider:

  • What was your original planned target allocation across equity, debt, and liquid instruments?
  • Has the drift exceeded your acceptable tolerance corridor (e.g., 5 percentage points)?
  • Can you realign weights simply by redirecting monthly fresh investments, avoiding capital gains taxation and exit loads?
Where to get personalised help: Determining the appropriate asset allocation for your personal goals and risk profile is a personalized exercise. Consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) via the directory at sebi.gov.in. For calculating exact capital gains and tax liability on mutual fund sales, consult a Chartered Accountant.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does portfolio rebalancing guarantee higher returns?

No. The primary purpose of portfolio rebalancing is risk control and maintaining volatility within an investor's comfort level. In a persistent bull market, rebalancing away from equities into debt may result in lower absolute returns than remaining fully invested in equities.

How is rebalancing between direct funds within the same AMC treated?

In Indian tax regulations, switching units between two schemes—even within the same fund house—is legally classified as a redemption followed by a fresh purchase, triggering applicable capital gains taxation.
