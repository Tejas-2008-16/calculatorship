Portfolio Rebalancing: How to Lock In Gains & Manage Risk in India

Portfolio rebalancing is the systematic process of resetting your asset allocation (e.g., 70% Equity / 30% Debt) back to its target weights after market movements cause one asset to drift. It enforces the fundamental rule of investing: selling high and buying low.

1. Why Portfolio Rebalancing Is Essential

During prolonged bull markets, equity assets outgrow fixed income, inadvertently increasing your portfolio's risk exposure. For example, a target 70% Equity / 30% Debt portfolio can drift to 85% Equity / 15% Debt after a sharp rally. If a severe market crash occurs, an un-rebalanced portfolio suffers disproportionate capital destruction.

2. The 5/25 Rebalancing Rule vs Calendar Rebalancing

  • Calendar Rebalancing: Review and rebalance portfolio weights once every year (e.g., every April during financial year opening). Simple and disciplined.
  • The 5/25 Rule (Opportunistic Trigger): Rebalance whenever an asset class deviates by 5% in absolute terms (e.g., 70% target rises to 75% or drops to 65%) or 25% in relative terms (for smaller allocations).

3. Tax-Efficient Rebalancing: Avoid Selling Friction

Selling equity units triggers 12.5% LTCG tax and potential exit loads. Smart investors rebalance using Cash Flow Redirection: instead of selling equity, redirect 100% of your monthly fresh salary SIPs and annual performance bonuses into debt funds until the target 70:30 ratio is restored without selling a single equity unit.

5. Rebalancing Frequency vs Cumulative Returns

Empirical research across 20-year rolling market cycles demonstrates that rebalancing once annually (or strictly when triggered by the 5/25 rule) produces optimal risk-adjusted Sharpe ratios. Daily or monthly rebalancing generates excessive transaction friction, brokerage fees, and short-term capital gains tax without improving returns.

For most Indian salaried investors accumulating wealth via monthly SIPs, implementing an annual review every April — coinciding with financial year closing and annual corporate appraisals — provides the ideal balance between emotional detachment, transaction simplicity, and long-term capital protection.

Tax-Efficient Rebalancing: Cash Flow Routing vs Asset Redemption

Selling profitable equity mutual funds to rebalance can trigger unnecessary capital gains taxes. Deploy these two zero-tax strategies first:

  • Strategy 1: Cash Flow Rebalancing (Zero Tax): Instead of selling equity units, redirect 100% of your incoming monthly SIPs and annual employer bonuses exclusively into the underweight asset class (debt funds or PPF) until the portfolio drifts back to its 70:30 target.
  • Strategy 2: Tax-Harvesting Rebalance: If selling equity is unavoidable, redeem only enough units to capture the ₹1,25,000 annual LTCG tax-free exemption under Section 112A, shifting proceeds to debt with zero tax liability.
  • The March 31 Pitfall: Avoid rebalancing blindly on March 31 when trading liquidity is thin and NAV cutoffs create settlement slippage. Perform rebalancing annually in mid-April or early May.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should an Indian retail investor rebalance their portfolio?

Rebalancing once a year (calendar rebalancing in March/April) or whenever your equity-debt allocation drifts by more than 5% is the optimal balance between risk management and tax efficiency.

Does rebalancing portfolio trigger capital gains tax in India?

Yes. If you rebalance by selling appreciated equity mutual fund units held over 12 months, LTCG above ₹1.25 Lakh is taxed at 12.5%. Rebalancing by redirecting fresh SIP inflows avoids this tax entirely.

What is the 5/25 rebalancing rule?

The 5/25 rule mandates rebalancing when an asset class deviates by 5% in absolute terms (e.g., 60% equity rises above 65%) or 25% in relative terms from its target weight.

Can I rebalance between direct equity mutual funds within the same AMC for free?

While AMCs do not charge commission to switch, a switch between funds is legally classified as a redemption followed by a purchase, triggering capital gains tax rules.

What happens if I never rebalance my portfolio?

Without rebalancing, your portfolio becomes excessively concentrated in whichever asset had the recent bull run, leaving you heavily exposed to catastrophic drawdowns when market cycles reverse.
