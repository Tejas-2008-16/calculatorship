Portfolio Rebalancing: How to Lock In Gains & Manage Risk in India

Portfolio rebalancing is the systematic process of resetting your asset allocation (e.g., 70% Equity / 30% Debt) back to its target weights after market movements cause one asset to drift. It enforces the fundamental rule of investing: selling high and buying low.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Portfolio rebalancing is the process of restoring your target asset allocation (e.g., 70% equity / 30% debt) after market movements cause drift — selling the outperforming asset class and buying the underperforming one.
  • Annual rebalancing is sufficient for most investors; quarterly rebalancing generates excessive tax friction without proportionate risk reduction based on Indian market volatility patterns.
  • Every rebalancing transaction that involves selling appreciated equity units triggers capital gains tax: 12.5% LTCG (units held 12+ months) or 20% STCG (under 12 months). Tax costs must be factored before executing rebalancing.
  • A tax-efficient rebalancing method: instead of selling overweight equity, redirect new SIP contributions and salary surplus to the underweight asset class (debt/gold) until target allocation is restored — zero tax triggered.
  • Balanced Advantage Funds (BAFs) rebalance internally between equity and debt based on valuation signals without triggering investor-level capital gains tax — making them uniquely tax-efficient for risk-conscious investors.

A 70:30 equity-to-debt portfolio left unrebalanced during a sustained equity bull run can drift to 90:10 without the investor touching a single button. At that allocation, the portfolio is far more volatile than the investor originally intended — and far more exposed to a sharp equity drawdown. Rebalancing is the discipline of restoring your intended risk profile before the market restores it for you, far more painfully.

Why Asset Allocation Drifts — and Why It Matters

Assume an investor starts with ₹10 Lakh in a 70:30 portfolio: ₹7 Lakh equity, ₹3 Lakh debt. After 2 years of equity bull market (equity grows 30%, debt grows 7%):

  • Equity portfolio: ₹7 Lakh × 1.30 = ₹9.10 Lakh
  • Debt portfolio: ₹3 Lakh × 1.07 = ₹3.21 Lakh
  • Total: ₹12.31 Lakh; New allocation: 73.9% equity / 26.1% debt

After 4 years at the same rate, equity can drift to 79%+ — far outside the original 70% target. If a 30% equity market correction hits now, the portfolio takes a much harder blow than the investor anticipated when they designed their 70:30 allocation.

The Annual Rebalancing Calendar for Indian Investors

The optimal time to review and rebalance your portfolio is April–May (start of the new financial year). Reasons:

  • Fresh ₹1.25 Lakh LTCG exemption resets on April 1 for new financial year.
  • ITR filing season (June–July) prompts you to review capital gains anyway.
  • Annual performance appraisals in March/April provide fresh surplus capital for new flow redirection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I rebalance during a market crash or bull run?

Both, actually — rebalancing is designed for both. During a market crash, equity falls below target allocation: buy more equity (redirect debt SIP flows temporarily, or sell small debt fund units and buy equity). During a bull run, equity rises above target: redirect new monthly SIP contributions to debt or gold to bring allocation back, avoiding taxable sell transactions. The discipline of rebalancing forces you to buy low and sell high systematically.

Action: Set a Rebalancing Alert Today

Open your Kuvera or MFCentral portfolio view and check your current equity-to-debt allocation. If it differs from your original target by more than 7%–10%, schedule a rebalancing review. Use the new flow redirection method first to avoid triggering taxes. Only if the drift is severe (15%+) should you consider selling appreciated units—and only after using the ₹1.25 Lakh annual LTCG exemption efficiently. Log this as an annual calendar reminder in April.
