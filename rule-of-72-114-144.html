The Rule of 72, 114 and 144: Instant Mental Math for Every Investor

You don't always need a calculator to estimate how your money grows. Three simple rules — the Rule of 72, 114 and 144 — let you compute doubling, tripling and quadrupling times in seconds. Every serious investor should have these numbers memorised.

The Rule of 72 (How to Estimate Doubling Time)

The Rule of 72 is a classic financial mental shortcut. It tells you approximately how many years it will take for your money to **double** at a given annual compound interest rate. Simply divide 72 by your expected annual return percentage!

Rule of 72 Formula
Years to Double = 72 ÷ Annual Rate of Return (%)

Example: At 12% equity CAGR → 72 ÷ 12 = 6 years to double
Example: At 7% bank FD rate → 72 ÷ 7 = ~10.3 years to double

Why 72? In logarithmic mathematics, the exact formula for doubling time is ln(2) / ln(1 + r) (~69.3/r). Financial planners use 72 because it is easily divisible by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, and 12, making mental math instantaneous on the go!

Look at how ₹1 Lakh doubles across different asset classes:

  • Bank FD at 7%: Takes ~10.3 years to double to ₹2 Lakhs.
  • Nifty 50 Index Fund at 12%: Takes just 6 years to double to ₹2 Lakhs!
  • Small-Cap Fund at 15%: Takes roughly 4.8 years to double.
Key Takeaway: At 12% returns, your money doubles every 6 years. In 24 years (4 doublings), ₹1 Lakh turns into ₹16 Lakhs!

The Rule of 114 (How to Estimate Tripling Time)

Want to know how long it takes for your investment to **triple** (grow 3x)? Simply divide 114 by your annual return rate!

Rule of 114 Formula
Years to Triple = 114 ÷ Annual Rate of Return (%)

Example: At 12% equity CAGR → 114 ÷ 12 = 9.5 years to triple
Example: At 7% bank FD rate → 114 ÷ 7 = ~16.3 years to triple

If you have ₹10 Lakhs saved today and want it to reach ₹30 Lakhs for a major milestone at 12% expected returns, you know mentally that you need roughly 9.5 years without pulling out a spreadsheet!

The Rule of 144 (How to Estimate Quadrupling Time)

To calculate how long it takes to **quadruple** (grow 4x) your money, divide 144 by your annual return rate.

Rule of 144 Formula
Years to Quadruple = 144 ÷ Annual Rate of Return (%)

Example: At 12% equity CAGR → 144 ÷ 12 = 12 years to quadruple
Example: At 8% return → 144 ÷ 8 = 18 years to quadruple

Notice the mathematical harmony: at 12% CAGR, your money doubles in 6 years (72 ÷ 12), triples in 9.5 years (114 ÷ 12), and quadruples in 12 years (144 ÷ 12). Since 72 × 2 = 144, doubling twice equals quadrupling!

Real-World Applications

These three rules give you instant clarity when evaluating real-world financial offers:

  • Spotting Unrealistic Schemes: If an unregulated scheme promises to quadruple your money in 4 years, test it: 144 ÷ 4 = 36% annual return! That is an immediate red flag for high-risk or fraudulent schemes.
  • Equity vs Fixed Deposit: An FD at 7% doubles your wealth in ~10 years. An equity mutual fund at 12% quadruples your wealth in 12 years. That's why long-term wealth requires equity exposure.

Inflation and These Rules

You can also use these rules to measure the erosion caused by inflation!

The Inflation Halving Rule: If India's inflation runs at 6%, apply the Rule of 72: 72 ÷ 6 = 12 years. That means your money's purchasing power **halves every 12 years** if kept in cash or low-yielding accounts!

Here's the inflation math in context:

  • At 6% inflation: Purchasing power halves in 12 years (Rule of 72)
  • At 6% inflation: Purchasing power falls to one-third in 19 years (Rule of 114)
  • At 6% inflation: Purchasing power falls to one-quarter in 24 years (Rule of 144)

Now layer your investments. If your FD earns 7% and inflation is 6%, your real return is just ~1%. At 1% real return, it takes 72 years to double in real terms. That's not wealth creation — that's barely staying above water. Equity investors targeting 12% with 6% inflation enjoy a 6% real return, meaning real doubling every 12 years.

Important: The Rule of 72, 114 and 144 are approximations — not precise calculations. They assume consistent, uninterrupted annual compounding and do not account for taxes, inflation, or variable returns. For accurate projections of your actual SIP or lumpsum investments, always use a dedicated calculator.

These three rules, taken together, give you a powerful mental model for evaluating virtually any investment opportunity. They help you spot unrealistic promises, benchmark different assets instantly, and understand inflation's compounding damage — all without a spreadsheet. Commit them to memory: 72, 114, 144. They may well be the three most important numbers in your investing life.
