Silver ETF vs Gold ETF in India (2026): Tax, Costs & How to Buy

When I was building the commodity tax and capital gains calculators on Calculatorship, the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024 brought a massive change that many retail investors missed. Under the revised capital gains framework, listed Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs now qualify for long-term capital gains (LTCG) status after just 12 months of holding, taxed at a flat twelve-point-five percent without indexation. Budget clarity ended months of confusion when commodity funds were lumped under Section 50AA slab-rate taxation.

With the Reserve Bank pausing fresh Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) issuances, demat metal ETFs have become the primary vehicle for precious metal allocation in India. Yet Gold and Silver behave very differently. Gold functions as a monetary hedge., while Silver is heavily driven by solar, EV, and electronics industrial demand. Here is the complete post-tax comparison, expense ratio analysis, and liquidity guide.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. 12-Month LTCG Threshold: Because Gold and Silver ETFs are listed securities, holding them for more than 12 months qualifies your profit as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) taxed at a flat twelve-point-five percent without indexation.
  • 2. Short-Term Gains at Slab: Selling either metal ETF within 12 months triggers Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG), adding the entire profit to your regular taxable income at your marginal slab rate (up to 30% plus cess).
  • 3. Zero Making Charges or Retail GST: Physical coins and jewellery carry 3% GST plus 5% to 15% in non-recoverable making charges. ETFs eliminate retail making waste, tracking spot bullion purity directly.
  • 4. Silver Is Twice as Volatile: Over 50% of global silver consumption is industrial (solar panels, electronics, electric vehicles). During industrial slowdowns, silver drops much faster than gold. Silver demand is cyclical.
  • 5. SGB Policy Reality: The 8-year maturity tax exemption on Sovereign Gold Bonds is now strictly limited to primary RBI subscribers who held continuously. Secondary market buyers on NSE/BSE no longer enjoy tax-free redemptions.
  • 6. Sensible Cap: Keep total precious metals to 5% to 10% of your broader net worth, treating them as a defensive inflation buffer rather than an aggressive growth engine.

Dhanteras Decisions: Physical Metal vs Digital Units?

Every October when Dhanteras rolls around, jewellery showrooms across Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Jaipur overflow with families waiting in line for gold coins, silver utensils, and jewellery. Physical coins feel auspicious, tangible, and deeply traditional. Looking at your bill on the drive home, however, the financial reality sets in: you paid a mandatory 3% Goods and Services Tax (GST) upfront, along with 8% to 15% in making charges that you will never recover when you sell.

If your goal is building wealth or creating a defensive hedge against inflation, buying physical metal in a retail showroom is honestly an uphill battle. Buyers start deep in the red before metal prices move a single rupee. Catching up stays an uphill climb. As a result, exchange-traded commodity funds (specifically Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs) have become the preferred route for disciplined investors. You get 99.5% to 99.9% purity backing, live stock-market liquidity, zero locker rent, and no making charge haircut.

Before you tap "buy" on your broking app, however, you need to understand how the two metals behave differently, how they are taxed under the current capital gains framework, and how recent Budget changes reshuffled the Sovereign Gold Bond ecosystem.

How Silver and Gold ETFs Actually Work Under SEBI Rules

Storage costs vanish. Purity stays guaranteed. Making charges disappear. Liquidity remains instant. Whenever retail savers transition away from traditional physical jewelry toward regulated SEBI-monitored commodity ETFs traded on Indian exchanges, they immediately eliminate opaque making fees, storage insurance worries, and purity discounts upon resale while holding dematerialized bullion units backed directly by vault-stored physical reserves.

Under Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) mutual fund regulations, both Gold and Silver ETFs must back at least 95% of their total assets with physical bullion stored in secure vaults insured against theft and damage. Vaults are audited regularly. Independent custodians verify stocks. Under SEBI guidelines, Gold ETF bullion must have a fineness of at least 995 parts per thousand (99.5% purity). In the case of Silver ETFs, introduced in 2022, SEBI mandates London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) Good Delivery bars with a minimum fineness of 999 parts per thousand (99.9% purity). Purity is legally non-negotiable.

Each time you purchase 1 unit of a Gold ETF (such as Nippon India ETF Gold BeES or HDFC Gold ETF), each unit typically represents approximately 0.01 gram or 1 gram of gold depending on the fund's denomination. Purchasing 1 unit of a Silver ETF (such as ICICI Prudential Silver ETF or Nippon India Silver ETF), each unit represents approximately 1 gram of physical silver.

Asset management companies procure standard wholesale bullion bars directly from accredited domestic and international suppliers. Because the trust buys at institutional spot rates, you bypass the retail markup completely. An independent custodian audits and verifies vault weights semi-annually, filing physical inspection reports with SEBI.

What is the Tax on Silver and Gold ETFs in India?

One date changes everything. Holding windows matter. Timing alters returns. Applying current Indian tax schedules following the removal of Section 50AA penalties on listed bullion exchange-traded funds, maintaining strict holding discipline past twelve full calendar months transforms punitive short-term slab taxation up to thirty percent directly into a predictable twelve-point-five percent long-term capital gains levy.

Gold and Silver ETFs held for more than 12 months qualify as long-term capital assets. They are taxed at a flat twelve-point-five percent rate without indexation. The 12-month clock is decisive. Selling within 12 months classifies your gains as short-term capital gains, taxed at your marginal slab rate. Check your purchase statement before selling.

Taxation on commodity mutual funds underwent substantial structural reform under the Finance Act framework. Historically, debt and commodity funds held past three years enjoyed indexation benefits. Back in 2023, the Union Budget briefly categorized them as short-term assets taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period, investors felt penalized. Thankfully, subsequent rationalization established listed exchange-traded funds as distinct financial assets:

  • Long-Term Threshold (12 Months): Because Gold and Silver ETFs are listed on recognized stock exchanges (NSE and BSE), their holding period threshold for long-term status is exactly 12 months. Twelve months is much shorter than physical gold or unlisted Fund of Funds, which require a 24-month holding period.
  • LTCG Tax Rate (twelve-point-five percent: Long-term gains realized after holding for 366 days or more are taxed at a flat twelve-point-five percent plus applicable 4% health and education cess. Note that unlike equity mutual funds, commodity ETFs do not receive the ₹1.25 lakh annual tax-free LTCG exemption under Section 112A. Every single rupee of long-term gain is taxed under Section 112.
  • STCG Tax Rate (Marginal Slab): If you sell your units after 6 months or 11 months, the gains are added to your gross income and taxed according to your regular income tax slab under either the Old vs New Tax Regime. In the top 30% tax bracket, short-term exits carry an effective tax burden of 31.2%. That hurts. (or higher with surcharge).

The ₹1.5 Lakh Exit Math: 11 Months vs 13 Months Compared

Waiting just two additional months to cross the 12-month holding threshold can save an Indian investor thousands of rupees in cash taxes. Consider Rahul, a salaried software engineer in Bengaluru who falls in the 30% tax bracket under the New Tax Regime. Rahul invests ₹5,00,000 into a Silver ETF. After experiencing a strong festive rally, his investment grows to ₹6,50,000, generating a clean capital gain of ₹1,50,000.

Let us look at Rahul's post-tax reality if he sells at 11 months versus waiting until 13 months:

Demat Route vs Fund of Funds (FoF) & Expense Ratios

Brokerage fees accumulate. Convenience costs extra. Always compare spreads. While direct demat exchange-traded fund investors routinely capture ultra-lean underlying tracking expenses alongside live market pricing during trading hours, salaried investors utilizing direct-plan bullion Fund of Funds conveniently automate monthly SIP mandates straight from bank accounts despite absorbing an extra layer of portfolio wrapper expenses every single year.

Investing in Silver or Gold ETFs requires an active demat and trading account with a registered Indian broker like Zerodha, Groww, AngelOne, or ICICI Direct. Trading occurs via market or limit orders during normal market hours (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM). Fast execution. However, what if you prefer automated monthly SIPs without logging into a stock terminal? FoFs bridge that brokerless gap.

A Silver or Gold Fund of Fund is an open-ended mutual fund scheme that pools your money and invests it directly into units of the underlying ETF. SIP mandates can be automated easily, just like any mutual fund SIP. Set and forget.. But convenience comes with two specific trade-offs:

  1. Double Expense Ratio Layer: The underlying ETF charges an annual total expense ratio (TER) of roughly 0.35% to 0.55% for vaulting, insurance, and AMC management. Investors also bear an underlying FoF fee of roughly 0.10% to 0.25% in scheme operating expenses. Over 10 years, that compounding layer chips away at your net returns.
  2. Holding Period Drag (24 Months vs 12 Months): Because FoFs are unlisted mutual funds, the tax law treats them differently from listed ETFs. An unlisted commodity FoF requires a 24-month holding period to achieve long-term capital gains classification. Redeeming an FoF between 12 and 24 months triggers tax at your regular slab rate, whereas an ETF investor pays twelve-point-five percent.

According to official factsheets from major fund houses (including Nippon India Mutual Fund, ICICI Prudential AMC, and HDFC AMC), direct ETF expense ratios consistently hover between 0.30% and 0.50%. Fees compound over time. To any saver comfortable trading on a demat app, buying the ETF directly is mathematically superior. You avoid redundant intermediary layers.

What Happened to SGBs? Budget 2026 SGB Update: Secondary Market Rule

Old exemptions tightened. Secondary buyers pay. Read tax clauses. Check dates carefully. Spreads dictate gains. Watch tracking error. Simplicity rewards patience. Investors acquiring existing Sovereign Gold Bond tranches through secondary stock exchange tickers rather than primary RBI subscriptions forfeit tax-free maturity exemptions under updated Budget 2026 interpretations, shifting substantial post-tax capital appreciation advantage squarely back toward transparent twelve-month listed bullion exchange-traded funds.

For almost a decade, Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) issued by the Reserve Bank of India were universally crowned the undisputed king of gold investments. They delivered a Fixed 2.50% annual interest payout and, most importantly, 100% tax-free capital gains upon 8-year maturity under Section 47 of the Income Tax Act. That era is changing. Supply dried up.

Many investors who missed original subscription tranches bought discounted SGB series trading on stock exchanges (NSE and BSE), assuming they would also pocket tax-free redemptions at maturity. Lawmakers closed that loophole starting 1 April 2026.

Under the revised statutory provision, tax exemption upon maturity is now strictly reserved for primary subscribers who applied during the original RBI issuance and held the bond continuously. Secondary-market SGB purchases no longer enjoy tax-free maturity; redemption proceeds at the end of 8 years are now treated as taxable capital gains subject to twelve-point-five percent LTCG tax. How Much Silver and Gold Should You Sensibly Hold?

Metals hedge currency risk. Overweight positions drag compounding. Discipline balances risk. Keeping precious metals capped between five and ten percent of household investable assets allows gold and industrial silver holdings to buffer steep equity bear drawdowns without sacrificing long-term real wealth creation powered by productive corporate earnings over multi-decade compounding horizons.

Financial planners recommend allocating no more than 5% to 10% of your total liquid investment portfolio to precious metals, keeping silver capped at 2% to 3% due to its sharp industrial volatility.

Retail buyers easily get caught up in festive enthusiasm, especially when commodity market commentary promises dramatic bull runs. Precious metals do not generate earnings, cash flows, or dividends. No payouts.. A tonne of gold locked in a vault will still be exactly one tonne fifty years from now. By contrast, equity mutual funds invest in productive businesses that manufacture products, hire talent, and compound profits year after year. Read our comparison of gold vs mutual funds to see how the historical wealth compounding numbers stack up over 20-year horizons.

Gold's true superpower in your portfolio is non-correlation. When geopolitical shocks strike or equity markets endure a sharp correction, gold prices usually hold firm or appreciate, dampening your total portfolio volatility. Silver, on the other hand, is a high-beta hybrid: during economic expansions with surging green-energy manufacturing, silver can sprint far ahead of gold. During recessions, manufacturing shutdowns can cause silver prices to crash violently.

Treat precious metals as your financial shock absorber, not your growth engine. For a ₹20 lakh portfolio, keeping ₹1 lakh in Gold ETFs and ₹50,000 in Silver ETFs gives you adequate crisis protection without starving your equity mutual fund SIPs of necessary compounding fuel.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the capital gains tax on Silver ETFs in India?

Because Silver ETFs are listed exchange-traded funds, holding them for more than 12 months qualifies them as long-term capital assets taxed at a flat twelve-point-five percent without indexation. Exiting before 12 months means short-term gains are taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate.

Can I buy Silver ETFs without having a demat account?

Yes. You can invest in Silver Fund of Funds (FoF) through any standard mutual fund investment app without a demat account. Keep in mind that Silver FoFs invest into the underlying Silver ETF, creating an additional small expense ratio and a 24-month holding requirement for long-term tax status.

Is GST charged when buying Gold or Silver ETFs?

No direct 3% GST is charged to retail investors when buying Gold or Silver ETF units on the stock exchange. Fund houses procure physical bullion at wholesale rates and absorb GST internally within NAV transactions, eliminating the upfront retail 3% GST and 5-15% jewellery making charges.

Did Budget 2026 remove the tax exemption on Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB)?

Statutory amendments restricted the 8-year maturity tax exemption strictly to original subscribers who bought bonds directly during RBI issuance windows and held them continuously. If you buy SGBs on the secondary stock market, your gains at maturity are no longer tax-free and face twelve-point-five percent LTCG tax.

Why is silver more volatile than gold as an investment?

Over 50% of global silver demand stems from industrial manufacturing including solar photovoltaic cells, electrical contacts, and electronics. As industrial output contracts, silver prices plunge faster than gold. Gold demand is primarily driven by central bank reserves and jewellery, providing steady defensive hedging.

How much of my portfolio should be allocated to Gold and Silver ETFs?

Most conservative financial planners suggest limiting precious metals to 5% to 10% of your total portfolio, with silver taking up no more than 2% to 3% due to its sharp price swings. Precious metals act as a currency hedge and inflation buffer rather than primary wealth compounding engines.

Actionable Next Step: Check Your Metal Holding Period in Your Demat Console

Before placing any sale orders during the festive season, open your broker's tax console or trade history tab. Check the exact acquisition dates of your Gold and Silver ETF holdings. Whenever you hold substantial profits and your units have crossed 10 or 11 months, consider waiting until they cross the 365-day threshold. That single scheduling adjustment transitions your tax rate from your 30% slab down to twelve-point-five percent, keeping more of your hard-earned festive returns in your bank account.
