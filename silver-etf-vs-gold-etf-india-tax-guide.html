Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. 12-Month LTCG Threshold: Because Gold and Silver ETFs are listed securities, holding them for more than 12 months qualifies your profit as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) taxed at a flat 12.5% without indexation.
  • 2. Short-Term Gains at Slab: Selling either metal ETF within 12 months triggers Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG), adding the entire profit to your regular taxable income at your marginal slab rate (up to 30% plus cess).
  • 3. Zero Making Charges or Retail GST: Physical coins and jewellery carry 3% GST plus 5% to 15% in non-recoverable making charges. ETFs eliminate retail making waste, tracking spot bullion purity directly.
  • 4. Silver Is Twice as Volatile: Over 50% of global silver consumption is industrial (solar panels, electronics, electric vehicles). When industrial activity cools, silver drops much faster than gold.
  • 5. Budget 2026 SGB Reality: The 8-year maturity tax exemption on Sovereign Gold Bonds is now strictly limited to primary RBI subscribers who held continuously. Secondary market buyers on NSE/BSE no longer enjoy tax-free redemptions.
  • 6. Sensible Cap: Keep total precious metals to 5% to 10% of your broader net worth, treating them as a defensive inflation buffer rather than an aggressive growth engine.

The Dhanteras Dilemma: Physical Metal or Digital Units?

Every October when Dhanteras rolls around, jewellery showrooms across Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Jaipur overflow with families waiting in line for gold coins, silver utensils, and jewellery. It feels auspicious, tangible, and deeply traditional. But when you look at your invoice on the drive home, the financial reality sets in: you paid a mandatory 3% Goods and Services Tax (GST) upfront, along with 8% to 15% in making charges that you will never recover when you sell.

If your goal is building wealth or creating a defensive hedge against inflation, buying physical metal in a retail showroom is honestly an uphill battle. You start deep in the red before the price of metal moves a single rupee. That is why exchange-traded commodity funds—specifically Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs—have become the preferred route for disciplined investors. You get 99.5% to 99.9% purity backing, live stock-market liquidity, zero locker rent, and no making charge haircut.

Before you tap "buy" on your broking app, however, you need to understand how the two metals behave differently, how they are taxed under the current capital gains framework, and how recent Budget changes reshuffled the Sovereign Gold Bond landscape.

How Silver and Gold ETFs Actually Work Under SEBI Rules

Under Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) mutual fund regulations, both Gold and Silver ETFs must back at least 95% of their total assets with physical bullion stored in secure vaults insured against theft and damage. For Gold ETFs, the underlying metal must have a fineness of at least 995 parts per thousand (99.5% purity). For Silver ETFs, introduced in 2022, SEBI mandates London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) Good Delivery bars with a minimum fineness of 999 parts per thousand (99.9% purity).

When you purchase 1 unit of a Gold ETF (such as Nippon India ETF Gold BeES or HDFC Gold ETF), each unit typically represents approximately 0.01 gram or 1 gram of gold depending on the fund's denomination. When you purchase 1 unit of a Silver ETF (such as ICICI Prudential Silver ETF or Nippon India Silver ETF), each unit represents approximately 1 gram of physical silver.

The mutual fund trust buys standard wholesale bullion bars directly from accredited domestic and international suppliers. Because the trust buys at institutional spot rates, you bypass the retail markup completely. An independent custodian audits and verifies vault weights semi-annually, filing physical inspection reports with SEBI.

What is the Tax on Silver and Gold ETFs in India?

Gold and Silver ETFs held for more than 12 months are treated as long-term capital assets and taxed at a flat 12.5% rate without indexation. If you sell within 12 months, your gains are classified as short-term capital gains and taxed at your marginal slab rate.

Taxation on commodity mutual funds underwent substantial structural reform under the Finance Act framework. Historically, debt and commodity funds held past three years enjoyed indexation benefits. When Budget 2023 briefly categorized them as short-term assets taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period, investors felt penalized. Thankfully, subsequent rationalization established listed exchange-traded funds as distinct financial assets:

  • Long-Term Threshold (12 Months): Because Gold and Silver ETFs are listed on recognized stock exchanges (NSE and BSE), their holding period threshold for long-term status is exactly 12 months. This is much shorter than physical gold or unlisted Fund of Funds, which require a 24-month holding period.
  • LTCG Tax Rate (12.5% Flat): Long-term gains realized after holding for 366 days or more are taxed at a flat 12.5% plus applicable 4% health and education cess. Note that unlike equity mutual funds, commodity ETFs do not receive the ₹1.25 lakh annual tax-free LTCG exemption under Section 112A. Every single rupee of long-term gain is taxed under Section 112.
  • STCG Tax Rate (Marginal Slab): If you sell your units after 6 months or 11 months, the gains are added to your gross income and taxed according to your regular income tax slab under either the Old vs New Tax Regime. For high earners in the 30% slab, short-term exits carry an effective tax burden of 31.2% (or higher with surcharge).

Waiting just two additional months to cross the 12-month holding threshold can save an Indian investor thousands of rupees in cash taxes. Consider Rahul, a salaried software engineer in Bengaluru who falls in the 30% tax bracket under the New Tax Regime. Rahul invests ₹5,00,000 into a Silver ETF. After experiencing a strong festive rally, his investment grows to ₹6,50,000—generating a clean capital gain of ₹1,50,000.

Demat Route vs Fund of Funds (FoF) & Expense Ratios

Investing in Silver or Gold ETFs requires an active demat and trading account with a SEBI-registered stockbroker. Orders are executed during normal market trading hours (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM). Alternatively, investors who prefer automated periodic contributions without operating a trading terminal can look at Fund of Funds (FoFs).

A Silver or Gold Fund of Fund is an open-ended mutual fund scheme that pools capital to invest directly into units of the underlying ETF. It can be accessed through direct AMC portals, MF Central, or mutual fund distributors via automated bank mandates. However, there are two distinct structural differences:

  1. Dual Expense Ratio Layer: The underlying ETF charges an annual total expense ratio (TER) typically ranging from 0.30% to 0.55% for vaulting, physical custody, insurance, and management. The Fund of Fund adds an additional scheme operating expense layer (around 0.10% to 0.25%). Over multi-year holding periods, this recurring layer reduces net compounding.
  2. Holding Period Classification (24 Months vs 12 Months): Because FoFs are unlisted mutual funds, statutory tax provisions treat them differently from listed ETFs. An unlisted commodity FoF requires a 24-month holding period to qualify for Long-Term Capital Gains classification. Redemptions between 12 and 24 months are taxed at applicable income tax slab rates, whereas listed ETF units qualify as LTCG after 12 months at 12.5%.

Factsheets published by mutual fund asset management companies provide transparent disclosure of daily TERs and tracking errors relative to domestic spot bullion prices.

What Happened to SGBs? The Budget Secondary Market Provisions

Historically, Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) issued by the Reserve Bank of India offered a 2.50% annual coupon payout and a statutory capital gains tax exemption upon 8-year maturity under Section 47 of the Income Tax Act.

Following rationalisation of capital gains under the Finance Act, secondary market purchases of SGBs on stock exchanges are distinguished from primary issuances. Redemption proceeds on maturity for original RBI subscribers who held continuously retain specific statutory protections, whereas secondary-market acquisitions on stock exchanges are subject to capital gains assessment (12.5% LTCG if held for more than 12 months as listed debt/bonds). Role of Precious Metals in Asset Allocation

In portfolio theory literature, precious metals are frequently analyzed for their diversification properties rather than cash-flow generation. Gold and silver do not produce operational profits, internal cash yields, or dividends. A vault holding one kilogram of bullion maintains that exact physical weight indefinitely. In contrast, equity mutual funds represent ownership interests in companies that produce goods and services and generate reinvestable earnings.

Gold's historical role in a multi-asset framework has been low correlation to equities during economic disruptions. Silver exhibits higher cyclical sensitivity: industrial applications (solar photovoltaic manufacturing, electronics, and automotive wiring) drive substantial demand, leading to wider price swings during changes in global industrial activity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the capital gains tax on Silver ETFs in India?

Because Silver ETFs are listed exchange-traded funds, holding them for more than 12 months qualifies them as long-term capital assets taxed at a flat 12.5% without indexation. If sold within 12 months, the short-term gains are taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate.

Can I buy Silver ETFs without having a demat account?

Yes. Investors can access Silver Fund of Funds (FoF) through direct AMC platforms or MF Central without a demat account. Note that FoFs invest into underlying ETF units, introducing a fund-level expense ratio and requiring a 24-month holding period for long-term capital gains classification.

Is GST charged when buying Gold or Silver ETFs?

No separate 3% retail GST is billed to investors on stock exchange transactions. The mutual fund trust purchases physical bullion at institutional rates and reflects operational costs in the scheme NAV, avoiding retail making and storage premiums.

How are secondary-market SGB purchases taxed?

Sovereign Gold Bonds purchased on the secondary market of stock exchanges are subject to capital gains tax upon transfer or redemption. Holding them for more than 12 months classifies them as long-term capital gains under the rationalised capital gains framework.

Why is silver price volatility typically higher than gold?

A substantial portion of global silver demand stems from industrial manufacturing including solar panels and electronics. Fluctuations in industrial output influence silver consumption alongside investment demand, creating wider cyclical price swings compared to gold.

How to think about this yourself

  • Holding structure: Listed ETFs offer 12-month LTCG qualification (12.5%) and lower recurring expense ratios but require a demat account and exchange trading. Unlisted Fund of Funds (FoF) allow automated bank mandates but require a 24-month holding period for LTCG and incur an additional scheme expense layer.
  • Tax calendar awareness: For listed ETFs, crossing the 365-day threshold changes taxation from applicable slab rates to 12.5% flat LTCG. Reviewing acquisition dates in trade confirmations helps avoid unintended short-term realization.
  • Functional role: Evaluating whether precious metals serve as short-term trading instruments or long-term portfolio diversification cushions helps guide decisions without relying on festive marketing campaigns.
