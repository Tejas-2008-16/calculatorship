Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. 12-Month LTCG Threshold: Because Gold and Silver ETFs are listed securities, holding them for more than 12 months qualifies your profit as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) taxed at a flat 12.5% without indexation.
  • 2. Short-Term Gains at Slab: Selling either metal ETF within 12 months triggers Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG), adding the entire profit to your regular taxable income at your marginal slab rate (up to 30% plus cess).
  • 3. Zero Making Charges or Retail GST: Physical coins and jewellery carry 3% GST plus 5% to 15% in non-recoverable making charges. ETFs eliminate retail making waste, tracking spot bullion purity directly.
  • 4. Silver Is Twice as Volatile: Over 50% of global silver consumption is industrial (solar panels, electronics, electric vehicles). When industrial activity cools, silver drops much faster than gold.
  • 5. Budget 2026 SGB Reality: The 8-year maturity tax exemption on Sovereign Gold Bonds is now strictly limited to primary RBI subscribers who held continuously. Secondary market buyers on NSE/BSE no longer enjoy tax-free redemptions.
  • 6. Sensible Cap: Keep total precious metals to 5% to 10% of your broader net worth, treating them as a defensive inflation buffer rather than an aggressive growth engine.

The Dhanteras Dilemma: Physical Metal or Digital Units?

Every October when Dhanteras rolls around, jewellery showrooms across Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Jaipur overflow with families waiting in line for gold coins, silver utensils, and jewellery. It feels auspicious, tangible, and deeply traditional. But when you look at your invoice on the drive home, the financial reality sets in: you paid a mandatory 3% Goods and Services Tax (GST) upfront, along with 8% to 15% in making charges that you will never recover when you sell.

If your goal is building wealth or creating a defensive hedge against inflation, buying physical metal in a retail showroom is honestly an uphill battle. You start deep in the red before the price of metal moves a single rupee. That is why exchange-traded commodity funds—specifically Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs—have become the preferred route for disciplined investors. You get 99.5% to 99.9% purity backing, live stock-market liquidity, zero locker rent, and no making charge haircut.

Before you tap "buy" on your broking app, however, you need to understand how the two metals behave differently, how they are taxed under the current capital gains framework, and how recent Budget changes reshuffled the Sovereign Gold Bond landscape.

How Silver and Gold ETFs Actually Work Under SEBI Rules

Under Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) mutual fund regulations, both Gold and Silver ETFs must back at least 95% of their total assets with physical bullion stored in secure vaults insured against theft and damage. For Gold ETFs, the underlying metal must have a fineness of at least 995 parts per thousand (99.5% purity). For Silver ETFs, introduced in 2022, SEBI mandates London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) Good Delivery bars with a minimum fineness of 999 parts per thousand (99.9% purity).

When you purchase 1 unit of a Gold ETF (such as Nippon India ETF Gold BeES or HDFC Gold ETF), each unit typically represents approximately 0.01 gram or 1 gram of gold depending on the fund's denomination. When you purchase 1 unit of a Silver ETF (such as ICICI Prudential Silver ETF or Nippon India Silver ETF), each unit represents approximately 1 gram of physical silver.

The mutual fund trust buys standard wholesale bullion bars directly from accredited domestic and international suppliers. Because the trust buys at institutional spot rates, you bypass the retail markup completely. An independent custodian audits and verifies vault weights semi-annually, filing physical inspection reports with SEBI.

What is the Tax on Silver and Gold ETFs in India?

Gold and Silver ETFs held for more than 12 months are treated as long-term capital assets and taxed at a flat 12.5% rate without indexation. If you sell within 12 months, your gains are classified as short-term capital gains and taxed at your marginal slab rate.

Taxation on commodity mutual funds underwent substantial structural reform under the Finance Act framework. Historically, debt and commodity funds held past three years enjoyed indexation benefits. When Budget 2023 briefly categorized them as short-term assets taxed at slab rates regardless of holding period, investors felt penalized. Thankfully, subsequent rationalization established listed exchange-traded funds as distinct financial assets:

  • Long-Term Threshold (12 Months): Because Gold and Silver ETFs are listed on recognized stock exchanges (NSE and BSE), their holding period threshold for long-term status is exactly 12 months. This is much shorter than physical gold or unlisted Fund of Funds, which require a 24-month holding period.
  • LTCG Tax Rate (12.5% Flat): Long-term gains realized after holding for 366 days or more are taxed at a flat 12.5% plus applicable 4% health and education cess. Note that unlike equity mutual funds, commodity ETFs do not receive the ₹1.25 lakh annual tax-free LTCG exemption under Section 112A. Every single rupee of long-term gain is taxed under Section 112.
  • STCG Tax Rate (Marginal Slab): If you sell your units after 6 months or 11 months, the gains are added to your gross income and taxed according to your regular income tax slab under either the Old vs New Tax Regime. For high earners in the 30% slab, short-term exits carry an effective tax burden of 31.2% (or higher with surcharge).

The ₹1.5 Lakh Exit Math: 11 Months vs 13 Months Compared

Waiting just two additional months to cross the 12-month holding threshold can save an Indian investor thousands of rupees in cash taxes. Consider Rahul, a salaried software engineer in Bengaluru who falls in the 30% tax bracket under the New Tax Regime. Rahul invests ₹5,00,000 into a Silver ETF. After experiencing a strong festive rally, his investment grows to ₹6,50,000—generating a clean capital gain of ₹1,50,000.

Let us look at Rahul's post-tax reality if he sells at 11 months versus waiting until 13 months:

Demat Route vs Fund of Funds (FoF) & Expense Ratios

Investing in Silver or Gold ETFs requires an active demat and trading account with a registered Indian broker like Zerodha, Groww, AngelOne, or ICICI Direct. You place market or limit orders during normal trading hours (9:15 AM to 3:30 PM). However, what if you prefer automated monthly SIPs without logging into a stock terminal? That is where Fund of Funds (FoFs) come in.

A Silver or Gold Fund of Fund is an open-ended mutual fund scheme that pools your money and invests it directly into units of the underlying ETF. You can set up an automated bank mandate just like a standard mutual fund SIP. But convenience comes with two specific trade-offs:

  1. Double Expense Ratio Layer: The underlying ETF charges an annual total expense ratio (TER) of roughly 0.35% to 0.55% for vaulting, insurance, and AMC management. The Fund of Fund adds an additional 0.10% to 0.25% in scheme operating expenses. Over 10 years, that compounding layer chips away at your net returns.
  2. Holding Period Drag (24 Months vs 12 Months): Because FoFs are unlisted mutual funds, the tax law treats them differently from listed ETFs. An unlisted commodity FoF requires a 24-month holding period to achieve long-term capital gains classification. If you redeem an FoF between 12 and 24 months, you pay tax at your slab rate, whereas an ETF investor pays 12.5%.

According to official factsheets from major fund houses (including Nippon India Mutual Fund, ICICI Prudential AMC, and HDFC AMC), direct ETF expense ratios consistently hover between 0.30% and 0.50%. For anyone comfortable placing trades on a demat app, buying the ETF directly is mathematically superior.

What Happened to SGBs? The Budget 2026 Secondary Market Rule

For almost a decade, Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) issued by the Reserve Bank of India were universally crowned the undisputed king of gold investments. They delivered a Fixed 2.50% annual interest payout and, most importantly, 100% tax-free capital gains upon 8-year maturity under Section 47 of the Income Tax Act.

Many investors who missed original subscription tranches bought discounted SGB series trading on stock exchanges (NSE and BSE), assuming they would also pocket tax-free redemptions at maturity. Budget 2026 put an end to that loophole from 1 April 2026.

The revised statutory provision stipulates that the tax exemption upon maturity is now strictly reserved for primary subscribers who applied during the original RBI issuance and held the bond continuously. If you purchased an SGB from the secondary market, your redemption proceeds at the end of 8 years are now treated as taxable capital gains subject to 12.5% LTCG tax. How Much Silver and Gold Should You Sensibly Hold?

Financial planners recommend allocating no more than 5% to 10% of your total liquid investment portfolio to precious metals, keeping silver capped at 2% to 3% due to its sharp industrial volatility.

It is easy to get caught up in festive enthusiasm, especially when commodity market commentary promises dramatic bull runs. But gold and silver do not generate earnings, cash flows, or dividends. A tonne of gold locked in a vault will still be exactly one tonne fifty years from now. By contrast, equity mutual funds invest in productive businesses that manufacture products, hire talent, and compound profits year after year. Read our comparison of gold vs mutual funds to see how the historical wealth compounding numbers stack up over 20-year horizons.

Gold's true superpower in your portfolio is non-correlation. When geopolitical shocks strike or equity markets endure a sharp correction, gold prices usually hold firm or appreciate, dampening your total portfolio volatility. Silver, on the other hand, is a high-beta hybrid: during economic expansions with surging green-energy manufacturing, silver can sprint far ahead of gold. But during recessions, industrial plant shutdowns can cause silver prices to crash violently.

Treat precious metals as your financial shock absorber, not your growth engine. If your portfolio is worth ₹20 lakh, keeping ₹1 lakh in Gold ETFs and ₹50,000 in Silver ETFs gives you adequate crisis protection without starving your equity mutual fund SIPs of necessary compounding fuel.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the capital gains tax on Silver ETFs in India?

Because Silver ETFs are listed exchange-traded funds, holding them for more than 12 months qualifies them as long-term capital assets taxed at a flat 12.5% without indexation. If sold within 12 months, the short-term gains are taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate.

Can I buy Silver ETFs without having a demat account?

Yes. You can invest in Silver Fund of Funds (FoF) through any standard mutual fund investment app without a demat account. Keep in mind that Silver FoFs invest into the underlying Silver ETF, creating an additional small expense ratio and a 24-month holding requirement for long-term tax status.

Is GST charged when buying Gold or Silver ETFs?

No direct 3% GST is charged to retail investors when buying Gold or Silver ETF units on the stock exchange. The mutual fund trust procures physical bullion at wholesale rates and absorbs GST internally within NAV transactions, eliminating the upfront retail 3% GST and 5-15% jewellery making charges.

Did Budget 2026 remove the tax exemption on Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB)?

Budget 2026 restricted the 8-year maturity tax exemption strictly to original subscribers who bought bonds directly during RBI issuance windows and held them continuously. If you buy SGBs on the secondary stock market, your gains at maturity are no longer tax-free and face 12.5% LTCG tax.

Why is silver more volatile than gold as an investment?

Over 50% of global silver demand stems from industrial manufacturing including solar photovoltaic cells, electrical contacts, and electronics. When industrial production slows, silver prices plunge faster than gold. Gold demand is primarily driven by central bank reserves and jewellery, providing steady defensive hedging.

How much of my portfolio should be allocated to Gold and Silver ETFs?

Most conservative financial planners suggest limiting precious metals to 5% to 10% of your total portfolio, with silver taking up no more than 2% to 3% due to its sharp price swings. Precious metals act as a currency hedge and inflation buffer rather than primary wealth compounding engines.

Actionable Next Step: Check Your Metal Holding Period in Your Demat Console

Before placing any sale orders during the festive season, open your broker's tax console or trade history tab. Check the exact acquisition dates of your Gold and Silver ETF holdings. If you are sitting on substantial profits and your units have been held for 10 or 11 months, consider waiting until they cross the 365-day threshold. That single scheduling adjustment transitions your tax rate from your 30% slab down to 12.5%, keeping more of your hard-earned festive returns in your bank account.
