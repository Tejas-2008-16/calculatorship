Old vs New Tax Regime in India (FY 2026-27): Complete Comparison Guide

Tejas | Published: February 2026 | Educational Resource

⚠️ Educational Disclaimer: This article is authored by Tejas for educational purposes only. Tejas is not a Chartered Accountant (CA) or SEBI-registered tax advisor. Indian income tax provisions and slab structures are governed by the Finance Act and notifications of the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT).

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. The New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) is the default tax regime in India with an enhanced ₹75,000 Standard Deduction for salaried earners in FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28).
  • 2. Salaried employees earning up to ₹7,75,000 gross annual income pay ₹0 income tax under the New Regime after the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction and the Section 87A rebate.
  • 3. The breakeven deduction threshold at ₹15 Lakh gross salary is ₹4,25,000: unless your combined deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest) exceed this exact figure, the New Regime leaves more take-home cash in your bank account.
  • 4. At ₹25 Lakh gross salary, even claiming ₹4.50 Lakh in itemized deductions under the Old Regime results in higher total tax—the New Regime saves you ₹36,400 in hard cash because the 30% slab starts only above ₹15 Lakh.
  • 5. Section 80C, Section 80D, and HRA exemptions provide zero tax benefit under the New Regime, turning ELSS mutual funds and tax-saving fixed deposits into purely voluntary choices.
  • 6. Employer NPS contributions under Section 80CCD(2) (up to 14% of Basic + DA) remain fully deductible under both the Old and New Tax Regimes.
  • 7. Salaried employees can switch between regimes every single financial year when filing ITR-1 or ITR-2 before the July 31 deadline, irrespective of what was declared to payroll HR in April.

The Breakeven Math That Settles the Regime Debate in 60 Seconds

You have ₹15 Lakh listed as your annual CTC, and your company HR portal asks for your tax regime election every April. Most salaried employees reflexively select the Old Regime, scramble every February to buy ₹1.5 Lakh in ELSS or insurance policies, hunt down landlord PANs for HRA slips, and still pay more tax than their colleague who chose the New Regime and submitted zero paperwork.

The math is absolute for FY 2026-27: unless your total deductions exceed ₹4,25,000 at a ₹15 Lakh salary, or ₹3,75,000 at a ₹12.5 Lakh salary, the Old Regime loses. Over 70% of Indian salaried professionals who think they are saving money under the Old Regime are burning cash through sub-optimal investments just to chase tax deductions that lower slab rates already beat.

Consider a software engineer in Pune earning ₹15,00,000 gross salary. She pays ₹25,000 monthly rent (claiming ₹1,80,000 HRA exemption), exhausts her ₹1,50,000 Section 80C via EPF and PPF, and pays ₹25,000 for family health insurance under Section 80D. Total deductions under Old Regime: ₹4,05,000 (including ₹50,000 standard deduction).

The Three Specific Situations Where the Old Regime Still Wins

The Old Regime is not obsolete for everyone. It is a specialized tool that wins in three specific household structures:

  1. You are servicing an active home loan on a self-occupied flat: Section 24(b) permits a deduction of up to ₹2,00,000 on home loan interest. Combine ₹2,00,000 interest with ₹1,50,000 80C principal and the ₹50,000 standard deduction, and your base deductions already hit ₹4,00,000 before adding health insurance.
  2. You pay steep metro rent in Mumbai, Bengaluru, or Gurugram: If your annual rent exceeds ₹35,000 per month, your eligible HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) frequently crosses ₹2,50,000 to ₹3,50,000. Stacked on top of 80C and 80D, total deductions easily breach ₹4,75,000.
  3. You pay high medical premiums for senior citizen parents: Under Section 80D, premiums paid for senior citizen parents (aged 60+) are deductible up to ₹50,000, in addition to ₹25,000 for yourself. If you also claim Section 80DD or 80U for dependent disability (up to ₹1,25,000), itemized deductions exceed the New Regime advantage.

The True Opportunity Cost of 80C Tax Traps

Many investors choose the Old Regime because they believe it "forces them to save." This psychological crutch comes at a devastating financial cost. To claim a ₹1.5 Lakh deduction under Section 80C, millions of Indians lock capital into traditional endowment life insurance policies or ULIPs yielding 4.5% to 5.5% annualized returns.

Doing the math reveals the damage: saving ₹46,800 in tax (at the 30% slab) by committing ₹1,50,000 annually into a 5% endowment plan produces ₹19.9 Lakh after 10 years. Switching to the New Regime, paying the ₹14,000 extra tax difference, and investing that same ₹1,50,000 into a low-cost Nifty 50 index fund (compounding historically at 12% to 13% CAGR) produces ₹29.5 Lakh over the same decade. Forcing investments to chase tax deductions costs over ₹9.6 Lakh in forfeited compound wealth.

Switching Rules: Salaried vs Business Professionals

A widespread misconception is that selecting a regime on your employer's HR payroll portal in April binds you for the year. This is legally incorrect under Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) circulars:

For Salaried Employees (ITR-1 and ITR-2): You can select the New Regime with your employer for monthly TDS deductions, and then freely switch to the Old Regime when filing your return under Section 139(1) in July if your actual deductions turn out higher. You can switch back and forth every financial year without penalty.

For Freelancers and Business Owners (ITR-3 and ITR-4): The rule is drastically stricter under Section 115BAC(6). Non-salaried professionals with business or professional income who opt out of the New Regime to the Old Regime are granted only one lifetime opportunity to switch back to the New Regime. Once they re-enter the New Regime, they can never return to the Old Regime again unless their business income ceases permanently.

Frequently Asked Questions

My HR deducted tax under the New Regime all year. Can I file my July ITR under the Old Regime and get a refund?

Yes. The regime chosen on your company payroll portal is solely an interim estimate for monthly TDS under Section 192. When filing your ITR-1 or ITR-2 before July 31 on the income tax portal, you can select the Old Regime, enter your 80C, 80D, and HRA proofs, and the Income Tax Department will refund the excess TDS directly to your verified bank account.

I earn ₹7.6 Lakh annual salary. Will I pay zero tax under the New Regime?

Yes, exactly ₹0. Salaried employees receive a flat ₹75,000 Standard Deduction, reducing your taxable income from ₹7,60,000 down to ₹6,85,000. Because your net taxable income is below ₹7,00,000, Section 87A provides a full rebate that reduces your computed tax liability to zero.

Can I claim my home loan interest under the New Tax Regime?

No. For a self-occupied property, home loan interest under Section 24(b) (up to ₹2,00,000) is strictly disallowed under the New Regime. If your home is rented out to a tenant, interest can still be set off against actual rental income, but losses from house property cannot be set off against your salary income.

What happens to my employer NPS contribution if I switch to the New Tax Regime?

Your employer contribution to NPS under Section 80CCD(2) remains fully tax-deductible up to 14% of your Basic + Dearness Allowance (DA) under both Old and New Tax Regimes. It is one of the very few corporate tax benefits preserved in the New Regime.

I have no home loan and pay ₹15,000 monthly rent. Is Old Regime worth it at ₹12 Lakh salary?

No. At ₹12 Lakh salary, your breakeven deduction is ₹3,62,500. With ₹1.5 Lakh under 80C, ₹50,000 standard deduction, and roughly ₹1,10,000 eligible HRA, your total deductions reach ₹3,10,000. Choosing the New Regime will save you approximately ₹10,920 in annual tax while freeing you from submitting rent receipts.

If I stop investing in ELSS funds because I moved to the New Regime, where should that money go?

Redirect that capital into a direct, low-cost broad-market index fund (such as UTI Nifty 50 Direct Plan) or an active flexi-cap fund (like Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Direct Plan). You eliminate the mandatory 3-year lock-in of ELSS while enjoying full liquidity and an identical long-term equity growth trajectory.

Actionable Next Step: How to Check Your Number Today

Pull out your Form 16 or December payslip today. Sum up your actual non-negotiable deductions: EPF contribution, eligible rent paid minus 10% of basic, and home loan interest certificate from your bank. If the total is less than ₹3,75,000 (for salaries up to ₹12L) or less than ₹4,25,000 (for salaries around ₹15L), log in to your employer payroll portal and switch your declaration to the New Tax Regime. Take the monthly cash savings and start a disciplined SIP in an equity index fund instead of buying insurance policies you do not need.

These figures and thresholds reflect statutory tax provisions under the Income Tax Act for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28); your specific tax liability depends on your exact salary structure and exemptions, which a qualified Chartered Accountant can evaluate for your scenario.
