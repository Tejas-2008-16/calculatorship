Old vs New Tax Regime in India (FY 2026-27): Complete Comparison Guide

Written by: Tejas (Independent Personal Finance Creator • YouTube Channel)
Published: February 2026 • Last Fact-Checked: September 2026 • Reading Time: 12 mins
⚠️ Educational Disclaimer: This article is authored by Tejas for educational purposes only. Tejas is not a Chartered Accountant (CA) or SEBI-registered tax advisor. Indian income tax provisions and slab structures are governed by the Finance Act and notifications of the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT).

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • The New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) is the default tax regime in India, featuring lower tax slab rates and an enhanced ₹75,000 Standard Deduction.
  • Salaried employees earning up to ₹7,75,000 gross annual income pay ZERO income tax under the New Regime due to Section 87A full rebate.
  • The Old Tax Regime remains superior only for taxpayers with substantial deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, and ₹2 Lakh Home Loan Interest under Section 24b) exceeding breakeven thresholds.
  • Salaried individuals can freely switch between regimes every financial year while filing their annual Income Tax Return (ITR).
  • Employer NPS contributions under Section 80CCD(2) remain fully tax-deductible under both the Old and New Tax Regimes.

Introduction: The Shifting Landscape of Indian Income Tax

In recent Union Budgets, the Ministry of Finance has fundamentally reshaped the Indian taxation landscape to establish the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) as the primary, default taxation system. By offering wide, concessional slab brackets, raising the standard deduction to ₹75,000, and extending the Section 87A tax rebate up to ₹7,00,000 of taxable income, the government has eliminated income tax for millions of middle-class earners.

However, the Old Tax Regime has been preserved for taxpayers who actively leverage itemized deductions. If you service a substantial home loan, pay high city rent, maintain health insurance for elderly parents, and max out provident fund investments, the Old Regime may still save you significant tax.

This comprehensive guide breaks down the exact slab structures for FY 2026-27 (Assessment Year 2027-28), reveals the deduction breakeven mathematical formulas, compares tax liabilities across salary bands, and provides clear actionable frameworks to choose your optimal regime.

Itemized Deductions: What You Keep vs What You Forfeit

The core tradeoff between regimes lies in deductions:

Deductions FORFEITED in the New Tax Regime:

  • Section 80C: Up to ₹1,50,000 (EPF, PPF, ELSS, Life Insurance, School Tuition, Home Loan Principal).
  • Section 80D: Up to ₹25,000 (Self/Family) + ₹50,000 (Senior Parents) for health insurance.
  • Section 24(b): Up to ₹2,00,000 for self-occupied home loan interest.
  • Section 10(13A): House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption.
  • Section 10(5): Leave Travel Allowance (LTA).
  • Section 80TTA / 80TTB: Savings account and FD interest deductions.

Deductions RETAINED in the New Tax Regime:

  • Standard Deduction: ₹75,000 for salaried employees and pensioners.
  • Section 80CCD(2): Employer contribution to NPS (up to 14% of Basic + DA).
  • Section 80JJAA: Deduction for new employment generation.

Key Insight: For an individual earning ₹15 Lakh, you must claim more than ₹4.25 Lakh in total deductions (Standard Deduction + 80C + 80D + HRA/Home Loan) to make the Old Regime worthwhile. If your deductions total only ₹2.5 Lakh (80C + 80D), the New Regime is mathematically cheaper by over ₹35,000!

Strategic Decision Checklist: Which Regime Should You Pick?

  • Pick the NEW Tax Regime If:
    • Your gross income is below ₹7.75 Lakh (you pay ₹0 tax).
    • You do not have a home loan or high rent payments.
    • You prefer investing in high-return equity mutual funds rather than locking money in 80C traditional insurance or tax-saving FDs.
    • You want simple, paperless ITR filing without submitting expense proofs to HR.
  • Pick the OLD Tax Regime If:
    • You are servicing an active home loan (claiming up to ₹2L interest under Section 24b and principal under 80C).
    • You pay substantial metro rent and claim significant HRA tax exemption.
    • Your total combined deductions comfortably exceed ₹4.50 Lakh to ₹5.00 Lakh.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can salaried employees switch between Old and New Tax Regimes every year?

Yes. Salaried employees and pensioners with no business or professional income have the legal freedom to choose between the Old and New Tax Regimes each financial year directly when filing their Income Tax Return (ITR) under Section 139(1).

What is the enhanced Standard Deduction in the New Tax Regime for FY 2026-27?

For salaried individuals and pensioners, the Standard Deduction under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) is ₹75,000. Under the Old Tax Regime, the Standard Deduction remains ₹50,000.

What is the zero-tax income threshold under the New Tax Regime?

For salaried individuals, a gross annual income up to ₹7,75,000 results in zero income tax due to the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction combined with the full Section 87A rebate for taxable income up to ₹7,00,000.

Can home loan interest and principal be claimed under the New Tax Regime?

No. Neither home loan interest under Section 24(b) (up to ₹2,00,000 for self-occupied properties) nor principal repayments under Section 80C can be claimed under the New Tax Regime. They are only deductible under the Old Tax Regime.

Which deductions are still allowed under the New Tax Regime?

The New Tax Regime permits the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction for salaried employees, transport allowance for specially-abled individuals, conveyance allowance for official duties, and employer contributions to the National Pension System (NPS) under Section 80CCD(2) up to 14% of Basic + DA.

What is the breakeven deduction threshold between Old and New Regimes for ₹15 Lakh salary?

For a gross annual salary of ₹15,00,000, an employee needs total itemized deductions exceeding approximately ₹4,25,000 to ₹4,50,000 (such as 80C + 80D + HRA/Home Loan Interest + Standard Deduction) for the Old Tax Regime to yield lower tax than the New Regime.
