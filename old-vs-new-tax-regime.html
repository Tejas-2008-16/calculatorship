Old vs New Tax Regime in India: Complete Comparison for FY 2026-27

The debate between the Old and New Income Tax Regimes is the single largest annual tax optimization decision for Indian salaried employees. With revised income slabs, enhanced standard deductions, and expanded Section 87A rebates under the default New Regime, calculating your individual break-even deduction threshold is vital.

2. Deduction Break-Even Analysis: When Does the Old Regime Win?

Under the Old Regime, you can claim exemptions such as HRA (Section 10(13A)), Home Loan Interest (Section 24(b) up to ₹2L), 80C (up to ₹1.5L), 80D Health Insurance (up to ₹75k), and 80CCD(1B) NPS (up to ₹50k). Under the New Regime, none of these deductions are permitted.

The Break-Even Deduction Threshold is the total amount of tax deductions you must claim in the Old Regime to match the lower tax slabs of the New Regime. If your total eligible deductions exceed this threshold, the Old Regime saves you money; otherwise, the New Regime is vastly superior.

4. Section 87A Rebate Mechanics

Under the New Tax Regime, Section 87A provides a full tax rebate for taxable incomes up to ₹12,00,000 (meaning net taxable income after ₹75,000 standard deduction up to ₹12,75,000 has zero net tax liability). Under the Old Regime, the rebate applies only up to ₹5,00,000 taxable income.

5. Strategic Checklist: Who Should Choose What?

  • Choose New Regime: If your gross salary is up to ₹12.75 Lakh (Zero Tax), or if you do not have heavy home loan interest payments (>₹2 Lakh/year) and substantial HRA claims.
  • Choose Old Regime: If your gross salary exceeds ₹15-20 Lakh AND your total deductions (HRA + 80C + 80D + 24b Home Loan Interest + NPS) comfortably exceed ₹4.50 to ₹5.00 Lakh.

Real-World Salaried Case Study: ₹18 Lakh CTC Analysis

Consider a mid-level manager earning ₹18,00,000 gross salary in Pune with ₹1.5L EPF/80C, ₹35,000 health insurance (80D), and ₹1.80L annual HRA:

  • Under Old Tax Regime: Gross Income ₹18.0L − ₹50k Std Ded − ₹1.5L (80C) − ₹35k (80D) − ₹1.8L (HRA) = Net Taxable Income ₹13,85,000. Total Tax = ₹2,28,000 + 4% Cess = ₹2,37,120.
  • Under New Tax Regime: Gross Income ₹18.0L − ₹75k Std Ded = Net Taxable Income ₹17,25,000. Applying FY 2026-27 slabs (0-4L nil, 4-8L 20k, 8-12L 40k, 12-16L 60k, 16-17.25L 25k) = Base Tax ₹1,45,000 + 4% Cess = ₹1,50,800.
  • Net Annual Tax Saved by Choosing New Regime: ₹86,320 in pure cash savings!

Compliance, Documentation & ITR Filing Protocols

Salaried taxpayers with capital gains from mutual funds or stocks must file ITR-2 rather than the simplified ITR-1 (Sahaj). Maintaining accurate digital statements from CAMS, KFintech, and stock brokerages ensures seamless reporting in Schedule CG. Remember that non-reporting of capital gains can attract scrutiny notices under Section 148A of the Income Tax Act.

Indian Regulatory Oversight & Investor Grievance Architecture

Indian retail investors are protected by robust statutory frameworks administered by SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India), RBI (Reserve Bank of India), and IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India). If you encounter service deficiencies from mutual fund AMCs, registrars (CAMS/KFintech), or banks, file a formal complaint through the unified SEBI SCORES 2.0 portal or the RBI Banking Ombudsman. Regulators mandate resolution of retail grievances within 21 to 30 calendar days.

Capital Gains Harvesting & Set-Off Best Practices

  1. Utilize Section 112A Annual Threshold: Realize up to ₹1,25,000 in long-term equity capital gains each financial year tax-free.
  2. Harvest Short-Term Losses (Tax-Loss Harvesting): Offset short-term capital losses against profitable gains before March 31st to minimize net taxable capital gains.
  3. Track Debt Fund Purchases Post-April 2023: Ensure debt fund units acquired after April 1, 2023, are accounted for under slab rates without expecting indexation benefits.
  4. File ITR on Time to Carry Forward Losses: File ITR-2 before the Section 139(1) deadline to preserve the right to carry forward unadjusted capital losses for up to 8 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a salaried employee switch between Old and New regimes every year?

Yes. Salaried individuals without business income can choose between the Old and New tax regimes each financial year when filing their Income Tax Return (ITR) under Section 139(1).

Is the standard deduction ₹75,000 available under both tax regimes?

No. The standard deduction is ₹75,000 under the New Tax Regime and ₹50,000 under the Old Tax Regime for salaried employees and pensioners.

What is the zero-tax income threshold under the New Tax Regime?

For salaried individuals, a gross annual income up to ₹12,75,000 results in zero income tax due to the ₹75,000 standard deduction combined with the Section 87A tax rebate.

Can I claim home loan interest under the New Tax Regime?

No. Home loan interest deduction under Section 24(b) (up to ₹2,00,000 for self-occupied property) cannot be claimed under the New Tax Regime.

What is the default tax regime in India?

The New Tax Regime is the default regime under the Income Tax Act. If an taxpayer does not actively opt out while filing ITR, tax is calculated automatically under the New Regime.
