7 Common SIP Pitfalls in Indian Mutual Funds: Mechanisms and Portfolio Impact

Hi, I'm Tejas — an engineering student researching personal finance in India. This page explains common operational pitfalls, expense ratios, plan types (Direct vs Regular), dividend options (IDCW vs Growth), and portfolio overlap in systematic investing. It is educational content compiled from SEBI and AMFI circulars, drafted with AI assistance, and checked against regulatory documentation. It is not investment advice or a recommendation to buy, sell, or switch any mutual fund scheme. I am not a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA), Research Analyst (RA), or financial professional. Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Investing in Regular plans through intermediaries instead of Direct plans directly via AMC portals or MF Central incurs recurring distributor commissions that compound into substantial fee differentials over 20–25 years.
  • Selecting "Dividend" or "IDCW" payout option instead of "Growth" triggers annual income taxation on distributed amounts at marginal income tax slab rates, dampening compounding efficiency.
  • Running multiple SIPs simultaneously in funds within the same broad market capitalization often creates substantial portfolio overlap without adding genuine diversification.
  • Checking portfolio values daily and discontinuing SIPs during market corrections disrupts the mathematical benefit of rupee cost averaging.
  • Deploying capital into pure equity funds with a time horizon under 3–5 years exposes capital to cyclical market volatility before recovery can take place.

Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) provide an automated mechanism for steady investing. However, fee drag, distributor commission deductions, IDCW tax incidence, and portfolio duplication can significantly reduce net terminal wealth. Analyzing these structural friction points mathematically helps investors evaluate their portfolio design objectively.

1. Regular Plans vs Direct Plans: The Expense Ratio Differential

Impact: Trailing commission difference compounding over multi-decade horizons

When mutual fund investments are routed through bank branches or distributors, transactions are classified as "Regular" plans. The fund house pays an ongoing trailing distributor commission (often 0.50% to 1.25% annually), which is deducted directly from the scheme's Net Asset Value (NAV) daily. Over a 20-to-25-year horizon, this recurring fee differential can compound to tens of lakhs of rupees compared to an identical Direct plan portfolio.

Mechanism: Direct plans are accessible through direct AMC websites, MF Central, or SEBI-registered direct execution platforms. Because no intermediary distribution commissions are incurred, the Total Expense Ratio (TER) of Direct plans is structurally lower.

2. IDCW (Dividend) vs Growth Option: Annual Tax Leakage

Impact: Payouts taxed at personal slab rates rather than deferred compounding

The "IDCW" (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal) option provides periodic cash distributions. Under prevailing Indian income tax provisions, every rupee distributed under IDCW is treated as ordinary income and taxed at the investor's marginal slab rate in the year of receipt. In contrast, under the Growth option, profits remain invested and compound within the fund until redemption, deferring tax incidence until units are actually sold.

Mechanism: For long-term wealth accumulation where regular income is not immediately required, selecting the Growth option avoids premature taxation and preserves compounding momentum.

3. Portfolio Overlap and Fund Duplication

Impact: Multiple fund management fees without distinct risk diversification

Investors frequently accumulate 6 to 10 equity schemes across large-cap and flexi-cap categories, assuming each additional fund enhances diversification. When examined through portfolio disclosure factsheets, many actively managed large-cap and flexi-cap funds hold largely identical top constituents. The investor ends up paying multiple fund management fees while holding a portfolio that closely mirrors standard market benchmarks.

Mechanism: Reviewing portfolio factsheets published monthly by AMCs helps identify whether underlying security holdings are genuinely diversified across sectors and market capitalization tiers.

4. Discontinuing SIPs During Market Downturns

Impact: Missing the acquisition of lower-cost units

Rupee cost averaging depends on continuous automated investing across fluctuating market cycles. When equity benchmarks correct, a fixed periodic instalment purchases a higher quantity of mutual fund units. Discontinuing or pausing SIPs during corrections prevents the portfolio from accumulating units at discounted NAVs, dampening the eventual rebound when markets recover.

5. Chasing Past Top Performers and Frequent Portfolio Churning

Impact: Short-term capital gains tax liabilities and exit load deductions

SEBI categorisation reports show that schemes ranked in the top quartile over a single year rarely remain top performers indefinitely. Switching out of recently underperforming funds into previous year's winners triggers capital gains tax (such as 20% STCG or 12.5% LTCG) and potential exit loads, creating structural drag that offsets any speculative outperformance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an NFO (New Fund Offer) at ₹10 NAV cheaper than an existing fund?

No. The ₹10 base NAV of an NFO is simply an arbitrary starting unit price; it does not indicate valuation or expected returns. Investment return depends on the percentage change of the underlying portfolio's holdings, not the nominal face value of a single unit. Existing funds offer multi-year operational history and portfolio transparency that NFOs lack.

Can an investor hold schemes across different AMCs in one consolidated view?

Yes. Centralised platforms like MF Central, along with Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA) services like CAMS and KFintech, provide consolidated account statements (CAS) combining folios across all SEBI-registered Asset Management Companies.

Framework for Periodic Portfolio Review

Conducting an annual portfolio audit allows investors to verify whether their holdings remain aligned with intended goals without over-trading: checking plan classifications (Direct vs Regular), confirming Growth option selection, evaluating portfolio overlap via AMC factsheets, and assessing whether contributions should adjust with income changes.

How to think about this yourself

  • Cost transparency: Evaluate Total Expense Ratios (TER) disclosed in monthly factsheets. The difference between Regular and Direct plans is a quantifiable cost deducted continuously from fund assets.
  • Tax efficiency: Consider how IDCW distributions interact with your marginal tax bracket versus the capital gains treatment of Growth units upon ultimate redemption.
  • Discipline vs emotion: Recognize that rupee cost averaging requires consistency across negative market sentiment to achieve lower average acquisition costs.

Where to get personalised help

Asset allocation, scheme selection, and risk management depend on individual financial circumstances and goals. For personalized financial advice, consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA). You can verify registrations through the official SEBI RIA Directory.
