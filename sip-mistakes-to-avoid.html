7 Costly SIP Mistakes Indian Mutual Fund Investors Must Avoid

Mutual fund Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) are India's premier wealth creation vehicle, yet behavioral mistakes quietly erode crores in compound returns. Choosing regular plans over direct plans incurs an annual 1.00% commission drag that strips ₹12.56 Lakh from a 20-year ₹10,000 monthly SIP, while stopping investments during market corrections and neglecting annual step-ups sacrifices substantial long-term alpha.

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 18 August 2026  •  Last reviewed: 24 November 2026  •  Direct vs Regular Drag, Annual Step-Up Compounding & Behavioral Pitfalls

Educational mutual fund investment guide only. Not certified SEBI advisory. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks; read all scheme-related documents carefully. Read our full disclaimer.

Because building long-term wealth through mutual fund systematic investment plans requires avoiding avoidable distributor commissions, preventing portfolio overlap, and continuing monthly investments through turbulent equity corrections, catching structural portfolio errors early protects decades of compounding. When I first built the SIP calculator and portfolio overlap tool on Calculatorship, I analyzed mutual fund portfolios from college friends and relatives. Almost every single portfolio had the exact same flaws: holding 8 to 12 different funds that had 70% overlapping stocks, picking "Regular" plans through a local bank RM that silently extracted over 1% in trailing commissions every year, or pausing SIPs the moment headlines screamed about a market correction. Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) are one of the most reliable ways to build long-term wealth in Indian equities, but common behavioral errors and commission traps can cost retail investors lakhs in lost compounding over a 15-to-25-year horizon.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Investing in Regular plans through a bank instead of Direct plans on Groww, Kuvera, or MFCentral is the single most expensive SIP mistake: it silently costs investors ₹25 Lakh to ₹38 Lakh over 20 to 25 years in distributor commissions.
  • Selecting "Dividend" or "IDCW" payout option instead of "Growth" triggers annual income tax on dividends at your slab rate (up to 39%), reducing the compounding engine.
  • Running 8 to 12 different SIPs simultaneously in overlapping funds creates the illusion of diversification while actually concentrating risk in the same underlying large-cap stocks repeated across funds.
  • Checking your SIP portfolio daily and panic-selling during 15% to 20% corrections is the behaviour that has historically cost Indian retail investors 2% to 4% annual returns versus simply holding.
  • Starting an SIP with a horizon of less than 5 years in an equity mutual fund is a structural mismatch: equity requires a minimum of 5 to 7 years to smooth out full market cycles and deliver positive real returns with high confidence.

SIP investing looks simple from the outside. You pick a fund, set up a monthly debit, and let compounding work. In practice, most retail investors make between 3 and 6 structural mistakes that cut their eventual corpus by 30% to 60%, not through poor market timing, but through avoidable behavioral and structural errors. Every mistake below has a precise financial cost attached to it.

Error 1: Investing in Regular Plans Through Your Bank

Cost: ₹25 Lakh to ₹38 Lakh over 20 to 25 years

When your HDFC Bank relationship manager or LIC agent recommends a mutual fund, they're placing you in a "Regular" plan. They receive a trailing 0.75% to 1.40% annual commission from the fund house, deducted from your total corpus every single year. Such distributor commissions never appear on your bank statement; AMC fees get silently deducted from your NAV daily.

Fix: Open accounts on MFCentral, Groww, or Kuvera and select "Direct Plan - Growth" for every fund. Direct plans have TERs 0.75% to 1.20% lower than Regular plans and the exact same fund portfolio. Consider how the fitment factor alters gross salary step-by-step:

Pitfall 2: Selecting "IDCW" Instead of "Growth" Option

Cost: 20% to 39% of all dividends paid (your tax slab rate)

The "IDCW" (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal) option sounds attractive: you receive monthly or quarterly payouts. But every rupee paid out as "dividend" is taxed as income at your personal income slab rate in the year received. For someone in the 30% bracket, a ₹10,000 IDCW payout nets only ₹7,000 after tax. That same ₹10,000 left in a Growth option compounds tax-free inside the fund until you redeem.

Fix: Always choose "Direct Plan - Growth" for any investment with a horizon exceeding 3 years.

Trap 3: Running Too Many Overlapping Funds

Cost: 60%+ portfolio overlap; zero added diversification

A common pattern: investor holds Mirae Asset Large Cap, HDFC Flexi Cap, Axis Bluechip, and Kotak Multicap. Running all four funds on Morningstar India's portfolio overlap tool typically reveals 65% to 75% overlap in top holdings. All four funds hold Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, Infosys, ICICI Bank, TCS, and L&T in their top 10. The investor pays 4× the TER for the illusion of diversification.

Fix: For SIP portfolios below ₹30 Lakh, 2 to 3 funds is optimal. One Nifty 50 index fund + one mid-cap or flexi-cap active fund covers 95% of what you need. Check portfolio overlap on Value Research Online or Morningstar India before adding any new fund.

Mistake 4: Pausing the SIP During Market Crashes

Cost: Missing 25% to 40% of eventual portfolio returns

A falling NAV is not a risk to a monthly SIP investor; it is an opportunity. When the Nifty 50 crashes 20%, your ₹10,000 monthly installment buys 25% more units than at the pre-crash price. Pausing your SIP during a crash means you miss the months of maximum unit accumulation at the lowest prices, which are the exact months that generate the highest returns when the market recovers.

For investors holding direct equities alongside mutual funds, avoid unweighted price guesses when adding shares during market dips by checking your true cost with our share average down calculator.

Blunder 5: Chasing "Top Performer" Funds

Cost: 1.5% to 3% annual drag from constant churning and mistimed exits

Last year's top-performing fund almost never remains this year's top performer. SEBI's Categorisation data shows that funds ranked in the top quartile in a 1-year window fall to the bottom two quartiles in 3 out of 5 subsequent years. Switching to the current top-performer resets your LTCG clock, triggers tax, and buys into an already-expensive fund at peak valuations. Every switch costs you 12.5% to 20% in capital gains tax plus the lost compounding of the exited corpus.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a mistake to invest in an NFO (New Fund Offer) at ₹10 NAV?

An NFO at ₹10 is not cheap: NAV level is completely meaningless for expected returns. NFOs have zero track record, making performance evaluation impossible. The only justifiable reason to invest in an NFO is if it offers a genuinely unique investment mandate unavailable in any existing fund (e.g., a new factor-based index). For standard equity exposure, an existing fund with 5+ years of audited returns is always more reliable than an NFO.

Is it a mistake to keep all my SIPs in the same AMC?

Not inherently, but you lose flexibility. If you hold all funds with HDFC AMC and decide to switch one fund, your money moves within the same asset manager. More importantly, restricting yourself to one AMC means you can't always access the best fund in each category across all 44 Indian AMCs. Consider diversifying across 2 to 3 AMCs (e.g., one from HDFC/ICICI, one from Parag Parikh/Mirae, one passive from UTI/Nippon) using a central platform like Kuvera or MFCentral that consolidates everything.

The SIP Health Check

Once a year, review your SIPs against these four criteria. Check your statements: (1) Are all plans "Direct-Growth"? (2) Does each fund serve a distinct purpose with minimal overlap? (3) Has performance versus benchmark been consistent over 3 years? (4) Have I increased my SIP amount with salary increments (Step-Up)? If you answer yes to all four, continue. If any answer is no, make the targeted correction on Groww, Kuvera, or MFCentral, and nothing more.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the financial cost of investing in Regular mutual fund plans instead of Direct plans?

Regular plans pay ongoing distributor commissions ranging from 0.75% to 1.25% annually out of your scheme's NAV. On a 20-year monthly SIP of ₹10,000 at a 12% benchmark return, a 1% commission drag costs you over ₹12.55 Lakh in lost wealth compared to a zero-commission Direct plan.

Why is choosing the IDCW (dividend) option considered a mistake for wealth accumulation?

The Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal (IDCW) option distributes regular cash payouts that are taxed annually at your income tax slab rates (up to 31.2%). By selecting the Growth option, profits remain invested to compound tax-free until redemption, where they qualify for favorable 12.5% LTCG under Section 112A.

What happens to long-term wealth if an investor pauses or stops SIPs during a market crash?

Stopping SIPs during market crashes subverts the foundational mechanic of rupee cost averaging. By exiting during downturns, you miss the opportunity to purchase units at heavily discounted NAVs, which generate the majority of long-term wealth when the market eventually recovers.

How many mutual fund schemes should an investor hold in their portfolio to avoid over-diversification?

Holding 3 to 5 well-chosen schemes across distinct categories (such as one Large/Flexi-cap, one Mid-cap, one Small-cap, and an Arbitrage/Debt fund) provides optimal diversification. Holding 10 to 15 funds causes severe portfolio overlap, simply replicating the index at a much higher active cost.

How does adding an annual Step-Up to an existing SIP impact the final maturity corpus?

Stepping up your monthly SIP by just 10% each year aligns your contributions with annual salary raises. On a 20-year baseline ₹10,000 SIP, a 10% annual step-up expands the final corpus from ₹99.91 Lakhs to nearly ₹1.99 Crore—almost doubling your accumulated retirement wealth.
