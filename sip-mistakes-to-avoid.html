7 Costly SIP Mistakes Indian Mutual Fund Investors Must Avoid

Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) are the simplest way to build long-term wealth in Indian equities. However, common behavioral errors, commission traps, and structural mistakes often cost retail investors lakhs of rupees in lost returns.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Investing in Regular plans through a bank instead of Direct plans on Groww, Kuvera, or MFCentral is the single most expensive SIP mistake — it silently costs investors ₹25–38 Lakh over 20–25 years in distributor commissions.
  • Selecting "Dividend" or "IDCW" payout option instead of "Growth" triggers annual income tax on dividends at your slab rate (up to 39%), destroying the compounding engine.
  • Running 8–12 different SIPs simultaneously in overlapping funds creates the illusion of diversification while actually concentrating risk in the same underlying large-cap stocks repeated across funds.
  • Checking your SIP portfolio daily and panic-selling during 15%–20% corrections is the behaviour that has historically cost Indian retail investors 2%–4% annual returns versus simply holding.
  • Starting an SIP with a horizon of less than 5 years in an equity mutual fund is a structural mismatch — equity requires minimum 5–7 years to smooth out full market cycles and deliver positive real returns with high confidence.

SIP investing looks simple from the outside. You pick a fund, set up a monthly debit, and let compounding work. In practice, most retail investors make between 3 and 6 structural mistakes that cut their eventual corpus by 30%–60% — not through poor market timing, but through avoidable behavioral and structural errors. Every mistake below has a precise financial cost attached to it.

Mistake 1: Investing in Regular Plans Through Your Bank

Cost: ₹25–38 Lakh over 20–25 years

When your HDFC Bank relationship manager or LIC agent recommends a mutual fund, they're placing you in a "Regular" plan. They receive a trailing 0.75%–1.40% annual commission from the fund house, deducted from your total corpus every single year. This commission never appears on your bank statement — it is silently extracted from your NAV daily.

Fix: Open accounts on MFCentral, Groww, or Kuvera and select "Direct Plan - Growth" for every fund. Direct plans have TERs 0.75%–1.20% lower than Regular plans and the exact same fund portfolio.

Mistake 2: Selecting "IDCW" Instead of "Growth" Option

Cost: 20%–39% of all dividends paid (your tax slab rate)

The "IDCW" (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal) option sounds attractive — you receive monthly or quarterly payouts. But every rupee paid out as "dividend" is taxed as income at your personal income slab rate in the year received. For someone in the 30% bracket, a ₹10,000 IDCW payout nets only ₹7,000 after tax. That same ₹10,000 left in a Growth option compounds tax-free inside the fund until you redeem.

Fix: Always choose "Direct Plan - Growth" for any investment with a horizon exceeding 3 years.

Mistake 3: Running Too Many Overlapping Funds

Cost: 60%+ portfolio overlap; zero added diversification

A common pattern: investor holds Mirae Asset Large Cap, HDFC Flexi Cap, Axis Bluechip, and Kotak Multicap. Running all four funds on Morningstar India's portfolio overlap tool typically reveals 65%–75% overlap in top holdings. All four funds hold Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, Infosys, ICICI Bank, TCS, and L&T in their top 10. The investor pays 4× the TER for the illusion of diversification.

Fix: For SIP portfolios below ₹30 Lakh, 2–3 funds is optimal. One Nifty 50 index fund + one mid-cap or flexi-cap active fund covers 95% of what you need. Check portfolio overlap on Value Research Online or Morningstar India before adding any new fund.

Mistake 4: Pausing the SIP During Market Crashes

Cost: Missing 25%–40% of eventual portfolio returns

A falling NAV is not a risk to a monthly SIP investor — it is an opportunity. When the Nifty 50 crashes 20%, your ₹10,000 monthly installment buys 25% more units than at the pre-crash price. Pausing your SIP during a crash means you miss the months of maximum unit accumulation at the lowest prices — the months that generate the highest returns when the market recovers.

Mistake 5: Chasing "Top Performer" Funds

Cost: 1.5%–3% annual drag from constant churning and mistimed exits

Last year's top-performing fund almost never remains this year's top performer. SEBI's Categorisation data shows that funds ranked in the top quartile in a 1-year window fall to the bottom two quartiles in 3 out of 5 subsequent years. Switching to the current top-performer resets your LTCG clock, triggers tax, and buys into an already-expensive fund at peak valuations. Every switch costs you 12.5%–20% in capital gains tax plus the lost compounding of the exited corpus.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a mistake to invest in an NFO (New Fund Offer) at ₹10 NAV?

Yes, for most investors. An NFO at ₹10 is not cheap — NAV level is meaningless for expected returns. NFOs have zero track record, making performance evaluation impossible. The only justifiable reason to invest in an NFO is if it offers a genuinely unique investment mandate unavailable in any existing fund (e.g., a new factor-based index). For standard equity exposure, an existing fund with 5+ years of audited returns is always more reliable than an NFO.

Is it a mistake to keep all my SIPs in the same AMC?

Not inherently — but you lose flexibility. If you hold all funds with HDFC AMC and decide to switch one fund, your money moves within the same asset manager. More importantly, restricting yourself to one AMC means you can't always access the best fund in each category across all 44 Indian AMCs. Consider diversifying across 2–3 AMCs (e.g., one from HDFC/ICICI, one from Parag Parikh/Mirae, one passive from UTI/Nippon) using a central platform like Kuvera or MFCentral that consolidates everything.

The SIP Health Check

Once a year, review your SIPs against these four criteria: (1) Are all plans "Direct-Growth"? (2) Does each fund serve a distinct purpose with minimal overlap? (3) Has performance versus benchmark been consistent over 3 years? (4) Have I increased my SIP amount with salary increments (Step-Up)? If you answer yes to all four, continue. If any answer is no, make the targeted correction on Groww, Kuvera, or MFCentral — and nothing more.
