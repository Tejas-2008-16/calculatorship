National Savings Certificate (NSC): Interest Rates, Tax Rules & Compounding

Backed by the sovereign guarantee of the Government of India, the National Savings Certificate (NSC VIII Issue) represents one of the most reliable fixed-income instruments available to conservative savers. With a competitive 7.7% annual compounded interest rate, zero credit risk, and a unique tax-saving deemed reinvestment mechanism under Section 80C, discover how NSC fits into modern financial planning.

  • 7.7% Sovereign Return: Offers a guaranteed 7.7% per annum interest rate, compounded annually and payable upon maturity at the end of 5 years.

1. What is the National Savings Certificate (NSC VIII Issue)?

The National Savings Certificate (NSC) is a flagship government savings instrument offered through India Post and designated public and private sector banks. Established under the Government Savings Certificates Act, 1959, and governed by the National Savings Certificates (VIII Issue) Rules, 2019, NSC was created to mobilize small savings for nation-building while providing citizens with an uncompromised, zero-credit-risk wealth accumulator.

Unlike market-linked mutual funds or corporate fixed deposits, your investment in NSC carries an explicit, unconditional sovereign guarantee from the Government of India. The interest rate agreed upon on the date of purchase remains locked and fixed for the entire 5-year tenure, shielding investors against future interest rate declines.

Previously issued as physical printed paper certificates, modern NSCs are issued electronically either in book-adjustment mode via Post Office Savings Bank (POSB) passbooks or in dematerialized form deposited directly into your NSDL or CDSL demat account.

2. Current Interest Rate & Compounding Math (₹1,000 Maturity Table)

For the ongoing quarter of FY 2026-27, the Ministry of Finance has fixed the NSC interest rate at 7.7% per annum. Interest is compounded annually but is paid out in a single lump sum along with the initial principal at the end of the 5-year tenure.

To understand the exact compounding progression, examine how a baseline deposit of ₹1,000 grows year-on-year:

Every ₹1,00,000 invested in NSC yields approximately ₹1,44,903 at the conclusion of 5 years, generating a cumulative guaranteed return of 44.9% across the tenure.

3. Eligibility Criteria, Deposit Limits & Holding Modes

The operational rules governing NSC accounts are simple and accessible:

  • Eligible Investors: Any resident Indian citizen can open an NSC account. Accounts can also be opened on behalf of a minor child by a legal guardian, or by a person of unsound mind through an appointed guardian. Minors aged 10 years and above can operate the account in their own name.
  • Ineligible Investors: Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), trusts, societies, and corporate entities are strictly disqualified from purchasing new NSCs. (If an individual was a resident when purchasing NSC and subsequently becomes an NRI, the certificate can be held until maturity on a non-repatriable basis).
  • Deposit Limits: The minimum deposit is ₹1,000 and in multiples of ₹100 thereafter. There is no maximum upper investment ceiling. You can invest ₹5 Lakh, ₹25 Lakh, or more in NSC.
  • Holding Modes: NSC accounts can be held as:
    • Single Holder Type
    • Joint A Type (payable to both holders jointly or to the survivor)
    • Joint B Type (payable to either survivor, similar to 'Either or Survivor')

4. The Unique Section 80C "Deemed Reinvestment" Tax Loop

The single most powerful feature distinguishing NSC from conventional fixed deposits is its tax treatment under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act:

  1. Initial Investment Deduction: The principal amount invested in NSC qualifies for tax deduction under Section 80C up to the aggregate ceiling of ₹1,50,000 in the year of deposit (under the Old Tax Regime).
  2. Annual Deemed Reinvestment: In years 1 through 4, the annual interest generated is automatically credited back to your certificate balance. Under Indian tax jurisprudence, this accrued interest is treated as reinvested capital. Consequently, the interest earned in each of the first four years qualifies as a fresh deduction under Section 80C for that respective financial year!
  3. The Year 5 Exception: In the final (5th) year, the interest accrued (₹103.60 per ₹1,000) is paid out along with the principal to your savings account. Because it is not reinvested into the certificate, the 5th year interest cannot be claimed as an 80C deduction and is fully taxable according to your marginal income tax slab.

How to Report in Your ITR: In each of the first 4 years, declare the accrued interest under "Income from Other Sources" in your tax return, and simultaneously claim the identical figure under "Chapter VI-A Deductions — Section 80C". This effectively neutralizes your tax liability on the intermediate interest while preserving compounding growth.

5. TDS Rules & Taxation at Maturity

Unlike bank fixed deposits where banks are legally mandated to deduct 10% TDS under Section 194A once annual interest exceeds ₹40,000 (or ₹50,000 for senior citizens), zero TDS is deducted on NSC.

India Post will disburse the entire maturity amount without withholding a single rupee of tax. However, freedom from TDS is not freedom from tax liability. The aggregate interest earned over the 5 years must be reported in your ITR. If you have been declaring and deducting the interest on an accrual basis annually, you will only pay income tax on the fifth year's interest portion at maturity.

6. Premature Closure Rules & Penalties

To discourage speculation and maintain capital stability, the National Savings Certificates Rules strictly forbid premature encashment under ordinary personal circumstances. You cannot withdraw money from NSC simply because you face a cash crunch or wish to invest in equity markets.

Premature encashment is legally permissible under only three conditions:

  • Death of the Depositor: On the demise of the sole account holder or all joint account holders, the legal heirs or nominees can redeem the certificate.
  • Court Order: When an encashment order is issued by a competent court of law in insolvency, liquidation, or matrimonial settlements.
  • Forfeiture by Pledgee: When a bank or government entity that holds the pledged certificate invokes forfeiture due to loan default.

Penalty Structure: If encashed within one year of purchase, only the principal is refunded with zero interest. If encashed after one year but before three years, simple interest at the Post Office Savings Account rate (4.0%) is credited rather than the 7.7% compounded rate.

7. Pledging NSC as Bank Collateral: Step-by-Step Procedure

One of the most practical liquidity advantages of NSC is that it is widely accepted as prime security or collateral by commercial banks and financial institutions for issuing secured loans and overdraft limits:

  1. Obtain Sanction Letter: Approach your lending bank and obtain a formal loan sanction letter agreeing to accept the NSC as collateral.
  2. Submit Application at Post Office: Submit the prescribed transfer form (Form-5) along with the original NSC certificate/passbook and the bank's pledge sanction letter to the post office where the certificate is registered.
  3. Post Office Endorsement: The postmaster verifies the account and endorses the certificate: "Pledged to [Bank Name] under the Government Savings Certificates Act".
  4. Loan Disbursement: The bank retains the endorsed certificate and issues the loan (typically up to 80% to 90% of the certificate's accrued surrender value) at interest rates substantially lower than unsecured personal loans.
  5. Release of Pledge: Once the loan is fully repaid, the bank issues a No Objection Certificate (NOC), allowing the post office to cancel the lien and restore full ownership to you.

9. Common Mistakes Indian Investors Make with NSC

  • Failing to Claim Deemed Reinvestment: Many taxpayers declare the initial deposit under Section 80C in Year 1, but forget to claim the accrued interest deductions in Years 2, 3, and 4, paying unnecessary tax on maturity.
  • Ignoring the New Tax Regime Reality: Under the default Section 115BAC New Tax Regime, Section 80C deductions are not permitted. If you are under the New Regime, NSC's interest is fully taxable at your slab rate without any offsetting deductions.
  • Assuming Early Liquidity: Treating NSC as an emergency fund is a critical error. The post office will decline premature encashment requests unless backed by a court order or holder demise.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current interest rate on National Savings Certificate (NSC)?

The Ministry of Finance fixes the NSC (VIII Issue) interest rate quarterly. Currently, NSC offers 7.7% per annum, compounded annually but payable at maturity. A deposit of ₹1,000 matures to approximately ₹1,449 after the complete 5-year tenure.

How does the Section 80C deemed reinvestment tax rule work for NSC?

The initial investment in NSC qualifies for deduction under Section 80C up to ₹1.5 Lakh. Furthermore, because interest accrued in years 1, 2, 3, and 4 is not paid out but automatically reinvested, it is considered deemed reinvestment and qualifies as a fresh deduction under Section 80C for that respective year. Only the final 5th year interest cannot be reinvested and is fully taxable.

Is TDS deducted on NSC interest at maturity?

No. Under the Income Tax Act, there is zero Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on NSC payouts at maturity. However, the interest earned is fully taxable according to the investor's applicable income tax slab under 'Income from Other Sources'.

Can NSC be closed prematurely before the 5-year maturity?

Premature encashment of NSC is strictly prohibited during normal circumstances. It is permitted only under three exceptional situations: (1) on the death of the certificate holder (or any holder in a joint account), (2) on forfeiture by a pledgee being a Gazetted government officer or commercial bank, or (3) when ordered by a court of law.

Can I pledge NSC as collateral for bank loans?

Yes. NSC can be officially pledged as security or collateral for loans from scheduled commercial banks, cooperative banks, RBI-registered NBFCs, local government authorities, housing finance companies, or the President of India / Governor of a State upon submission of an official sanction letter at the post office.
