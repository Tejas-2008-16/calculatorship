HRA Exemption Calculation in India: Formula, Rules & Worked Examples

HRA exemptions apply exclusively under the Old Tax Regime; consult an ICAI-registered Chartered Accountant or your company payroll department regarding specific rent receipt validations.

Most salaried employees assume their entire House Rent Allowance is tax-free simply because they pay rent. In reality, HRA exemption is governed by a three-part formula under Section 10(13A), and the smallest of the three figures is what you actually get to deduct — often far less than the HRA sitting on your payslip.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Least of Three: Your HRA exemption is the smallest of actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of salary, and 50% (metro) or 40% (non-metro) of salary — not the full HRA on your payslip.
  • 2. Old Regime Only: HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) does not exist under the New Tax Regime. Under the New Regime, your entire HRA is taxed as normal salary.
  • 3. Low Rent Trap: If your rent is close to or below 10% of your salary, your exemption can shrink to nearly zero even though you receive a healthy HRA every month.
  • 4. PAN Requirement: Annual rent above ₹1,00,000 requires your landlord's PAN on file with your employer, or the exemption gets disallowed at source.
  • 5. Only Four Metro Cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai use the 50% limb. Every other city, including Bengaluru and Pune, uses 40%.
  • 6. Rent to Parents Works: You can legitimately claim HRA by paying rent to parents who own the house, as long as they report it as rental income.

The HRA Myth That Costs Salaried Employees Money

Ask ten salaried employees what happens to their HRA at tax time and most will say some version of "it's exempt because I pay rent." That belief is only half right, and the missing half is expensive. HRA exemption isn't a blanket pass on the allowance you receive — it's capped by a formula that compares three separate numbers and hands you the smallest one. Depending on how your salary is structured and how much rent you actually pay, that smallest number can be close to your full HRA, or it can be a fraction of it.

The gap matters most for two groups: people whose Basic Salary is high relative to their rent, and people living in a city where their landlord won't hand over a PAN. Both situations quietly convert what looks like a tax-free allowance into fully taxable salary. Understanding the formula before you submit rent proofs to HR — not after your Form 16 arrives — is the only way to plan around it.

1. The Exact Section 10(13A) Formula

HRA exemption is computed under Section 10(13A) read with Rule 2A of the Income Tax Rules. For any given period, your exemption is the lowest of these three amounts:

Limb Calculation What It Represents
(a) Actual HRA HRA actually received from employer Upper ceiling — you can never exempt more than you were paid
(b) Rent minus 10% Rent paid − (10% × Basic + DA) Only rent above the 10% threshold counts — the first slice is assumed affordable and gets no relief
(c) City limit 50% (metro) or 40% (non-metro) × (Basic + DA) A hard ceiling regardless of how much rent you actually pay

"Salary" for this formula means Basic Salary plus Dearness Allowance, but only the DA component that counts toward retirement benefits — it excludes every other allowance, bonus, and perquisite you might see on your CTC breakup. Most private-sector employees without a formal DA structure simply use Basic Salary. If your salary changes during the year — an increment, a transfer, or a rent change — the exemption is computed period by period and then added up, not on the annual average.

2. Metro vs Non-Metro: Why Your City Matters

The 50%-versus-40% distinction is based on where you actually reside and work, not where your company is headquartered. Only four cities get the higher 50% limb:

  • Delhi (National Capital Territory)
  • Mumbai
  • Kolkata
  • Chennai

Every other city — Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Gurugram, Noida, Ahmedabad, and the rest — falls under the 40% limb, even though several of these cities now have rental markets as expensive as the four metros. This is a legacy classification from decades ago that the tax code has never updated, and it is a genuine disadvantage for high-cost non-metro renters.

3. Three Worked Examples

The formula only makes sense once you see it applied to real numbers. Here are three common scenarios for FY 2026-27, all assuming the Old Tax Regime.

Example 1: Metro city, healthy rent-to-salary ratio

Basic Salary ₹50,000/month (₹6,00,000/year), HRA received ₹25,000/month (₹3,00,000/year), rent paid ₹22,000/month (₹2,64,000/year), residing in Mumbai.

  • (a) Actual HRA received = ₹3,00,000
  • (b) Rent minus 10% of salary = ₹2,64,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹2,04,000
  • (c) 50% of salary (metro) = ₹3,00,000

Exemption = ₹2,04,000 (the lowest of the three). The remaining ₹96,000 of HRA received is added back to taxable salary.

Example 2: Non-metro city

Basic Salary ₹40,000/month (₹4,80,000/year), HRA received ₹16,000/month (₹1,92,000/year), rent paid ₹14,000/month (₹1,68,000/year), residing in Pune.

  • (a) Actual HRA received = ₹1,92,000
  • (b) Rent minus 10% of salary = ₹1,68,000 − ₹48,000 = ₹1,20,000
  • (c) 40% of salary (non-metro) = ₹1,92,000

Exemption = ₹1,20,000. Taxable HRA = ₹72,000. Notice that switching this same employee to a metro city would not change the exemption here at all — the binding constraint is limb (b), not the city limit.

Example 3: The low-rent trap

Basic Salary ₹60,000/month (₹7,20,000/year), HRA received ₹20,000/month (₹2,40,000/year), rent paid just ₹8,000/month (₹96,000/year) — perhaps living with family and paying a token rent, or sharing accommodation cheaply — in a metro city.

  • (a) Actual HRA received = ₹2,40,000
  • (b) Rent minus 10% of salary = ₹96,000 − ₹72,000 = ₹24,000
  • (c) 50% of salary = ₹3,60,000

Exemption = only ₹24,000. Despite receiving ₹2,40,000 in HRA over the year, this employee gets to exempt just ₹24,000 of it — the rest, ₹2,16,000, is fully taxable. This is the scenario that surprises people most: a comfortable HRA on paper delivers almost no tax benefit when rent is low relative to salary.

4. Rent Receipts, Landlord PAN & Documentation

Your employer will not process an HRA exemption without paperwork, and the tax department can reopen a return years later if the paperwork doesn't hold up. Keep the following in place before the March proof-submission deadline:

  • A signed rent agreement covering the financial year, stating the monthly rent and the address.
  • Monthly rent receipts signed by the landlord, ideally with a revenue stamp for cash payments above ₹5,000.
  • Landlord's PAN — mandatory if your total annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000. Without it, most employers will refuse to process the exemption at source, and you would need to claim it directly while filing your return instead, which invites more scrutiny.
  • Bank transfer proof for rent payments. Paying rent in cash without any trail is one of the most common reasons HRA claims get rejected in scrutiny assessments.

5. Special Cases

Paying rent to parents. This is legal and commonly used. Your parents must own the property, the arrangement must be genuine with real money changing hands, and your parents must declare the rent as income from house property in their own return — they can also claim a standard 30% deduction and municipal taxes against it, which often makes the overall household tax bill lower even after they pay tax on the rental income. Paying rent to a spouse, by contrast, is generally disallowed because tax officers treat it as a circular arrangement rather than a genuine landlord-tenant relationship, especially where the property is jointly owned.

Owning a house in the same city. If you own a self-occupied house in the city where you work and also claim HRA for a different rented flat in the same city, the claim invites scrutiny unless there's a genuine reason — for instance, the owned house is far from your workplace, is under construction, or is occupied by parents. If you own a house in a different city and rent one where you work, both HRA exemption and home loan interest deduction under Section 24(b) can typically be claimed together.

New Tax Regime. If you opt for the New Regime under Section 115BAC — the default regime since FY 2023-24 — none of this formula applies. Your HRA is simply added to gross salary and taxed at the applicable slab rate. The New Regime compensates with a wider basic exemption and a flat ₹75,000 standard deduction, but there is no separate HRA relief. Whether the Old Regime's HRA plus 80C plus 80D deductions beat the New Regime's lower slabs depends entirely on your numbers — there's no universal answer.

6. Common Mistakes That Trigger Notices

A handful of errors show up again and again in HRA claims, and most of them are avoidable with basic diligence:

  • Using gross salary instead of Basic + DA. Inflating the salary figure understates limb (b) in your favor on paper, but it's easy for an assessing officer to catch against Form 16.
  • Fabricated rent receipts with no real payment trail. The Income Tax Department has cross-matched HRA claims against landlord PAN data for years now; a claim with no corresponding bank transfer is a red flag.
  • Forgetting to prorate for mid-year changes. If your Basic Salary or rent changed partway through the year — a promotion, a house move — the exemption must be computed separately for each period, not on annual averages.
  • Claiming a city's 50% limb while living elsewhere. Only your actual city of residence and employment counts, not your employer's registered office location.
  • Not reconciling with Form 26AS/AIS. If your landlord reports the rental income and it doesn't match what you claimed, both sides can face queries.

Actionable Next Step: Check Your Own Numbers Today

Pull up your latest payslip and compute all three limbs of the formula for yourself. If limb (b) — rent minus 10% of salary — is the smallest of the three and meaningfully lower than your actual HRA, you now know exactly how much of your allowance is genuinely tax-free and how much isn't, well before your employer's proof-submission window closes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim HRA exemption under the New Tax Regime?

No. HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is available only under the Old Tax Regime. Under the New Regime, your full HRA amount is added to taxable salary with no exemption, though you still receive the standard deduction.

Which cities count as metro cities for the 50% HRA rule?

Only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai are treated as metro cities for HRA purposes. Every other city, including Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, and Gurugram, uses the 40% limb instead of 50%.

Do I need my landlord's PAN to claim HRA exemption?

Yes, if your total annual rent paid exceeds ₹1,00,000. CBDT rules require the landlord's PAN on the declaration submitted to your employer. If the landlord genuinely has no PAN, a signed declaration confirming this is often accepted, though employer policies vary.

Can I claim HRA exemption if I pay rent to my parents?

Yes, provided the arrangement is genuine: you actually transfer rent, hold a valid rent agreement, and your parents declare the rent as income from house property in their own return. Paying rent to a spouse is generally not accepted.

Can I claim both HRA exemption and home loan interest deduction together?

Yes, if the two properties are in different cities, or if your owned house in the same city isn't reasonably usable by you due to distance, ongoing construction, or being let out. If you live in a self-occupied house in the same city, you generally cannot also claim HRA for a nearby rented flat.

What if my employer does not pay HRA as part of my salary?

If HRA is not part of your salary structure, or you are self-employed, you can claim rent deduction under Section 80GG instead, capped at the lowest of ₹5,000/month, 25% of total income, or rent paid minus 10% of total income, up to ₹60,000 a year.

How to Think About This Yourself

The HRA exemption formula is a statutory minimum function with three distinct variables:

  • The 3-Limb Minimum Rule: Your tax exemption is strictly the least of: (1) Actual HRA received, (2) 50% of Basic Salary (40% in non-metros: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, etc. are non-metros under Rule 2A), or (3) Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic Salary.
  • The Rent-to-Basic Spread: Limb (3) is almost always the limiting constraint. If your annual rent paid does not exceed 10% of your Basic Salary, your eligible HRA tax exemption is exactly zero, regardless of how much HRA is stated on your salary slip.
  • PAN of Landlord & Genuine Banking Trail: Under CBDT Circular 8/2013, if rent paid exceeds ₹1,00,000 annually, declaring the landlord's PAN is mandatory. Maintain clean bank transfer trails to defend against automated AIS reconciliation scrutiny.

Where to Get Personalised Help

Rent paid to parents, joint home ownership vs rented accommodation, and Section 80GG alternative claims carry formal legal requirements:

  • Consult an ICAI-registered Chartered Accountant (CA) via the ICAI Official Portal to draft valid tenancy agreements (especially when paying rent to parents) and verify Schedule HP disclosures.
  • For comparing your total deductions under the Old Regime against the simplified New Regime, speak with a fee-only SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) via the SEBI RIA Directory.
