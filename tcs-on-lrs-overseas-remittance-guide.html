TCS on LRS: Section 206C(1G) Tax on Overseas Remittances, Forex Cards & Foreign Education Payments: Complete Guide for FY 2026-27

Under Section 206C(1G) of the Income-tax Act, Tax Collected at Source (TCS) on Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) remittances applies at 0.5% for education financed by an approved loan, 5% for self-financed education and medical expenses above ₹7 Lakh, and 20% for overseas investments and general transfers above ₹7 Lakh. Overseas tour packages attract 5% up to ₹7 Lakh and 20% thereafter, but all TCS is fully adjustable against your final income tax liability.

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 4 October 2026  •  Last reviewed: 23 November 2026  •  Section 206C(1G), ₹7L Threshold, 0.5%/5%/20% Slabs & ITR Credit Rules

Educational tax compliance guide only. Not formal chartered accountancy or foreign exchange advisory. TCS rules governed by Section 206C(1G) and CBDT circulars. Read our full disclaimer.

When I was writing the tax calculators on Calculatorship, an overseas student was preparing to fly to Germany for his master's degree. As his father wired tuition fees and living expenses, their bank deducted Tax Collected at Source (TCS) under Section 206C(1G). His family panicked, assuming the government had levied a flat penalty on studying abroad. But TCS is not a final tax: it is an advance tax credit that sits in your Form 26AS, ready to offset your regular income tax or come back as a full refund when you file your ITR. Here is how the rules work for FY 2026-27.

Note
TCS is Not a Final Tax: It is an Advance Credit

TCS collected on LRS remittances is not a permanent charge or penalty. It is collected as an advance against your total income tax liability and appears in your Form 26AS. You can set it off in full when filing your annual ITR. Whenever your final tax liability is less than TCS collected, the excess is refunded by the Income Tax Department.

Key Takeaways at a Glance.

  • General LRS TCS Rate: 20% on remittances exceeding ₹7 Lakh per financial year (investments abroad, gifts, maintenance, travel packages).
  • Education / Medical TCS: 5% on self-funded foreign education or medical treatment above ₹7 Lakh per year.
  • Education Loan TCS: zero-point-five percent on overseas education funded through a financial institution loan, the most concessional rate.
  • Credit Card Exemption: International credit card transactions abroad are currently NOT under LRS TCS. Only formal bank wire transfers and forex card top-ups through authorized dealers attract TCS.
  • Claim It Back: Every rupee of TCS deducted shows up in Form 26AS and can be claimed as a credit against your tax liability in your ITR, it is recoverable as a refund if you have zero or lower tax.

1. What is the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)?

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) is a framework established by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) that allows Indian resident individuals to remit money abroad freely, without requiring prior RBI approval, up to a cumulative limit of USD 250,000 per financial year (approximately ₹2.1 Crore at current exchange rates).

Statutory provisions encompass diverse cross-border remittances by resident Indians:

  • Current Account Transactions: Foreign travel expenses, overseas education fees, medical treatment abroad, maintenance of close relatives living overseas, gifts and donations to foreign entities, and purchase of foreign goods and services.
  • Capital Account Transactions: Investment in overseas stocks and bonds (see our detailed guide on Tax on US Stocks in India & Schedule FA), acquisition of immovable property abroad, opening and maintaining foreign currency accounts, making foreign direct investments, and setting up joint ventures abroad.

Central banking authorities launched the scheme back in 2004 and has undergone several revisions in the annual limit over the years. Regulatory changes replaced an older, highly restrictive regime where virtually every outward remittance required explicit RBI permission. Annual limits of USD 250,000 reset every April 1 that resets every April 1. LRS applies only to resident individuals, NRIs operating through NRE or FCNR accounts use separate RBI frameworks and are not subject to the LRS TCS rules discussed here.

2. What is TCS on LRS and Which Law Governs It?

TCS stands for Tax Collected at Source. Unlike TDS (Tax Deducted at Source, which is deducted by the payer before credit), TCS is collected by the seller or service provider from the buyer at the time of the transaction and deposited with the government on the buyer's behalf.

Section 206C(1G) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 specifically mandates TCS on remittances made under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme. Legislators introduced the levy under the Finance Act, 2020, originally at a 5% rate, and subsequently revised significantly upward by the Finance Act, 2023 for most LRS categories, effective October 1, 2023.

The party responsible for collecting and remitting the TCS to the government is the Authorized Dealer (AD), which is typically your bank (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, etc.) or an authorized money changer through whom you process the LRS remittance. Banks collect this amount directly from you when executing the foreign exchange transfer.

3. TCS Rates on LRS for FY 2026-27: Complete Rate Card.

Purpose of LRS Remittance Threshold TCS Rate on Amount Exceeding Threshold
Overseas Education, Loan from Financial Institution (Section 80E eligible loan) ₹7 Lakh per FY zero-point-five percent, Most Concessional Rate
Overseas Education, Funded from Own Sources ₹7 Lakh per FY 5%
Medical Treatment Abroad ₹7 Lakh per FY 5%
All Other LRS Purposes, Investments, Gifts, Maintenance, Family Support, etc. ₹7 Lakh per FY 20%, General Rate
Overseas Tour Package (purchased from Indian tour operator) No threshold, TCS from ₹1 5% on first ₹7L; 20% on amount above ₹7L

Note: If the remitter does not furnish PAN, the TCS rate doubles to 40% for most categories. Always ensure your PAN is registered and linked with your authorized dealer bank. Rates reflect the Finance Act, 2023 provisions effective October 1, 2023, continuing through FY 2026-27.

4. The ₹7 Lakh Annual Threshold: How It Works.

That ₹7 Lakh benchmark operates as a per-fiscal-year cumulative threshold on LRS remittances before TCS kicks in. That threshold applies cumulatively across all remittances made for the same category through the same authorized dealer during the financial year.

Here is how it works in practice:

  • If you remit ₹5 Lakh abroad in June for investment in US stocks: No TCS (below ₹7 Lakh).
  • Suppose you subsequently remit another ₹4 Lakh in September (same FY, same authorized dealer): TCS at 20% applies only on ₹2 Lakh (the amount by which the cumulative ₹9 Lakh crosses the ₹7 Lakh threshold). TCS = ₹40,000.
  • Any further remittances in the same FY will attract full 20% TCS with no threshold deduction (threshold is already exhausted).

An important nuance: the ₹7 Lakh threshold is tracked by each authorized dealer separately for their own customers. Whenever someone splits remittances across two different banks, each bank tracks its own cumulative total against the threshold for its own transactions. Remember that splitting transfers across multiple banks does not bypass reporting, the Income Tax Department has visibility across all transactions through your PAN, and the aggregate LRS exposure is reported in your Annual Information Statement (AIS).

Overseas Tour Package, No ₹7 Lakh Threshold

Overseas tour packages sold by Indian tour operators are a special carve-out. TCS at 5% applies from the very first rupee up to ₹7 Lakh in the package cost, and at 20% on the portion of the package price exceeding ₹7 Lakh. There is no zero-TCS zone for tour packages, every holiday package payment attracts TCS immediately.

5. Which Transactions Attract LRS TCS?

LRS TCS applies to outward remittances processed through authorized dealers under the RBI's LRS framework. Everyday transactions triggering TCS include:

  • Wire transfers to foreign bank accounts, sending money to your own account abroad, to a child studying overseas, to a relative living in another country, or to a foreign vendor.
  • Forex card top-ups, loading foreign currency onto a multi-currency travel card at a bank or authorized money changer for international travel.
  • International travel packages, purchasing overseas holiday packages from Indian tour operators (TCS from ₹1, no threshold).
  • Overseas education fees, paying tuition fees directly to a foreign university from an Indian bank account.
  • Foreign medical treatment, remitting money for treatment at overseas hospitals.
  • Overseas stock/ETF investments, investing in US stocks through platforms like INDmoney, ICICI Direct Global, or similar that route purchases through LRS.
  • Foreign real estate purchases, remitting purchase consideration for overseas property.

Transactions That Do NOT Attract LRS TCS.

  • International credit card transactions made directly abroad (not currently under LRS).
  • Remittances made under separate RBI frameworks that fall outside LRS (for example, NRI NRO/NRE account transfers under FEMA specific provisions).
  • Remittances for imports of goods and services made by businesses under current account liberalization rules outside LRS.
  • Amounts up to the ₹7 Lakh annual threshold for education, medical, and general LRS purposes.

6. How TCS is Collected: Role of Authorized Dealers.

Authorized Dealers (ADs), typically banks and authorized money changers licensed by the RBI, are legally obligated under Section 206C(1G) to collect TCS from the remitter at the time the foreign exchange transaction is processed. Operational processing unfolds as follows:

  1. You initiate the remittance, either at a bank branch, through net banking, or via a SWIFT transfer request.
  2. Next, your bank verifies cumulative LRS numbers, checks the remittances already processed for you in the current financial year, compares against the ₹7 Lakh threshold, and determines the TCS amount.
  3. Collection at source: The authorized dealer collects this advance tax at transaction time, either added to your invoice or deducted from gross remittance.
  4. Remittance to government: Your bank deposits the money with the Central Government using your PAN. Every single rupee is tracked.
  5. Ledger reflection: Within a few weeks, the credit reflects in your Form 26AS under Part E and inside your AIS. Keep your transaction slips handy. Always.

7. How to Claim TCS Credit in Your ITR.

Claiming TCS credit when filing your Income Tax Return is straightforward. Here is the step-by-step process:

  1. Download your Form 26AS from the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in), go to "View Form 26AS" under e-file → Income Tax Returns. Verify that all TCS amounts appear correctly with the authorized dealer's TAN.
  2. Cross-reference with AIS, also check your Annual Information Statement (AIS) for TCS entries. AIS captures data from multiple sources and is increasingly detailed.
  3. File your ITR (ITR-2 for most cases), if you have LRS remittances, you are likely also reporting capital gains (foreign investments) or foreign income, which would require ITR-2. In ITR-2, go to Schedule TCS (Tax Collected at Source) and enter the TCS details from Form 26AS.
  4. Automated import: When tax filing season opens, the pre-filled ITR utility pulls these entries straight into Schedule TCS. Historical returns show no sustained edge for one calendar date over another.
  5. Final tax adjustment: The e-filing portal offsets the collected sum against your annual tax obligation. Any excess turns into an immediate bank refund.

Illustrative Example: TCS Credit in ITR.

  • Resident Indian remits: ₹12,00,000 abroad for US stock investments in FY 2026-27.
  • LRS TCS deducted by bank: 20% on ₹5,00,000 (the amount above ₹7 Lakh threshold) = ₹1,00,000 TCS.
  • Investor's total income tax liability (from salary, business income, capital gains): ₹3,50,000.
  • TDS on salary already deducted: ₹2,20,000.
  • Net tax computation: ₹3,50,000 (liability) − ₹2,20,000 (TDS) − ₹1,00,000 (TCS credit) = ₹30,000 self-assessment tax to pay.
Result: The ₹1,00,000 TCS was not a loss, it reduced the investor's final tax payment from ₹1,30,000 to ₹30,000. No refund required in this case, but the TCS was fully utilized.

8. Worked Numerical Examples.

Example 1: Parent Sending Money for Child's Overseas Education (Own Funds).

  • Amount remitted: ₹30,00,000 (₹30 Lakh) to a UK university
  • Funding source: Parent's own savings (no education loan)
  • Applicable TCS rate: 5% above ₹7 Lakh
  • Taxable amount: ₹30,00,000 − ₹7,00,000 = ₹23,00,000
  • TCS deducted: 5% × ₹23,00,000 = ₹1,15,000
  • This ₹1,15,000 appears in Form 26AS and can be claimed as credit in the parent's ITR.

Example 2: Parent Sending Money for Child's Overseas Education (Education Loan).

  • Amount remitted: ₹30,00,000 to a UK university, funded via an education loan from an Indian bank
  • Applicable TCS rate: zero-point-five percent above ₹7 Lakh
  • Taxable amount: ₹30,00,000 − ₹7,00,000 = ₹23,00,000
  • TCS deducted: zero-point-five percent × ₹23,00,000 = ₹11,500
  • Saving vs own-funded route: ₹1,15,000 − ₹11,500 = ₹1,03,500 lower TCS, a substantial cash flow advantage for borrowers.

Example 3: Investing ₹15 Lakh in US Stocks Through a Broker.

  • Amount remitted via LRS for US stock purchase: ₹15,00,000
  • Applicable TCS rate: 20% above ₹7 Lakh (general investment purpose)
  • Taxable amount: ₹15,00,000 − ₹7,00,000 = ₹8,00,000
  • TCS deducted: 20% × ₹8,00,000 = ₹1,60,000
  • Total outflow from investor's account: ₹15,00,000 + ₹1,60,000 = ₹16,60,000
  • ₹1,60,000 TCS is recoverable via ITR credit or refund, but it creates a significant short-term cash flow impact.

9. Forex Cards and International Credit Cards: TCS Applicability.

Forex Cards (Multi-Currency Travel Cards).

When you load foreign currency onto a multi-currency forex card at a bank or authorized money changer, this is treated as an LRS remittance. Once the total amount loaded onto the travel card exceeds ₹7 Lakh in the financial year, TCS at 20% applies on the excess. For most Indian travelers using forex cards for a single international holiday, the total card load typically stays well under ₹7 Lakh, so TCS does not apply. Business travelers or frequent flyers who load large amounts should be aware of the cumulative LRS tracking.

International Credit Cards, Currently NOT Under LRS TCS.

Remittance taxation generated widespread confusion following Budget 2023. The Finance Act 2023 initially proposed including international credit card transactions made abroad under the LRS/TCS framework. Originally scheduled for July 2023, the proposed rule sparked intense industry pushback among frequent international travelers and business professionals.

However, the Government of India deferred the implementation of this provision following considerable pushback, pending further review. As of FY 2026-27, international credit card transactions made abroad (where you swipe your Indian Rupee credit card at an overseas merchant or website) are NOT subject to LRS TCS. These transactions continue to be settled through the card network and do not fall under the formal LRS route through an authorized dealer.

This means: if you book a Singapore Airlines ticket on the airline's website using your HDFC or SBI credit card, no TCS applies. If you instead wire the equivalent rupee amount to Singapore Airlines' account via an outward wire transfer, TCS would apply. In practical terms, the differentiator lies in the payment method and channel, credit card vs bank wire transfer.

10. TCS vs TDS: Key Differences.

Feature TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) TCS (Tax Collected at Source)
Who Deducts/Collects Payer (employer, bank, etc.) deducts before paying Seller/service provider (authorized dealer) collects from buyer
Trigger Payment of income (salary, interest, professional fees) Purchase/transaction (foreign remittance, luxury goods, alcohol, timber, scrap)
For LRS Specifically Not applicable Section 206C(1G), on outward remittances above ₹7L
Appears In Form 26AS (Part A), Form 16 (for salary) Form 26AS (Part B, "Tax Collected at Source")
ITR Schedule Schedule TDS in ITR Schedule TCS in ITR
Is It Final Tax? No, credit against tax liability No, credit against tax liability
