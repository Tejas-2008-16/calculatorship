Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds: The Hidden Cost of Commissions

Every mutual fund scheme has two options: Direct and Regular. While they hold identical stocks and are managed by the same fund manager, the regular option pays commissions out of your investment corpus. Over 20 years, this subtle difference can cost you over 10 lakhs in hidden fees.

What is the Difference Between Direct and Regular Plans?

Back in 2013, SEBI introduced a game-changing mandate: every mutual fund scheme in India had to offer two distinct options — **Direct Plans** and **Regular Plans**.

Here is how they differ in plain English:

  • Direct Plans: Bought directly from the fund house or through zero-commission apps (Groww, Zerodha Coin, Kuvera, MF Central). No distributor or bank relationship manager is involved, so zero commission is charged.
  • Regular Plans: Bought through a bank advisor, mutual fund agent, or traditional distributor. The fund house pays an annual ongoing commission to that agent — taken directly out of your investment corpus every single day.

Here's the kicker: the underlying portfolio is 100% identical! If you buy Direct vs. Regular of the exact same fund, both hold the exact same stocks, managed by the exact same fund manager. The only difference is that the Regular plan quietly takes 0.5% to 1% of your wealth every year to pay agent commissions.

Key Takeaway

Distributor commissions are not a one-time fee paid when you buy. They are ongoing annual fees deducted daily from your mutual fund portfolio's NAV, compounding silently against you every single year.

The Compounding Drag of a 1% Fee

A 1% annual commission sounds small when you hear it over a phone call with your bank representative. But in compounding, small percentages create massive gulfs over time. Let's look at real numbers over a 20-year horizon.

Net Compounded Return Net Return = Gross Portfolio CAGR - Total expense ratio (TER)

Imagine running a ₹10,000 monthly SIP for 20 years in an equity fund earning an average gross return of 12% per year:

  • Direct Plan (expense ratio 0.5%): Your net annual return is 11.5%. After 20 years, your ₹24 Lakh investment grows to ₹91.98 Lakhs.
  • Regular Plan (expense ratio 1.5%): Your net annual return is 10.5%. After 20 years, your ₹24 Lakh investment grows to ₹80.57 Lakhs.

By simply holding the Regular plan, you handed over ₹11.41 Lakhs in commissions to the agent! That is over ₹11 lakh lost from your retirement pool for holding the exact same fund.

When is a Regular Plan Justified?

Are Regular plans ever worth it? Yes, but only in specific cases. If you are not comfortable using smartphones, don't want to manage tax statements yourself, and have a genuinely certified financial planner who gives you holistic tax and goal planning advice, that 1% fee pays for their time.

Warning

Be careful of agents who push you to frequently switch or "churn" funds every year. Some distributors earn new transaction incentives when you move money between schemes, destroying your long-term compounding timeline.

How to Transition to Direct Plans

Switching your existing Regular investments into Direct plans is straightforward on modern platforms. Keep these two rules in mind:

  1. Check Exit Loads: Most equity funds charge a 1% exit load if you redeem or switch within 1 year of purchase. Switch units older than 12 months first to avoid fees.
  2. Consider Capital Gains Tax: Switching mutual fund units counts as a redemption event. Under current rules, equity gains held over 1 year are taxed as LTCG at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year.
  3. Execute the Switch: Place a switch request on CAMS, KFintech, MF Central, or directly through your preferred zero-commission app.
