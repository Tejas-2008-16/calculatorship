Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds: The Hidden Cost That reduces Compounding in India

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 03 July 2026  •  Last reviewed: 23 November 2026  •  Regulatory Framework: SEBI Direct Plan Mandate (CIR/IMD/DF/21/2012)

Educational mutual fund guide only. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks. Read all scheme related documents carefully. Not personal financial or investment advice. Read our full disclaimer.

Direct mutual fund plans carry a 0.5% to 1.5% lower annual expense ratio than Regular plans because they eliminate distributor trail commissions completely. Over a 20-year investment horizon with a ₹15,000 monthly SIP at 12% gross equity returns, choosing a Direct plan delivers ₹1.40 Crore versus ₹1.19 Crore in a Regular plan — preserving ₹21.42 Lakhs of your wealth from intermediary distribution fees.

Key Summary: Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds

  • Identical Underlying Portfolio: Direct and Regular plans of the same mutual fund hold the exact same securities, same fund manager, and same market risk; only the annual Total Expense Ratio (TER) differs.
  • Daily Compounding Drag: Regular plans pay an ongoing distributor trail fee deducted daily from the scheme's Net Asset Value (NAV), whereas Direct plans eliminate intermediary trail commissions.
  • SEBI TER Ceiling (Regulation 52(6)(c)): Active open-ended equity scheme base TER caps start at 2.25% on the first ₹500 crore AUM and taper down to 1.05% above ₹50,000 crore AUM.
  • Switching Triggers Redemption Tax: Moving existing units from Regular to Direct counts as a sale and fresh purchase, making exit loads and LTCG/STCG applicable.

1. Structural Mechanics: Direct Plan vs Regular Plan TER

Introduced across Indian mutual funds by SEBI in January 2013, Direct plans allow investors to subscribe straight through AMCs, MFCentral (by CAMS and KFintech), or Direct-plan platforms without routing commissions to an intermediary. Under SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, Regulation 52(6)(c) caps open-ended equity scheme expense ratios from 2.25% on the first ₹500 crore of assets down to 1.05% above ₹50,000 crore, and AMC factsheets publish exact daily Direct and Regular TER separately.

3. How to Switch Existing Units Without Unnecessary Exit Loads or Tax

  1. Redirect Future SIPs Immediately: Cancel active Regular SIP mandates and restart them in the Direct Plan — Growth option via MFCentral or an AMC/Direct portal. Stopping new Regular installments triggers zero tax.
  2. Check Exit-Load Windows (Usually 1 Year): Before switching older Regular units to Direct, verify holding periods on your Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) so units are past any 12-month equity exit load.
  3. Use the ₹1,25,000 Annual Equity LTCG Exemption: Under Section 112A, switching long-term equity units (>12 months) up to ₹1,25,000 in realized profit per financial year attracts zero LTCG tax, allowing gradual tax-efficient migration over multiple financial years.

4. Frequently Asked Questions

When does a Regular plan or fee-only adviser make sense?

Investors who want comprehensive financial planning without distributor commissions can engage a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) who charges a transparent flat or AUM advisory fee while recommending Direct plans, whereas Regular plans suit investors who consciously rely on an AMFI-registered distributor for ongoing execution support.

Do I pay capital gains tax if I switch from Regular to Direct within the same fund?

Yes. Legally, switching between Regular and Direct options in the same scheme is treated as redeeming existing units and subscribing to new Direct units at prevailing NAV, triggering STCG (20% for equity ≤12 months) or LTCG (12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh/yr for >12 months).
