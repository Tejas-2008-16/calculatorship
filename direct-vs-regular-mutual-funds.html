Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds: The Hidden Cost That Destroys Compounding in India

In January 2013, SEBI mandated that all mutual fund schemes offer Direct Plans alongside traditional Regular Plans. The difference in Total Expense Ratio (TER) seems minor on paper (typically 0.5% to 1.5%), but compounded over decades, regular plan distributor commissions can silently strip 25% to 40% of your total final wealth.

1. Structural Mechanics: Where Does the TER Difference Go?

Every mutual fund deducts a daily percentage called the Total Expense Ratio (TER) to cover fund management, compliance, registrar servicing, and administrative costs. In a Direct Plan, the investor transacts directly with the Asset Management Company (AMC). The AMC charges only the core fund management and operating fees (often 0.10% to 0.80%).

In a Regular Plan, the AMC pays an ongoing annual trailing commission (typically 0.50% to 1.50% of your total assets under management every year) to the broker, distributor, bank relationship manager, or agent who sold you the fund. This fee is deducted continuously from your NAV, every single day, for as long as you hold the fund.

Over a 30-year horizon, an investor in a regular plan with a 1.5% higher expense ratio loses nearly ₹1.00 Crore in trailing distributor commissions. That is money deducted from your retirement corpus to compensate a middleman.

3. How to Check If Your Portfolio Holds Regular Plans

Download your Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) from CAMS or KFintech, or open your banking/broker app:

  • Direct Plan Name: “[Fund Name] - Direct Plan - Growth”
  • Regular Plan Name: “[Fund Name] - Regular Plan - Growth” or simply “[Fund Name] - Growth” without the word “Direct”.

4. Step-by-Step Guide to Switch from Regular to Direct

  1. Create an account on an official direct investment platform like MFCentral, AMC official websites, or direct discount brokerages.
  2. Identify folios where units have been held for more than 1 year (to avoid short-term capital gains tax and exit loads).
  3. Execute a “Switch” order from the Regular scheme to the Direct scheme within the same AMC.
  4. Cancel existing SIP mandates on regular distributor portals and register fresh SIPs on direct platforms.

5. Tax & Exit Load Implications of Switching

A switch from Regular to Direct is legally classified as a redemption followed by a fresh purchase. For equity funds, ensure units have completed 365 days of holding to qualify for the preferential 12.5% Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax rate and avoid 1% exit loads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Direct Plans and Regular Plans have the same portfolio?

Yes. A scheme's Direct and Regular variants invest in the exact same underlying portfolio of stocks or bonds and are managed by the same fund manager. The only difference is the lower Expense Ratio and higher NAV in the Direct Plan.

Why is the NAV of a Direct Plan higher than a Regular Plan?

Because Direct Plans deduct lower daily operating expenses and zero distributor commissions, their daily Net Asset Value (NAV) grows faster and compounds higher over time.

Can my bank or distributor switch my existing regular mutual funds to direct for me?

Generally no, because distributors earn their living from ongoing regular commissions. You must initiate the switch yourself via direct platforms like MFCentral or the respective AMC portals.

Is there any capital gains tax when switching from Regular to Direct?

Yes. A switch is treated as a redemption under the Income Tax Act. If equity units are held over 12 months, LTCG above ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year is taxed at 12.5%.

How do I ensure future SIPs are strictly invested in Direct Plans?

Always verify that the scheme name contains the exact word 'Direct' when setting up new SIP mandates.
