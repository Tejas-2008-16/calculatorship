Capital Gains Tax on Mutual Funds & Stocks: Complete Guide for Indian Investors

Understanding capital gains taxation is essential for maximizing your post-tax investment returns. With post-Budget amendments revising equity LTCG to 12.5% and STCG to 20%, alongside new taxation rules for debt mutual funds, this guide breaks down every tax rule with clear calculations.

2. Equity Mutual Funds: LTCG (12.5%) vs STCG (20%)

For a mutual fund to qualify as an equity-oriented fund, it must maintain an average domestic equity allocation of at least 65% across the financial year:

  • STCG (Units held < 12 months): Taxed at a flat rate of 20% plus 4% Health & Education Cess.
  • LTCG (Units held ≥ 12 months): Gains up to ₹1,25,000 per financial year across all equity assets are 100% tax-free. Gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh are taxed at 12.5% plus cess.

3. Debt Mutual Funds: Post-April 2023 Slab-Rate Rules

Under Section 50AA of the Income Tax Act, mutual funds investing less than 35% in domestic equities acquired on or after April 1, 2023, do not receive indexation benefits. All capital gains on these funds, regardless of holding duration, are deemed short-term capital gains and taxed at your applicable personal income tax slab rate.

4. Tax-Loss Harvesting & Set-Off Rules in India

Investors can systematically offset capital losses against gains to minimize total tax liability:

  1. Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL): Can be set off against both Short-Term Capital Gains and Long-Term Capital Gains.
  2. Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL): Can be set off strictly against Long-Term Capital Gains.
  3. Loss Carry-Forward: Unadjusted capital losses can be carried forward for up to 8 consecutive financial years, provided your ITR is filed before the statutory due date under Section 139(1).

5. How to Report Mutual Fund Capital Gains in ITR

Salaried taxpayers with capital gains must file ITR-2 (or ITR-3 for individuals with business income) instead of ITR-1 (Sahaj). Download your Annual Capital Gains Statement from CAMS, KFintech, or your brokerage to import pre-calculated STCG and LTCG figures directly into Schedule CG.

Strategic Tax Optimization Architecture for Indian Taxpayers

Navigating the Indian tax code requires integrating income slab analysis, capital gains timing, and statutory exemptions to maximize post-tax take-home pay and net investment returns.

Regime Selection & Deduction Threshold Strategy

Under FY 2026-27 rules, the default New Tax Regime provides lower tax slabs and zero tax liability up to ₹12.75 Lakh gross salary for individuals via the Section 87A rebate and ₹75,000 Standard Deduction. Real-World Salaried Case Study: ₹18 Lakh CTC Analysis

Consider a mid-level manager earning ₹18,00,000 gross salary in Pune with ₹1.5L EPF/80C, ₹35,000 health insurance (80D), and ₹1.80L annual HRA:

  • Under Old Tax Regime: Gross Income ₹18.0L − ₹50k Std Ded − ₹1.5L (80C) − ₹35k (80D) − ₹1.8L (HRA) = Net Taxable Income ₹13,85,000. Total Tax = ₹2,28,000 + 4% Cess = ₹2,37,120.
  • Under New Tax Regime: Gross Income ₹18.0L − ₹75k Std Ded = Net Taxable Income ₹17,25,000. Applying FY 2026-27 slabs (0-4L nil, 4-8L 20k, 8-12L 40k, 12-16L 60k, 16-17.25L 25k) = Base Tax ₹1,45,000 + 4% Cess = ₹1,50,800.
  • Net Annual Tax Saved by Choosing New Regime: ₹86,320 in pure cash savings!

Compliance, Documentation & ITR Filing Protocols

Salaried taxpayers with capital gains from mutual funds or stocks must file ITR-2 rather than the simplified ITR-1 (Sahaj). Maintaining accurate digital statements from CAMS, KFintech, and stock brokerages ensures seamless reporting in Schedule CG. Remember that non-reporting of capital gains can attract scrutiny notices under Section 148A of the Income Tax Act.

Indian Regulatory Oversight & Investor Grievance Architecture

Indian retail investors are protected by robust statutory frameworks administered by SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India), RBI (Reserve Bank of India), and IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India). If you encounter service deficiencies from mutual fund AMCs, registrars (CAMS/KFintech), or banks, file a formal complaint through the unified SEBI SCORES 2.0 portal or the RBI Banking Ombudsman. Regulators mandate resolution of retail grievances within 21 to 30 calendar days.

Capital Gains Harvesting & Set-Off Best Practices

  1. Utilize Section 112A Annual Threshold: Realize up to ₹1,25,000 in long-term equity capital gains each financial year tax-free.
  2. Harvest Short-Term Losses (Tax-Loss Harvesting): Offset short-term capital losses against profitable gains before March 31st to minimize net taxable capital gains.
  3. Track Debt Fund Purchases Post-April 2023: Ensure debt fund units acquired after April 1, 2023, are accounted for under slab rates without expecting indexation benefits.
  4. File ITR on Time to Carry Forward Losses: File ITR-2 before the Section 139(1) deadline to preserve the right to carry forward unadjusted capital losses for up to 8 years.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the annual tax-free LTCG exemption limit on equity in India?

Under the Income Tax Act, long-term capital gains on listed equity shares and equity mutual funds are completely exempt from tax up to ₹1,25,000 in aggregate per financial year.

What is the tax rate on equity mutual funds held for less than 1 year?

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) on equity mutual funds held for less than 12 months are taxed at a flat rate of 20% plus 4% Health & Education Cess.

Do debt mutual funds still get indexation benefit in India?

No. For debt mutual funds acquired on or after April 1, 2023, indexation benefits have been abolished. All capital gains are taxed at your applicable personal income tax slab rates.

Can I offset short-term capital losses against salary income?

No. Capital losses cannot be set off against salary, house property, or business income. Capital losses can only be set off against capital gains.

Which ITR form should I use to report mutual fund capital gains?

If you have capital gains from mutual funds or stocks, you cannot file ITR-1 (Sahaj). Salaried individuals must file ITR-2, and business owners/freelancers must file ITR-3.
