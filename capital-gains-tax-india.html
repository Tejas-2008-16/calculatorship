Capital Gains Tax on Mutual Funds & Stocks in India (2026): STCG & LTCG Rates

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Listed Equity LTCG (held >12 months): Taxed at 12.5% (hiked from 10%) on net gains exceeding the updated annual threshold of ₹1,25,000 under Section 112A for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28).
  • 2. Listed Equity STCG (held ≤12 months): Taxed at a flat 20% (hiked from 15%) under Section 111A, regardless of your personal income tax bracket.
  • 3. Debt Mutual Funds (Section 50AA): All gains from funds with ≤35% equity exposure are taxed at your slab rate as short-term gains, with indexation permanently gone.
  • 4. Unlisted Shares & Real Estate: Long-term holding period is 24 months, taxed at 12.5% without indexation (pre-July 23, 2024 real estate retains a 20% indexation option).
  • 5. Tax-Loss Harvesting: Short-term capital losses can offset both STCG and LTCG, while long-term losses can only offset LTCG—unabsorbed losses carry forward for up to 8 assessment years.
  • 6. ITR Filing Requirement: Earning even ₹1 in taxable capital gains disqualifies you from ITR-1 (Sahaj); salaried investors must file ITR-2 with detailed Schedule CG entries.

The Holding Period Arithmetic: Comparing Short-Term and Long-Term Tax Rates

Consider an illustrative holding: ₹5,00,000 invested in listed equity shares or an equity mutual fund scheme. Eleven months later, suppose the position has grown to show a gain of ₹2,00,000. If redeemed prior to completing 12 months, the gain is classified as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) under Section 111A, attracting a 20% tax rate plus 4% cess (₹41,600). If redeemed after completing 12 full months, the gain qualifies as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) under Section 112A, where the first ₹1,25,000 is exempt and the remaining ₹75,000 is taxed at 12.5% plus cess (₹9,750).

In this numerical illustration, crossing the 12-month statutory threshold changes the computed tax obligation from ₹41,600 to ₹9,750 on the exact same realized gain. Understanding these statutory cut-offs helps investors calculate actual net returns accurately.

Section 112A Tax-Gain Harvesting: How to Pocket ₹15,625 Tax-Free Every March

Section 112A of the Income Tax Act provides resident individuals with an annual exemption of up to ₹1,25,000 on long-term capital gains from listed equity shares and equity mutual funds. This exemption operates on a "use it or lose it" basis—it does not carry forward into the next financial year.

Smart investors exploit this through systematic Tax-Gain Harvesting every February and March:

  1. Log in to your investment platform (Groww, Zerodha Coin, Kuvera, or MFCentral) and download your Capital Gains Realized/Unrealized Statement for the current financial year.
  2. Identify long-term equity units (held for over 365 days) that possess unrealized profits up to ₹1,25,000.
  3. Sell those specific units on the exchange to crystallize the ₹1,25,000 capital gain before March 31st. Because total LTCG falls within the statutory exemption, your tax liability is exactly ₹0.
  4. Immediately repurchase the same fund or equivalent index ETF on the next trading day. Your portfolio asset allocation remains unchanged, but your purchase cost basis has been legally reset upward.

By stepping up your acquisition cost each year, you legally eliminate up to ₹1,25,000 of taxable gains annually. At the 12.5% LTCG rate plus cess, this strategy saves you ₹16,250 in cash taxes every single year.

Tax-Loss Harvesting and Set-Off Rules Under Sections 70 & 71

When markets correct, unrealized losses in your portfolio are not useless—they are valuable tax-deductible credits if booked strategically. However, the Income Tax Act enforces strict asymmetric set-off boundaries under Sections 70 and 71:

Loss Type Incurred Can Be Set Off Against STCG? Can Be Set Off Against LTCG? Can Set Off Salary / Business Income?
Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL) YES (Permitted) YES (Permitted) NO (Strictly prohibited)
Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL) NO (Prohibited by law) YES (Permitted) NO (Strictly prohibited)

The 8-Year Carry-Forward Rule: If your capital losses exceed your taxable gains in the current financial year, the unabsorbed loss can be carried forward for up to 8 consecutive assessment years under Section 74. Crucial legal requirement: to carry forward losses, you must file your Income Tax Return on or before the statutory due date (July 31st for non-audit individual taxpayers). Filing even one day late permanently forfeits your right to carry forward capital losses.

The Section 50AA Trap on Debt Mutual Funds

Until March 31, 2023, debt mutual funds held for over 3 years enjoyed 20% LTCG tax with indexation benefits—often driving the real effective tax rate down to 6% to 9%. The Finance Act permanently dismantled this structure by introducing Section 50AA.

Under Section 50AA, any mutual fund where domestic equity holding does not exceed 35% is classified as a "Specified Mutual Fund." When you redeem units purchased on or after April 1, 2023, 100% of the gain is deemed short-term capital gains and taxed at your marginal personal income tax slab rate (up to 30% plus cess and surcharge). Holding for 5, 7, or 10 years provides zero tax protection.

How Savvy Investors Bypass This: If you want conservative fixed income exposure with equity-like tax efficiency, look at Multi-Asset Allocation Funds or Equity Savings Funds that maintain at least 65% combined equity and arbitrage exposure. These funds qualify for 12.5% LTCG treatment with the ₹1.25 Lakh exemption, slashing tax drag by more than half compared to pure corporate bond or liquid funds.

Frequently Asked Questions

I earned ₹90,000 in equity LTCG this year. Do I need to pay any tax or even report it?

You owe zero tax because your ₹90,000 gain is comfortably below the ₹1,25,000 Section 112A annual exemption threshold. However, you must report it. In your ITR-2 return, enter the gross sale consideration and acquisition cost in Schedule CG; the portal software will automatically compute the exemption and reflect zero tax liability.

Can I file ITR-1 (Sahaj) if I sold a few mutual fund units with minimal profit?

No. The Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) strictly prohibits individuals with any capital gains or losses from filing ITR-1. Even if your net capital gain is ₹500, you must file ITR-2. Filing ITR-1 will result in a defective return notice under Section 139(9), requiring rectification.

How does FIFO (First-In, First-Out) affect tax on my monthly SIP redemptions?

Mutual fund redemptions are processed strictly on a First-In, First-Out basis under Section 45(2A). When you redeem units, the units purchased in your oldest SIP installments are sold first. If you have run an SIP for 3 years and withdraw partial funds, those oldest units qualify for the favorable 12.5% LTCG rate and ₹1.25 Lakh exemption, while your recent installments remain untouched.

I have ₹1 Lakh in short-term trading losses in stocks. Can I set that off against my salary?

No. Section 71(1) of the Income Tax Act strictly prohibits setting off capital losses against salary, business, or house property income. You can only set off that ₹1 Lakh short-term loss against short-term or long-term capital gains from other stocks, mutual funds, or gold. If unabsorbed, carry it forward for up to 8 years in ITR-2.

Are grandfathered mutual fund units purchased before January 31, 2018, still protected?

Yes. The grandfathering provisions under Section 55(2)(ac) remain intact. For equity shares and equity mutual fund units acquired before February 1, 2018, the cost of acquisition is stepped up to the fair market value (highest traded price or NAV) as of January 31, 2018. All gains accrued prior to January 31, 2018, remain permanently tax-free.

What is the grandfathering clause for real estate sold after the 2024 budget amendments?

For residential or commercial properties acquired prior to July 23, 2024, resident individuals and HUFs can compute tax under two methods: 12.5% without indexation, or 20% with cost inflation index (CII) indexation. You pay whichever computed amount is lower. For properties bought after July 23, 2024, only the flat 12.5% rate without indexation applies.

Understanding Annual Realization: How Section 112A Exemption Accounting Works

Investors frequently review their Consolidated Capital Gains Statement or Annual Information Statement (AIS) from depositories (NSDL/CDSL) or RTAs (CAMS/KFintech) toward the end of each financial year. Under Section 112A, the first ₹1,25,000 of net aggregate long-term capital gains across listed equity and equity mutual funds is tax-free each financial year. Unutilized portions of this annual threshold do not carry forward to subsequent years.

These figures reflect statutory capital gains provisions under Sections 111A, 112, 112A, and 50AA of the Income Tax Act for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28); consult a registered Chartered Accountant for scenario-specific tax filings.

How to think about this yourself

  • Holding Period Tracking: Check whether units have crossed 365 days before selling to avoid the 20% flat STCG rate under Section 111A.
  • Loss Offsetting Hierarchy: Short-term losses can offset both STCG and LTCG, but long-term losses can strictly offset LTCG. Unabsorbed losses can be carried forward up to 8 assessment years if the return is filed before the due date.
  • ITR Form Selection: Having even ₹1 of taxable capital gains makes you ineligible for ITR-1; Schedule CG must be filled in ITR-2 or ITR-3.
