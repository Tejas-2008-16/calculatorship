Capital Gains Tax on Mutual Funds & Stocks in India (2026): Complete Guide

Written by: Tejas (Independent Personal Finance Creator • YouTube Channel)
Published: February 2026 • Last Fact-Checked: September 2026 • Reading Time: 12 mins
⚠️ Educational Disclaimer: This article is authored by Tejas for educational purposes only. Tejas is not a Chartered Accountant or certified tax consultant. Tax laws are subject to periodic legislative amendments by Parliament under the Union Budget and Finance Acts.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Equity LTCG (Holding >12 months): Taxed at a flat rate of 12.5% on aggregate gains exceeding the annual ₹1.25 Lakh tax-free threshold under Section 112A.
  • Equity STCG (Holding ≤12 months): Taxed at a flat rate of 20% under Section 111A.
  • Debt Mutual Funds (Section 50AA): 100% of capital gains are taxed at your applicable personal income tax slab rate; indexation has been permanently abolished.
  • Real Estate & Gold: Long-term holding period is 24 months, taxed at a flat 12.5% without indexation.
  • Tax-Loss Harvesting: Realizing capital losses before March 31st allows you to offset taxable gains and reset purchase costs with zero tax.

Introduction: The Rationalized Capital Gains Framework in India

In recent legislative reforms, the Government of India completely overhauled the Indian capital gains taxation structure to simplify asset classification, eliminate complex indexation calculations, and establish uniform tax rates across financial and physical assets.

Understanding how capital gains are taxed in FY 2026-27 (Assessment Year 2027-28) is essential for every investor. When you redeem mutual fund units or sell shares, your net take-home profit depends heavily on your holding period and asset classification. Failing to plan for capital gains tax can lead to unexpected tax notices, penalties, or unnecessary tax overpayments.

This master guide covers the comprehensive tax architecture for listed equities, debt funds, real estate, and gold, and details legal tax optimization strategies like annual tax-loss harvesting and loss carry-forward rules.

How Equity Mutual Fund LTCG is Calculated (Worked Example)

Let us walk through a concrete numerical calculation for an investor who redeems units from an equity mutual fund held for 3 years, realizing a total capital gain of ₹3,50,000 in FY 2026-27:

  • Total Realized Long-Term Capital Gains = ₹3,50,000
  • Section 112A Statutory Exemption = -₹1,25,000
  • Net Taxable LTCG = ₹3,50,000 - ₹1,25,000 = ₹2,25,000
  • Base Tax at 12.5% = ₹2,25,000 × 0.125 = ₹28,125
  • 4% Health and Education Cess = ₹28,125 × 0.04 = ₹1,125
  • Total Final Tax Liability = ₹29,250

Effective Tax Rate: On a total profit of ₹3.50 Lakh, the actual effective tax paid is just 8.35% due to the ₹1.25 Lakh exemption buffer!

Tax-Loss Harvesting: How to Legally Reduce Your Tax to Zero

Tax-Loss Harvesting is an advanced, 100% legal wealth optimization technique used by savvy investors before the financial year closes on March 31st:

The Two Core Tax-Harvesting Strategies:

1. Harvesting Tax-Free LTCG Gains (The ₹1.25 Lakh Reset): If you have unrealized long-term capital gains in your portfolio, redeem enough units to realize exactly ₹1,25,000 in profit before March 31st. Reinvest that money into the exact same fund the following day. You pay ₹0 tax, while raising your acquisition cost base, eliminating tax on that ₹1.25 Lakh forever!

2. Offsetting Realized Losses Against Gains: If you realized ₹2,00,000 in short-term trading profits, identify underperforming stocks in your portfolio that have an unrealized loss of ₹1,50,000. Sell those loss-making shares to realize the Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL). Your net taxable gain drops from ₹2,00,000 down to ₹50,000, saving you ₹30,000 in immediate cash tax!

Set-Off & Carry-Forward Rules for Capital Losses

The Income Tax Act enforces strict rules on how capital losses can be set off:

  • Short-Term Capital Losses (STCL): Can be set off against BOTH Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) in the same financial year.
  • Long-Term Capital Losses (LTCL): Can ONLY be set off against Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). LTCL cannot be set off against STCG.
  • No Set-Off Against Other Income: Capital losses can never be set off against salary, business profits, house property, or interest income.
  • 8-Year Carry-Forward Rule: If your capital losses exceed your gains in a financial year, you can carry forward the unadjusted loss for up to 8 consecutive assessment years, provided you file your ITR on or before the due date (July 31st).

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the updated LTCG and STCG tax rates for listed equities in India?

For listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds, Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG on units held for 12 months or less) are taxed at a flat rate of 20% plus 4% cess. Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG on units held over 12 months) enjoy a ₹1,25,000 annual tax-free exemption per financial year, with gains exceeding that threshold taxed at a flat rate of 12.5% plus 4% cess.

How are gains from Debt Mutual Funds taxed in India in 2026?

Under Section 50AA of the Income Tax Act (effective for units acquired on or after April 1, 2023), indexation benefits have been abolished for debt mutual funds where equity exposure is less than 35%. All gains, regardless of the holding period, are deemed as Short-Term Capital Gains and added to your taxable income to be taxed at your personal income tax slab rate.

What is Tax-Loss Harvesting and is it legal in India?

Tax-Loss Harvesting is the 100% legal practice of selling loss-making stocks or mutual fund units before the end of the financial year (March 31st) to realize capital losses. These losses can be set off against taxable capital gains, reducing your net tax liability. You can simultaneously reinvest the proceeds into similar funds to maintain asset allocation.

Can capital losses be set off against salary or business income?

No. Under Section 70 and 71 of the Income Tax Act, capital losses (both STCL and LTCL) cannot be set off against salary, house property, or business income. Short-Term Capital Losses (STCL) can be set off against both STCG and LTCG. Long-Term Capital Losses (LTCL) can only be set off against LTCG. Unadjusted losses can be carried forward for up to 8 consecutive assessment years.

What is the tax rate on Real Estate and Physical Gold capital gains?

For real estate and physical gold, assets held over 24 months are classified as long-term and taxed at a flat rate of 12.5% without indexation. Assets held for 24 months or less are classified as short-term and taxed at personal income tax slab rates.

Which ITR form is mandatory for reporting capital gains in India?

If you have realized capital gains from stocks, mutual funds, or property during the financial year, you are legally ineligible to file the simplified ITR-1 (Sahaj). Salaried taxpayers must file ITR-2, while individuals with business or professional income must file ITR-3.
