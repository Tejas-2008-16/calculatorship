What is the Public Provident Fund (PPF)? — A Complete Guide for Indian Investors

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a premier small savings scheme established by the Central Government of India under the Public Provident Fund Act of 1968. Regulated and guaranteed directly by the Ministry of Finance, the PPF is engineered to promote disciplined, long-term retirement savings among Indian citizens while offering absolute sovereign safety of capital and unmatched tax benefits.

In India's financial ecosystem, the PPF holds the rare and prestigious Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) tax status. This means that: (1) your annual contributions are eligible for tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act up to ₹1.50 Lakh per financial year (under the Old Tax Regime); (2) the interest compounded annually in your account is 100% tax-free under Section 10(11); and (3) the entire maturity lump-sum withdrawn at the end of the tenure is completely exempt from income tax under both Old and New Tax Regimes.

Unlike volatile equity mutual funds or stock markets, PPF returns are immune to market downturns. The interest rate is reviewed and notified quarterly by the Ministry of Finance (currently set at 7.1% per annum compounded annually). The initial lock-in tenure is 15 complete financial years, which can be extended indefinitely in convenient 5-year blocks with or without ongoing contributions. This long lock-in acts as an automated behavioral barrier, preventing investors from prematurely liquidating their core retirement safety net.

Whether you are building a debt anchor for your asset allocation, saving for your child's future wedding, or accumulating a guaranteed tax-free retirement corpus, the PPF remains an indispensable pillar of Indian household wealth.

The Math Behind the Calculator — Formula Explained

PPF interest is calculated monthly on the lowest balance between the 5th day and the end of each calendar month, but it is credited to the account once annually at the end of the financial year (March 31st). When deposits are made uniformly at the beginning of each financial year, the maturity amount is calculated using the compound interest annuity formula:

Maturity Value (F) = P × [ ( (1 + r)n - 1 ) / r ] × (1 + r)

Where the mathematical variables represent:

  • F: Total tax-free maturity corpus at the end of the tenure in Indian Rupees (₹).
  • P: Annual contribution deposited at the start of each financial year (₹).
  • r: Annual interest rate expressed as a decimal (e.g. 7.1% is 7.1 / 100 = 0.071).
  • n: Number of completed financial years (15, 20, 25, or 30).

Step-by-Step Worked Mathematical Example in Indian Rupees

Let us calculate the exact maturity value for an investor who deposits the maximum permissible limit of ₹1,50,000 every year for the full 15-year statutory tenure at the official rate of 7.1% p.a.:

  • Annual Deposit (P) = ₹1,50,000
  • Interest Rate (r) = 7.1% = 0.071
  • Tenure (n) = 15 years
  • Compound Multiplier = (1 + 0.071)15 = (1.071)15 ≈ 2.8028714
  • Annuity Factor = (2.8028714 - 1) / 0.071 ≈ 25.392555
  • Annuity Due Adjustment = 25.392555 × (1 + 0.071) ≈ 27.195426
  • Final Maturity Corpus (F) = ₹1,50,000 × 27.195426 ≈ ₹40,68,209

Wealth Summary for 15-Year Max PPF:

Total Capital Deposited = 15 years × ₹1,50,000 = ₹22,50,000.
Total Tax-Free Interest Earned = ₹40,68,209 - ₹22,50,000 = ₹18,18,209.
Key Observation: The interest earned represents nearly 45% of the total corpus, and all ₹18.18 Lakh is 100% exempt from income tax!

Key Things to Know — Regulations, Taxes & Rules for Indian Investors

To maximize your returns and comply with statutory rules, understand the key operating provisions of the PPF scheme:

  • The '5th of the Month' Interest Rule: Interest in a PPF account is calculated monthly on the minimum balance between the 5th day and the last day of the calendar month. If you deposit funds on the 6th of April, you miss out on interest for the entire month of April. Always deposit between April 1st and April 5th for lump-sum deposits, or on or before the 5th of every month for monthly deposits.
  • 15-Year Financial Year Calculation: The 15-year maturity is calculated from the end of the financial year in which the initial deposit was made. For example, an account opened on August 15, 2025 completes its 15th financial year on March 31, 2041 (effective duration is ~15 years and 7 months).
  • Account Extension Rules (Form H): Upon completing 15 years, you have two options:
    • Extension with Contribution: You must submit Form H to your bank/post office within 1 year of maturity. You can then continue making annual deposits up to ₹1.5 Lakh.
    • Extension without Contribution: If no form is submitted, the account automatically extends without contributions. The entire balance continues earning interest, and you can withdraw any amount once per financial year.
  • Immunity from Court Attachment: Under Section 9 of the Public Provident Fund Act, PPF balances cannot be attached by any court of law in respect of any debt or liability incurred by the account holder. (Note: Sovereign tax dues are excluded).
  • Loan & Partial Withdrawal Rules:
    • Loan Facility: Permitted from Year 3 to Year 6 (up to 25% of balance at end of 2nd preceding financial year) at 1% interest rate above PPF rate.
    • Partial Withdrawal: Allowed once per year starting from Year 7 (up to 50% of the balance at the end of the 4th preceding year or end of preceding year, whichever is lower).

Common Mistakes Indian Investors Make with PPF

Avoid these costly errors when managing your Public Provident Fund account:

  1. Depositing Funds After the 5th of the Month: Depositing on the 10th or 20th of the month causes you to lose an entire month's interest compounding. Over a 15-year period, this simple timing mistake can cost you tens of thousands of rupees in lost interest.
  2. Exceeding the ₹1,50,000 Annual Ceiling: Depositing more than ₹1.5 Lakh across personal and minor accounts in a single financial year is prohibited. Excess funds earn zero interest and complicate tax filings.
  3. Missing the 1-Year Deadline for Form H: If you continue depositing money after 15 years without formally submitting Form H within the 1-year window, fresh deposits will not earn interest and will not qualify for Section 80C deductions.
  4. Relying Solely on PPF for Long-Term Retirement: While PPF offers rock-solid 7.1% guaranteed returns, relying exclusively on PPF without equity mutual funds can leave you vulnerable to real purchasing power erosion caused by high lifestyle inflation. A balanced blend of PPF (debt) and equity SIPs is optimal.
  5. Opening Multiple PPF Accounts in One's Own Name: An individual is legally permitted to hold only one PPF account in their own name. Opening two accounts in different banks is illegal; the second account will be treated as irregular and will earn 0% interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum yearly deposit limit in a PPF account?

Under Ministry of Finance regulations, the maximum deposit permitted in a Public Provident Fund (PPF) account is ₹1,50,000 per financial year. This statutory ceiling applies cumulatively across all PPF accounts opened in the individual's name, including any account opened as a legal guardian on behalf of a minor child. Any deposit exceeding ₹1.5 Lakh earns 0% interest and is refunded without tax benefits.

What is the '5th of the Month' rule in PPF interest calculation?

In PPF accounts, interest is calculated on the lowest balance recorded between the close of the 5th day and the end of each calendar month. Therefore, if you deposit your funds on or before the 5th of a month, you earn interest for that entire month. If you deposit on the 6th or later, that deposit earns zero interest for that month and only begins earning interest from the following month.

Is PPF interest and maturity corpus 100% tax-free in India?

Yes. PPF enjoys the coveted Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) tax status in India. The annual contribution qualifies for deduction under Section 80C (up to ₹1.50 Lakh under the Old Tax Regime), the annual compounded interest is 100% exempt from income tax under Section 10(11), and the final lump-sum maturity proceeds are completely tax-free under both the Old and New Tax Regimes.

Can a PPF account be extended after the mandatory 15-year maturity?

Yes. Upon completion of the initial 15-year tenure, an investor can extend their PPF account indefinitely in 5-year blocks. You can choose to extend with fresh contributions (by submitting Form H to your bank or post office within 1 year of maturity) or extend without making further deposits, during which the accumulated corpus continues to earn guaranteed sovereign interest.

When can partial withdrawals and loans be taken from a PPF account?

A loan against PPF can be availed between the 3rd and 6th financial year (up to 25% of the balance at the end of the 2nd preceding financial year at a concessional interest rate of 1% above the prevailing PPF rate). Partial withdrawals are permitted once per financial year starting from the 7th financial year, capped at 50% of the account balance.

Can Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) open a PPF account?

Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are prohibited from opening new PPF accounts. However, if a resident Indian opened a PPF account and subsequently acquired NRI status during the 15-year tenure, they may maintain the existing account on a non-repatriable basis until its original 15-year maturity. Extensions are not permitted for NRIs.
