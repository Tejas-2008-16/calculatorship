Mutual Fund Categories in India: SEBI Framework & Portfolio Construction

Written by Tejas Waghmare Published: 01 July 2026 Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Educational guide only, not personalized investment or tax advice. Rules and rates can change; verify official sources and see our disclaimer.

SEBI organizes all Indian mutual funds into 5 groups—Equity, Debt, Hybrid, Solution-Oriented, and Index/ETF/FoF—with strict market-cap and duration mandates. Most long-term investors only need 1–2 Core Equity funds (Nifty 50 Index or Flexi-Cap) paired with Liquid, Arbitrage, or Short Duration debt.

1. The 5 SEBI Mutual Fund Super-Categories Overview

SEBI's October 2017 Rationalisation framework (expanded with Flexi-Cap in November 2020) classifies Indian mutual funds into 5 super-categories so schemes across AMCs follow identical mandates and risk labels. Per AMFI data as of October 2026, total Indian mutual fund industry AUM stands at ₹83.39 lakh crore with ₹32,160 crore in monthly SIP inflows.

3. Debt & Hybrid Categories (Including BAFs & Arbitrage)

  • Overnight & Liquid Funds: Hold securities maturing in 1 day (Overnight) or up to 91 days (Liquid, graded exit load within 7 days). Suited for emergency funds; taxed at slab rate on redemption under Section 50AA.
  • Short Duration & Corporate Bond Funds: Hold 1–3 year debt or min 80% AA+ corporate paper for medium-term stability (still taxed at slab under Section 50AA).
  • Balanced Advantage (Dynamic Asset Allocation) & Aggressive Hybrid Funds: BAFs dynamically adjust equity vs debt inside the scheme without triggering investor-level capital gains tax on internal rebalancing; Aggressive Hybrid stays 65%–80% equity (qualifying for 12.5% LTCG >12 months).
  • Arbitrage & Multi-Asset Funds: Arbitrage funds maintain ≥65% cash-futures hedged exposure (low volatility with equity tax rates for 12+ month holders in 20%–30% tax slabs). Multi-Asset funds hold ≥10% each across at least 3 asset classes (e.g., equity, debt, gold).

4. Practical Core-Satellite Allocation Framework

Most retail investors do not need 10 funds. Keeping 2 to 4 complementary funds avoids portfolio overlap and lowers tracking effort: a Core (50%–70%) anchored in a Direct Nifty 50 Index Fund or Flexi-Cap Fund, optional Satellite Growth (15%–25%) in a Mid-Cap fund only for 10+ year horizons, and Stability / Buffer (10%–30%) in Short Duration Debt, Arbitrage, or Liquid reserves matched to near-term liquidity needs. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Flexi-Cap and Multi-Cap mutual funds?

Multi-Cap funds must maintain at least 25% each in Large, Mid, and Small Cap stocks at all times (75% minimum equity). Flexi-Cap funds only require 65% total equity and allow the manager total freedom across market-cap tiers.

Is an Arbitrage Fund better than a Liquid Fund for parking emergency or short-term money?

For investors in the 20% or 30% tax slab parking cash for 12+ months, Arbitrage funds benefit from equity taxation (12.5% LTCG above ₹1.25L vs slab rate under Section 50AA). However, for ultra-short emergency cash (<30 days), Liquid or Overnight funds offer tighter T+1 stability without exit load past 7 days.

How are Debt Mutual Funds taxed compared to Hybrid Funds?

Debt mutual funds (≤35% domestic equity) are taxed at your marginal income slab rate under Section 50AA regardless of holding duration. Equity-oriented Hybrid funds (≥65% equity, including Aggressive Hybrid and Arbitrage funds) qualify for 12.5% LTCG after 12 months and 20% STCG within 12 months.

What is an ETF vs an Index Fund (or FoF)?

ETFs trade live on NSE/BSE and require a Demat and trading account. Index Funds and Fund-of-Funds use standard mutual fund SoA folios, require no Demat account, allow automated fractional SIPs cleanly, and carry slightly higher TER when structured as an FoF.
