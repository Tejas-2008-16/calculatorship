Mutual Fund Categories in India: Complete 2026 Guide

Hi, I'm Tejas — an engineering student researching personal finance. This guide provides a breakdown of SEBI's 36 standardized mutual fund categories, investment mandates, and asset allocation rules. It was written with AI assistance and checked against official SEBI circulars and AMFI regulations. It is strictly educational and does not constitute investment advice or fund recommendations.

SEBI has classified all Indian mutual fund schemes into well-defined categories so that investors can compare like-for-like. Understanding each category — its mandate, risk, typical returns, and who it suits — is the single most important step before investing a single rupee. Once you know the categories, explore our companion Portfolio Construction & Asset Allocation Guide to build a diversified long-term portfolio.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • SEBI's October 2017 categorisation circular created exactly 36 mutual fund categories across Equity (10), Debt (16), Hybrid (6), Solution-Oriented (2), and Other (2) — every AMC can offer one fund per category (with exceptions).
  • Large-cap funds invest at least 80% in the top 100 companies by market cap (Nifty 100); mid-cap funds invest at least 65% in companies ranked 101–250; small-cap funds invest at least 65% in companies ranked 251 and beyond.
  • Flexi-cap funds are SEBI-mandated to invest across market-cap segments with no fixed allocation, giving the fund manager maximum flexibility — this is the best starting fund category for new investors without strong market views.
  • Liquid funds (overnight to 91-day instruments) and overnight funds are not debt mutual funds for tax purposes — they are still taxed at your income slab rate under Section 50AA regardless of holding period.
  • ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme) is the only equity fund category that qualifies for ₹1.5 Lakh Section 80C deduction, but comes with a mandatory 3-year lock-in per installment.

Before October 2017, Indian AMCs could create funds with almost any mandate and name them anything they wanted. A "Large Cap" fund from one house might hold 40% mid-caps. A "Balanced Fund" could range from 40% to 80% equity. Investors had no reliable way to compare similar products. SEBI's categorisation circular changed everything by forcing every fund into one of 36 strictly defined boxes with fixed investment mandates. Key Debt Fund Categories You Need to Know

  • Liquid Fund: Invests in instruments maturing within 91 days (T-Bills, Commercial Papers). Ideal for parking emergency funds; yields 6.5%–7.5%. Taxed at your slab rate under Section 50AA.
  • Short Duration Fund: Duration of 1–3 years. Moderate credit risk; suitable for 2–3 year financial goals.
  • Corporate Bond Fund: Minimum 80% in AA+ and above rated bonds. Relatively safe with higher yields than government securities.
  • Gilt Fund: 80%+ in government securities (sovereign bonds). Zero credit risk; significant interest rate risk on long-duration versions.

Critical 2026 Tax Update: Under Section 50AA (effective April 2023), all debt fund capital gains—regardless of holding period—are taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate. The earlier flat LTCG tax advantage for debt funds held 3+ years no longer exists.

Frequently Asked Questions About Fund Categories

Which fund category should a 28-year-old salaried investor with ₹8,000/month to invest start with?

Start with a single Direct-Growth Flexi-Cap Fund or a Nifty 50/Nifty 100 Index Fund. These provide broad diversification, give the fund manager (or the index) flexibility to allocate across market-cap segments, and have consistently performed across 15+ year horizons. Add a mid-cap fund only after your equity SIP corpus exceeds ₹5 Lakh and you have a confirmed 10+ year horizon.

Are hybrid funds better than separate equity + debt fund portfolios?

Balanced Advantage Funds (BAFs) and Aggressive Hybrid Funds automatically rebalance between equity and debt—a genuine convenience advantage. However, BAF rebalancing has no tax implication for the investor (the fund handles it internally), while manually rebalancing a separate equity + debt portfolio triggers capital gains tax on the sold portion. For tax-sensitive investors in the 20%–30% bracket, hybrid funds have a meaningful tax efficiency advantage.

Your Starting Framework

For most salaried Indian investors with horizons above 7 years: one Nifty 50 or Nifty 100 Index Fund (passive) + one Flexi-Cap or Mid-Cap actively managed fund covers 80% of what you need. Every additional fund you add increases tracking complexity without proportionally increasing returns. How to Think About This Yourself

SEBI's category framework was designed to eliminate misleading scheme names and provide comparability. When evaluating categories for your portfolio:

  • Mandate Rigidity: Recognize that category definitions legally bind fund managers — Large Cap funds must maintain at least 80% in top 100 stocks, whereas Flexi Cap funds offer unconstrained cross-cap allocation.
  • Overlap Avoidance: Holding multiple schemes within the same category (e.g., three Large Cap funds) generates redundant portfolio overlap and fees without additional diversification.
  • Tax Rules by Underlying Exposure: Verify whether a fund qualifies as equity-oriented (>=65% domestic equity) for Section 112A/111A treatment versus debt funds taxed at marginal slab rates under Section 50AA.

Where to Get Personalised Help

Category selection and asset allocation depend heavily on personal time horizons and risk profiles. For individualized investment advice, consult a fee-only planner through the SEBI Registered Investment Advisers Directory or a tax expert via the ICAI Members Directory.
