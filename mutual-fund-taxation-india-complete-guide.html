Introduction & Budget Overhaul Summary

The taxation of capital gains in India underwent substantial restructuring under the Union Budget. For mutual fund investors, understanding these statutory tax classifications is vital to calculating post-tax net portfolio returns. Whether you invest via Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) or one-time lumpsums, capital gains taxes apply whenever units are liquidated or switched.

Below is the master summary of mutual fund taxation applicable for Financial Year 2026-27:

Category Holding Period for LTCG Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)
Equity-Oriented Funds (≥65% Equity) ≥ 12 Months 20% flat 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1,25,000 / year
Specified Debt Funds (≤35% Equity, post-01/04/23) No LTCG classification Taxed at applicable Income Slab rate Taxed at applicable Income Slab rate
Conservative Hybrid Funds (35%-65% Equity) ≥ 24 Months Taxed at applicable Income Slab rate 12.5% without indexation
Arbitrage Funds (Derivatives ≥65% Equity) ≥ 12 Months 20% flat 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1,25,000 / year

1. Equity Mutual Fund Taxation Rules FY 2026-27

A mutual fund is classified as equity-oriented if it maintains a minimum of 65% of its total portfolio in domestic listed Indian equities. This encompasses Large Cap, Mid Cap, Small Cap, Flexi Cap, Value, Sectoral, and ELSS funds.

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) — Units Held < 12 Months

If you redeem equity mutual fund units before completing 365 calendar days from the date of allotment, the resultant profit is treated as STCG under Section 111A. The updated tax rate is a flat 20% (increased from 15% under earlier budgets), plus applicable surcharge and 4% Health & Education Cess.

Worked Example: If you purchase equity mutual fund units for ₹2,00,000 and redeem them 8 months later for ₹3,00,000, your net STCG is ₹1,00,000. Your tax liability is ₹1,00,000 × 20% = ₹20,000 (excluding cess).

Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) — Units Held ≥ 12 Months

When equity units are held for 12 months or longer, gains are classified as LTCG under Section 112A. Under current tax provisions:

  • Annual Exemption Limit: Total aggregate LTCG up to ₹1,25,000 per financial year across listed shares and equity mutual funds is completely tax-free.
  • Tax Rate: Net LTCG in excess of ₹1,25,000 is taxed at a flat rate of 12.5% without indexation benefits.

Worked Example: If your total long-term capital gain across equity funds during the financial year is ₹3,00,000, your taxable LTCG is ₹3,00,000 − ₹1,25,000 = ₹1,75,000. Your tax payable is ₹1,75,000 × 12.5% = ₹21,875 (plus 4% cess = ₹22,750).

2. Debt Mutual Fund Taxation Rules (Post April 2023)

The Finance Act fundamentally reformed the taxation of Specified Mutual Funds (funds investing ≤35% in domestic equities, such as Liquid Funds, Overnight Funds, Short Duration, and Corporate Bond Funds). For investments made on or after April 1, 2023:

  • Removal of Indexation: The traditional benefit of cost inflation indexation (CII) has been eliminated.
  • Slab-Rate Taxation: All capital gains from debt funds, irrespective of whether held for 1 month or 10 years, are treated as short-term capital gains and added directly to your gross taxable income, taxed at your applicable personal income tax slab rate (e.g., 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, or 30%).
Feature Pre-April 1, 2023 Rules Current Rules (FY 2026-27)
LTCG Holding Period 36 Months Not Applicable (All gains deemed STCG)
Indexation Benefit Available (Cost adjusted for Inflation) Completely Eliminated
Tax Rate (>3 Years) 20% after indexation Taxed at Personal Income Tax Slab

When Debt Funds Still Make Sense: Unlike bank Fixed Deposits where interest is taxed annually on an accrual basis, debt mutual funds defer tax until actual redemption, enabling uninterrupted compounding of pre-tax capital.

3. Hybrid Fund & Arbitrage Fund Taxation

Hybrid funds invest in a combination of equity and debt assets. Their tax status depends entirely on their average domestic equity allocation:

  • Aggressive Hybrid & Dynamic Asset Allocation (65%+ Equity): Enjoy complete equity fund taxation status (20% STCG / 12.5% LTCG above ₹1.25L).
  • Conservative Hybrid Funds (35% to 65% Equity): Classified as non-equity funds. Held < 24 months = slab rate; held ≥ 24 months = 12.5% without indexation.
  • Arbitrage Funds: Allocate ≥65% to equity shares while hedging price risk through derivative futures contracts. Consequently, they are statutorily taxed as equity funds, making them highly tax-efficient alternatives to liquid debt funds for individuals in the 30% tax slab.

4. ELSS Mutual Fund Taxation & Section 80C

Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) come with a mandatory 3-year statutory lock-in period from the date of each allotment. Because units cannot be redeemed before 36 months, all redemptions from ELSS funds invariably generate Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG).

ELSS gains are eligible for the annual ₹1.25 Lakh tax-free LTCG threshold. For taxpayers opting for the Old Tax Regime, investments in ELSS qualify for a deduction up to ₹1,50,000 under Section 80C. Learn more in our dedicated ELSS Tax Saving Guide.

5. Tax Harvesting Strategy: How to Legally Save Tax

Tax harvesting is an institutional wealth strategy where an investor systematically redeems long-term equity mutual fund units every financial year to realize gains up to the tax-free limit of ₹1,25,000, and immediately reinvests the proceeds back into the fund.

  1. Step 1: Identify equity mutual fund units held for over 12 months with unrealized gains close to ₹1,25,000.
  2. Step 2: Redeem the units before March 31st. Because total LTCG is within the annual ₹1.25L threshold, your tax liability is ₹0.
  3. Step 3: Reinvest the redeemed funds into the same or another fund. This resets your cost basis of acquisition higher, permanently eliminating tax liability on those historical gains.

6. SIP Redemption Tax Calculation (FIFO Method)

Tax authorities in India calculate mutual fund capital gains using the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method. Each monthly SIP installment represents a distinct acquisition batch with its own independent purchase NAV, date, and holding duration.

Installment Date Investment Amount Redemption Date Holding Period Tax Status
01 Jan 2025 ₹10,000 15 Feb 2026 13.5 Months (>12m) LTCG (12.5% > ₹1.25L)
01 Feb 2025 ₹10,000 15 Feb 2026 12.5 Months (>12m) LTCG (12.5% > ₹1.25L)
01 Mar 2025 ₹10,000 15 Feb 2026 11.5 Months (<12m) STCG (20% flat)
01 Oct 2025 ₹10,000 15 Feb 2026 4.5 Months (<12m) STCG (20% flat)

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I pay tax on every monthly SIP installment?

No. Investing via monthly SIP triggers zero tax at the time of investment. Capital gains tax is levied exclusively when you redeem or switch units out of the fund.

Is dividend income from mutual funds taxable in India?

Yes. Dividend income from mutual fund IDCW plans is added to your gross total income and taxed at your applicable personal income tax slab rate. Additionally, TDS at 10% is deducted by AMCs if dividend payouts exceed ₹5,000 in a financial year.

What is the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) on mutual fund redemption?

For equity-oriented mutual funds, an STT of 0.001% is deducted automatically by the fund house on redemption or sale value. Debt mutual funds do not attract any STT.

How do I obtain a Capital Gains Statement for ITR filing?

You can download a consolidated Capital Gains Statement across all your mutual fund folios in a single PDF/Excel sheet from MFCentral, CAMS (myCAMS), or KFintech (KFinKart) after the end of the financial year.

Are international and global mutual funds taxed like debt funds?

Yes. Overseas and fund-of-funds (FoF) investing in foreign equities do not hold 65% domestic Indian equities, so their capital gains are classified as debt-oriented instruments and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates.
