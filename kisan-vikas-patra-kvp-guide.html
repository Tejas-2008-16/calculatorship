Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP): How India's Post Office Doubling Scheme Works

Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP) is a sovereign-backed post office savings scheme currently offering 7.50% annual compounding, doubling your invested capital in exactly 115 months (9 years and 7 months). Backed by a full Government of India guarantee with no maximum deposit ceiling, KVP provides absolute capital security, though interest is fully taxable at your applicable income slab rate with no Section 80C deduction.

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 26 September 2026  •  Last reviewed: 24 November 2026  •  7.50% Sovereign Compounding, 115-Month Doubling & Zero Upper Limit

Educational small savings scheme guide only. Not certified tax or financial advice. KVP is governed by the Government Savings Promotion Act, 1873 and KVP Scheme, 2019. Read our full disclaimer.

Kisan Vikas Patra is the Post Office's lump-sum doubling certificate: not a monthly income instrument, not a tax-saving vehicle, but a straightforward sovereign-backed promise to double your principal at a fixed rate over a defined period. Our guide covers the math, current rules, premature closure mechanics, tax implications, and when KVP makes sense versus FDs, PPF, or POMIS.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Doubling Instrument: KVP is not a monthly income scheme. It doubles your lump sum at maturity. At 7.5% per annum (Q2 FY 2026-27), the doubling period is 115 months (9 years 7 months).
  • No Maximum Limit: Unlike PPF (₹1.5L/year) or POMIS (₹9L/₹15L), KVP has no upper ceiling. Useful for parking large lump sums sovereignly.
  • No Section 80C Benefit: KVP deposits are not deductible under Section 80C. Accrued gains are fully taxable at your slab rate.
  • Sovereign Safety: Backed directly by the Government of India. Zero default risk on principal or promised interest.
  • Lock-in Reality: No encashment for the first 2.5 years (30 months) except on death or court order. After that, premature closure returns less than the contracted doubling amount.

1. What is Kisan Vikas Patra?

Kisan Vikas Patra is a Post Office small savings certificate scheme administered by the Department of Posts under the Ministry of Communications. Despite the name ("Kisan" means farmer in Hindi), it has been open to all Indian citizens since its relaunch in 2014: not just agricultural workers.

Operation is simple: you invest a lump sum, and the principal doubles on a date specified at the time of purchase. There are no monthly payouts, no quarterly interest credits, and no partial withdrawals during the tenure. Interest compounds annually inside the certificate and is received as a single lump sum at maturity.

The scheme was originally introduced in 1988, discontinued in 2011 following recommendations from the Shyamala Gopinath Committee (which found it was being misused for money laundering due to its then bearer-certificate format), and relaunched in 2014 with mandatory KYC requirements to prevent anonymous cash parking.

2. How the Doubling Math Works

KVP uses annual compound interest. Unlike bank FDs where monthly or quarterly compounding is common, KVP compounds once per year on the anniversary of the purchase date.

Maturity Amount = Principal × (1 + r)n
r = annual rate (0.075 at current 7.5%), n = years

At 7.5% compounded annually, doubling requires solving 2 = (1.075)n, giving n ≈ 9.58 years = approximately 115 months. Such timing forms the maturity date printed on the certificate at the time of purchase.

Worked Examples at 7.5% per annum

Investment Maturity Period Maturity Amount Interest Earned
₹1,00,000 115 months ₹2,00,000 ₹1,00,000
₹5,00,000 115 months ₹10,00,000 ₹5,00,000
₹10,00,000 115 months ₹20,00,000 ₹10,00,000
₹25,00,000 115 months ₹50,00,000 ₹25,00,000

Note: Your existing certificate retains the rate and maturity date fixed at the time of purchase. Future quarterly rate revisions affect only new certificate purchases.

3. Eligibility, Denominations & KYC Requirements

Who Can Invest

  • Any resident Indian adult (individually or jointly with another adult)
  • A guardian on behalf of a minor or person of unsound mind
  • A minor aged 10 years or above in their own name
  • NRIs, HUFs, and Trusts are not eligible

Certificate Denominations

KVP certificates are available in denominations of ₹1,000, ₹5,000, ₹10,000, ₹50,000, and ₹1,00,000. Any amount can be purchased as a combination of these denominations. There is no maximum purchase limit.

KYC and PAN Requirements

Since the 2014 relaunch, the Government mandated identity documentation to prevent anonymous cash parking:

  • Below ₹50,000: Standard identity proof (Aadhaar, Voter ID, Passport) is sufficient.
  • ₹50,000 and above: PAN card is mandatory.
  • ₹10 lakh and above: Also requires a declaration of income source; post office may request supporting documents.

Certificates are now held in electronic form in the investor's post office savings account (POSA) for most post offices. Certificates can be transferred between post offices by submitting a transfer application.

4. Premature Closure Rules

Period from Purchase Closure Permitted? Effective Payout
Less than 2.5 years (30 months) Not permitted Allowed only on holder's death, court order, or gazette officer forfeiture
2.5 years to maturity Permitted Principal + interest at a lower rate than the contracted 7.5% (exact schedule per Post Office Small Savings Rules)

After 2.5 years, premature closure does not deduct from your principal (unlike POMIS's 1-2% deduction). Instead, the post office recalculates interest at a lower rate applicable for the actual holding period, so you get less than the contracted doubling amount.

Example: Premature Closure at 3 Years

Invest ₹1,00,000 at 7.5% per annum, close at exactly 3 years: you receive approximately ₹1,24,230 (₹1,00,000 × 1.0753). Maturity value contracted at 115 months would have been ₹2,00,000. Early exit at 3 years forfeits more than half the total interest benefit.

Pledging as a loan alternative: Instead of breaking KVP prematurely, it can be pledged as collateral to obtain a loan (typically up to 80-90% of the encashment value) from a bank. Borrowing costs on such loans typically run around 2% above the KVP rate.

5. Tax Treatment: No 80C, No Exemption

No Section 80C Deduction

KVP deposits do not qualify for deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. Schemes eligible for 80C include PPF, ELSS, NSC, SSY, and 5-year tax-saver FDs: KVP is not among them. Investing ₹1 lakh in KVP provides zero income tax reduction in the year of investment.

Interest is Fully Taxable at Slab Rate

KVP interest must be reported as "Income from Other Sources" in your ITR. The question is when to report it:

  • Accrual basis: Report interest as it accrues each year, even though you haven't received cash. Accrual reporting represents the technically correct approach and avoids a large taxable income spike in the maturity year. Many Chartered Accountants recommend this for KVP and NSC.
  • Cash/receipt basis: Report all interest only in the year you encash the certificate. Simpler, but creates a large one-year income peak that could push you into a higher bracket.

Total tax liability over the holding period is identical under either method: only the timing differs. Consult a CA on the approach appropriate for your situation.

Zero TDS at Source

The post office deducts no TDS on KVP interest. Gross maturity amounts are paid in full. However, you remain legally obligated to self-report and pay advance tax if your total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a year.

When KVP Makes Sense

  • Large lump sum beyond the PPF/POMIS ceiling: ₹20 to ₹50 Lakh or more needing sovereign safety with no investment cap.
  • 80C limit already exhausted: If EPF, ELSS, or insurance premium has used the full ₹1.5L, the absence of an 80C benefit from KVP is irrelevant.
  • Accumulation goal, not income distribution: Unlike POMIS which disburses monthly cash flow, KVP compounds entirely to a lump sum at maturity: suitable for long-horizon capital goals.

When KVP Is Not the Right Choice

  • If you are in the 30% tax bracket: PPF's EEE status offers significantly better post-tax outcomes
  • If you need regular income: use POMIS or an SWP strategy instead
  • If you need the funds within 2.5 years: KVP is unsuitable; use a liquid fund or short-term FD
  • If you want 80C savings: PPF, ELSS, NSC, or 5-year tax-saver FD serve that purpose

7. Practical Considerations Before Investing

Nomination

Register a nominee at the time of purchase or subsequently at the post office. For large investments, this is essential: it ensures the maturity proceeds reach your family without probate delays if you die before maturity.

Advance Tax Obligation

Even though no TDS is deducted, if your total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a year, you must pay advance tax in four quarterly instalments. If reporting KVP interest on an accrual basis, include the accrued interest in your advance tax calculations to avoid penalties under Sections 234B and 234C.

Rate Lock-in on Purchase

Once purchased, the doubling period and effective rate are permanently locked in. Future quarterly government rate changes affect only new certificate purchases: your existing certificates are unaffected. Such mechanics mirror a fixed-rate FD.

NSC Comparison for Tax-Conscious Investors

National Savings Certificates (NSC) offer Section 80C deduction and the same sovereign backing as KVP. NSC has a 5-year term vs. KVP's ~9.6-year term. For investors where 80C space is available, see the Section 80C Deductions Guide for a full comparison of qualifying instruments.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Is KVP better than NSC?

Both are sovereign-backed Post Office instruments. NSC qualifies for Section 80C (KVP does not) and has a 5-year term. KVP's advantage is no investment ceiling: useful when the ₹1.5L 80C limit is already used up and you have a large lump sum. For smaller amounts with available 80C headroom, NSC typically offers better tax efficiency.

Can an NRI purchase KVP?

No. Eligibility is restricted to resident Indian citizens. If a resident Indian holder becomes an NRI after purchasing, the certificate continues to maturity but cannot be renewed or reinvested in KVP.

Does the PAN rule apply per transaction or per year?

PAN is mandatory for any single KVP transaction of ₹50,000 or more. Splitting transactions artificially to avoid PAN requirements is not advisable and may attract scrutiny. Once you are KYC-compliant, it is best practice to provide PAN for all transactions regardless of amount.

Can KVP be held jointly?

Yes. Accounts can be held jointly by up to three adults. In a joint account, any of the joint holders can encash the certificate. Maturity proceeds flow to the first-named holder by default, unless otherwise specified.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the current maturity period for Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP)?

Under the current 7.50% interest rate notified by the Ministry of Finance, Kisan Vikas Patra strictly doubles your principal investment in 115 months (9 years and 7 months). For example, a ₹1,00,000 deposit matures to exactly ₹2,00,000.

Is there an upper investment limit in Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP)?

No. Unlike the Public Provident Fund (capped at ₹1.5 Lakh/year) or Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (capped at ₹30 Lakh), KVP has no maximum investment limit. You can invest any amount in multiples of ₹100, subject to standard PAN and KYC documentation.

Is the interest earned on Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP) taxable?

Yes. KVP interest is fully taxable at your applicable income tax slab rates under 'Income from Other Sources'. Furthermore, deposits do not qualify for any Section 80C deduction. However, the Post Office does not deduct any TDS upon maturity.

What is the premature withdrawal lock-in period for KVP?

KVP accounts have a statutory lock-in period of 2 years and 6 months (30 months) from the date of deposit. Encashment before 30 months is permitted only upon the death of the account holder or on order by a court of law.

Can Kisan Vikas Patra certificates be pledged as loan collateral?

Yes. KVP certificates can be pledged as security/collateral with public sector banks, private scheduled banks, and housing finance companies for availing secured loans under official Post Office pledging rules.
