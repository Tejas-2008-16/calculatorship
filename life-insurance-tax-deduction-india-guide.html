Life Insurance Tax Benefits in India: Section 80C, 10(10D) & ULIP Rules After Budget 2021

For decades, life insurance policies were marketed with the blanket claim that 'premiums are deductible and maturity is 100% tax-free.' Sweeping legislative amendments in the Union Budgets of 2021 and 2023 dramatically curtailed this tax shelter. Understand the exact Section 80C premium deduction limits, the critical 10% premium-to-sum-assured rule, the ₹2.5 Lakh ULIP cap, the ₹5 Lakh traditional policy threshold, and Section 194DA TDS rules.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Premium Deduction under Section 80C: Life insurance premiums qualify for deduction up to ₹1.5 Lakh per year (Old Tax Regime only), provided the annual premium does not exceed 10% of the sum assured.
  • Death Benefit is Always 100% Tax-Free: Sum assured received by nominees on death of the insured is completely exempt from tax under Section 10(10D), with zero financial caps or premium tests.
  • The 10% Sum Assured Test: For policies issued after April 1, 2012, if annual premium exceeds 10% of the minimum sum assured, Section 80C is restricted to 10% and the entire maturity proceeds become fully taxable!
  • Budget 2021 ULIP Rule: For ULIP policies issued on or after February 1, 2021, if total annual premium exceeds ₹2.5 Lakh in any year, maturity proceeds lose Section 10(10D) exemption and are taxed as capital gains.
  • Budget 2023 Traditional Policy Rule: For non-linked endowment/money-back policies issued on or after April 1, 2023, if aggregate annual premium exceeds ₹5 Lakh, maturity returns are taxed under Income from Other Sources.

1. The Three Pillars of Life Insurance Taxation in India

Indian tax law evaluates life insurance across three distinct transaction stages:

  1. Entry Stage (Premium Payments): Whether the premium paid can be subtracted from your gross taxable income under Section 80C.
  2. Holding Stage (Accruals & Bonuses): Annual reversionary bonuses and internal portfolio returns inside the policy accumulate tax-free during the policy term.
  3. Exit Stage (Maturity / Surrender / Death Claim): Whether the payout is completely tax-free under Section 10(10D) or taxable under capital gains or other sources, triggering TDS under Section 194DA.

Understanding these three stages is vital because a policy that receives a deduction at the entry stage does not automatically guarantee tax exemption at the maturity stage if statutory ceilings are breached.

2. Section 80C Premium Deductions: Eligible Entities & the 10% Rule

Under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961, an individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) can claim a deduction for life insurance premium payments up to the statutory composite limit of ₹1,50,000 per financial year (available exclusively under the Old Tax Regime).

Permissible Beneficiaries

  • Individual Taxpayer: Can claim premium paid for policies covering oneself, spouse, and children (whether dependent or independent, minor or adult, married or unmarried). Premiums paid for parents, siblings, or in-laws are not eligible for deduction under Section 80C.
  • HUF: Can claim deduction on premiums paid for policies insuring any member of the Hindu Undivided Family.

The 10% Premium-to-Sum-Assured Statutory Ceiling

Under Section 80C(5), the allowable deduction is subject to strict proportionality ratios based on the policy issuance date:

Policy Issuance Date Max Premium Deductible under 80C Maturity Exemption Status under 10(10D)
Issued prior to April 1, 2012 Up to 20% of minimum capital sum assured Exempt if premium ≤ 20% of sum assured
Issued on or after April 1, 2012 Up to 10% of minimum capital sum assured Exempt ONLY if premium ≤ 10% of sum assured
For Persons with Disability (Sec 80U/80DDB) issued on/after April 1, 2013 Up to 15% of minimum capital sum assured Exempt if premium ≤ 15% of sum assured

Lock-in Requirement: If a policyholder surrenders or terminates a traditional life policy before paying premiums for at least 2 full years (or terminates a ULIP before 5 years), all Section 80C deductions previously claimed are reversed and added back to taxable income in the year of termination.

3. Section 10(10D) Maturity Exemption: Core Statutory Conditions

Section 10(10D) is the primary provision that exempts any sum received under a life insurance policy, including allocated bonuses. However, complete tax exemption requires satisfying critical conditions:

  • Condition 1 (Proportionality Test): For policies issued after April 1, 2012, if the annual premium payable in any single year exceeds 10% of the actual capital sum assured, the entire maturity proceeds become fully taxable. Not just the excess, but the whole amount received!
  • Condition 2 (Keyman Insurance Exclusion): Any sum received under a Keyman insurance policy is excluded from exemption and taxed as business profit or salary.
  • Condition 3 (Employer-Sponsored Plans): Payouts received under an employer-sponsored insurance plan or salary deduction scheme may have separate tax treatments under Section 17.

4. The Budget 2021 Watershed: ULIP Taxation Above ₹2.5 Lakh

Prior to February 2021, high-net-worth individuals routinely used Unit Linked Insurance Plans (ULIPs) as tax shelters, investing tens of lakhs annually into equity ULIPs and withdrawing hundreds of lakhs completely tax-free under Section 10(10D), while equity mutual fund investors paid LTCG tax under Section 112A.

The Finance Act, 2021 amended Section 10(10D) by inserting the fourth and fifth provisos:

The ₹2,50,000 ULIP Cap Rule

  • Applicability: Applicable to all ULIP policies issued on or after February 1, 2021.
  • The Threshold: If the aggregate annual premium payable for one or more ULIPs exceeds ₹2,50,000 in any financial year during the policy term, the maturity proceeds from such policy(ies) are NOT exempt under Section 10(10D).
  • Capital Gains Treatment: Under Section 45(1B) and Section 112A, the surplus gains upon maturity or surrender are taxed as capital gains from equity-oriented mutual funds (12.5% on long-term gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh, or 20% for short-term gains).
  • Multi-Policy Aggregation: If an investor holds three ULIP policies with annual premiums of ₹1 Lakh, ₹1.2 Lakh, and ₹1.5 Lakh, the investor can select policies whose total premium does not exceed ₹2.5 Lakh (e.g., Policy 1 + Policy 2 = ₹2.2 Lakh) to claim Section 10(10D) exemption; the third policy's maturity will be taxed under Section 112A.

5. The Budget 2023 Amendment: Traditional Endowment Policies Above ₹5 Lakh

Following the 2021 ULIP restriction, affluent investors migrated capital toward high-ticket traditional endowment policies and non-participating guaranteed return insurance plans. In response, the Finance Act, 2023 inserted the sixth and seventh provisos to Section 10(10D):

  • Effective Date: Applies to all traditional (non-linked) life insurance policies issued on or after April 1, 2023.
  • The ₹5,00,000 Premium Ceiling: If the aggregate annual premium payable across all non-linked life insurance policies exceeds ₹5,00,000 in any financial year, the maturity proceeds from the policies causing the breach lose exemption under Section 10(10D).
  • Taxation Head: The net gain (maturity payout minus total premiums paid) is taxed as "Income from Other Sources" at the taxpayer's normal slab rate (unlike ULIPs which enjoy 12.5% concessional capital gains rates!).
  • Grandfathering Protection: Traditional policies issued prior to April 1, 2023, remain fully exempt under Section 10(10D) regardless of premium amount, provided they satisfy the basic 10% sum assured condition.

6. Death Benefit Taxation: The Unshakable 100% Sovereign Exemption

Amidst all the restrictive caps on maturity payouts, one fundamental protection remains absolute under Indian tax law:

The Death Benefit Exemption: Any sum received by legal nominees or beneficiaries upon the untimely death of the insured individual is 100% exempt from income tax under Section 10(10D) without any monetary limitation whatsoever. This exemption applies whether the annual premium was ₹10,000 or ₹50,00,000, and whether the policy was a pure term plan, an endowment policy, or a high-ticket ULIP.

This statutory guarantee reinforces why pure term insurance is universally recognized as the purest financial protection tool: 100% tax-exempt death protection with maximum risk cover per rupee of premium.

7. Section 194DA TDS Rules on Non-Exempt Maturity Payouts

When an insurance policy fails to satisfy Section 10(10D) criteria, the insurer is legally required to withhold tax before disbursing the maturity or surrender proceeds:

  • Statutory Trigger: If the aggregate payout on a non-exempt life insurance policy exceeds ₹1,00,000 in a financial year.
  • TDS Rate: Section 194DA mandates TDS at 5% on the net income portion only (total maturity payout minus total cumulative premiums paid over the policy term).
  • Historical Comparison: Prior to September 2019, Section 194DA deducted 1% on the gross maturity amount. The amendment ensuring 5% deduction on net income brought significant relief to investors, ensuring TDS matches true economic gain.
  • Final Tax Liability: The 5% TDS is merely an advance withholding credit. You must report the net income under Schedule OS in your annual ITR and pay any balance tax according to your marginal tax bracket (which can be up to 30% plus cess).

8. Comparative Matrix: Term Plan vs Endowment vs ULIP vs Annuity

Compare the tax treatment across four major life insurance categories in India:

9. Old vs New Tax Regime: Impact on Life Insurance Planning

The rise of the New Tax Regime under Section 115BAC as the default tax system in India fundamentally changes the financial calculus of buying insurance for tax saving:

  • Under New Tax Regime: Section 80C deductions are completely abolished. You cannot deduct insurance premiums from your taxable income. However, the Section 10(10D) maturity exemption still applies to policies that satisfy the statutory conditions!
  • Separate Protection from Investment: Buying expensive endowment policies primarily to save Section 80C tax is obsolete under the new tax regime. The mathematically optimal strategy is to purchase low-cost pure term life insurance for pure family protection, and invest the remaining surplus into wealth-building instruments like diversified mutual funds (SIP) or Public Provident Fund (PPF).

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Are life insurance maturity proceeds completely tax-free under Section 10(10D)?

Maturity proceeds are exempt under Section 10(10D) only if the annual premium payable does not exceed 10% of the actual capital sum assured for policies issued on or after April 1, 2012. Additionally, for ULIPs issued after Feb 1, 2021, total annual premium must not exceed ₹2.5 Lakh. For traditional policies issued after April 1, 2023, aggregate annual premium must not exceed ₹5 Lakh.

Is the death benefit paid to nominees taxable in life insurance?

No. Any death benefit received by legal heirs or nominees upon the death of the life assured is 100% tax-free under Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act, regardless of the annual premium amount or policy type.

Can I claim Section 80C deduction for life insurance premium under the New Tax Regime?

No. Under the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), deductions under Section 80C (including life insurance premium, PPF, ELSS, EPF) are not available. Section 80C deductions can only be claimed if you actively opt for the Old Tax Regime.

How are high-premium ULIPs taxed after the Budget 2021 amendment?

For ULIPs issued on or after February 1, 2021, where the aggregate annual premium across all ULIP policies exceeds ₹2.5 Lakh in any financial year, the maturity proceeds lose Section 10(10D) exemption and are taxed as capital gains from equity-oriented mutual funds under Section 112A (12.5% for LTCG above ₹1.25 Lakh) or Section 111A (20% for STCG).

What is the TDS rate under Section 194DA if a life insurance policy is taxable?

If maturity proceeds are not exempt under Section 10(10D) and the payout exceeds ₹1,00,000, the insurance company must deduct TDS at 5% on the net income portion (maturity amount minus total premiums paid) under Section 194DA.
