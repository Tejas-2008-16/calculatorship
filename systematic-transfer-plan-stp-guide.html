Systematic Transfer Plan (STP): Moving Lumpsum Money Into Equity Safely

When I was coding the lumpsum calculator for Calculatorship, a friend asked me what to do with a ₹4 Lakh year-end bonus. He was terrified of putting the entire sum into equities right when the market was hovering near record highs, but keeping it in a bank savings account earning 3% meant inflation was chewing away at his money. A Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) bridges that exact gap: you park the capital in a low-risk liquid fund earning 6.5% to 7% while automatically routing a fixed slice into equity funds every single month.

A Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) automates the phased transfer of a fixed rupee sum from a liquid or debt mutual fund into an equity mutual fund within the same AMC, mitigating market timing risk on large lump sums. Each monthly transfer legally constitutes a redemption from the source fund, attracting capital gains tax at investor slab rates for debt funds under Section 50AA or 20% STCG for equity funds under Section 111A. In a 12-month STP of ₹12 Lakh @ 6.5% liquid yield, the idle balance earns an additional ₹43,963 in interest.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • A Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) moves a fixed amount from a source fund (typically a liquid or debt fund) to a target fund (typically an equity fund) on a regular schedule, combining the safety of debt returns on idle cash with the rupee cost averaging benefits of gradual equity entry.
  • During the STP holding period in a liquid fund, your undeployed capital earns approximately 6.5% to 7.5% annually, which beats the 3% to 3.5% offered by typical savings bank accounts where most investors park cash.
  • Every STP transfer is a taxable event: each withdrawal from the source liquid/debt fund is treated as a redemption, and any capital gains are taxed at your personal income slab rate (Section 50AA, effective April 2023).
  • The optimal STP deployment window is 6 months for lump sums of ₹2 to ₹5 Lakh, and 12 to 18 months for ₹10 to ₹50 Lakh. Longer windows reduce timing risk, but accumulate more slab-rate tax on debt fund gains.
  • STP is available within the same AMC only; you cannot STP from an HDFC liquid fund to a Mirae equity fund. Choose your AMC based on which offers the best combination of liquid and equity fund options.

You've received a ₹8 Lakh performance bonus. The Nifty 50 is at a 52-week high. Every financial pundit on television says "markets are overvalued." Your instinct is to park the money in a savings account and wait for a correction. That hesitation is natural, but leaving cash idle creates severe opportunity cost. A Systematic Transfer Plan resolves this exact dilemma. Your ₹8 Lakh goes into a liquid fund immediately, earning around 7% annualized returns while moving steadily into equity each month.

How an STP Works: Step-by-Step

  1. Invest the lump sum into a liquid or ultra-short-duration fund within the same AMC as your target equity fund. Example: HDFC Liquid Fund Direct Growth.
  2. Register an STP instruction specifying: source fund (HDFC Liquid), target fund (HDFC Flexi Cap Direct Growth), transfer amount (such as ₹50,000/month), and frequency (monthly).
  3. Each month, the AMC automatically redeems ₹50,000 from your liquid fund and purchases equivalent units in your equity fund at that day's NAV.
  4. Repeat until the liquid fund balance is depleted or you cancel the STP instruction.

The Tax Reality: Every STP Transfer Is Taxable

This is the critical limitation most STP guides gloss over. Under Section 50AA (effective April 2023), all debt and liquid fund capital gains are taxed at your income slab rate regardless of holding period. If you park ₹6 Lakh in a liquid fund for 6 months earning 7% (₹21,000 in interest/gains), those ₹21,000 are added to your gross income and taxed at 30% if you sit in the 30% tax bracket, costing ₹6,300 in taxes.

For investors in the 20% or 30% slab, this slab-rate taxation on liquid fund gains partially erodes the STP advantage compared to a direct lumpsum into equity. Run the numbers specific to your bracket before automatically choosing STP over a direct lumpsum investment.

Setting Up an STP: Platform Guide

  • Groww: After investing in the source fund, go to "My Investments" → select source fund → "Transfer" → select target fund and frequency.
  • Zerodha Coin: Source and target must be same AMC. Navigate to source fund → "Manage" → "STP" → configure target and amount.
  • Direct AMC Portals (such as HDFC AMC, ICICI Pru): Available under "Transactions" → "New STP." Most detailed options for intra-AMC transfers.

Frequently Asked Questions About STP

What is the difference between an STP, a SIP, and a SWP? +

A SIP debits money from your savings bank account to purchase mutual fund units periodically. An STP automatically moves money between two mutual fund schemes within the same fund house (typically from a liquid fund to an equity fund). A SWP redeems units periodically to credit monthly cash back to your bank account.

Is every monthly STP transfer subject to capital gains tax in India? +

Yes. Under Indian tax law, every STP installment is legally treated as a redemption from the source fund and a fresh purchase in the target fund. For debt/liquid source funds, gains are taxed at your income slab rates under Section 50AA. For equity source funds, STCG is taxed at 20% and LTCG at 12.5%.

Can I set up an STP between two different mutual fund companies (AMCs)? +

No. An automated STP can only be executed between schemes managed by the same Asset Management Company (AMC). To move money between two different AMCs, you must set up an SWP from the first fund into your bank account and a parallel SIP into the second fund.

What is a Flexi STP compared to a Fixed STP? +

A Fixed STP transfers a constant rupee amount on a set date each month. A Flexi STP (or Variable STP) dynamically adjusts the transfer amount based on market valuations: transferring higher amounts when equity NAVs are depressed and lower amounts when markets are trading at elevated valuation multiples.

The Bottom Line on STP

An STP is the financially correct choice for large lumpsum deployments (₹5 Lakh+) from an investor who cannot tolerate the psychological impact of a large immediate market drawdown. It is not the optimal mathematical strategy in every market cycle: an immediate lumpsum into equity outperforms during extended bull runs. But for risk-conscious investors with large windfalls, the combination of liquid fund returns on uninvested capital plus gradual equity entry via rupee cost averaging makes STP the most defensible strategy across varied market conditions.
