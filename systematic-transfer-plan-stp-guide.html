Systematic Transfer Plan (STP): Moving Lumpsum Money Into Equity Safely

Receiving a substantial annual bonus, property sale proceeds, or an inheritance presents a common investor dilemma: committing a lumpsum directly risks buying at market peaks, while leaving money in a low-yield savings account causes inflation drag. Learn how an STP resolves this balance through phased liquid-to-equity allocation.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • A Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) moves a fixed amount from a source fund (typically a liquid or debt fund) to a target fund (typically an equity fund) on a regular schedule — combining the safety of debt returns on idle cash with the rupee cost averaging benefits of gradual equity entry.
  • During the STP holding period in a liquid fund, your undeployed capital earns approximately 6.5%–7.5% annually — far better than the 3%–3.5% offered by savings bank accounts where most investors park windfalls.
  • Every STP transfer is a taxable event: each withdrawal from the source liquid/debt fund is treated as a redemption, and any capital gains are taxed at your personal income slab rate (Section 50AA, effective April 2023).
  • The optimal STP deployment window is 6 months for lump sums of ₹2–5 Lakh, and 12–18 months for ₹10–50 Lakh — longer windows reduce timing risk but accumulate more slab-rate tax on debt fund gains.
  • STP is available within the same AMC only; you cannot STP from an HDFC liquid fund to a Mirae equity fund — choose your AMC based on which offers the best combination of liquid and equity fund options.

You've received a ₹8 Lakh performance bonus. The Nifty 50 is at a 52-week high. Every financial pundit on television says "markets are overvalued." Your instinct is to park the money in a savings account and wait for a correction. That instinct is understandable — and largely wrong. A Systematic Transfer Plan resolves this exact dilemma: your ₹8 Lakh goes into a liquid fund immediately and earns 7%+ while you deploy it into equity monthly over the next 12 months, regardless of market levels.

How an STP Works: Step-by-Step

  1. Invest the lump sum into a liquid or ultra-short-duration fund within the same AMC as your target equity fund. Example: HDFC Liquid Fund Direct Growth.
  2. Register an STP instruction specifying: source fund (HDFC Liquid), target fund (HDFC Flexi Cap Direct Growth), transfer amount (e.g., ₹50,000/month), and frequency (monthly).
  3. Each month, the AMC automatically redeems ₹50,000 from your liquid fund and purchases equivalent units in your equity fund at that day's NAV.
  4. Repeat until the liquid fund balance is depleted or you cancel the STP instruction.

The Tax Reality: Every STP Transfer Is Taxable

This is the critical limitation most STP guides gloss over. Under Section 50AA (effective April 2023), all debt and liquid fund capital gains are taxed at your income slab rate regardless of holding period. If you park ₹6 Lakh in a liquid fund for 6 months earning 7% (₹21,000 in interest/gains), those ₹21,000 are added to your gross income and taxed at 30% if you're in the top bracket — costing ₹6,300 in tax.

For investors in the 20% or 30% slab, this slab-rate taxation on liquid fund gains partially erodes the STP advantage compared to a direct lumpsum into equity. Run the numbers specific to your bracket before automatically choosing STP over a direct lumpsum investment.

Setting Up an STP: Platform Guide

  • Groww: After investing in the source fund, go to "My Investments" → select source fund → "Transfer" → select target fund and frequency.
  • Zerodha Coin: Source and target must be same AMC. Navigate to source fund → "Manage" → "STP" → configure target and amount.
  • Direct AMC Portals (e.g., HDFC AMC, ICICI Pru): Available under "Transactions" → "New STP." Most detailed options for intra-AMC transfers.

Frequently Asked Questions About STP

Can I set up an STP between different AMCs?

No. STP is an intra-AMC facility. Your source and target funds must be from the same fund house. This is a meaningful constraint — if the best liquid fund (e.g., SBI Liquid Fund) is from a different AMC than your preferred equity fund (e.g., Parag Parikh Flexi Cap), you cannot run an STP between them. In this case, deploy your lump sum directly into the equity fund via periodic manual purchases, or choose an alternative liquid fund within the same AMC.

Should I use an STP for my annual ELSS investment to save Section 80C tax?

For ELSS, a regular monthly SIP during the financial year is almost always better than a year-end lumpsum via STP. STP deploys your capital systematically but still subjects the source fund gains to slab-rate tax. A monthly SIP on Groww or Kuvera into an ELSS fund costs you nothing extra in fees and builds better investment discipline than a frantic March lumpsum STP to beat the 80C deadline.

The Bottom Line on STP

An STP is the financially correct choice for large lumpsum deployments (₹5 Lakh+) from an investor who cannot tolerate the psychological impact of a large immediate market drawdown. It is not the optimal mathematical strategy in every market condition — a lumpsum into equity outperforms in bull markets. But for risk-conscious investors with large windfalls, the combination of liquid fund returns on uninvested capital plus gradual equity entry via rupee cost averaging makes STP the most defensible strategy across varied market conditions.
