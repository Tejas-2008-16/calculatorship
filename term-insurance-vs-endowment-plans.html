Term Insurance vs Endowment Policy: Pure Protection vs Low Returns Math

Traditional endowment policies combine insurance with low-yield debt returns, generating a sub-inflation internal rate of return (IRR) of only 4.5% to 5.5% alongside inadequate life cover. By executing a Buy Term and Invest the Difference (BTID) strategy—purchasing ₹1 Crore pure term cover for ₹12,000/year and investing the remaining ₹38,000 in an equity SIP—a 30-year-old accumulates ₹1.12 Crore at age 60, generating an extra ₹76.90 Lakh in wealth and ₹90 Lakh higher coverage.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Endowment insurance plans typically deliver internal returns (IRR) of 4% to 5% annually , well below the 6% to 7% you'd earn in an FD and dramatically below the 11% to 12% from equity mutual funds over comparable horizons.
  • An LIC endowment plan requiring ₹1 Lakh annual premium for 20 years provides ₹20 to ₹25 Lakh maturity value , giving a 4.2% to 4.8% IRR. Investing that exact ₹1 Lakh annually in a Nifty 50 index fund at 12% CAGR grows to ₹80 Lakh.
  • Endowment policies have extremely high surrender charges in early years . Surrendering in Years 1 to 3 typically recovers only 30% to 50% of total premiums paid.
  • "Money-back plans" are endowment variants with periodic payout milestones; their IRR (after accounting for timing of payouts) is typically even lower than regular endowment plans, often 3% to 4%.
  • If you have existing endowment policies, evaluate whether to continue or surrender based on the specific policy's current surrender value versus future premium burden and projected maturity, do not blindly continue or surrender without calculating the IRR.

Endowment insurance is the most successfully mis-sold financial product in Indian history. It combines two things that should be kept strictly separate, life insurance protection and long-term savings , giving and does both badly. The insurance cover is inadequate (₹5 to 10 Lakh on a ₹1 Lakh annual premium), and the investment return is terrible (4% to 5% IRR in a world where equity delivers 12%). Yet hundreds of millions of Indians continue paying endowment premiums annually, locked in by surrender penalties and family sentiment.

Side-by-Side Math That Exposes Endowment Plans

Compare two investors, both 30 years old, both investing ₹1 Lakh per year for 20 years:

Strategy Annual Outflow Life Cover Maturity Value at Year 20 Effective IRR
LIC Endowment Policy ₹1,00,000 ₹10 to ₹15 Lakh ~₹25,00,000 ~4.5% per annum
Term Plan + Nifty 50 SIP ₹9,000 (term) + ₹91,000 (SIP) ₹1 Crore+ ~₹80,00,000 (at 12% CAGR) ~11.5% per annum

The "Buy Term + Invest the Rest" approach delivers 3.2× more wealth at year 20 while providing 6.7× more insurance cover. Such numbers prove more than a marginal edge, showing clear structural superiority.

When Endowment Plans "Make Sense", A Realistic Assessment

Endowment plans are never optimal from a pure financial return perspective. However, they have limited merit for very specific behavioral profiles:

  • Investors with zero investment discipline: If you genuinely cannot maintain an SIP for 20 years without withdrawing it, the locked-in nature of an endowment policy (with high surrender penalties) enforces savings you would otherwise spend. The 4.5% return is better than nothing.
  • Corporate salary tax planning (pre-2014 grandfathered policies): Older endowment policies with certain premium structures had different tax treatment. Modern endowment plans have no special tax advantage not available through ELSS funds.

What to Do If You Already Have Endowment Policies

Do not blindly surrender or blindly continue. Calculate the specific decision:

  1. Call your insurer and get the current surrender value and future paid-up policy value.
  2. Calculate the IRR of continuing to pay premiums until maturity versus surrendering now and investing the surrender value + future premiums in equity.
  3. If your policy is more than 50% through its premium payment term and the surrender value is substantial, continuing may be more optimal than surrendering at heavy loss.
  4. If the policy is in its first 5 to 7 years with high surrender charges, evaluate whether the mathematical case for switching to Term + SIP justifies the early surrender penalty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are ULIP plans better than endowment plans?

ULIPs (Unit Linked Insurance Plans) are hybrid insurance-investment products where the premium is split between insurance charges and market-linked fund investments. Post the IRDAI 2010 reforms, ULIPs are significantly more transparent. However, the first-year charges (premium allocation charges, policy admin charges, fund management charges) still reduce net investment significantly versus buying a direct equity mutual fund with 0.20% TER. ULIPs with 10+ year horizons in equity funds can generate reasonable returns, but rarely match a direct term plan + direct equity mutual fund combination.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the typical internal rate of return (IRR) of an endowment or money-back life insurance policy in India?

Most traditional endowment and money-back policies yield an effective annual IRR of only 4.5% to 5.5%. Because high administrative costs and agent commissions consume substantial portions of the early premiums, endowment returns consistently lag behind inflation and public provident funds.

What is the 'Buy Term and Invest the Difference' (BTID) strategy?

BTID is a personal finance framework that separates protection from wealth creation. Instead of paying ₹50,000 annually for an endowment plan offering ₹10 Lakh cover, you buy pure term insurance offering ₹1 Crore cover for approx. ₹12,000, and invest the remaining ₹38,000 into a mutual fund equity SIP.

What is Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938 (the Incontestability Clause)?

Section 45 mandates that no life insurance policy can be called in question or rejected by an insurer on any grounds whatsoever (including misstatement of material facts) after 3 continuous years from the policy issuance or revival date, guaranteeing claim certainty for beneficiaries.

How are maturity proceeds from traditional life insurance policies taxed under Section 10(10D)?

Maturity proceeds from life insurance policies issued on or after 1 April 2023 are tax-exempt under Section 10(10D) only if the aggregate annual premium across all non-unit-linked policies does not exceed ₹5,00,000 in any financial year. If aggregate premiums exceed ₹5 Lakh, net gains are taxable at slab rates.

What should an investor do if they already hold an underperforming endowment policy?

If the policy is within its first 2 to 3 years, calculating the surrender value versus redirecting future premiums into an equity index fund often shows that stopping the policy (or converting it to a paid-up policy) yields vastly superior net wealth over a 15- to 20-year horizon.
