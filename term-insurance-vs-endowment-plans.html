Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Endowment insurance plans typically deliver internal returns (IRR) of 4%–5% annually — below the 6%–7% you'd earn in an FD and dramatically below the 11%–12% from equity mutual funds over comparable horizons.
  • An LIC endowment plan requiring ₹1 Lakh annual premium for 20 years provides ₹20–25 Lakh maturity value — a 4.2%–4.8% IRR. The same ₹1 Lakh invested annually in a Nifty 50 index fund at 12% CAGR grows to ₹80 Lakh.
  • Endowment policies have extremely high surrender charges in early years — surrendering in Years 1–3 typically recovers only 30%–50% of total premiums paid.
  • "Money-back plans" are endowment variants with periodic payout milestones; their IRR (after accounting for timing of payouts) is typically even lower than regular endowment plans — often 3%–4%.
  • If you have existing endowment policies, evaluate whether to continue or surrender based on the specific policy's current surrender value versus future premium burden and projected maturity — do not blindly continue or surrender without calculating the IRR.

Endowment insurance is the most successfully mis-sold financial product in Indian history. It combines two things that should be kept strictly separate — life insurance protection and long-term savings — and does both badly. The insurance cover is inadequate (₹5–10 Lakh on a ₹1 Lakh annual premium), and the investment return is terrible (4%–5% IRR in a world where equity delivers 12%). Yet hundreds of millions of Indians continue paying endowment premiums annually, locked in by surrender penalties and family sentiment.

The Math That Exposes Endowment Plans

Compare two investors, both 30 years old, both investing ₹1 Lakh per year for 20 years:

Strategy Annual Outflow Life Cover Maturity Value at Year 20 Effective IRR
LIC Endowment Policy ₹1,00,000 ₹10–15 Lakh ~₹25,00,000 ~4.5% per annum
Term Plan + Nifty 50 SIP ₹9,000 (term) + ₹91,000 (SIP) ₹1 Crore+ ~₹80,00,000 (at 12% CAGR) ~11.5% per annum

The "Buy Term + Invest the Rest" approach delivers 3.2× more wealth at year 20 while providing 6.7× more insurance cover. This is not a marginal difference — it is a fundamental structural superiority.

When Endowment Plans "Make Sense" — A Realistic Assessment

Endowment plans are never optimal from a pure financial return perspective. However, they have limited merit for very specific behavioral profiles:

  • Investors with zero investment discipline: If you genuinely cannot maintain an SIP for 20 years without withdrawing it, the locked-in nature of an endowment policy (with high surrender penalties) enforces savings you would otherwise spend. The 4.5% return is better than nothing.
  • Corporate salary tax planning (pre-2014 grandfathered policies): Older endowment policies with certain premium structures had different tax treatment. Modern endowment plans have no special tax advantage not available through ELSS funds.

What to Do If You Already Have Endowment Policies

Do not blindly surrender or blindly continue. Calculate the specific decision:

  1. Call your insurer and get the current surrender value and future paid-up policy value.
  2. Calculate the IRR of continuing to pay premiums until maturity versus surrendering now and investing the surrender value + future premiums in equity.
  3. If your policy is more than 50% through its premium payment term and the surrender value is substantial, continuing may be more optimal than surrendering at heavy loss.
  4. If the policy is in its first 5–7 years with high surrender charges, evaluate whether the mathematical case for switching to Term + SIP justifies the early surrender penalty.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are ULIP plans better than endowment plans?

ULIPs (Unit Linked Insurance Plans) are hybrid insurance-investment products where the premium is split between insurance charges and market-linked fund investments. Post the IRDAI 2010 reforms, ULIPs are significantly more transparent. However, the first-year charges (premium allocation charges, policy admin charges, fund management charges) still reduce net investment significantly versus buying a direct equity mutual fund with 0.20% TER. ULIPs with 10+ year horizons in equity funds can generate reasonable returns, but rarely match a direct term plan + direct equity mutual fund combination.
