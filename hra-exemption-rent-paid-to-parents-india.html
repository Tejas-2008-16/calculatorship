HRA on Rent Paid to Parents (FY 2026-27): Rules, Form 124 & Tax Saving Math

Living with your parents and want to claim House Rent Allowance? Taking this deduction under the Old Tax Regime is completely lawful when done right. Whenever you plan to restructure family financial arrangements to claim statutory exemptions under Section 10(13A), both generations must maintain absolute synchronization across executed lease agreements, monthly electronic banking records, municipal tax receipts, and corresponding annual income tax return disclosures. Here is how Form 124 relationship disclosures work, the expanded 8-metro 50% list, and the exact math that protects your claim from scrutiny notices.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Old Regime Only: HRA exemption can only be claimed under the Old Tax Regime. Zero HRA deduction applies in the default New Tax Regime.
  • 2. Parents Must Own the Property: You cannot pay rent to yourself, nor can you pay rent to a parent if you are a co-owner of the property title. Your parent must hold sole or joint title to the home.
  • 3. Form 124 Compliance: Under Rule 205 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, Form 124 has replaced Form 12BB. Employers require an explicit declaration of your landlord's PAN, Aadhaar, and your relationship with them.
  • 4. 8 Metro Cities for 50%: Under Rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, the 50% basic pay cap applies to Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad. All other cities get 40%.
  • 5. Parents Must Report Rental Income: The rent you pay is taxable for your parent under "Income from House Property." However, they enjoy a flat 30% statutory deduction under Section 24(a).
  • 6. Bank Paper Trail Is Non-Negotiable: Cash rent payments to family members are an open invitation to an automated scrutiny notice. Always use NEFT, RTGS, or UPI with written rent receipts.

A Sunday Conversation Over Chai: Can You Really Pay Rent to Mom or Dad?

Say Neha, a 28-year-old software engineer working in Pune, moved back into her parents' apartment after remote and hybrid work policies became standard. Every month, her salary slip shows a generous House Rent Allowance of ₹40,000. But because she lives with her family and doesn't write a cheque to a third-party landlord, her entire HRA gets taxed at 30% plus cess. Sitting over Sunday evening chai, her father asks: "Why don't you pay rent to me? We own the flat, you live here, and I'm retired with income below the tax limit."

It is one of the most common questions salaried folks in India ask. In short? Yes, paying rent to your parents and claiming HRA is completely legal under Indian tax law. Multiple Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) rulings have upheld that an adult child living in a parent's house can enter into a genuine landlord-tenant arrangement. But honestly, most people overpay here or execute it so poorly that the tax department disallows the deduction on day one.

Treating this as a casual paper exercise without signed agreements, bank transfers, and income reporting by your parents, an automated scrutiny notice is almost Fixed. Here is exactly how to do it right, backed by verified statutes and clean math.

Regime Choice: Why HRA Lives Exclusively in the Old Tax Regime

Math decides everything. Numbers never lie. Review salary slips. Track monthly debits. While sorting through complex Indian direct tax provisions often feels overwhelming for salaried professionals juggling rent receipts and corporate payroll portals, taking time to calculate your net deductions under both tax systems ensures that you never forfeit legitimate statutory allowances guaranteed by Parliament. HRA exemption is strictly an Old Tax Regime benefit. Choosing the default New Tax Regime provides a standard deduction of ₹75,000, but every single rupee of your House Rent Allowance is added to your taxable income and taxed at slab rates.

Finance ministry officials structured the New Tax Regime to be simple, low-paperwork, and deduction-free. That means Sections 10(13A) for HRA, 80C for PF and ELSS, 80D for health insurance, and Section 24(b) for self-occupied home loan interest vanish entirely under the new framework. Substantial rent transfers, however, make opting for the Old Tax Regime can easily swing your total tax liability by ₹50,000 to ₹1,50,000 in your favor. Least-of-Three Formula: A ₹35,000 Monthly Rent Example

Compare every condition carefully. Run the numbers first. Draft lease terms. Stamp papers matter. Under Section 10(13A) of the Income-tax Act, your exempt HRA is calculated as the minimum of three statutory conditions: the actual HRA received, the rent paid minus 10% of your basic salary, or 50% (metro) / 40% (non-metro) of your basic salary.

Let us walk through a concrete example. Suppose Neha earns a basic salary of ₹80,000 per month (₹9,60,000 annually) and receives an HRA of ₹40,000 per month (₹4,80,000 annually). She lives with her mother in Pune and transfers a rent of ₹35,000 per month (₹4,20,000 annually) to her mother's bank account.

Because Pune is one of the designated 8 metro cities under the updated rules, the 50% ceiling applies:

Condition Formula Annual Amount
Condition 1 Actual HRA received from employer ₹4,80,000
Condition 2 Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic Salary (₹4,20,000 - ₹96,000) ₹3,24,000
Condition 3 50% of Basic Salary (Metro city Pune) ₹4,80,000
Tax-Exempt HRA Least of Conditions 1, 2, and 3 ₹3,24,000
Taxable HRA Actual HRA (₹4,80,000) minus Exempt HRA (₹3,24,000) ₹1,56,000

By executing this legitimate arrangement, Neha wipes ₹3,24,000 off her taxable income under the Old Tax Regime. At the 30% slab rate plus 4% cess, that single deduction saves her exactly ₹1,01,088 in annual tax.

Eight Metro Cities Under Income-tax Rules, 2026: Who Gets 50%?

Under Rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, the list of cities qualifying for the 50% basic salary cap was expanded to 8 major urban agglomerations: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad. All other cities across India remain subject to the 40% cap.

Decades of precedent under legacy Rule 2A, the 50% HRA cap was frozen to the four original colonial presidency metros. Salaried professionals renting in high-cost IT hubs like Bengaluru and Hyderabad were capped at 40%, creating an unfair tax penalty relative to actual living expenses. Rules stay rigid. Recent codification under Income-tax Rules, 2026 finally brought parity to all eight major tech and commercial centers.

Keep one important detail in mind: the designation is based on municipal boundaries. Location dictates percentages. Tenants in Gurgaon or Noida still fall under the 40% category unless your residence falls directly within the National Capital Territory of Delhi boundary.

Form 124: The Mandatory Relationship Disclosure Explained

Form 124 is the statutory declaration form submitted under Rule 205 by employees to their employers to claim deductions and exemptions for TDS computation, replacing Form 12BB. New filing protocols introduced an explicit relationship disclosure when claiming rent paid to relatives.

When you submit your annual investment proof to your company's HR or payroll portal, Form 124 requires specific disclosures for HRA claims:

  • Landlord Identification: Full legal name, permanent residential address, PAN, and Aadhaar of the landlord. Providing the landlord's PAN is legally mandatory whenever the aggregate annual rent paid exceeds ₹1,00,000.
  • Explicit Relationship Specification: Unlike the legacy Form 12BB where relationship disclosures were not standardized, Form 124 includes a dedicated field inquiring whether the landlord is related to the employee (e.g., father, mother, or other relative).
  • Rental Agreement and Receipts: You must attach stamped rent receipts and a copy of the active rental agreement.

Do not be intimidated by the relationship disclosure. Paying rent to parents is entirely lawful. Such disclosures enable the tax department's automated verification engine can match your claimed rent against your parent's Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS. As long as your parent reports the rent on their return, the cross-check clears without incident.

Reporting Rental Receipts: How Parents File Correctly

Rental income received by your parents must be declared in their income tax return under the head 'Income from House Property'. Both returns count. Omitting this income from your parents' filing causes AIS to flag an unexplained financial discrepancy, triggering scrutiny.

Dates must match. Never delay filing. Both returns reconcile automatically. Match AIS records. Store rental receipts. Tax officers reviewing rental claims between blood relatives look specifically for commercial realism, meaning the agreed monthly amount must mirror actual market rents charged for equivalent properties in your immediate neighborhood rather than an arbitrary figure created to eliminate salary tax. Fortunately, rental earnings qualify for major statutory deductions under Section 24 of the Income-tax Act:

  • Municipal Taxes Deduction: Any local property or municipal taxes paid by the parent during the financial year can be deducted directly from the gross rental value.
  • Flat 30% Standard Deduction: Section 24(a) allows a flat 30% statutory deduction on the net annual value for repairs and maintenance, regardless of whether your parents actually spent a single rupee on home painting or plumbing.
  • Senior Citizen Slab Relief: If your parent is over 60 years old, their basic exemption limit under the Old Regime is ₹3,00,000 (or ₹3,50,000 under the New Regime). In the case of super senior citizens aged 80+, the basic exemption reaches ₹5,00,000. If your mother or father has no other taxable income, receiving ₹3,00,000 in annual rent may result in zero net tax liability for them.

No HRA from Employer? Section 80GG Relief for Non-HRA Earners

Salaried perks vary. Log tax challans. Inspect municipal receipts. Confirm property ownership. Observe statutory limits. Compare deduction totals. File returns early. When employers omit HRA allowances from compensation letters, alternate relief exists. What if your employer does not provide HRA in your CTC, or you are a consultant or freelancer living with your parents? You can claim rent deductions under Section 80GG under the Old Tax Regime.

Section 80GG provides relief to individuals who pay rent for furnished or unfurnished accommodation but do not receive any HRA component. Statutory caps limit this deduction to the lowest among ₹5,000 per month (₹60,000 per year), 25% of your total adjusted income, or rent paid minus 10% of total income. To claim Section 80GG, you must submit Form 10BA online. Note that you, your spouse, or your minor child must not own any residential accommodation in the city where you reside. Old vs New Tax Regime Breakeven: Is Claiming HRA Worth It?

Choosing between the Old and New Tax Regimes depends on whether your total deductions (HRA + Section 80C + 80D + home loan interest) exceed your personalized breakeven threshold. A salaried professional earning ₹15 lakh generally needs over ₹3.75 lakh to ₹4.25 lakh in genuine deductions before the Old Regime yields lower tax.

Salaried workers living in high-rent metro cities often find that an annual HRA exemption of ₹2.5 lakh to ₹3.5 lakh often tips the balance decisively in favor of the Old Tax Regime. Combine that with ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C and ₹25,000 under Section 80D, and your deductions easily cross ₹4.5 lakh. However, if your rent is modest (say ₹10,000 per month), the lower slab rates and ₹75,000 standard deduction of the New Tax Regime will almost always leave you ahead. Seven Costly Mistakes That Trigger Tax Scrutiny Notices

The Income Tax Department uses automated cross-referencing algorithms to flag artificial transactions between relatives. To ensure your exemption survives official scrutiny, avoid seven common mistakes:

  1. You Are a Co-Owner of the House: You cannot pay rent to yourself. Any presence of your name on the property deed, home loan agreement, or municipal tax receipt as a co-owner, you are legally a landlord, not a tenant. Any HRA claim will be dismissed immediately.
  2. No Formal Rent Agreement: A written, signed rent agreement on non-judicial stamp paper stating the monthly rent, security deposit, address, and tenant-landlord names is essential. Verbal understandings carry zero evidentiary weight during an audit.
  3. Cash Rent Disallowance: Handing over physical cash leaves no audit trail. Avoid cash handovers. Retain cancelled cheques. If your parents share joint ownership of the residential property, both names should appear on the formal registered or notarized lease contract, and your monthly electronic banking remittances must align exactly with their respective ownership shares when reporting rental earnings in their annual filings. Cash leaves no trail. Retain banking records. Electronic transfers leave undeniable timestamps. Proof wins arguments. Audit trails win. Sign every agreement. Even when family relationships are completely harmonious and financial transfers between parents and adult children feel entirely informal, the Income Tax Department views the tenancy strictly through the lens of contract law and taxable property receipts.. Transfer the exact rent amount every single month directly from your bank account to your parent's bank account via NEFT, RTGS, or UPI.
  4. Parent Forgets to Report Rent in ITR: If you claim ₹3,60,000 in HRA using your mother's PAN on Form 124, that transaction feeds directly into her Annual Information Statement (AIS). Filing an ITR that shows ₹0 under house property, an automated mismatch notice will be issued.
  5. Spousal Tenancy Claims: Under Indian matrimonial and tax jurisprudence, spouses share a statutory duty to reside together. Rental contracts between husbands and wives face near-universal disallowance by assessing officers.
  6. Paperwork prevents disputes. Rules are strict. Distant City Rent Claims: If your company's physical office is in Bengaluru and your employer requires your in-person presence, claiming rent paid to parents in Lucknow without documented hybrid approvals or proof of remote work will trigger scrutiny.
  7. Unrealistic Rent Above Market Rates: If standard two-bedroom apartments in your parent's residential locality rent for ₹25,000 per month, paying your parents ₹70,000 per month simply to exhaust your HRA quota will be viewed as a sham transaction under General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR). Ensure rent remains aligned with local market standards. City limits matter. Note metro limits. Calculate both regimes. Before selecting your tax regime at the start of the financial year, sit down with your employer's compensation worksheet to contrast the standard ₹75,000 new regime deduction against your aggregated housing allowance, chapter VIA savings, and home loan interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my parents while living in their house?

Yes, legally you can claim HRA exemption by paying rent to your parents, provided your parents are the legal owners of the residential property. You must execute a registered or notarized rent agreement, transfer rent via bank channels, collect monthly rent receipts, and ensure your parents declare this rental income in their ITR.

Can I claim HRA exemption under the New Tax Regime in FY 2026-27?

No. Under the default New Tax Regime, HRA exemption is completely unavailable. You can only claim HRA tax exemption if you actively choose the Old Tax Regime at the start of the financial year or while filing your return.

What is Form 124 and what new details does it require for HRA?

As enacted in Rule 205 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, Form 124 replaced the legacy Form 12BB for employee investment declarations. For HRA claims, Form 124 mandates disclosing the landlord's name, address, PAN, Aadhaar, and an explicit relationship disclosure identifying whether the landlord is a parent or relative.

Which cities qualify for the 50% HRA rate under the Income-tax Rules, 2026?

Pursuant to Rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, the 50% metro cap applies to 8 cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad. All other cities across India fall under the 40% cap.

Do my parents have to pay income tax on the rent I pay them?

Yes. Rental income is taxable in the hands of the parent under 'Income from House Property'. However, the parent is entitled to a flat 30% statutory deduction under Section 24(a) for repairs, plus deduction of municipal taxes. If the parent is a senior citizen or has low taxable income, the tax impact is often minimal or zero.

Can I claim both HRA and a home loan tax deduction?

Yes, provided the two properties are distinct. For example, if you own a home in your hometown on which you service a home loan, but you live with your parents in another city for work and pay them rent, you can claim home loan tax deductions and HRA exemption simultaneously under the Old Tax Regime.

Actionable Next Step: Set Up Your Rental Paper Trail Before Quarter-End

Planning to claim HRA on rent paid to parents? Start early. Draft a formal rental agreement on stamp paper today. Never pay cash. Set up an automated monthly bank transfer (via standing instruction or UPI) from your salary account to your parent's account. Keep numbered rent receipts signed by your parent in a dedicated folder. When submitting your year-end declarations via Form 124, you will have an unshakeable audit trail ready for payroll verification.
