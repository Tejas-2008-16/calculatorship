HRA on Rent Paid to Parents (FY 2026-27): Rules, Form 124 & Tax Saving Math

Living with your parents and want to claim House Rent Allowance? It is 100% legal under the Old Tax Regime—if you follow the rules. Here is how Form 124 relationship disclosures work, the expanded 8-metro 50% list, and the exact math that protects your claim from scrutiny notices.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Old Regime Only: HRA exemption can only be claimed under the Old Tax Regime. The default New Tax Regime provides zero HRA deduction.
  • 2. Parents Must Own the Property: You cannot pay rent to yourself, nor can you pay rent to a parent if you are a co-owner of the property title. The parent must be the sole or joint owner of the home.
  • 3. Form 124 Compliance: Under Rule 205 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, Form 124 has replaced Form 12BB. It requires explicit declaration of your landlord's PAN, Aadhaar, and your relationship with them.
  • 4. 8 Metro Cities for 50%: Under Rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, the 50% basic pay cap applies to Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad. All other cities get 40%.
  • 5. Parents Must Report Rental Income: The rent you pay is taxable for your parent under "Income from House Property." However, they enjoy a flat 30% statutory deduction under Section 24(a).
  • 6. Bank Paper Trail Is Non-Negotiable: Cash rent payments to family members are an open invitation to an automated scrutiny notice. Always use NEFT, RTGS, or UPI with written rent receipts.

A Sunday Conversation Over Chai: Can You Really Pay Rent to Mom or Dad?

Say Neha, a 28-year-old software engineer working in Pune, moved back into her parents' apartment after remote and hybrid work policies became standard. Every month, her salary slip shows a generous House Rent Allowance of ₹40,000. But because she lives with her family and doesn't write a cheque to a third-party landlord, her entire HRA gets taxed at 30% plus cess. Sitting over Sunday evening chai, her father asks: "Why don't you pay rent to me? We own the flat, you live here, and I'm retired with income below the tax limit."

It is one of the most common questions salaried folks in India ask. The short answer? Yes, paying rent to your parents and claiming HRA is completely legal under Indian tax law. Multiple Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) rulings have upheld that an adult child living in a parent's house can enter into a genuine landlord-tenant arrangement. But honestly, most people overpay here or execute it so poorly that the tax department disallows the deduction on day one.

If you treat it as a casual paper exercise without signed agreements, bank transfers, and income reporting by your parents, an automated scrutiny notice is almost Fixed. Here is exactly how to do it right, backed by verified statutes and clean math.

The Regime Choice: Why HRA Lives Only in the Old Tax Regime

HRA exemption is strictly an Old Tax Regime benefit. If you stick with the default New Tax Regime, you receive a standard deduction of ₹75,000, but every single rupee of your House Rent Allowance is added to your taxable income and taxed at slab rates.

The Income Tax Department designed the New Tax Regime to be simple, low-paperwork, and deduction-free. That means Sections 10(13A) for HRA, 80C for PF and ELSS, 80D for health insurance, and Section 24(b) for self-occupied home loan interest vanish entirely under the new framework. If your rent payments are substantial, however, opting for the Old Tax Regime can easily swing your total tax liability by ₹50,000 to ₹1,50,000 in your favor. The Least-of-Three Formula with a ₹35,000/Month Example

Under Section 10(13A) of the Income-tax Act, your exempt HRA is calculated as the minimum of three statutory conditions: the actual HRA received, the rent paid minus 10% of your basic salary, or 50% (metro) / 40% (non-metro) of your basic salary.

Let us walk through a concrete example. Suppose Neha earns a basic salary of ₹80,000 per month (₹9,60,000 annually) and receives an HRA of ₹40,000 per month (₹4,80,000 annually). She lives with her mother in Pune and transfers a rent of ₹35,000 per month (₹4,20,000 annually) to her mother's bank account.

Because Pune is one of the designated 8 metro cities under the updated rules, the 50% ceiling applies:

Condition Formula Annual Amount
Condition 1 Actual HRA received from employer ₹4,80,000
Condition 2 Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic Salary (₹4,20,000 - ₹96,000) ₹3,24,000
Condition 3 50% of Basic Salary (Metro city Pune) ₹4,80,000
Tax-Exempt HRA Least of Conditions 1, 2, and 3 ₹3,24,000
Taxable HRA Actual HRA (₹4,80,000) minus Exempt HRA (₹3,24,000) ₹1,56,000

By executing this legitimate arrangement, Neha wipes ₹3,24,000 off her taxable income under the Old Tax Regime. At the 30% slab rate plus 4% cess, that single deduction saves her exactly ₹1,01,088 in annual tax.

The 8 Metro Cities Under Income-tax Rules, 2026: Who Gets 50%?

Under Rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, the list of cities qualifying for the 50% basic salary cap was expanded to 8 major urban agglomerations: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad. All other cities across India remain subject to the 40% cap.

For decades under legacy Rule 2A of the 1962 rules, the 50% HRA cap was frozen to the four original colonial presidency metros. Salaried professionals renting in high-cost IT hubs like Bengaluru and Hyderabad were capped at 40%, creating an unfair tax penalty relative to actual living expenses. The formal codification under the Income-tax Rules, 2026 brought parity to all eight major tech and commercial centers.

Keep one important detail in mind: the designation is based on municipal boundaries. If you rent in Gurgaon or Noida, you still fall under the 40% category unless your residence falls directly within the National Capital Territory of Delhi boundary.

Form 124: The Mandatory Relationship Disclosure Explained

Form 124 is the statutory declaration form submitted under Rule 205 by employees to their employers to claim deductions and exemptions for TDS computation, replacing Form 12BB. It introduces a mandatory relationship disclosure when claiming rent paid to relatives.

When you submit your annual investment proof to your company's HR or payroll portal, Form 124 requires specific disclosures for HRA claims:

  • Landlord Identification: Full legal name, permanent residential address, PAN, and Aadhaar of the landlord. Providing the landlord's PAN is legally mandatory whenever the aggregate annual rent paid exceeds ₹1,00,000.
  • Explicit Relationship Specification: Unlike the legacy Form 12BB where relationship disclosures were not standardized, Form 124 includes a dedicated field inquiring whether the landlord is related to the employee (e.g., father, mother, or other relative).
  • Rental Agreement and Receipts: You must attach stamped rent receipts and a copy of the active rental agreement.

Do not be intimidated by the relationship disclosure. Paying rent to parents is entirely lawful. The disclosure exists so the tax department's automated cross-verification engine can match your claimed rent against your parent's Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS. As long as your parent reports the rent on their return, the cross-check clears without incident.

The Other Side of the Ledger: How Parents Report Rental Income

Rental income received by your parents must be declared in their income tax return under the head 'Income from House Property'. If your parent fails to report it, their AIS will flag an unexplained financial discrepancy, triggering scrutiny.

The good news is that rental income enjoys substantial statutory relief under Section 24 of the Income-tax Act:

  • Municipal Taxes Deduction: Any local property or municipal taxes paid by the parent during the financial year can be deducted directly from the gross rental value.
  • Flat 30% Standard Deduction: Section 24(a) allows a flat 30% statutory deduction on the net annual value for repairs and maintenance, regardless of whether your parents actually spent a single rupee on home painting or plumbing.
  • Senior Citizen Slab Relief: If your parent is over 60 years old, their basic exemption limit under the Old Regime is ₹3,00,000 (or ₹3,50,000 under the New Regime). For super senior citizens aged 80+, the exemption reaches ₹5,00,000. If your mother or father has no other taxable income, receiving ₹3,00,000 in annual rent may result in zero net tax liability for them.

No HRA from Employer? Claiming Rent Under Section 80GG

What if your employer does not provide HRA in your CTC, or you are a consultant or freelancer living with your parents? You can claim rent deductions under Section 80GG under the Old Tax Regime.

Section 80GG provides relief to individuals who pay rent for furnished or unfurnished accommodation but do not receive any HRA component. The deduction is capped at the lowest of: ₹5,000 per month (₹60,000 per year), 25% of your total adjusted income, or rent paid minus 10% of total income. To claim Section 80GG, you must submit Form 10BA online. Note that you, your spouse, or your minor child must not own any residential accommodation in the city where you reside. If you work on contract, read our freelancer and gig worker tax guide to see how presumptive taxation interacts with rental expenses.

Old vs New Tax Regime Breakeven: Is Claiming HRA Worth It?

Choosing between the Old and New Tax Regimes depends on whether your total deductions (HRA + Section 80C + 80D + home loan interest) exceed your personalized breakeven threshold. For a salaried professional earning ₹15 lakh, you generally need over ₹3.75 lakh to ₹4.25 lakh in genuine deductions before the Old Regime yields lower tax.

For salaried folks living in high-rent metro areas, an annual HRA exemption of ₹2.5 lakh to ₹3.5 lakh often tips the balance decisively in favor of the Old Tax Regime. Combine that with ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C and ₹25,000 under Section 80D, and your deductions easily cross ₹4.5 lakh. However, if your rent is modest (say ₹10,000 per month), the lower slab rates and ₹75,000 standard deduction of the New Tax Regime will almost always leave you ahead. Seven Costly Mistakes That Trigger Tax Scrutiny Notices

The Income Tax Department uses automated cross-referencing algorithms to flag artificial transactions between relatives. If you want your claim to survive scrutiny, avoid these seven fatal errors:

  1. You Are a Co-Owner of the House: You cannot pay rent to yourself. If your name appears on the property deed, home loan agreement, or municipal tax receipt as a co-owner, you are legally a landlord, not a tenant. Any HRA claim will be dismissed immediately.
  2. No Formal Rent Agreement: A written, signed rent agreement on non-judicial stamp paper stating the monthly rent, security deposit, address, and tenant-landlord names is essential. Verbal understandings carry zero evidentiary weight during an audit.
  3. Paying Rent in Cash: Transferring money via cash withdrawals leaves no audit trail. Transfer the exact rent amount every single month directly from your bank account to your parent's bank account via NEFT, RTGS, or UPI.
  4. Parent Forgets to Report Rent in ITR: If you claim ₹3,60,000 in HRA using your mother's PAN on Form 124, that transaction feeds directly into her Annual Information Statement (AIS). If she files an ITR showing ₹0 under house property, an automated mismatch notice will be issued.
  5. Paying Rent to Your Spouse: In the eyes of Indian matrimonial and tax jurisprudence, spouses have a shared legal obligation to reside together. Paying rent to your husband or wife is almost universally disallowed by assessing officers.
  6. Claiming Rent in a City Where You Don't Work or Reside: If your company's physical office is in Bengaluru and your employer requires your in-person presence, claiming rent paid to parents in Lucknow without documented hybrid approvals or proof of remote work will trigger scrutiny.
  7. Unrealistic Rent Above Market Rates: If standard two-bedroom apartments in your parent's residential locality rent for ₹25,000 per month, paying your parents ₹70,000 per month simply to exhaust your HRA quota will be viewed as a sham transaction under General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR). Keep rent aligned with local market standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my parents while living in their house?

Yes, legally you can claim HRA exemption by paying rent to your parents, provided your parents are the legal owners of the residential property. You must execute a registered or notarized rent agreement, transfer rent via bank channels, collect monthly rent receipts, and ensure your parents declare this rental income in their ITR.

Can I claim HRA exemption under the New Tax Regime in FY 2026-27?

No. Under the default New Tax Regime, HRA exemption is completely unavailable. You can only claim HRA tax exemption if you actively choose the Old Tax Regime at the start of the financial year or while filing your return.

What is Form 124 and what new details does it require for HRA?

Under Rule 205 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, Form 124 replaced the legacy Form 12BB for employee investment declarations. For HRA claims, Form 124 mandates disclosing the landlord's name, address, PAN, Aadhaar, and an explicit relationship disclosure identifying whether the landlord is a parent or relative.

Which cities qualify for the 50% HRA rate under the Income-tax Rules, 2026?

Under Rule 279 of the Income-tax Rules, 2026, the 50% metro cap applies to 8 cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad. All other cities across India fall under the 40% cap.

Do my parents have to pay income tax on the rent I pay them?

Yes. Rental income is taxable in the hands of the parent under 'Income from House Property'. However, the parent is entitled to a flat 30% statutory deduction under Section 24(a) for repairs, plus deduction of municipal taxes. If the parent is a senior citizen or has low taxable income, the tax impact is often minimal or zero.

Can I claim both HRA and a home loan tax deduction?

Yes, provided the two properties are distinct. For example, if you own a home in your hometown on which you service a home loan, but you live with your parents in another city for work and pay them rent, you can claim home loan tax deductions and HRA exemption simultaneously under the Old Tax Regime.

Actionable Next Step: Set Up Your Rental Paper Trail Before Quarter-End

If you plan to claim HRA on rent paid to parents this financial year, draft a formal rental agreement on appropriate stamp paper today. Set up an automated recurring monthly bank transfer (via standing instruction or UPI) from your salary account to your parent's savings account. Keep numbered rent receipts signed by your parent in a dedicated digital folder. When submitting your year-end investment declarations via Form 124, you will have an unshakeable audit trail ready for payroll verification.

How to Think About This Yourself

Claiming HRA by paying rent to parents requires balancing tax savings against family compliance burdens:

  • Net Family Tax Optimization: Rent paid to parents is taxable income in their hands under 'Income from House Property'. While they receive a statutory 30% deduction under Section 24(a) plus municipal tax write-offs, evaluate whether this pushes them into taxable income brackets or impacts senior citizen rebate ceilings.
  • Ownership Condition: You cannot pay rent to parents if you are a co-owner of the property. The house must be legally registered solely in your parents' name(s).
  • Audit Proofing: Avoid cash payments. A formal rent agreement on stamp paper, monthly bank transfers, and matching declarations on your parent's ITR are essential to withstand scrutiny.
