How Systematic Investment Plans (SIP) Work in India: Mechanics, Mandates, and Operational Steps

Hi, I'm Tejas. I'm an engineering student researching Indian financial systems. This guide explains how Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) operate mechanically, how e-mandates and e-KYC function under SEBI and NPCI guidelines, and how compounding arithmetic behaves over time. This is strictly educational research and arithmetic—it is not financial advice or a recommendation to purchase any mutual fund scheme. For personalized financial planning, please consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA).

Key Concepts: Operational Highlights

  • A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is an automated facility provided by mutual funds to invest a fixed sum at regular intervals (monthly, quarterly) rather than a separate financial product.
  • Mutual fund schemes offer two structural routes: Direct plans (where investments bypass distribution commissions) and Regular plans (where an ongoing distributor commission is embedded in the Total Expense Ratio).
  • Opening an investment folio requires three core KYC documents: PAN card, Aadhaar linked to an active mobile number for OTP verification, and bank account proof (cancelled cheque or statement).
  • Automating monthly debits operates through NPCI e-Mandate (NetBanking, Debit Card, or UPI Autopay), executing transfers directly between your bank account and the fund's custodian.
  • Under prevailing income tax rules, Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) on equity mutual funds held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year, while Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) are taxed at 20%.

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows an individual to commit a predetermined sum at set intervals into a mutual fund scheme. This institutional mechanism automates investment discipline and applies rupee-cost averaging across market fluctuations. Most asset management companies (AMCs) in India accept monthly SIP contributions starting at ₹500 or ₹1,000, with some micro-SIP initiatives accepting lower thresholds.

Setting up an SIP does not necessitate a demat account; mutual fund units can be held in Statement of Account (SoA) format directly through the AMC or Registrar and Transfer Agents (RTAs) like CAMS and KFintech, or through SEBI-registered intermediaries.

Direct Plans vs. Regular Plans: Expense Ratio Mechanics

Every mutual fund scheme in India is mandated by SEBI to offer two distinct plan variants: Direct Plans and Regular Plans. Both plans hold the identical underlying security portfolio and are managed by the same fund management team. The difference lies strictly in the Total Expense Ratio (TER).

In a Regular Plan, an ongoing distribution commission (typically ranging from 0.40% to 1.25% per annum) is deducted from the scheme's assets to compensate the distributor, broker, or banking channel. In a Direct Plan, no intermediary commission is incurred, resulting in a lower TER and a correspondingly higher Net Asset Value (NAV).

To examine how expense differentials compound mathematically over long investment horizons, consider an illustrative ₹15,000 monthly contribution across multiple theoretical gross return rates over 25 years:

The 5 Standard Steps to Establishing a Mutual Fund SIP

Step 1: Complete Paperless e-KYC

Under SEBI regulations, every investor must complete Know Your Customer (KYC) registration once across the financial ecosystem. To complete e-KYC online:

  • PAN Card: Enter your Permanent Account Number for financial identity verification.
  • Aadhaar Verification: Complete DigiLocker authentication via Aadhaar OTP sent to your UIDAI-registered mobile number.
  • In-Person Verification (IPV): Complete digital verification (such as a brief selfie capture or OTP video) to confirm live identity.
  • Bank Account Proof: Submit a bank statement or cancelled cheque verifying your name, account number, and IFSC code for dividend/redemption credits.

Step 2: Choose Your Channel

Investors can execute direct mutual fund orders through several official channels:

  • RTA & Industry Portals (MF Central, myCAMS, KFinKart): Platforms operating on Statement of Account (SoA) format that link directly to AMC folios.
  • Direct AMC Websites: Individual portals operated directly by asset management companies for their own schemes.
  • SEBI-Registered Intermediaries & Brokers: Investment platforms and registered brokers providing both Demat and non-demat execution interfaces.

Step 3: Evaluate Scheme Categories and Growth Options

Investors review fund categories based on their investment horizon and risk tolerance. For broad equity exposure, index funds track benchmarks like the Nifty 50 or Sensex, while active funds (such as flexi-cap or large-and-mid-cap schemes) involve active security selection by fund managers.

Within any scheme, investors choose between Growth (where returns are retained and reflected in rising NAV) and IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal, where periodic payouts are declared and taxed at individual tax slabs).

Step 4: Set Up an Automated e-Mandate (OTM)

A One-Time Mandate (OTM) registers a standing instruction with your bank via NPCI. You authorize this once using your debit card, NetBanking credentials, or UPI Autopay. Every month on your designated date, the payment gateway requests the exact SIP sum from your bank account automatically.

Scheduling the SIP debit a few business days after typical income credit dates helps accommodate banking holidays and avoid accidental overdrafts.

Step 5: Review Optional Facilities (Step-Up SIP)

A Step-Up (top-up) SIP is an optional facility allowing investors to automatically increase their monthly contribution by a specified percentage (e.g., 5% or 10%) or fixed rupee amount annually as their savings capacity grows.

Capital Gains Taxation Rules Applicable to Mutual Funds

Understanding taxation prevents unexpected liabilities upon redemption. Mutual fund taxation is governed by underlying asset allocation and holding periods:

  • Equity Mutual Funds (>65% domestic equity): Units held for 12 months or less attract Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) tax at 20%. Units held for more than 12 months attract Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax at 12.5% on aggregate annual gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh.
  • First-In, First-Out (FIFO) Accounting: Each monthly SIP installment is treated as an independent purchase with its own separate holding period.
  • Debt Mutual Funds (<35% equity): Under Section 50AA of the Income Tax Act, capital gains on specified debt funds are taxed at the investor's applicable income tax slab rate regardless of holding period.

Frequently Asked Questions About Starting an SIP

What happens if my bank account doesn't have enough money on the SIP debit date?

AMCs do not levy a penalty if an SIP installment fails due to insufficient balance. However, your commercial bank may levy an ECS/NACH mandate bounce charge (typically ₹250 to ₹500 plus GST). If multiple consecutive debits fail, the AMC may deactivate the automated SIP schedule, though all previously accumulated units remain intact in your folio.

How does rupee-cost averaging work during market corrections?

When equity markets experience a downturn, scheme Net Asset Values (NAVs) decline. Because an SIP allocates a fixed rupee amount, the same monthly outlay purchases a higher quantity of units during market drops and fewer units when prices are elevated, averaging the acquisition cost per unit over time.

What is the difference between Demat format and Statement of Account (SoA) format?

Statement of Account (SoA) is the traditional non-demat mutual fund structure maintained directly with AMCs and RTAs, requiring no brokerage account or depository annual maintenance charges. Demat holding consolidates mutual funds alongside equity shares under a depository participant (CDSL or NSDL).

Can an investor pause or modify an SIP schedule?

Yes. Mutual fund SIPs offer flexibility. Investors can pause contributions for 1 to 6 months, adjust installment amounts, or terminate the recurring mandate entirely without exit charges on future uncommitted installments (though existing units remain subject to scheme exit loads if redeemed early).

How to Think About This Yourself

An SIP is an operational conduit for long-term investing, not an investment product in itself. The outcome depends on your chosen underlying asset classes, fund expense ratios, investment duration, and discipline through market cycles. Review your cash flow to ensure that any committed monthly installment fits sustainably within your personal budget.

If you need tailored guidance on designing an asset allocation strategy, choosing between mutual fund categories, or assessing tax implications, consider speaking with a fee-only SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) via the SEBI Directory of Intermediaries or a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA).
