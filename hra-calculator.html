How HRA Tax Exemption Works Under Section 10(13A)

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is an integral component of the salary package for most Indian salaried employees. Under Section 10(13A) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 read alongside Rule 2A of the Income-tax Rules, 1962, you can claim an exemption on the rent paid towards residential accommodation occupied by you.

However, HRA is not fully exempt by default. The exact exemption allowed is strictly the least (minimum) of three statutory calculations:

  • Condition 1: The actual House Rent Allowance received from your employer during the relevant financial year.
  • Condition 2: The actual residential rent paid by you minus 10% of your Basic Salary (+ Dearness Allowance if forming part of retirement benefits).
  • Condition 3: 50% of Basic Salary if your rented residence is situated in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, or Chennai (Metro cities), OR 40% of Basic Salary if residing in any other city across India (such as Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad, or Gurugram).

Step-by-Step Worked Example (₹18,000 Monthly Rent)

Consider Rajesh, a software engineer living in Mumbai (Metro) earning a Basic Salary of ₹50,000/month (₹6,00,000/year). His employer pays him an HRA of ₹20,000/month (₹2,40,000/year), and he pays monthly house rent of ₹18,000 (₹2,16,000/year):

• Condition 1 (Actual HRA Received) = ₹2,40,000
• Condition 2 (Rent Paid − 10% of Basic) = ₹2,16,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹1,56,000
• Condition 3 (50% of Basic for Mumbai Metro) = 50% × ₹6,00,000 = ₹3,00,000

Least of the three figures = ₹1,56,000 (Exempt under Section 10(13A))
Taxable HRA = ₹2,40,000 − ₹1,56,000 = ₹84,000 added to Gross Salary

Critical Compliance & Proof Submission Guidelines

  • Mandatory Landlord PAN: Circular No. 08/2013 issued by the CBDT mandates that if your annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000 (approx ₹8,333/month), you MUST submit your landlord's Permanent Account Number (PAN) to your employer for Form 16 verification.
  • New Tax Regime Exclusion: HRA exemption is exclusively available under the Old Tax Regime. Under the Default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), HRA is completely taxable without exemptions.
  • Paying Rent to Parents: You can legally claim HRA exemption by paying rent to your parents, provided they own the property, an authentic registered/notarized rental agreement exists, monthly bank transfers are made, and your parents declare the rental earnings in their annual ITR.

Frequently Asked Questions About HRA Exemption

Is Bengaluru, Hyderabad, or Pune considered a Metro city for HRA?

No. For the purposes of the Income-tax Act Rule 2A, the 50% metro rate applies strictly to the four constitutional presidency metros: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai. All other tech hubs including Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Gurugram, and Noida fall under the 40% non-metro rate.

Can I claim both Home Loan interest (Section 24b) and HRA simultaneously?

Yes, provided there is a valid reason: for example, your owned home is in a different city or location where you cannot reside due to employment, or your purchased home is currently under construction.

What if my employer does not provide HRA in my CTC?

If you pay rent for residential accommodation but do not receive any HRA from your employer, you can claim a deduction under Section 80GG up to a statutory cap of ₹5,000 per month (₹60,000/year).

Strategic Tax Optimization Checklist

When deciding whether to retain the Old Regime for HRA or migrate to the New Regime:

  1. Calculate Total Deductions: Add your HRA exemption + Section 80C (₹1.5L) + Section 80D (Health Insurance) + Standard Deduction (₹50k). If the combined deductions exceed ₹3.75 Lakh to ₹4.25 Lakh, the Old Regime usually yields lower net tax.
  2. Execute Valid Rent Agreements: Maintain genuine bank transfer records and rent receipts stamped with revenue stamps to withstand scrutiny during scrutiny assessments.
  3. Submit Declarations On Time: Submit landlord details before January to prevent excessive TDS deductions by your company payroll team in February and March.
