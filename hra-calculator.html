HRA Exemption Calculator (Section 10(13A))

Written by Tejas Waghmare • Last updated: November 2026
Educational content only: HRA exemption is available solely under the Old Tax Regime. In the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), HRA is fully taxable. Check official Income Tax rules before submitting Form 12BB.

Direct Answer: House Rent Allowance (HRA) is tax-exempt under Section 10(13A) and Rule 2A based on the lowest of three limits: actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of basic salary, or 50% (metro) / 40% (non-metro) of basic salary.

How HRA Exemption Works Under Section 10(13A)

The Income Tax Department evaluates your tax-exempt House Rent Allowance based on Rule 2A of the Income Tax Rules. The exempt amount is strictly the lowest of the following three conditions:

  • Condition 1: Total actual HRA received from your employer during the financial year.
  • Condition 2: Actual rent paid minus 10% of your annual Basic Salary (including Dearness Allowance).
  • Condition 3: 50% of your Basic Salary + DA if you reside in a metro city (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai), or 40% of Basic Salary + DA for all other Indian cities.

2 Worked Mathematical Examples in Rupees

Example 1: Salaried Professional in Bengaluru (Non-Metro)

A software engineer in Bengaluru earns a Basic salary of ₹50,000/month (₹6,00,000/year), receives ₹20,000/month HRA (₹2,40,000/year), and pays ₹18,000/month rent (₹2,16,000/year).

  • Calculation 1: Actual HRA received = ₹2,40,000
  • Condition 2: Rent Paid (₹2,16,000) - 10% of Basic (₹60,000) = ₹1,56,000
  • Condition 3: 40% of Basic (Non-Metro) = ₹2,40,000
  • Exemption = Lowest of the three = ₹1,56,000
  • Taxable HRA = ₹2,40,000 - ₹1,56,000 = ₹84,000

Example 2: Salaried Professional in Mumbai (Metro)

A marketing manager in Mumbai earns Basic ₹80,000/month (₹9,60,000/year), receives ₹35,000/month HRA (₹4,20,000/year), and pays ₹32,000/month rent (₹3,84,000/year).

  • Calculation 2: Actual HRA received = ₹4,20,000
  • Condition 2: Rent Paid (₹3,84,000) - 10% of Basic (₹96,000) = ₹2,88,000
  • Condition 3: 50% of Basic (Metro) = ₹4,80,000
  • Exemption = Lowest of the three = ₹2,88,000
  • Taxable HRA = ₹4,20,000 - ₹2,88,000 = ₹1,32,000

3 Common Mistakes When Claiming HRA Exemption

1. Claiming Under the New Tax Regime

HRA exemption works only under the Old Tax Regime. In the New Regime, it disappears completely. Your employer's entire HRA payout becomes taxable as regular salary.

2. Missing Landlord PAN for Rent > ₹1 Lakh

Paying over ₹1 Lakh annual rent? You must submit your landlord's PAN. It is mandatory on Form 12BB. If you skip it, payroll will deduct full TDS.

3. Cash Rent to Parents Without Bank Trail

Paying rent to your parents? Use bank transfers like NEFT or UPI. Never pay cash. Your parents must also report the rental income on their ITR. The tax department cross-checks AIS data.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Can I claim both HRA and home loan deductions?

Yes. If your own house is in a different city due to your employment, or is under construction, you can simultaneously claim HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) and home loan interest deduction under Section 24(b) in the Old Tax Regime.

Can I pay rent to my spouse and claim HRA?

No. The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) has repeatedly disallowed HRA claims for rent paid to spouses because husband and wife live together in a matrimonial home, negating a genuine landlord-tenant commercial relationship.

What if my employer does not provide HRA in my CTC?

If you pay rent but do not receive any HRA allowance from your employer, you can claim a deduction under Section 80GG, subject to a statutory cap of ₹5,000 per month (₹60,000 per year) under the Old Tax Regime.
