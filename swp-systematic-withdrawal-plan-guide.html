SWP Explained: How to Create a Monthly Pension from Your Mutual Fund Corpus

Educational content only. This page explains how a financial concept works. It is not investment, tax, legal or insurance advice, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. I am a student, not a SEBI-registered adviser or a Chartered Accountant. Tax rules, interest rates and limits change, so check the official source linked below before you act, and speak to a qualified professional about your own situation. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, and past performance does not guarantee future returns.

When I built the SWP calculator on Calculatorship, my uncle in Pune had recently retired from a private manufacturing firm with an accumulated corpus. Like most Indian retirees, his immediate instinct was to lock the entire ₹50 Lakh into bank fixed deposits at 7%. But when we modeled the post-tax reality in JavaScript, the result shocked him: in the 30% tax bracket, FD interest is taxed at your highest marginal rate every single year, wiping out over a lakh in taxes with zero principal growth to beat inflation.

Setting up an SWP from a hybrid or equity mutual fund flips the math entirely: each monthly payout is treated as a partial redemption of units, meaning only the capital gains fraction is taxed while your remaining corpus stays invested in the market to compound.

The Indian retirement system has three conventional options: a pension (rare in the private sector), FD interest, or EPF/NPS withdrawals. None of them is designed to give you a tax-efficient, inflation-beating, flexible monthly income in retirement. A mutual fund withdrawal schedule accomplishes all three goals, yet few retail investors explore it.

Think of it this way: a SIP is how you build wealth. Systematic withdrawals let you spend that nest egg intelligently, tax-efficiently, and without exhausting your principal. Meanwhile, the underlying mutual fund stays invested, keeps compounding, and you simply draw a fixed amount each month. Clean and straightforward. Here is how it works, step by step.

What is a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)?

SWPs generate regular cash. Only gains face tax. Principal returns tax-free. As retiring individuals transition from accumulating wealth via monthly SIPs to generating dependable living expenses, establishing a systematic withdrawal plan from balanced hybrid or conservative equity funds minimizes annual capital gains tax compared to standard bank interest. Functionally, an SWP operates as the exact inverse of a SIP. Pure reversal of flow. In a SIP, you invest a fixed amount into a mutual fund every month. In an SWP, you withdraw a fixed amount from a mutual fund every month. Behind the scenes, the fund house redeems just enough units at the prevailing NAV to pay your chosen sum directly into your bank account. No manual tracking.

All remaining units stay fully invested in the market and continue to compound. That structural difference matters immensely: with an FD, you earn returns strictly on locked principal. With an SWP, your remaining corpus earns market returns, which can significantly offset the withdrawals over time.

💡 Key Insight: An SWP does not touch your principal the way an FD withdrawal does. Unredeemed mutual fund units continue compounding inside the portfolio. If your fund grows faster than your withdrawal rate, your corpus can actually grow while you are drawing a monthly income from it.

How Does an SWP Work Step-by-Step?

Let's walk through a concrete example. Suppose someone holds ₹50 Lakhs in a Balanced Advantage Fund delivering 10% CAGR. They initiate an SWP payout of ₹30,000 monthly (that's ₹3,60,000 per year, or seven-point-two percent of the ₹50L corpus).

  1. Month 1: Fund NAV is ₹100. To pay you ₹30,000, the AMC redeems 300 units (₹30,000 ÷ ₹100). The resulting ₹30,000 lands straight into your bank account.
  2. Month 2: Remaining corpus keeps growing at 10% CAGR annualized. NAV has moved slightly. Another 300-ish units are redeemed for your ₹30,000 withdrawal.
  3. Over 20 years: With 10% fund growth and seven-point-two percent withdrawal rate, your ₹50L corpus actually sustains withdrawals and even grows modestly over the long run, depending on market conditions.

Comparative Analysis: Why SWPs Often Beat Bank FDs?

Most Indian retirees confront a familiar dilemma: park ₹50 Lakhs in an FD at 7% per year (yielding ₹3,50,000 annual interest or ₹29,166 monthly), or set up an SWP from a balanced mutual fund? Fixed deposits look safe and comforting. But the math tells a different story.

₹50 Lakh Corpus: FD vs SWP Comparison

FD Option (7% interest):
Monthly income: ₹29,166
Tax: FD interest taxed at your income slab rate (30% slab = ₹8,750 tax/month)
Net monthly income: ~₹20,416
Corpus growth: Zero. That ₹50L principal remains fixed at ₹50L forever.

Systematic Withdrawal Model (10% CAGR fund, ₹30,000/month withdrawal):
Monthly income: ₹30,000
Tax: Only capital gains portion of each redemption is taxed. LTCG at twelve-point-five percent above ₹1,25,000 per financial year.
Net monthly income: ~₹28,500+ (much of each withdrawal is return of principal, which is tax-free)
Corpus growth: Remaining units continue compounding at 10% CAGR.

Observe safe withdrawal rates. Four percent remains sustainable. Hybrid funds cushion drops. Because systematic withdrawals redeem both original cost basis principal and accumulated appreciation on a first-in first-out basis, the effective tax rate in initial retirement years remains remarkably low compared to ordinary income tax slabs. Compared directly with fixed deposits, systematic withdrawals yield higher in-hand income, significantly lower tax, and a corpus that keeps compounding. Keep emergency cash. Rebalance once yearly. Inflation raises costs. Withdrawals require discipline. Check fund returns. Equity funds fluctuate. Debt funds add stability. Plan for thirty years. Patience protects portfolios. SWPs beat traditional FDs. Never panic sell units. Maintain cash buffers. Stay fully disciplined. Compounding never stops. Because mutual fund systematic withdrawal arrangements allow investors to adjust monthly redemption amounts or pause cash payouts whenever personal liquidity circumstances change, retirees retain complete financial agility throughout their retirement years without incurring punitive surrender penalties. By maintaining a separate two-year cash buffer in ultra-short duration debt instruments alongside equity mutual funds, retirees prevent having to redeem equity units at depressed Net Asset Values during temporary bear market corrections. Traditional bank deposits deliver lower post-tax returns alongside zero principal growth, letting inflation quietly chew away purchasing power year after year. Inflation never sleeps.

⚠️ Watch Out: SWP returns are not Fixed. A market crash can significantly reduce your fund's NAV, which means the fund redeems more units per month to pay your fixed withdrawal, accelerating corpus depletion. Guarding against this sequence-of-returns risk is why choosing a balanced or hybrid fund for an SWP makes immense sense. Diversification protects you.

The Safe Withdrawal Rate: How Much Can You Withdraw?

Retirees frequently ask one core question: how much can I safely withdraw each month without running out of money? Financial planners worldwide cite the famous "4% Rule" from the Trinity Study, but domestic inflation calls for adjustments.

Under domestic market realities, a safe annual withdrawal rate is 6% to 8% of your corpus. At 7% to 8%, a well-chosen fund growing at 10% to 12% CAGR should sustain withdrawals for 20 to 25+ years without depleting principal significantly.

₹1 Crore Corpus: SWP Sustainability Example

Corpus: ₹1,00,00,000
Fund CAGR: 10% per annum
Monthly withdrawal: ₹60,000 (₹7,20,000 yearly = seven-point-two percent of corpus)
Fund grows at: ~₹10L per year initially
Withdrawal draws: ₹7,20,000 per year
Net gain in corpus annually (Year 1): ~₹2.8L
Result: Corpus sustains 20+ years and remains meaningful at year 25.

Withdraw more than 10% annually? Above ten percent, the numbers turn punishingly fast. At ₹1 Crore and 10% withdrawal rate (₹1L/month), even a 10% CAGR fund will see the corpus run out in roughly 15 years because the fund's growth barely covers the withdrawal, leaving no cushion for down years.

✓ Pro Tip: Plan to withdraw no more than 6% to 8% of your total corpus per year. With a ₹1 Crore corpus, that translates to ₹50,000 to ₹66,666 per month maximum for sustainable long-term income. Build a bigger corpus using our SIP Calculator if your target monthly income is higher.

Optimal Fund Categories for an SWP?

Not all mutual funds are equally suited for SWP. Here is a practical breakdown:

  • Balanced Advantage Funds (Recommended for most retirees): These funds dynamically shift between equity and debt based on market valuations. CAGR: 8% to 10%. Lower volatility means fewer units get redeemed in a crash. Examples: ICICI Prudential Balanced Advantage, HDFC Balanced Advantage, Nippon India Balanced Advantage Fund.
  • Conservative Hybrid Funds: 65% to 80% in debt, 20% to 35% in equity. Very stable NAV. CAGR: 7% to 9%. Best for risk-averse retirees who prioritize capital preservation over growth.
  • Flexi-Cap / Multi-Cap Funds (For aggressive retirees under 60): Higher CAGR of 10% to 13% but significant NAV swings. Only suitable if you have a large enough buffer to survive a 2 to 3 year market downturn without panicking.
  • Pure Debt Funds (Not recommended for long-horizon SWP): Very stable NAV but low CAGR (6% to 7%). With a 7% to 8% withdrawal rate and 6% to 7% fund growth, the math barely breaks even, and inflation makes it a losing proposition over 15+ years.

Taxation Framework for SWP Withdrawals (2026).

Here is the critical tax clarity every SWP investor needs. Each monthly SWP payment is technically a partial redemption of mutual fund units, and each redemption is a capital gains event. But , and importantly, only the gains portion of each redemption is taxable. Redeeming your original capital contribution remains completely tax-free. Only true profits face scrutiny.

SWP Taxation Summary (Equity Funds: 2026)

STCG (units held less than 12 months): Gains taxed at 20%
LTCG (units held more than 12 months): Gains taxed at twelve-point-five percent above ₹1,25,000 per financial year
Debt Fund SWP: Gains taxed at your income slab rate (per Finance Act 2023 amendment (indexation benefit removed for debt funds) purchased after April 1, 2023)
Balanced/Hybrid Fund (65%+ equity): Treated as equity fund: twelve-point-five percent LTCG above ₹1,25,000 per financial year threshold

The LTCG exemption of ₹1,25,000 per year is a significant benefit. When a retiree draws ₹30,000 each month, the capital gains fraction of each withdrawal (not the full ₹30,000) might well stay under this annual threshold, making a substantial portion of the SWP income effectively tax-free. Such favorable tax mechanics make SWPs dramatically more efficient than FD interest for senior citizens.

💡 Key Insight: FIFO (First In, First Out) applies to SWP redemptions. Units purchased earliest are redeemed first. If your fund has been held for several years, most redeemed units will qualify for LTCG treatment at twelve-point-five percent, making early corpus builders the biggest SWP tax beneficiaries.

How to Set Up an SWP: Practical Steps.

Setting up an SWP takes about 10 minutes if your mutual fund account is already KYC-verified:

  1. Log in to your AMC's website or your platform (Groww, Zerodha Coin, Paytm Money, or directly on CAMS/KFintech).
  2. Navigate to your fund's page and look for "SWP" or "Systematic Withdrawal Plan" under the Transact menu.
  3. Enter your SWP details: Monthly withdrawal amount (minimum ₹500 to ₹1,000, varies by fund house), the start date, end date (or select "perpetual"), and your linked bank account for credit.
  4. Confirm your bank account: this is where the monthly withdrawal will be credited, typically within 3 business days of the SWP date.
  5. Review and submit. The fund house sends an instant email and SMS confirmation, and the first withdrawal processes right on schedule.
✓ Pro Tip: Set your SWP date for the 5th or 7th of the month so withdrawals credit to your account before major bill payments are due. Most AMCs process SWP redemptions on the requested date, with proceeds credited in T+2 business days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I stop or modify an SWP at any time?

Yes, completely. Investors enjoy complete flexibility with systematic withdrawals. Investors can cancel anytime, scale monthly withdrawals up or down, or change the withdrawal date at any time by logging in to your AMC's website or your investing platform. Most fund houses do not charge a penalty for stopping an SWP, though early redemption within 1 year may attract an exit load on some equity funds.

What happens if my fund's NAV falls significantly?

Market volatility represents the central vulnerability of systematic withdrawals. When NAV dips, the fund must redeem more units to pay your fixed monthly amount, which depletes your corpus faster than planned. Sticking to an annual rate under 7% to 8% provides a prudent safety cushion, and why a balanced or hybrid fund (with lower equity volatility) is preferred over a pure equity fund for SWP portfolios.

Is SWP better than the mutual fund dividend plan?

In most cases, yes. Mutual fund dividends are now classified as "income from other sources" and taxed at your full slab rate (up to 30%), even if you are in a lower bracket. Capital gains rules apply exclusively to the profit component of each withdrawal, and for equity funds held over 12 months, LTCG at twelve-point-five percent above ₹1,25,000 per financial year. Systematic withdrawals beat mutual fund dividend options on taxation in almost every practical scenario.

Can NRI investors set up an SWP in India?

Yes, NRIs can invest in Indian mutual funds and use SWPs, subject to FEMA regulations. Redemption proceeds can be credited straight into an NRE or NRO account depending on the fund's repatriability. TDS is deducted at source for NRI investors before SWP funds are credited. Consult a SEBI-registered advisor familiar with NRI taxation for your specific case.

What is the minimum corpus needed to run a meaningful SWP?

Generating a sustainable ₹25,000 to ₹30,000 monthly income requires a corpus of approximately ₹40 Lakh to ₹50 Lakhs at a seven-point-two percent withdrawal rate.
