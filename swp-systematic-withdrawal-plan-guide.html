SWP Explained: How to Create a Monthly Pension from Your Mutual Fund Corpus

Educational content only. This page explains how a financial concept works. It is not investment, tax, legal or insurance advice, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. You've spent 25 years building a ₹1 crore mutual fund corpus. Now what? How do you turn that mountain of capital into a reliable monthly salary — one that doesn't run out before you do? A Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) is the answer most retirement planners in India aren't talking about loudly enough.

July 30, 2026 By Tejas
← Back to Articles

The Indian retirement system has three conventional options: a pension (rare in the private sector), FD interest, or EPF/NPS withdrawals. None of them is designed to give you a tax-efficient, inflation-beating, flexible monthly income in retirement. An SWP from a mutual fund corpus does all three — and most investors have never heard of it.

Think of it this way: a SIP is how you build wealth. An SWP is how you spend it — intelligently, tax-efficiently, and without exhausting your principal. The fund stays invested, keeps compounding, and you simply draw a fixed amount each month. Here is how it works, step by step.

What is a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)?

An SWP is the exact opposite of a SIP. In a SIP, you invest a fixed amount into a mutual fund every month. In an SWP, you withdraw a fixed amount from a mutual fund every month. The fund house automatically redeems enough units at the prevailing NAV to pay you your chosen amount, and the proceeds land directly in your bank account.

Your remaining corpus — the units not yet redeemed — stays fully invested in the market and continues to grow. This is the key difference from an FD: with an FD, you earn only on the principal. With an SWP, your remaining corpus earns market returns, which can significantly offset the withdrawals over time.

💡 Key Insight: An SWP does not touch your principal the way an FD withdrawal does. Your remaining units continue compounding inside the fund. If your fund grows faster than your withdrawal rate, your corpus can actually grow while you are drawing a monthly income from it.

How Does an SWP Work? — Step-by-Step

Let's walk through a concrete example. You have ₹50 Lakhs invested in a Balanced Advantage Fund delivering 10% CAGR. You set up an SWP of ₹30,000/month (that's ₹3.6L per year, or 7.2% of the ₹50L corpus).

  1. Month 1: Fund NAV is ₹100. To pay you ₹30,000, the AMC redeems 300 units (₹30,000 ÷ ₹100). Your ₹30,000 hits your bank account.
  2. Month 2: Remaining corpus keeps growing at 10% CAGR annualized. NAV has moved slightly. Another 300-ish units are redeemed for your ₹30,000 withdrawal.
  3. Over 20 years: With 10% fund growth and 7.2% withdrawal rate, your ₹50L corpus actually sustains withdrawals and even grows modestly over the long run — depending on market conditions.

SWP vs FD Interest — Why SWP Often Wins

The comparison most Indian retirees face: park ₹50 Lakhs in an FD at 7% per year (₹3.5L annual interest = ₹29,166/month), or set up an SWP from a balanced mutual fund? The FD looks safe and familiar. But the math tells a different story.

₹50 Lakh Corpus — FD vs SWP Comparison

FD Option (7% interest):
Monthly income: ₹29,166
Tax: FD interest taxed at your income slab rate (30% slab = ₹8,750 tax/month)
Net monthly income: ~₹20,416
Corpus growth: Zero. Your ₹50L stays at ₹50L forever.

SWP Option (10% CAGR fund, ₹30,000/month withdrawal):
Monthly income: ₹30,000
Tax: Only capital gains portion of each redemption is taxed. LTCG at 12.5% above ₹1.25L/year.
Net monthly income: ~₹28,500+ (much of each withdrawal is return of principal, which is tax-free)
Corpus growth: Remaining units continue compounding at 10% CAGR.

The SWP delivers higher monthly income, significantly lower tax, and a corpus that keeps growing. The FD delivers lower post-tax income and zero corpus growth — which means inflation silently erodes its purchasing power every single year.

⚠️ Watch Out: SWP returns are not Fixed. A market crash can significantly reduce your fund's NAV, which means the fund redeems more units per month to pay your fixed withdrawal — accelerating corpus depletion. This risk is why choosing a balanced or hybrid fund (rather than a pure equity fund) for SWP is important.

The Safe Withdrawal Rate — How Much Can You Withdraw?

The biggest question in SWP planning: how much can I safely withdraw each month without running out of money? The global rule of thumb — popularized by the "4% Rule" from the Trinity Study — has been adapted for Indian conditions.

For Indian investors, a safe annual withdrawal rate is 6–8% of your corpus. At 7–8%, a well-chosen fund growing at 10–12% CAGR should sustain withdrawals for 20–25+ years without depleting principal significantly.

₹1 Crore Corpus — SWP Sustainability Example

Corpus: ₹1,00,00,000
Fund CAGR: 10% per annum
Monthly withdrawal: ₹60,000 (₹7.2L/year = 7.2% of corpus)
Fund grows at: ~₹10L per year initially
Withdrawal draws: ₹7.2L per year
Net gain in corpus annually (Year 1): ~₹2.8L
Result: Corpus sustains 20+ years and remains meaningful at year 25.

Withdraw more than 10% annually? The math turns against you quickly. At ₹1 Crore and 10% withdrawal rate (₹1L/month), even a 10% CAGR fund will see the corpus run out in roughly 15 years because the fund's growth barely covers the withdrawal, leaving no cushion for down years.

✓ Pro Tip: Plan to withdraw no more than 6–8% of your total corpus per year. For a ₹1 Crore corpus, that means ₹50,000–₹66,666 per month maximum for sustainable long-term income. Build a bigger corpus using our SIP Calculator if your target monthly income is higher.

What Funds Are Best for SWP?

Not all mutual funds are equally suited for SWP. Here is a practical breakdown:

  • Balanced Advantage Funds (Recommended for most retirees): These funds dynamically shift between equity and debt based on market valuations. CAGR: 8–10%. Lower volatility means fewer units get redeemed in a crash. Examples: ICICI Prudential Balanced Advantage, HDFC Balanced Advantage, Nippon India Balanced Advantage Fund.
  • Conservative Hybrid Funds: 65–80% in debt, 20–35% in equity. Very stable NAV. CAGR: 7–9%. Best for risk-averse retirees who prioritize capital preservation over growth.
  • Flexi-Cap / Multi-Cap Funds (For aggressive retirees under 60): Higher CAGR of 10–13% but significant NAV swings. Only suitable if you have a large enough buffer to survive a 2–3 year market downturn without panicking.
  • Pure Debt Funds (Not recommended for long-horizon SWP): Very stable NAV but low CAGR (6–7%). With a 7–8% withdrawal rate and 6–7% fund growth, the math barely breaks even — and inflation makes it a losing proposition over 15+ years.

Tax Rules on SWP Withdrawals (2026)

Here is the critical tax clarity every SWP investor needs. Each monthly SWP payment is technically a partial redemption of mutual fund units, and each redemption is a capital gains event. But — and this is key — only the gains portion of each redemption is taxable. The return of your original investment (cost basis) is tax-free.

SWP Taxation Summary (Equity Funds — 2026)

STCG (units held less than 12 months): Gains taxed at 20%
LTCG (units held more than 12 months): Gains taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year
Debt Fund SWP: Gains taxed at your income slab rate (per Finance Act 2023 amendment — indexation benefit removed for debt funds purchased after April 1, 2023)
Balanced/Hybrid Fund (65%+ equity): Treated as equity fund — 12.5% LTCG above ₹1.25L/year threshold

The LTCG exemption of ₹1.25 Lakh per year is a significant benefit. For a retiree withdrawing ₹30,000/month, the capital gains portion of each withdrawal (not the full ₹30,000) might well stay under this annual threshold — making a substantial portion of the SWP income effectively tax-free. This is the tax advantage that makes SWP dramatically more efficient than FD interest for Indian retirees.

💡 Key Insight: FIFO (First In, First Out) applies to SWP redemptions. Units purchased earliest are redeemed first. If your fund has been held for several years, most redeemed units will qualify for LTCG treatment at 12.5%, making early corpus builders the biggest SWP tax beneficiaries.

How to Set Up an SWP — Practical Steps

Setting up an SWP takes about 10 minutes if your mutual fund account is already KYC-verified:

  1. Log in to your AMC's website or your platform (Groww, Zerodha Coin, Paytm Money, or directly on CAMS/KFintech).
  2. Navigate to your fund's page and look for "SWP" or "Systematic Withdrawal Plan" under the Transact menu.
  3. Enter your SWP details: Monthly withdrawal amount (minimum ₹500–₹1,000, varies by fund house), the start date, end date (or select "perpetual"), and your linked bank account for credit.
  4. Confirm your bank account — this is where the monthly withdrawal will be credited, typically within 3 business days of the SWP date.
  5. Review and submit. You will receive an email/SMS confirmation, and the first withdrawal processes on the chosen date.
✓ Pro Tip: Set your SWP date for the 5th or 7th of the month so withdrawals credit to your account before major bill payments are due. Most AMCs process SWP redemptions on the requested date, with proceeds credited in T+2 business days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I stop or modify an SWP at any time?

Yes — completely. An SWP is fully flexible. You can stop it, increase or decrease the monthly withdrawal amount, or change the withdrawal date at any time by logging in to your AMC's website or your investing platform. Most fund houses do not charge a penalty for stopping an SWP, though early redemption within 1 year may attract an exit load on some equity funds.

What happens if my fund's NAV falls significantly?

This is the core risk of SWP. When NAV drops, the fund needs to redeem more units to pay your fixed monthly amount, which depletes your corpus faster than planned. This is why keeping your withdrawal rate under 7–8% annually is important, and why a balanced or hybrid fund (with lower equity volatility) is preferred over a pure equity fund for SWP portfolios.

Is SWP better than the mutual fund dividend plan?

In most cases, yes. Mutual fund dividends are now classified as "income from other sources" and taxed at your full slab rate (up to 30%), even if you are in a lower bracket. SWP withdrawals are taxed only on the gains portion — and for equity funds held over 12 months, LTCG at 12.5% above ₹1.25L/year. SWP is almost always more tax-efficient than the dividend plan.

Can NRI investors set up an SWP in India?

Yes, NRIs can invest in Indian mutual funds and use SWPs, subject to FEMA regulations. SWP proceeds can be credited to an NRE or NRO account depending on the fund's repatriability. TDS is deducted at source for NRI investors before SWP funds are credited. Consult a SEBI-registered advisor familiar with NRI taxation for your specific case.

What is the minimum corpus needed to run a meaningful SWP?

For a sustainable ₹25,000–₹30,000/month income, you need a corpus of approximately ₹40–₹50 Lakhs at a 7.2% withdrawal rate. For ₹60,000/month, target ₹1 Crore. Use our SIP Calculator to calculate exactly how much you need to invest monthly today to build your target SWP corpus by retirement.
