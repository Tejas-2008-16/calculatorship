Advance Tax in India: Due Dates, Calculation & Penalties Guide

The Indian tax system functions on a 'Pay As You Earn' principle. If your estimated annual tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 after TDS, waiting until July to file your tax return can lead to heavy penal interest under Sections 234B and 234C. Understand the quarterly schedules, senior citizen exemptions, capital gain windfalls, and exact online payment methods.

Statutory Compliance Notice: Advance Tax obligations are enacted under Sections 207 to 219 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. Non-payment, delayed payment, or under-payment triggers mandatory non-waivable statutory interest at 1% per month under Sections 234B and 234C. These provisions apply equally under both the Old and New Tax Regimes.

Key Takeaways & Executive Summary

  • The Trigger Threshold: Advance tax is mandatory if your net tax payable for the financial year after deducting TDS/TCS exceeds ₹10,000.
  • Quarterly Rhythm: Regular taxpayers must pay in 4 tranches: 15% by 15 June, 45% by 15 September, 75% by 15 December, and 100% by 15 March.
  • Senior Citizen Relief: Resident senior citizens (aged 60+) with zero business/professional income are 100% exempt from advance tax.
  • Presumptive Taxation Advantage: Small businesses and professionals under Section 44AD/44ADA pay 100% advance tax in a single tranche on or before 15 March.
  • Windfall Protection: If you incur capital gains or dividend income after an installment deadline, you can pay the tax in subsequent tranches without triggering Section 234C penal interest.

1. What is Advance Tax & The 'Pay As You Earn' Principle

Under Indian income tax law, taxes are not paid solely at the end of the financial year. The government mandates a staggered revenue collection framework known as Advance Tax, governed by Sections 207 to 219 of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

Commonly referred to as the 'Pay As You Earn' (PAYE) scheme, advance tax requires taxpayers to estimate their annual earnings and pay taxes in installments throughout the same financial year in which the income is generated, rather than postponing all payments to the time of filing the ITR.

2. Who is Liable to Pay? The ₹10,000 Statutory Rule

Section 208 provides the baseline threshold: Every person whose estimated tax liability for the financial year is ₹10,000 or more after adjusting for TDS, TCS, and foreign tax credits is statutorily obligated to pay advance tax.

Who is typically impacted?

  • Salaried Employees with Side Incomes: Salaried individuals whose employers deduct TDS under Section 192, but who earn substantial additional income from bank FD interest, mutual fund capital gains, stock trading, or rental property.
  • Freelancers & Independent Consultants: Tech contractors, content creators, advocates, and doctors receiving payments with only 10% or 1% TDS under Section 194J/194C, while falling into the 20% or 30% tax brackets.
  • Business Owners & Sole Proprietorships: Any trading, manufacturing, or service enterprise generating taxable business profits.
  • Stock & Crypto Investors: Active traders making short-term or long-term capital gains across domestic equities, international stocks, or Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs).

3. Senior Citizen Exemption Criteria (Section 207(2))

A crucial statutory exemption exists under Section 207(2) of the Income Tax Act designed to reduce compliance burdens on elderly citizens:

Section 207(2) Exemption Criteria:

An individual who is a resident in India, of the age of 60 years or more at any time during the financial year, and who does not have any income chargeable under the head 'Profits and gains of business or profession' is completely exempt from paying advance tax.

This means a 65-year-old pensioner earning ₹15 lakh from pension, bank interest, and capital gains does not need to calculate or pay quarterly advance tax installments. They can pay their balance tax as Self-Assessment Tax before filing their ITR in July, without any Section 234B/234C interest liability.

4. Advance Tax Due Dates & Installment Percentages (FY 2026-27)

For corporate entities and non-corporate taxpayers (salaried individuals, freelancers, LLPs, firms), the statutory payment schedule comprises four quarterly deadlines:

Installment Due Date Cumulative Minimum Tax Required Statutory Focus
On or before 15th June Not less than 15% of total tax liability First quarter estimated income & initial advance
On or before 15th September Not less than 45% of total tax liability Mid-year review of earnings, Q2 capital gains
On or before 15th December Not less than 75% of total tax liability Major tax reconciliation for festival season / annual bonuses
On or before 15th March 100% of total tax liability Final closing balance of tax before financial year ends

Note: Under Section 211(1), any tax paid on or before 31st March of the financial year is also legally treated as advance tax paid during the financial year, although Section 234C interest may apply for missing the 15th March milestone.

5. Special Rules for Presumptive Taxation (Section 44AD & 44ADA)

Eligible small businesses opting for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD and eligible professionals (software freelancers, doctors, chartered accountants, architects) opting for Section 44ADA enjoy a simplified single-installment schedule:

  • Single Due Date: They are exempt from the 15 June, 15 September, and 15 December installments.
  • Payment Obligation: They must pay 100% of their advance tax in a single tranche on or before 15th March of the relevant financial year.
  • Presumptive Margin: 44AD presumes net profit at 6% (digital receipts) or 8% (cash receipts) of gross turnover. 44ADA presumes net profit at 50% of gross receipts. Advance tax is computed on the resultant taxable income.

6. Step-by-Step Advance Tax Calculation Formula

To accurately estimate your quarterly liability, follow this 5-step mathematical workflow:

  1. Estimate Total Gross Income: Sum expected annual revenue across all heads: Salary + Business/Profession + Capital Gains + House Property Rent + Other Sources (Bank Interest/Dividends).
  2. Subtract Permissible Deductions: If opting for Old Regime, deduct eligible 80C, 80D, 80CCD, 80GG, and standard deductions. If opting for New Regime (115BAC), deduct standard deduction (₹75,000 for salaried) and 80CCD(2).
  3. Compute Gross Tax: Apply the prevailing tax slab rates to your net taxable income, and add 4% Health & Education Cess.
  4. Deduct Available Tax Credits: Subtract all expected TDS (reflected in 26AS/AIS) and TCS credits.
  5. Assess the Balance: If Balance Tax ≥ ₹10,000, multiply by the relevant quarterly cumulative percentages (15%, 45%, 75%, 100%) and pay the respective net installment.

7. Harsh Cost of Delay: Section 234B & 234C Penal Interest

Defaulting on advance tax attracts mandatory statutory interest. Understanding the mechanics of Section 234B and Section 234C helps you prevent costly penalties:

Section Default Condition Interest Rate & Duration
Section 234C (Deferment of Installments) Paid less than 12% by Jun 15, less than 36% by Sep 15, less than 75% by Dec 15, or less than 100% by Mar 15. 1% per month for 3 months on shortfall for Q1, Q2, Q3; 1% for 1 month for Q4.
Section 234B (Shortfall of Total Tax) Total advance tax paid before 31st March is less than 90% of assessed total tax. 1% per month computed from 1st April of Assessment Year until actual date of tax payment.

Statutory Buffer: Notice that for the 15th June installment (15%), no Section 234C interest is levied if the taxpayer pays at least 12%. Similarly for 15th September (45%), no 234C interest applies if at least 36% is paid. This 3% buffer prevents minor estimation errors from triggering penalties.

8. The Special Relief Provision for Capital Gains & Dividend Windfalls

Taxpayers cannot foresee when they will book a major capital gain from an equity rally, or when a company will announce a dividend. Recognizing this genuine practical challenge, the law includes a specific protective proviso under Section 234C(1):

Capital Gains Exemption Proviso:

No penal interest under Section 234C shall be levied in respect of any shortfall arising on account of under-estimating capital gains, dividend income, or lottery winnings, provided the assessee pays the entire tax payable on such windfall in the remaining installments which fall due after the date of receipt/realization, or before 31st March if no further installments remain.

For example, if you sell shares and make a ₹10 lakh capital gain on 20th November 2026, you will not be penalized for having paid zero advance tax on that gain in the June and September installments. You simply need to include the tax on this gain in the 15th December and 15th March installments.

9. How to Pay Advance Tax Online via e-Pay Tax Portal

Physical paper Challan 280 payments at bank branches have been replaced with the streamlined e-Pay Tax system on the Income Tax e-filing portal:

  1. Visit Portal: Navigate to incometax.gov.in and click on e-Pay Tax in Quick Links.
  2. Enter Identity: Input your PAN, confirm PAN, and verify your mobile number via OTP.
  3. Select Tax Tile: Click Proceed under the Income Tax tile.
  4. Choose Assessment Year: Select AY 2027-28 (for financial year 2026-27).
  5. Select Type of Payment: Choose Advance Tax (100) from the dropdown menu.
  6. Enter Tax Amounts: Split your payment into Basic Tax, Surcharge, and Cess as applicable.
  7. Execute Payment: Pay seamlessly using Net Banking, UPI, Debit Card, RTGS/NEFT, or Payment Gateway.
  8. Download Challan: Save the Challan Receipt containing the BSR Code, Challan Number, and CIN for your ITR records.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

I am a salaried employee. Why should I pay advance tax?

Your employer only deducts TDS on the salary they pay you. If you have significant interest from savings accounts and fixed deposits, dividend payouts, or capital gains from mutual funds, the tax on this extra income is not deducted by your employer. If this shortfall exceeds ₹10,000, you are legally required to pay advance tax directly.

What if I pay more advance tax than my actual tax liability?

If your advance tax plus TDS exceeds your final assessed tax liability, the Income Tax Department will refund the excess amount directly into your pre-validated bank account along with statutory interest at 0.5% per month under Section 244A.

Can I pay advance tax on 31st March?

Yes. Under Section 211(1), any tax deposited on or before 31st March is treated as advance tax for that financial year. However, you may still incur Section 234C interest for the missed June, September, and December installment deadlines.

What is the difference between Advance Tax (100) and Self-Assessment Tax (300)?

Advance Tax (Code 100) is paid during the financial year before 31st March. Self-Assessment Tax (Code 300) is paid after the financial year ends (after 31st March) when you calculate your final taxes before submitting your return.
