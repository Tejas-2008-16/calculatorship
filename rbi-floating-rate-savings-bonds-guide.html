Sovereign Backing & Liquidity Advisory

RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds carry a 100% sovereign guarantee from the Government of India. However, they are non-transferable, non-tradable, cannot be pledged for loans, and have a strict 7-year lock-in (with graded premature exit only for subscribers aged 60 and above). Invest only long-term surplus capital that will not be needed for emergency requirements.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Current Rate: 8.05% p.a., resets on 1 January and 1 July, pegged at NSC rate + 0.35%.
  • Sovereign Safety: Backed 100% by the Government of India with zero credit or default risk.
  • 7-Year Lock-in: Strict tenure for investors under 60; graded premature exit allowed only for senior citizens (ages 60+).
  • Semi-Annual Payouts: Interest credited every 1st January and 1st July; no compounding option exists.
  • Full Taxability: Interest is taxed as regular income at slab rates; subject to 10% TDS under Section 193.
  • Zero Secondary Trading: Cannot be transferred, sold on an exchange, or pledged for a bank loan.

1. What Are RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds (FRSB)?

The Reserve Bank of India Floating Rate Savings Bonds (Taxable), 2020—commonly abbreviated as FRSB 2020 (T)—are sovereign debt instruments issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India. Introduced in July 2020 to replace the earlier 7.75% fixed-rate savings bonds, these bonds were designed to safeguard conservative retail investors from locking into low fixed coupons during periods of volatile interest rate cycles.

Unlike regular bank fixed deposits or corporate debentures, RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds do not carry a fixed interest coupon throughout their 7-year life. Instead, the coupon is dynamic and periodically recalibrated against India’s small savings benchmark. Because they are sovereign obligations of the Union Government, they offer absolute capital safety, eliminating the credit risk inherent in private financial institutions.

The bonds are issued in electronic form through a Bond Ledger Account (BLA) maintained with the Reserve Bank of India or through authorized receiving offices (major public sector and private commercial banks), or directly via the RBI Retail Direct portal.

2. The Floating Rate Coupon Mechanism & 8.05% Payout

The defining characteristic of FRSB 2020 is its floating interest structure. Understanding how this coupon is derived ensures investors can anticipate changes to their semi-annual cash flows.

The bond’s coupon rate is explicitly linked to the rate of interest on the National Savings Certificate (NSC), plus a fixed positive spread:

RBI FRSB Coupon Formula: Bond Coupon Rate = Prevailing NSC Interest Rate + 0.35% (35 basis points)

The Ministry of Finance reviews and sets interest rates for small savings schemes on a quarterly basis. However, the coupon on RBI Floating Rate Bonds is reset only semi-annually on:

  • 1st January (reflecting the NSC rate declared for Q4 of the fiscal year)
  • 1st July (reflecting the NSC rate declared for Q2 of the fiscal year)

With the benchmark NSC rate standing steady at 7.70% p.a., the addition of the 35 bps markup establishes the current payout rate on RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds at 8.05% per annum.

NSC Benchmark Rate Fixed Spread Effective FRSB Coupon Rate Semi-Annual Payout per ₹10 Lakhs
7.00% +0.35% 7.35% p.a. ₹36,750
7.50% +0.35% 7.85% p.a. ₹39,250
7.70% (Current) +0.35% 8.05% p.a. ₹40,250
8.00% +0.35% 8.35% p.a. ₹41,750

Note: Interest is paid semi-annually. The coupon payout per half-year equals (Principal × Annual Coupon Rate ÷ 2).

5. Lock-in Period & Premature Exit Rules for Seniors

For investors below 60 years of age, the lock-in period is 7 years with no premature exit allowed. However, recognizing the liquidity needs of senior citizens, the Government permits graded premature encashment:

Age Bracket Minimum Holding Period Penalty on Exit
Below 60 years Full 7 Years (No early exit) Not applicable
60 to 70 years 6 Years Deduction of 50% interest due for last 6 months
70 to 80 years 5 Years Deduction of 50% interest due for last 6 months
80 years and above 4 Years Deduction of 50% interest due for last 6 months

6. Income Tax Treatment & Section 193 TDS Rules

The semi-annual interest earned is fully taxable under “Income from Other Sources” at the investor's marginal slab rates under either the Old or New Tax Regime. There is no Section 80C deduction on the principal investment.

Under Section 193 of the Income-tax Act, TDS is deducted at 10% if total interest exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year. Investors whose total taxable income is below the taxable threshold can avoid TDS by submitting Form 15G (below 60 years) or Form 15H (senior citizens).

8. How to Purchase FRSB: RBI Retail Direct & Banks

Investors can subscribe through two official channels:

  1. RBI Retail Direct: Open a Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account online at rbiretaildirect.org.in with Aadhaar and PAN via video KYC. Direct commission-free subscription with zero annual maintenance fees.
  2. Authorized Commercial Banks: Visit branches or internet banking portals of SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Bank of Baroda, or PNB to submit Form A. A Certificate of Holding in electronic Bond Ledger Account (BLA) format will be issued.

9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the current interest rate on RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds in 2026?

The current rate of interest is 8.05% per annum, derived from the benchmark National Savings Certificate (NSC) rate of 7.70% plus a fixed markup of 0.35% (35 bps). The coupon resets semi-annually on 1 January and 1 July.

Can I compound interest till the 7-year maturity?

No. FRSB 2020 is exclusively a non-cumulative bond. Interest is disbursed semi-annually on 1st January and 1st July directly into the subscriber’s registered bank account.

Can an investor take a loan or overdraft against RBI Floating Rate Bonds?

No. The Government of India guidelines strictly specify that RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds cannot be pledged as collateral, margin, or security to obtain loans or overdrafts.
