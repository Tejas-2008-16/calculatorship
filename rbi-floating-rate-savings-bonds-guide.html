1. What Are RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds (FRSB)?

The Reserve Bank of India Floating Rate Savings Bonds (Taxable), 2020—commonly abbreviated as FRSB 2020 (T)—are sovereign debt instruments issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India. Introduced in July 2020 to replace the earlier 7.75% fixed-rate savings bonds, these bonds were designed to safeguard conservative retail investors from locking into low fixed coupons during periods of volatile interest rate cycles.

Unlike regular bank fixed deposits or corporate debentures, RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds do not carry a fixed interest coupon throughout their 7-year life. Instead, the coupon is dynamic and periodically recalibrated against India’s small savings benchmark. Because they are sovereign obligations of the Union Government, they offer absolute capital safety, eliminating the credit risk inherent in private financial institutions.

The bonds are issued in electronic form through a Bond Ledger Account (BLA) maintained with the Reserve Bank of India or through authorized receiving offices (major public sector and private commercial banks), or directly via the RBI Retail Direct portal.

2. The Floating Rate Coupon Mechanism & Current 8.05% Payout

The defining characteristic of FRSB 2020 is its floating interest structure. Understanding how this coupon is derived ensures investors can anticipate changes to their semi-annual cash flows.

The Benchmark Formula

The bond’s coupon rate is explicitly linked to the rate of interest on the National Savings Certificate (NSC), plus a fixed positive spread:

RBI FRSB Coupon Formula: Bond Coupon Rate = Prevailing NSC Interest Rate + 0.35% (35 basis points)

Reset Dates and Current Standing

The Ministry of Finance reviews and sets interest rates for small savings schemes on a quarterly basis. However, the coupon on RBI Floating Rate Bonds is reset only semi-annually on:

  • 1st January (reflecting the NSC rate declared for Q4 of the fiscal year)
  • 1st July (reflecting the NSC rate declared for Q2 of the fiscal year)

With the benchmark NSC rate standing steady at 7.70% p.a., the addition of the 35 bps markup establishes the current payout rate on RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds at 8.05% per annum.

3. Key Features & Scheme Specifications

Before committing capital to FRSB, investors must evaluate its structural parameters:

Investment Limits

Minimum investment is ₹1,000 (and in multiples of ₹1,000 thereafter). Crucially, there is no maximum upper limit. High-net-worth individuals can deploy crores of rupees with 100% sovereign protection.

Tenure

The standard maturity tenure is 7 years from the date of issue. Capital is returned in full upon maturity to the investor's designated bank account.

Interest Payment Frequency

Payouts occur strictly twice a year on 1 January and 1 July. There is no cumulative or reinvestment option; compounding within the bond is not supported.

Transferability & Collateral

The bonds are non-transferable (except by succession to a nominee or legal heir). They cannot be traded on stock exchanges or pledged as collateral for bank loans.

4. Eligibility Criteria: Who Can and Cannot Invest

The Government of India has prescribed clear residency boundaries governing who can subscribe to Floating Rate Savings Bonds.

✅ Who CAN Invest

  • Individual Indian Residents: In sole capacity or joint holding mode (Either or Survivor, Jointly, Former or Survivor).
  • Minors: Subscriptions can be made by a natural parent (father or mother) or a court-appointed legal guardian on behalf of an Indian minor child.
  • Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs): HUFs acting through their Karta are permitted to invest.

❌ Who CANNOT Invest

  • Non-Resident Indians (NRIs): NRIs and Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs) are strictly prohibited from subscribing. If an existing investor becomes an NRI after purchasing the bond, they may hold it until maturity on a non-repatriation basis.
  • Companies & Corporates: Private limited, public limited companies, and partnerships are ineligible.
  • Trusts & Institutions: Charitable trusts, mutual funds, and foreign institutional investors cannot participate.

5. Lock-in Period & Graded Premature Exit Rules for Seniors

A frequent deterrent for potential subscribers is the rigid 7-year lock-in period. For general investors below the age of 60, there is zero early exit permitted under normal circumstances. Capital remains locked for the entire seven years.

Special Windows for Senior Citizens

Recognizing the liquidity and healthcare requirements of older citizens, the RBI framework permits graded early encashment exclusively based on the subscriber’s age as of the date of application for redemption:

Age Category Minimum Holding Period Required Earliest Exit Permitted Penalty on Premature Withdrawal
Below 60 years 7 Years (Full Lock-in) At maturity only Not applicable (no exit allowed)
60 to 70 years 6 Years After completing 6 years Deduction of 50% of interest due for the last 6 months
70 to 80 years 5 Years After completing 5 years Deduction of 50% of interest due for the last 6 months
80 years and above 4 Years After completing 4 years Deduction of 50% of interest due for the last 6 months

Crucial Joint Account Rule: In case of joint accounts where one holder is a senior citizen, the early redemption eligibility is determined by the age of the first applicant. If the first holder meets the senior citizen age bracket, the account qualifies for the early exit privilege.

6. Income Tax Treatment & Section 193 TDS Rules

Taxation is a paramount consideration when evaluating RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds, especially for individuals in the upper income tax brackets.

No Section 80C Deduction on Investment

Unlike Public Provident Fund (PPF), National Pension System (NPS), or Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), the principal amount invested in FRSB does not qualify for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

Taxation of Interest Income

The semi-annual interest earned is treated as ordinary income and classified under “Income from Other Sources”. It is added to the subscriber’s total income and taxed at their marginal income tax slab rate under either the Old Tax Regime or the default New Tax Regime.

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) under Section 193

  • Threshold: Under Section 193 of the Income-tax Act, TDS is mandatory at the rate of 10% if the aggregate interest credited during a financial year exceeds ₹10,000.
  • PAN Requirement: A valid PAN must be linked to the Bond Ledger Account. If a valid PAN is not furnished, TDS is levied at the penal rate of 20% under Section 206AA.
  • Exemption Forms: Investors whose total estimated income for the fiscal year is below the basic tax exemption limit can avoid TDS by submitting:
    • Form 15G: For resident individuals below 60 years and HUFs.
    • Form 15H: For resident senior citizens (aged 60 and above).

8. How to Purchase FRSB: RBI Retail Direct & Bank Branches

Investing in RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds is completely digitized and accessible through multiple convenient channels:

Channel 1: RBI Retail Direct Portal (Recommended)

  1. Visit the official RBI Retail Direct portal (rbiretaildirect.org.in).
  2. Open a Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) Account using your PAN, Aadhaar, and bank account details via video KYC.
  3. Navigate to the Floating Rate Savings Bonds (FRSB) subscription window.
  4. Enter the subscription amount (minimum ₹1,000) and complete payment using UPI, Net Banking, or ASBA.
  5. Bonds are credited electronically to your Bond Ledger Account, with half-yearly interest deposited directly into your linked bank account.

Channel 2: Authorized Commercial Banks

Investors can subscribe physically or via net banking through authorized receiving banks, including:

  • Public Sector Banks: State Bank of India (SBI), Bank of Baroda, Punjab National Bank, Canara Bank, and Union Bank of India.
  • Private Commercial Banks: HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, and IDBI Bank.

Complete Form A (Application for FRSB), attach PAN, proof of address, and a cancelled cheque. A Certificate of Holding is generated and provided to the subscriber.

9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the current interest rate on RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds in 2026?

The current rate of interest on RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds (FRSB) 2020 is 8.05% per annum. This rate is derived from the benchmark National Savings Certificate (NSC) rate of 7.70% plus a fixed markup of 0.35% (35 bps). The rate is reviewed and reset semi-annually on 1 January and 1 July.

Can I choose a cumulative option to compound my interest until the 7-year maturity?

No. FRSB 2020 is exclusively a non-cumulative bond. Interest is disbursed semi-annually on 1st January and 1st July directly into the subscriber’s registered bank account. If you wish to compound your returns, you must manually reinvest the half-yearly interest payouts into recurring deposits, mutual funds, or other instruments.

What happens if the NSC interest rate declines in the future?

Because the bond has a floating coupon, any reduction in the government’s declared NSC interest rate will result in a corresponding reduction in your semi-annual payout at the next reset date (1st January or 1st July). However, your coupon will always remain exactly 0.35% above the prevailing NSC rate.

Can an investor take a loan or overdraft against RBI Floating Rate Bonds?

No. The Government of India guidelines strictly specify that RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds cannot be pledged as collateral, margin, or security to obtain loans or overdrafts from commercial banks, financial institutions, or NBFCs.

Can I appoint a nominee for my RBI Floating Rate Bonds?

Yes. A sole holder or joint holders can nominate one or more individuals to receive the bond proceeds in the event of death. Nominations can be registered at the time of subscription or updated later by submitting Form B to the issuing bank or through the RBI Retail Direct portal.

Is the ₹5 Lakh DICGC insurance limit applicable to RBI Bonds?

No. DICGC insurance applies only to bank deposits. RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds are direct sovereign debt issued by the Central Government. The entire invested capital carries a full sovereign guarantee, making it theoretically risk-free regardless of whether you invest ₹10 Lakhs, ₹1 Crore, or more.
