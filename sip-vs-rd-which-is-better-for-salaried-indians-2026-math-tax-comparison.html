SIP vs RD: Which Is Better for Salaried Indians? (2026 Math &amp; Tax Comparison)

Key Takeaways

  • RD gives 6.5–7.5% guaranteed returns but the interest is 100% taxable at your income slab rate
  • SIP in equity (held 1+ year) is taxed at just 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh annually
  • At ₹10,000/month over 10 years, SIP generates roughly ₹2.32L more than RD after tax
  • RD is the right choice when your goal is under 3 years away and you cannot afford any loss
  • SIP is the right choice for 7+ year goals — retirement, child education, first home down payment
  • The best strategy is not SIP or RD — it's both, for different goals simultaneously

When RD Wins: The 3 Situations Where It's the Right Choice

  1. Your goal is less than 3 years away — wedding next year, down payment in 18 months, international trip in 2 years. For these, you cannot afford the risk of markets falling 25% right before you need the money. RD's guarantee is genuinely valuable here.
  2. You have zero risk tolerance — if seeing your balance drop by 15–20% would cause you to stop investing entirely, an RD is a better long-term outcome than an equity SIP you abandon halfway through.
  3. You need predictable cash flow — RDs pay out exactly at maturity, on a date you know. Some people need to know exactly how much they'll have and when, particularly for business planning or EMI preparation. RD is the right tool for that predictability.

When SIP Wins: The 3 Situations Where It's Clearly Better

  1. Your goal is 7+ years away — retirement 20 years out, child education in 15 years, first home purchase in a decade. At these timescales, the market volatility that makes short-term SIP scary is exactly what generates compounding returns over the long run.
  2. You're in the 20% or 30% tax bracket — the tax efficiency of LTCG at 12.5% vs RD interest at your slab rate matters more the higher your income. At 30% tax, an RD's effective yield barely beats inflation.
  3. You want to increase your investment over time — SIPs can be stepped up easily (5–10% annually). RDs are fixed. If your salary grows 8% every year, a Step-Up SIP keeps pace with your income, while an RD forces you to open a new deposit.

The Smartest Strategy: Use Both Simultaneously

Frequently Asked Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SIP better than RD for a 5-year goal?

For a strict 5-year goal where you cannot afford losses, RD is safer. However, if you have flexibility and the goal can stretch to 7 years without serious consequences, SIP historically outperforms significantly. The 5-year window sits in an uncomfortable middle ground — markets can deliver either strong gains or temporary losses in exactly that period.

What happens if I stop my SIP early?

Unlike an RD, there's no penalty for stopping a SIP. Your existing units remain invested and continue earning returns. You can stop, pause, or reduce a SIP at any time. The only cost of stopping early is missing out on future compounding — not a financial penalty.

Can I do both SIP and RD at the same time?

Absolutely — and most financial planners recommend doing exactly this. Use an RD for short-term goals (emergency fund top-up, vacation, 2-year goal) and a SIP for long-term wealth building. They serve different purposes and work well together.

What is the minimum SIP amount compared to RD?

Most bank RDs start at ₹100–500/month. Most mutual fund SIPs start at ₹500/month. Some platforms and funds allow ₹100/month SIPs. In terms of accessibility, both are comparable for small amounts.
