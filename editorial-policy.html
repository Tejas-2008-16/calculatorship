Editorial Integrity & Transparency

Editorial & Verification Policy

Lead Editor: Tejas Waghmare • Last verified: November 2026 • Applicable FY: 2026-27 (AY 2027-28)

1. Educational Purpose & Independence

Calculatorship stays strictly an educational personal finance project for Indian salaried workers, students, and retail investors trying to decode compounding math, home loan interest schedules, income tax comparisons, and post office savings returns without wading through sales pitches. Zero paid articles get published. Sponsored bank reviews, loan referral payouts, and mutual fund commission links stay off this domain completely. Every calculator stays independent.

2. Primary Source Hierarchy

Indian finance blogs recycle stale ceilings daily. Rules get checked three ways before going live:

Tier 1: Official Statutory & Regulator Portals (Mandatory)

Primary notifications anchor every cutoff: the Income Tax e-Filing portal for tax slabs, Reserve Bank of India circulars for repo and lending rules, SEBI guidelines for mutual fund categories, India Post official schemes for small-savings notifications, and the NPS Trust website for pension exits.

Tier 2: Institutional Market Infrastructure Feeds

AMFI fund disclosures, NSE and BSE index specs, and EPFO annual interest bulletins fill in operational details alongside statutory gazettes.

Tier 3: News Headlines & Blogs (Rejected as Proof)

News blogs never qualify. Even when major headlines break a tax rule change, the raw CBDT circular or Finance Act section gets inspected first before any number shifts.

3. Calculator Formula Checks & Edge Testing

Scripts face messy edge cases before launch:

  • Boundary Checks: Formulas test extreme cases from ₹0 principal and ₹1,50,000 PPF caps or ₹2,50,000 EPF tax-free ceilings up to 50-year compounding spans and fractional interest rates. Edge inputs fail loudly.
  • Reconciling Official Tax & Loan Tables: Reducing-balance EMI output matches standard banking amortization math, while income tax models are compared line by line against CBDT calculator results under Old and New Regimes across salaries of ₹7 lakh, ₹10 lakh, ₹15 lakh, ₹25 lakh, and surcharge slabs past ₹50 lakh.
  • Browser-Only Speed: Execution stays completely local. Light vanilla scripts keep mobile browsers snappy without database calls.

4. Authorship & Plain Language Style

Accountability starts with open names:

  • Author Attribution: Calculators and articles are written and maintained by Tejas Waghmare (Author Profile).
  • Regulatory Cross-Verification: Specialized statutory topics such as corporate gratuity formulas, Section 54F reinvestment windows, and NRI taxation limits note the exact governing section and verified Fiscal Year visibly on the page.
  • Conversational Tone: Straight English beats jargon. Lock-in penalties, hidden tax traps, and scenarios where a product disappoints get highlighted upfront.

5. Update Calendar & Public Fixes

Rates change every quarter. Three triggers force immediate page audits:

  • Quarterly Rate Audits: April, July, October, and January bring fresh checks on Finance Ministry circulars for PPF, SCSS, SSY, NSC, and KVP yields.
  • Union Budget Overhauls: Right after Parliament passes each Finance Bill, tax slabs, standard deductions, Section 87A rebates, and capital gains exemptions get rebuilt across guides and calculators.
  • Logged Fixes: Verified bugs land promptly inside the Corrections Log, and readers report issues directly by emailing Tejas.

6. Ad Placement Independence

Google AdSense slots stay cleanly isolated from formulas and prose. Advertisers hold zero say over outputs.
