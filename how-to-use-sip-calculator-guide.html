How to Use a SIP Calculator: A Complete Beginner's Guide (2026)

You've heard that SIPs build wealth. You open a SIP calculator. You see three boxes asking for a monthly amount, an expected return, and a time period. And then… you freeze. What number goes where? What return rate is realistic? Is 20 years too long? This guide answers every one of those questions.

July 30, 2026 7 min read By tejascopywriter
← Back to Articles

SIPs — Systematic Investment Plans — are the most popular way for Indian salaried professionals to invest in mutual funds. Every month, a fixed amount gets auto-debited from your account and invested. Simple enough. But the moment most beginners try to plan their SIP using a calculator, they hit a wall. The three input boxes feel deceptively simple, and the results feel deceptively large. Let's fix that — one step at a time.

What Does a SIP Calculator Actually Do?

A SIP calculator uses a mathematical formula called the Future Value of Annuity to project how much your regular investments will grow over time. It assumes you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals (monthly), at a constant rate of return, for a defined number of years.

The formula under the hood looks like this:

FV = P × [((1 + r)ⁿ – 1) / r] × (1 + r)

Where:
FV = Future Value (your final corpus)
P = Monthly SIP amount
r = Monthly return rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12)
n = Total number of months

You do not need to memorize this formula. The calculator runs it instantly when you hit calculate. What matters is understanding what inputs to feed it — and why those inputs have a massive impact on the output you see.

💡 Key Insight: The SIP calculator is a planning tool, not a crystal ball. It tells you what could happen if returns stayed constant — which they never do. Use it to set direction and build confidence, not to set hard expectations.

Step 1 — Enter Your Monthly Investment Amount

This is the easiest field to fill — but most people overthink it. Start by asking yourself: what can you invest every month without disrupting your rent, groceries, or EMI? A common rule of thumb is to invest at least 20% of your take-home salary.

On a ₹30,000 take-home salary, that is ₹6,000/month. On ₹50,000, that is ₹10,000/month. If you are just starting out and feel uncertain, even ₹3,000/month is a perfectly legitimate beginning — you can increase it every year using a Step-Up SIP.

Here is a quick sense-check of how starting amounts play out over 15 years at 12% CAGR:

  • ₹3,000/month → Total invested: ₹5.4L → Estimated corpus: ~₹15.1L
  • ₹5,000/month → Total invested: ₹9L → Estimated corpus: ~₹25.2L
  • ₹10,000/month → Total invested: ₹18L → Estimated corpus: ~₹50.4L
✓ Pro Tip: Do not wait until you can afford to invest a "big" amount. ₹3,000/month started today will outperform ₹10,000/month started 3 years from now. Compounding rewards early starters, not large starters.

Step 2 — Set a Realistic Expected Annual Return

This field trips up most beginners. The expected return is expressed as a CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate — and it represents the average yearly growth rate you expect your fund to deliver. The key word is "average": real market returns swing wildly year to year, but CAGR smooths that into one representative number.

Here are historically grounded ranges you should use, based on AMFI and NSE long-run data:

  • Equity Mutual Funds (Nifty 50 / Large-Cap Index): 10–12% per annum (Nifty 50 TRI has delivered ~12.5% CAGR over rolling 20-year periods)
  • Flexi-Cap / Active Large-Cap Funds: 11–14% per annum (top performers in 5Y data from AMFI)
  • Hybrid / Balanced Advantage Funds: 8–10% per annum
  • Debt Funds / Short Duration: 6–7% per annum
⚠️ Watch Out: Never enter 18%, 20%, or higher into a SIP calculator unless you enjoy being disappointed. Even the top-performing funds rarely sustain those returns across a full decade. Use 10–12% for equity funds and call it a day.

Remember: these are historical return ranges. Past performance does not guarantee future results. That said, using realistic CAGR ranges based on long-term index data is far better than guessing.

Step 3 — Choose Your Investment Duration

This is the most powerful input in the entire calculator — and the one most people underestimate. Duration is where compounding does its heaviest lifting. Every additional year you stay invested has an outsized effect on your final corpus.

Here is a real example. Priya and Rahul both invest ₹5,000/month at 12% CAGR:

  • Priya (invests for 20 years): Total invested = ₹12L → Final corpus ≈ ₹49.9L
  • Rahul (invests for 30 years): Total invested = ₹18L → Final corpus ≈ ₹1.76 Crore

Rahul invested only ₹6L more than Priya — but his final corpus is ₹1.26 Crore larger. Those last 10 years are where compounding goes exponential. The lesson: start early, stay long. Every 5 extra years roughly doubles your final wealth at 12% CAGR.

💡 Key Insight: The minimum recommended horizon for an equity SIP is 7 years. For retirement wealth-building, 15–25 years is ideal. Use 10 years as your absolute floor when entering duration into the calculator.

Reading Your Results — What the Numbers Mean

After you click Calculate, our SIP Calculator shows you three numbers. Here is exactly what each one means:

  • Total Amount Invested: Simply Monthly SIP × Number of Months. This is real money that left your bank account.
  • Estimated Returns: The wealth created purely by market growth — the interest earned on your investment. This is the "magic" part.
  • Total Wealth / Maturity Value: Total Invested + Estimated Returns. This is your projected final corpus.

The gap between "Total Invested" and "Total Wealth" is your investment gain — and in equity SIPs held for 15+ years, this gap is often 3x to 6x the invested amount. That ratio is compounding doing its job.

⚠️ Watch Out: These projections assume a constant return rate every single year. Real markets do not work that way — they swing up 35% one year and down 20% the next. Your actual corpus at maturity will likely be different. Plan conservatively and review annually.

Common Mistakes When Using a SIP Calculator

These mistakes are extremely common, especially among first-time investors:

  1. Using 18–20% expected returns: This leads to dangerously optimistic projections. Stick to 10–12% for equity funds.
  2. Ignoring inflation: ₹1 crore in 2026 buys far more than ₹1 crore will in 2046. Your real return after 6% average inflation is roughly 6–7% for an equity fund with 12% nominal CAGR.
  3. Using Regular Plan NAV as benchmark: Regular plans charge 0.5–1% extra in commission. Always model returns from Direct Plans for accuracy.
  4. Forgetting exit tax: Equity fund LTCG (held > 12 months) is taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year. Your real take-home at redemption will be slightly lower.
  5. Treating projections as a guarantee: The calculator shows you a mathematically possible outcome, not a promised one. Markets can — and do — underperform for extended periods.

What to Do After Running the Calculator

You've run the numbers, you're excited, and your target corpus looks motivating. Here is the exact sequence of next steps:

  1. Open a mutual fund account: Use Zerodha Coin, Groww, or MF Central (the official AMFI-backed platform). KYC takes about 10 minutes with Aadhaar + PAN.
  2. Pick a Direct Plan index fund: A Nifty 50 Index Fund (Parag Parikh, UTI, or HDFC) or Nifty Next 50 is an excellent starting point. Low expense ratio, market-matching returns, zero fund manager risk.
  3. Set your SIP date: The 1st, 5th, or 10th of the month works best — ideally 2–3 days after your salary credit date so your account always has funds for the debit.
  4. Enable auto-pay: Set up NACH mandate on your bank account so the SIP runs automatically. You should never have to manually transfer money each month.
  5. Use the Goal Planner: Once your SIP is running, visit our Goal Planner Calculator to reverse-engineer exactly how much you need to invest per month to hit a specific target — like a ₹50L down payment or ₹2 Crore retirement fund.
✓ Pro Tip: Increase your SIP by 10% every April when salary hikes typically land. A ₹5,000 SIP that grows by 10% annually will reach the same corpus as a static ₹12,000 SIP — with less initial strain on your budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a SIP calculator accurate?

A SIP calculator gives projections based on a fixed assumed return rate. Actual mutual fund returns vary because markets fluctuate. Treat calculator outputs as planning estimates, not guaranteed figures. Review your plan every 6–12 months and adjust if needed.

What return rate should I use in a SIP calculator?

For equity mutual funds, 10–12% per annum is a conservative, realistic long-term estimate based on Nifty 50 TRI historical data. For hybrid funds, use 8–10%. Avoid using anything above 14%, as it creates dangerously optimistic projections that rarely materialize over full market cycles.

Can I use a SIP calculator for lumpsum investments?

No. A SIP calculator uses the Future Value of Annuity formula and assumes equal monthly payments. For a one-time investment, use our dedicated Lumpsum Calculator instead, which uses the correct compound interest formula.

Does the SIP calculator account for inflation?

Standard SIP calculators show nominal (pre-inflation) returns. To get inflation-adjusted real returns, subtract India's average inflation rate (approximately 5–6%) from your expected return. A 12% nominal return translates to roughly 6–7% in real purchasing power terms.

What is the minimum SIP amount I can invest?

Most fund houses in India allow SIPs starting at ₹100 to ₹500 per month. Even a ₹500/month SIP in an index fund, started at age 22, can grow into a meaningful corpus by retirement age through the power of compounding and consistent contributions.
