Mahila Samman Savings Certificate (MSSC): 7.5% Rate, Rules & Math

Introduced by the Ministry of Finance to accelerate financial inclusion and wealth empowerment for women, the Mahila Samman Savings Certificate (MSSC) provides a high-yield, sovereign-backed 2-year deposit option. Delivering an attractive 7.5% quarterly compounded return, a ₹2 Lakh investment ceiling, and 40% partial liquidity after 1 year, discover how MSSC compares against standard bank fixed deposits.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • 7.5% Sovereign Guarantee: Generates a fixed 7.5% annual return with quarterly compounding, delivering an effective annual yield of ~7.71%.
  • Compact 2-Year Tenure: Ideal for short-term capital preservation without committing to the 5-year lock-in of NSC or the 15-year lock-in of PPF.
  • ₹2 Lakh Statutory Cap: A woman or guardian of a girl child can deposit up to a maximum aggregate of ₹2,00,000 across accounts.
  • 40% Mid-Term Liquidity: After 12 months, up to 40% of the account balance can be withdrawn prematurely for emergency liquidity.
  • Zero TDS Mandate: CBDT circulars confirm no TDS is withheld on MSSC interest payouts, though interest remains taxable at slab rates under Other Sources.

1. What is the Mahila Samman Savings Certificate (MSSC)?

Announced in the Union Budget and formalized under the Mahila Samman Savings Certificate Scheme Rules, 2023 by the Ministry of Finance, MSSC is a specialized small savings instrument designed specifically for women and girl children across India.

Available through all computerized Department of Posts (India Post) branches and authorized scheduled commercial banks (including State Bank of India, Bank of Baroda, Punjab National Bank, Canara Bank, and Union Bank of India), MSSC combines sovereign safety with a short 2-year horizon and higher interest rates than prevailing bank fixed deposits of comparable tenures.

2. 7.5% Quarterly Compounding Math: ₹2 Lakh Deposit Returns

MSSC offers an interest rate of 7.5% per annum. Crucially, interest is calculated on a quarterly compounding basis, which enhances the effective annual yield over simple annual compounding.

The standard compounding formula applicable is:

A = P × (1 + r / 400)^(4 × n)

Where P is principal, r is 7.5%, and n is 2 years (8 compounding quarters). For the maximum eligible deposit of ₹2,00,000, let us track the quarterly balance progression:

Quarter (Q) Opening Principal Quarterly Interest Earned Closing Balance
Q1 (End of Month 3) ₹2,00,000.00 ₹3,750.00 ₹2,03,750.00
Q2 (End of Month 6) ₹2,03,750.00 ₹3,820.31 ₹2,07,570.31
Q3 (End of Month 9) ₹2,07,570.31 ₹3,891.94 ₹2,11,462.25
Q4 (End of Year 1) ₹2,11,462.25 ₹3,964.92 ₹2,15,427.17
Q5 (End of Month 15) ₹2,15,427.17 ₹4,039.26 ₹2,19,466.43
Q6 (End of Month 18) ₹2,19,466.43 ₹4,115.00 ₹2,23,581.43
Q7 (End of Month 21) ₹2,23,581.43 ₹4,192.15 ₹2,27,773.58

At the end of 24 months, a ₹2,00,000 deposit yields a guaranteed payout of ₹2,32,044, generating a total interest gain of ₹32,044.

3. Eligibility Criteria & Account Opening Mechanics

The operational guidelines published by India Post outline the following eligibility parameters:

  • Exclusively for Females: Accounts can only be opened by a woman for herself, or by a natural guardian (father or mother) or legal guardian on behalf of a minor girl. There is no minimum age bar — a newborn baby girl is eligible.
  • Mandatory Documents: Standard KYC documents (Aadhaar Card, PAN card, recent passport-size photographs, and birth certificate if opening for a minor).
  • Single Holding Only: Joint accounts are not permitted under the MSSC scheme; every account is issued in the individual name of the beneficiary.

4. Deposit Ceilings, Multiple Accounts & 3-Month Gap Rule

The Ministry of Finance has instituted strict anti-abuse rules regarding deposit limits:

  • Minimum Deposit: ₹1,000 (and in multiples of ₹100).
  • Maximum Aggregate Ceiling: ₹2,00,000 per individual beneficiary.
  • Multiple Accounts Permitted: An investor is allowed to open multiple MSSC accounts (for instance, depositing ₹50,000 initially and ₹1,50,000 later). However, two conditions must be satisfied:
    1. The sum total of all deposits across all post offices and bank branches cannot exceed ₹2 Lakh.
    2. There must be a mandatory waiting interval of at least 3 months between opening the first account and opening the second account.

5. The 40% Partial Withdrawal Facility After 1 Year

A distinctive advantage of MSSC over traditional locked fixed deposits is its structured liquidity relief. Under Rule 7 of the scheme:

Once one year (12 months) has elapsed from the date of account opening, the account holder is entitled to execute a single partial withdrawal of up to 40% of the eligible balance standing in the account.

Example: If you deposited ₹2,00,000, your balance at the end of Year 1 stands at ₹2,15,427. You can withdraw up to 40% of this figure (approximately ₹86,170). The remaining 60% balance continues to compound at 7.5% quarterly until maturity at Month 24.

6. Premature Closure Rules & Interest Penalty Clause

The scheme allows premature closure under specific statutory provisions:

  • No-Penalty Grounds (Demise or Compassionate Care): Premature closure is permitted with full 7.5% interest if the account holder passes away, or on extreme medical grounds (life-threatening illness of the account holder or death of guardian) upon furnishing documentary proof to the satisfaction of the Department of Posts.
  • Voluntary Early Closure (After 6 Months): If the account holder wishes to exit voluntarily without medical emergency, premature closure is permitted only after completing a minimum of 6 months from the date of deposit. In such cases, the applicable interest rate is penalized by 2.0%, meaning interest will be calculated at 5.5% per annum instead of 7.5%.

7. Taxation Framework & CBDT Clarification on Zero TDS

Investors must clearly understand the tax implications of MSSC:

  • No Section 80C Deduction: Unlike PPF, SSY, or 5-year tax-saver bank FDs, investments into MSSC do not qualify for any deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act.
  • Taxable Interest: The interest earned (e.g. ₹32,044 on a ₹2L deposit) is treated as ordinary unearned income and is taxed according to the recipient's marginal income tax slab under the head "Income from Other Sources".
  • CBDT Exemption on TDS: Through Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) Notification No. 27/2023, the government amended Sub-rule (2) of Rule 31A and clarified that no TDS under Section 194A will be deducted on interest disbursed under the Mahila Samman Savings Certificate Scheme. You receive your gross maturity proceeds without any tax withholding, simplifying cash flow for non-taxable earners.

9. Strategic Portfolio Role: Who Should Invest in MSSC?

MSSC serves three distinct personal finance use cases:

  • Women in Low or Nil Tax Slabs: For housewives, students, or retired women whose total annual taxable income is below ₹7 Lakh / ₹12 Lakh (rebatable under Section 87A), MSSC delivers an effective, tax-free 7.5% guaranteed yield without the annoyance of bank TDS deductions.
  • Short-Term Goal Funding: Funding known expenses 24 months away (such as college admission fees, planned laptop purchase, or wedding jewellery savings) where equity market volatility is unacceptable.
  • Emergency Capital Parking: Due to the 40% partial withdrawal permission after 1 year, MSSC offers a disciplined parking slot for conservative emergency funds.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Who is eligible to invest in Mahila Samman Savings Certificate (MSSC)?

Any female citizen of India can open an MSSC account. It can be opened by an adult woman in her own name, or by a natural or legal guardian on behalf of a minor girl child of any age. Male individuals, NRIs, and HUFs are strictly ineligible.

What is the maximum deposit limit under the MSSC scheme?

The minimum deposit is ₹1,000, and the maximum aggregate ceiling is ₹2,00,000 per woman across all accounts. An individual can open multiple accounts, but a mandatory minimum gap of 3 months must be maintained between opening consecutive accounts, and the combined deposit across all accounts cannot exceed ₹2 Lakh.

How is interest calculated and compounded in MSSC?

MSSC offers a fixed interest rate of 7.5% per annum. Unlike annual compounding schemes, MSSC interest is compounded quarterly and credited to the account, payable upon maturity at the end of the 2-year tenure. An investment of ₹2,00,000 grows to approximately ₹2,32,044 at maturity.

Is there any partial withdrawal facility in MSSC?

Yes. After completing one year from the account opening date, the account holder is permitted a single partial withdrawal of up to 40% of the eligible balance by submitting Form-3 at the post office or bank branch.

How is interest from Mahila Samman Savings Certificate taxed?

Interest earned on MSSC is fully taxable under 'Income from Other Sources' according to the account holder's applicable marginal income tax slab. The scheme does not qualify for Section 80C deductions. However, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) has clarified via notification that zero TDS is deducted on MSSC interest under Section 194A.
