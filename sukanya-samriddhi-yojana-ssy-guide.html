Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) Guide 2026: 8.2% Interest, Rules & ₹70L+ Math

The Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) offers 8.2% government-backed interest with completely tax-free maturity under EEE status. Here is the exact 21-year math, deposit limits, and how to pair SSY with an equity SIP to beat college inflation.

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 31 August 2026  •  Last reviewed: 23 November 2026  •  8.20% Sovereign Interest, 15-Yr Deposit Schedule & Section 10(11A) Tax Exemption

Educational girl child savings roadmap only. Not certified financial planning advisory. Small savings rules governed by Ministry of Finance guidelines. Read our full disclaimer.

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) is a Government of India small savings scheme for resident girl children aged below 10, offering a sovereign 8.20% interest rate compounded annually under full EEE tax exemption. Requiring deposits for 15 years with maturity at 21 years (or marriage after 18), an annual deposit of ₹1,50,000 accumulates ₹44,75,989 by Year 15 and compounds untouched to a final tax-free corpus of ₹71,82,119 at Year 21 under Section 10(11A).

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) offers 8.2% annual interest (Q4 FY 2023 to 24 & FY 2024 to 26), compounded annually , the highest sovereign-Fixed, risk-free interest rate among all small savings schemes in India.
  • SSY enjoys complete EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax status: deposits qualify for Section 80C deduction up to ₹1.5 Lakh/year, annual compounding interest is 100% tax-free, and maturity proceeds are completely tax-exempt.
  • Eligible only for a girl child below 10 years of age (maximum 2 accounts per family; exceptions for twins/triplets). Deposits must be made for 15 years from account opening date.
  • The account matures after 21 years from account opening, or upon marriage of the girl child after she turns 18. Partial withdrawal of up to 50% is allowed for higher education after she turns 18 or completes Class 10.
  • SSY alone is insufficient for long-term college funding due to double-digit education inflation (10% to 12%). Optimal allocation: pair SSY (fixed-income anchor) with a diversified Equity Mutual Fund SIP (growth engine).

If you are a parent to a daughter under the age of 10 in India, the Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) is mathematically the single best fixed-income instrument available to you. Backed directly by the Government of India, offering an 8.2% sovereign yield, and carrying ironclad EEE tax exemption, it outclasses bank fixed deposits, National Savings Certificates (NSC), and Public Provident Fund (PPF) on sheer interest rate.

However, treating SSY as the entirety of your daughter's future education and marriage plan is a perilous mistake. While 8.2% beats general CPI inflation (5% to 6%), it trails domestic higher education inflation (10% to 12%) and international college costs. To build true generational financial security for your daughter, you must understand both the extraordinary strengths of SSY and its mathematical limitations.

SSY Wealth Projection: Maximizing the ₹1.5 Lakh Annual Limit

If you invest the statutory maximum of ₹1,50,000 every financial year for 15 years at an assumed average interest rate of 8.2% per annum, observe how compounding accelerates between year 15 and maturity at year 21 (during which no fresh deposits are required, but the entire balance earns interest):

Milestone Total Deposited Accumulated Interest Account Balance
Year 5 ₹7,50,000 ₹1,77,294 ₹9,27,294
Year 10 ₹15,00,000 ₹8,01,280 ₹23,01,280
Year 15 (Deposits End) ₹22,50,000 ₹21,11,440 ₹43,61,440

By contributing ₹22.5 Lakh spread across 15 years, you receive roughly ₹69.3 Lakh upon maturity. Interest accounts for over 67% of the total maturity payout , completely free of income tax.

The Ideal Strategy: The 50:50 Barbell Portfolio

Do not pit SSY against Equity SIPs. Higher education costs in premier engineering, medical, management, or foreign universities can easily touch ₹40 to 80 Lakh fifteen years from now, relying exclusively on fixed debt poses an underfunding risk.

  • 50% into SSY: Provides Fixed, sovereign capital protection and tax-free certainty that cannot be derailed by a sudden stock market crash when fees are due.
  • 50% into Flexi-Cap / Nifty 50 Index Mutual Funds: Harnesses corporate India's compounding to deliver inflation-beating alpha, ensuring you accumulate enough real purchasing power.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current interest rate for Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) in 2026? +

The Government of India offers a sovereign interest rate of 8.20% per annum on Sukanya Samriddhi accounts, compounded annually and reset quarterly by the Ministry of Finance. It remains one of the highest guaranteed fixed-income returns in India.

What is the maximum and minimum deposit limit in an SSY account per financial year? +

The minimum annual deposit is ₹250, while the maximum allowable deposit is ₹1,50,000 per financial year. Deposits can be made in any number of installments in multiples of ₹50.

How long must I deposit money in SSY, and when does the account mature? +

Deposits are required for 15 years from the date of account opening. The account matures upon completion of 21 years from opening, or at the time of the girl's marriage after attaining 18 years. During years 16 to 21, the accumulated corpus earns full compound interest without requiring fresh deposits.

What is the tax status of Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana? +

SSY enjoys complete Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) tax status. Deposits qualify for deduction up to ₹1,50,000 under Section 80C (Old Tax Regime), while annual interest accruals and final maturity proceeds are 100% tax-free under Section 10(11A) under both Old and New Tax Regimes.

Can I open SSY accounts for three daughters? +

Generally, SSY accounts can only be opened for a maximum of two girl children in a family. However, an exception is granted for a third account if twin or triplet girls are born in the second birth, provided medical birth certificates are submitted.
