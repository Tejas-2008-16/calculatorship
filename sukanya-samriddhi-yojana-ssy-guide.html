Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) offers 8.2% annual interest (Q4 FY 2023–24 & FY 2024–26), compounded annually — the highest sovereign-guaranteed, risk-free interest rate among all small savings schemes in India.
  • SSY enjoys complete EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax status: deposits qualify for Section 80C deduction up to ₹1.5 Lakh/year, annual compounding interest is 100% tax-free, and maturity proceeds are completely tax-exempt.
  • Eligible only for a girl child below 10 years of age (maximum 2 accounts per family; exceptions for twins/triplets). Deposits must be made for 15 years from account opening date.
  • The account matures after 21 years from account opening, or upon marriage of the girl child after she turns 18. Partial withdrawal of up to 50% is allowed for higher education after she turns 18 or completes Class 10.
  • SSY alone is insufficient for long-term college funding due to double-digit education inflation (10%–12%). Optimal allocation: pair SSY (fixed-income anchor) with a diversified Equity Mutual Fund SIP (growth engine).

If you are a parent to a daughter under the age of 10 in India, the Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) is mathematically the single best fixed-income instrument available to you. Backed directly by the Government of India, offering an 8.2% sovereign yield, and carrying ironclad EEE tax exemption, it outclasses bank fixed deposits, National Savings Certificates (NSC), and Public Provident Fund (PPF) on sheer interest rate.

However, treating SSY as the entirety of your daughter's future education and marriage plan is a perilous mistake. While 8.2% beats general CPI inflation (5%–6%), it trails domestic higher education inflation (10%–12%) and international college costs. To build true generational financial security for your daughter, you must understand both the extraordinary strengths of SSY and its mathematical limitations.

Core Eligibility and Rules for SSY in 2026

Accounts per Family Maximum 2 girl children (allowed for 3 if twins/triplets are born)
Deposit Limits Minimum ₹250 per financial year; Maximum ₹1,50,000 per financial year
Deposit Duration 15 years from the date of account opening
Account Maturity 21 years from account opening (or marriage after age 18)
Tax Status EEE (Deduction u/s 80C, Interest Tax-Free, Maturity Tax-Free)

SSY Wealth Projection: Maximizing the ₹1.5 Lakh Annual Limit

If you invest the statutory maximum of ₹1,50,000 every financial year for 15 years at an assumed average interest rate of 8.2% per annum, observe how compounding accelerates between year 15 and maturity at year 21 (during which no fresh deposits are required, but the entire balance earns interest):

Milestone Total Deposited Accumulated Interest Account Balance
Year 5 ₹7,50,000 ₹1,77,294 ₹9,27,294
Year 10 ₹15,00,000 ₹8,01,280 ₹23,01,280
Year 15 (Deposits End) ₹22,50,000 ₹21,11,440 ₹43,61,440
Year 21 (Maturity) ₹22,50,000 ₹46,77,700 ₹69,27,700 (Tax-Free)

By contributing ₹22.5 Lakh spread across 15 years, you receive roughly ₹69.3 Lakh upon maturity. Interest accounts for over 67% of the total maturity payout — completely free of income tax.

The Ideal Strategy: The 50:50 Barbell Portfolio

Do not pit SSY against Equity SIPs — combine them. Because higher education costs in premier engineering, medical, management, or foreign universities can easily touch ₹40–80 Lakh fifteen years from now, relying exclusively on fixed debt poses an underfunding risk.

  • 50% into SSY: Provides guaranteed, sovereign capital protection and tax-free certainty that cannot be derailed by a sudden stock market crash when fees are due.
  • 50% into Flexi-Cap / Nifty 50 Index Mutual Funds: Harnesses corporate India's compounding to deliver inflation-beating alpha, ensuring you accumulate enough real purchasing power.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an NRI open an SSY account for their daughter?

No. The girl child must be an Indian resident at the time of opening the account. If the girl child subsequently acquires NRI status or citizenship of another country, the account stops earning interest from the date of change in residential status and must be closed prematurely.

What happens if I fail to deposit the minimum ₹250 in a year?

The account becomes "defaulted." You can regularize it anytime before maturity by paying a minor penalty fee of ₹50 along with the minimum deposit of ₹250 for each defaulted year. Defaulted accounts still earn the applicable scheme interest rate until maturity.
