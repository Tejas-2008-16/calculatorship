National Pension System (NPS): Tier I vs II, Tax Benefits & Strategy in India

The National Pension System (NPS) is a voluntary, long-term retirement savings scheme regulated by PFRDA. Offering ultra-low fund management charges (under 0.09%), market-linked asset allocation, and exclusive tax deductions, NPS is a cornerstone of Indian retirement planning.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • NPS (National Pension System) is India's lowest-cost retirement investment vehicle, with annual fund management fees capped at an astonishingly low 0.03%–0.09%.
  • Tax Benefits: Under the Old Regime, claim exclusive deductions up to ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B) over and above the ₹1.5L 80C limit. Under BOTH Old and New Regimes, employer contributions up to 10% (14% for central govt) of Basic + DA are tax-exempt under Section 80CCD(2).
  • Maturity Rule at Age 60: Up to 60% of the accumulated corpus can be withdrawn as a completely tax-free lump sum. The remaining 40% MUST be used to purchase an immediate annuity, which yields taxable monthly pension.
  • Asset Allocation Choices: Choose Active Choice (up to 75% equity in Scheme E) or Auto Choice (Lifecycle Funds like LC75, LC50, LC25 that automatically derisk as you age).
  • NPS Tier 2 is an open-ended voluntary account with zero lock-in and instant liquidity, but carries no special tax deductions for private employees.

The National Pension System (NPS), administered by PFRDA, is one of the most powerful — and misunderstood — retirement instruments in India. While critics lament its strict lock-in until age 60 and mandatory 40% annuity purchase, no other product in India offers corporate tax deductions under Section 80CCD(2) under the New Tax Regime combined with ultra-low fund management fees of just 0.09% per year.

Asset Allocation: Active Choice vs. Auto Choice

NPS lets you invest across four broad asset classes managed by top pension fund managers (HDFC, ICICI Pru, SBI, UTI):

  • Scheme E (Equity): Up to 75% in top-tier Indian listed equities. Historically delivered 12%–14% CAGR.
  • Scheme C (Corporate Bonds): High-grade debt and corporate debentures. Yields 7.5%–8.5%.
  • Scheme G (Government Securities): Sovereign central and state bonds. Risk-free, yields 7.0%–7.5%.
  • Scheme A (Alternative Assets): Up to 5% in REITs, InvITs, and AIFs.

If you are under 40 years old, opt for Active Choice with 75% Scheme E + 15% Scheme C + 10% Scheme G to maximize your wealth accumulation phase.

The Maturity Structure at Age 60

When you turn 60, the accumulated corpus is handled as follows:

  1. 60% Tax-Free Lump Sum: You can withdraw up to 60% of the entire accumulated corpus completely tax-free under Section 10(12A).
  2. 40% Mandatory Annuity: At least 40% of the corpus must be deployed to purchase an annuity plan from an IRDAI-regulated life insurance provider (such as LIC, HDFC Life, ICICI Pru). This annuity generates a lifetime monthly pension that is taxed at your income slab rate upon receipt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I withdraw money from NPS before turning 60?

Partial withdrawals up to 25% of your own contributions (excluding employer share and accrued returns) are permitted after 3 years for specific life milestones: higher education or wedding of children, home purchase/construction, or critical medical treatments. If you exit NPS prematurely before age 60, you can withdraw only 20% as a lump sum, and 80% must be locked into an annuity.
