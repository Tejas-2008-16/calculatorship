Income-tax Act 2025 Explained: Old vs New Section Numbers & What "Tax Year" Means

India's six-decade-old direct tax code has been restructured. Here is a clear, plain-language breakdown of the Income-tax Act, 2025, how the new unified "Tax Year" works, and the official section mapping table for every provision you search for.

By Tejas, Calculatorship | Published: 22 Oct 2026 | Last updated: 22 Oct 2026 | 13 min read
Editorial Note: Our other tax guides use the familiar 1961-Act section names (like Section 80C and Section 115BAC), which most employers, payroll software, Form 16 templates, and banking portals still reference during the transition period.

Quick Answer: What Changed & What Stayed

The Income-tax Act, 2025 takes effect on 1 April 2026, replacing the 1961 Act. It condenses 819 sections into 536 and replaces Previous Year / Assessment Year with a unified "Tax Year". Tax slabs and deduction caps remain unchanged. Section 80C maps to Section 123, Section 87A to Section 156, and Section 115BAC to Section 202.

1. Why Was the Income-tax Act 1961 Replaced?

For more than six decades, India's direct tax framework operated under the Income-tax Act, 1961. Over sixty-five annual Union Budgets, hundreds of amendments, provisos, sub-clauses, and judicial precedents were layered onto the statute.

By 2024, the statute book had ballooned to over 819 sections spread across 47 chapters. Simple provisions carried dense provisos running multiple pages. Everyday clauses had evolved awkward alphanumeric designations like Section 80CCD(1B), Section 115BAC, and Section 194-IA.

The central objective of enacting the Income-tax Act, 2025 was legislative decluttering: pruning redundant sections, organizing scattered provisions into clear tabular formats, eliminating archaic legal terminology, and creating a modern direct tax code that an educated citizen can read without legal translation.

2. Effective Date & The Transition Timeline (1 April 2026)

The Income-tax Act, 2025 was notified in the Government Gazette with a designated commencement date of 1 April 2026. This milestone marks a clear legislative division between legacy tax periods and the new code.

To ensure smooth administrative continuity, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) designed a transitional schedule:

  • Up to 31 March 2026: All income earned and financial transactions conducted fall under the Income-tax Act, 1961.
  • 1 April 2026 Onward: All direct taxation, advance tax obligations, and statutory documentation operate under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

3. What "Tax Year" Means and How It Replaces PY & AY

One of the biggest sources of confusion for Indian taxpayers has always been the distinction between Previous Year (PY) and Assessment Year (AY).

Under the 1961 Act, if you earned salary between April 2024 and March 2025, that period was your "Previous Year 2024-25". But when filing your ITR in July 2025, you were required to select "Assessment Year 2025-26". Every year, thousands of taxpayers selected the wrong year on tax challans or e-filing portals, leading to mismatched advance tax credits.

The Unified "Tax Year" Framework

The Income-tax Act, 2025 discards the dual concept entirely. There is no separate "Assessment Year". The new law introduces a single term: "Tax Year". The Tax Year is defined as the 12-month period starting 1 April in which your income is earned. When you file your return for income earned between 1 April 2026 and 31 March 2027, you simply file for Tax Year 2026-27.

4. Which Returns Follow the 1961 Act vs the 2025 Act?

Understanding which Act applies to your filing is essential during the transition period:

Income Period Filing Window Applicable Statute Statutory Designations
FY 2024-25 (Earned up to 31 Mar 2025) July / Dec 2025 Income-tax Act, 1961 AY 2025-26, Section 80C, Section 115BAC
FY 2025-26 (Earned up to 31 Mar 2026) July / Dec 2026 Income-tax Act, 1961 AY 2026-27 (Final return cycle under 1961 Act)
Earned from 1 April 2026 onward July / Dec 2027 onward Income-tax Act, 2025 Tax Year 2026-27, Section 123, Section 202

Illustrative Scenario: Amit, a salaried software engineer in Hyderabad, reviews his Form 16 in June 2026 for salary earned between April 2025 and March 2026. Because his income was earned before 1 April 2026, Amit files under the Income-tax Act, 1961 using AY 2026-27 and Section 80C. Only when filing returns in 2027 will he use the new 2025 Act sections.

6. What Did NOT Change vs What Changed

Taxpayers often worry that a new Act means higher taxes or eliminated exemptions. It helps to separate substantive tax rules from structural reorganization:

What Did NOT Change

  • Tax Rates & Slabs: Base tax rate percentages and income brackets are set by the annual Finance Act, not altered by the reorganization.
  • Deduction Limits: The ₹1.5 Lakh cap for PPF/ELSS, ₹25,000/₹50,000 for health insurance, and ₹2 Lakh home loan interest cap remain intact.
  • Dual Tax Regimes: Taxpayers can continue choosing between the New Default Regime and the Old Regime (with eligible deductions). Check our Income Tax Slabs guide.
  • Exemption Categories: Agricultural income, standard deductions for salaried individuals, and gratuity limits continue without disruption.

What Changed

  • Section Numbering: Completely re-sequenced into a cleaner, contiguous 1 to 536 layout.
  • Unified Terminology: "Assessment Year" and "Previous Year" are replaced by a single "Tax Year".
  • Language Modernization: Obsolete phrases from colonial-era drafting were replaced with plain English expressions.
  • Consolidated Schedules: Dozens of fragmented deduction provisions now refer to unified Schedules (such as Schedule XV).

7. Major Structural Overhaul: TDS and TCS Table Consolidation

Under the 1961 Act, non-salary Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) was notorious for legislative fragmentation. Taxpayers and accountants had to navigate dozens of individual sections: 194A (interest), 194C (contractors), 194H (commission), 194I (rent), and 194J (professional fees).

The Income-tax Act, 2025 drastically simplifies withholding taxes:

  • Section 392: Retains dedicated rules for TDS on salaried employment (legacy Section 192).
  • Section 393: Merges non-salary TDS categories into a single, detailed master table. Instead of dozens of overlapping sections, Section 393 contains a structured schedule specifying transaction types, threshold limits, and deduction percentages.
  • Section 394: Consolidates Tax Collected at Source (TCS) provisions that were previously scattered across Section 206C.

8. Official Note on Form Names (ITR-1, Form 16, AIS)

Will everyday tax documents disappear? CBDT guidelines confirm that familiar brand names for common tax forms are preserved to avoid disrupting compliance systems:

  • ITR-1 (Sahaj) & ITR-2: Continue as the standard return forms for salaried individuals and capital gains filers, updated with new section fields. Learn how to file in our ITR-1 online filing guide.
  • Form 16: Remains the annual salary TDS certificate issued by employers, citing Section 392 instead of Section 192 for Tax Year 2026-27 onward.
  • Annual Information Statement (AIS) & TIS: The digital taxpayer reporting systems continue unchanged on the e-filing portal. Compare statements in our Form 26AS vs AIS vs TIS guide.
  • Updated Return (ITR-U): The facility to update returns for omitted income continues under Section 264. See our ITR-U guide.

9. Key Takeaways for Taxpayers

  • Effective Date: 1 April 2026. Returns filed in 2026 for FY 2025-26 still use the 1961 Act.
  • Say Goodbye to "AY": "Assessment Year" is replaced by a single, intuitive "Tax Year".
  • Core Mappings: 80C becomes Section 123, 87A becomes Section 156, and 115BAC becomes Section 202.
  • Your Tax Liability Doesn't Spike: Slabs and deduction amounts are preserved; this is a structural and numbering modernization.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

When does the Income-tax Act, 2025 come into force?

The Income-tax Act, 2025 comes into effect on 1 April 2026. It applies to income earned during Tax Year 2026-27 onward. Income earned in Financial Year 2025-26 (filed in 2026) continues to be governed by the legacy Income-tax Act, 1961.

What is the new section number for Section 80C under the 2025 Act?

Under the official concordance table of the Income-tax Act, 2025, individual deductions for life insurance, PPF, EPF, and ELSS previously governed by Section 80C are mapped to Section 123 (read with Schedule XV).

What does 'Tax Year' mean and how does it replace Previous Year and Assessment Year?

The Income-tax Act, 2025 eliminates the confusing dual concepts of 'Previous Year' (the earning year) and 'Assessment Year' (the tax filing year). Instead, it adopts a single unified 'Tax Year', defined as the 12-month period beginning on 1 April in which income is earned.

Did tax slabs, deduction limits, or tax rates change under the Income-tax Act, 2025?

No. The Income-tax Act, 2025 is primarily a structural simplification and language overhaul. Statutory deduction caps (like ₹1.5 Lakh for 80C/123), rebate amounts (Section 87A/156), and prevailing tax slabs were preserved, while legal phrasing was streamlined.

What is the new section for the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC)?

The concessional tax regime (popularly known as the New Tax Regime under Section 115BAC of the 1961 Act) is codified under Section 202 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 as the standard default individual tax pathway.

What is the new section for filing Income Tax Returns (previously Section 139)?

General return filing provisions previously found in Section 139 of the 1961 Act are consolidated under Section 263 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Will existing tax forms like ITR-1, ITR-2, and Form 16 change names?

CBDT is progressively updating statutory return rules. While core designations like ITR-1 (Sahaj) and Form 16 continue in common practice, their internal statutory references and data schemas are aligned to the new section numbering for Tax Year 2026-27 filings.

Which Act governs tax returns filed during the 2026 calendar year?

Returns filed during the 2026 calendar year for income earned during FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) are filed entirely under the Income-tax Act, 1961. The 2025 Act provisions govern filings for income earned from 1 April 2026 onward.

How to Think About This Yourself

The transition from the Income-tax Act, 1961 to the Income-tax Act, 2025 is primarily a legislative modernization and codification exercise:

  • Structural Simplification, Not a Tax Hike: The reduction from 819 sections to 536 is aimed at eliminating redundant historical provisions and clarifying ambiguous legalese. Your basic tax calculation, slab rates, and standard deductions remain consistent.
  • The "Tax Year" Unification: The abolition of the distinct "Previous Year" (earning year) and "Assessment Year" (reporting year) removes a 60-year-old semantic confusion. For income earned from 1 April 2026 onwards, taxpayers simply reference "Tax Year 2026-27".
  • Transition Period Dual-Reference: During the multi-year transition, employers, banks, and software vendors will cite both old and new sections (e.g., Section 80C / Section 123; Section 115BAC / Section 202). Keep a concordance table handy when reviewing Form 16 or filing returns.
