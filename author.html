Background & Why I Built This

Name's Tejas Waghmare, a college dropout in India who picked up front-end web coding while trying to untangle compounding formulas, payslip deductions, and Indian tax slabs.

Skipping a conventional degree pushed me toward shipping real tools in vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Curious questions came first: why monthly take-home pay drops so steeply below offer-letter CTC, how EPF monthly running balances credit annual interest every March, and whether Section 80C deductions still change tax payable under the New Regime, yet searching Indian finance portals left most calculations buried under mobile-number popups and aggressive loan telemarketers. Clean tools barely existed.

Calculatorship fixed that mess. Browser memory handles every rupee calculation in milliseconds without database capture, displaying exact step-by-step formulas beside realistic Indian salary and mutual fund examples. Zero login walls. No cold calls. Just open browser arithmetic.

Source Checks & Math Verification

Zero formal credentials mean guessing stops immediately. Sponsored reviews stay off limits. Every tax cutoff and compounding curve pulls from official primary portals:

  • Income Tax Rules: Verified directly against the Income Tax Department portal and Union Budget notifications.
  • Banking & Loans: Checked against Reserve Bank of India master circulars covering reducing-balance EMI rules and EBLR benchmarks.
  • Provident Fund & Pension: Reconciled with circulars on the EPFO member portal and PFRDA NPS notifications.
  • Mutual Funds & Securities: Aligned with SEBI regulatory circulars and official AMFI category definitions.

Whenever Finance Ministry circulars adjust quarterly small-savings yields or Parliament passes fresh Union Budget income brackets, affected JavaScript functions and worked tables are checked line by line against zero-rupee and high-surcharge boundary inputs before anything stays published.

Reader Feedback & Bug Reports

Learning happens openly. Found a rounding slip, stale tax cap, or broken slider? Review the public change log on our Corrections Policy page, or send the exact link by emailing Tejas directly. Reports get read daily. Bugs get fixed fast.
