NRI Investment in India 2026: NRE vs NRO Accounts, Mutual Funds, TDS & Repatriation Rules

Comprehensive guide to NRI investments in India. Understand NRE vs NRO bank accounts, mutual fund KYC (FATCA/CRS), TDS rates, PIS rules, and Form 15CA/15CB repatriation.

This guide is intended strictly for international tax and investment awareness under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) and Indian Income Tax Act, 1961. Cross-border tax liabilities and Double Tax Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) depend on your country of tax residency (USA, UAE, UK, Singapore, Canada). Consult a qualified Chartered Accountant or international tax attorney before executing capital transfers.

1. FEMA Residential Status vs Income Tax Act Rules

When an Indian citizen moves abroad for employment, business, or indefinite stay, their legal and regulatory status shifts under two distinct statutory frameworks:

  • Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA, 1999): Administered by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Under FEMA, once you leave India for employment, business, or vacation with intention to stay abroad indefinitely, you immediately become a Person Resident Outside India (PROI / NRI). It is illegal for an NRI to hold regular resident savings accounts in India.
  • Income Tax Act, 1961 (Section 6): Determines whether your global or Indian income is subject to Indian taxation based on the physical presence rule (less than 182 days in India during the financial year).

2. Banking Architecture: NRE vs NRO Accounts Decoded

Every NRI investor must convert existing resident accounts or open new designated non-resident bank accounts:

Feature Dimension NRE (Non-Resident External) Account NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) Account
Primary Purpose Park and invest foreign income converted to INR Manage income originating inside India (rent, dividends)
Indian Tax on Interest Earned 100% Tax-Free in India Taxable at 30% + Cess (31.2% TDS)
Repatriability Abroad Freely & Fully Repatriable (No limits) Restricted to $1 Million USD per FY (Requires 15CA/CB)
Permitted Credits Foreign inward remittances only Indian rental income, dividends, pensions, local credits
Joint Account Option With another NRI or resident relative (Former/Survivor) With resident Indian on Former/Survivor basis

3. NRI Mutual Fund Investing & FATCA/CRS Guidelines

Investing in Indian equities through mutual funds is one of the most effective ways for NRIs to participate in India's economic expansion. Key investment operational guidelines include:

  1. KYC Compliance: NRIs must complete one-time mutual fund KYC with an overseas address proof, copy of passport, visa/work permit, and Indian PAN card.
  2. Direct Bank Mandates: SIPs and lumpsum transactions must be funded directly from your NRE or NRO bank account via net banking or NACH mandates. Mutual fund redemption proceeds are credited back only to the originating account type.
  3. US & Canada FATCA Rules: Due to IRS PFIC (Passive Foreign Investment Company) rules in the US and CRS in Canada, several AMCs restrict direct online investments. However, leading fund houses (SBI, ICICI Pru, HDFC, UTI, Nippon) accept US/Canada NRI applications with proper FATCA declarations.

4. Capital Gains Taxation & TDS Mechanics for NRIs

The most important operational difference between resident and non-resident mutual fund investors is mandatory TDS at the point of redemption:

Asset Class Holding Period Tax & TDS Rate for NRIs (FY 2026-27)
Equity Mutual Funds ≤ 12 Months (STCG) 20% + Applicable Surcharge & Cess
Equity Mutual Funds > 12 Months (LTCG) 12.5% + Cess (First ₹1.25L exempt under Sec 112A)
Debt Mutual Funds Any Period 30% + Applicable Surcharge & Cess

If your actual overall Indian income results in a lower tax bracket, you can file an Indian Income Tax Return (ITR-2) to claim a direct refund of excess TDS deducted.

5. Repatriation Protocol: Form 15CA & Form 15CB Blueprint

To transfer up to $1 Million USD per financial year from your Indian NRO account to your overseas bank account:

  • Obtain Form 15CB from a Chartered Accountant: A practicing Indian CA audits the origin of funds (e.g. inheritance, property sale, mutual fund redemption) to certify all applicable Indian taxes have been paid.
  • File Form 15CA Online: Submit the self-declaration on the official Income Tax e-Filing portal using details from Form 15CB.
  • Submit to Authorized Dealer Bank (A2 Form): Provide the generated Form 15CA acknowledgement, signed Form 15CB, and RBI FEMA A2 form to your NRO branch to execute the outward foreign wire.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the key difference between an NRE and an NRO bank account?

An NRE (Non-Resident External) account is used to park foreign earnings converted into Indian Rupees; interest earned is 100% tax-free in India and funds are freely repatriable abroad. An NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account is used to manage income originating in India (rent, dividends, pensions); interest is subject to 30% TDS (plus cess) and repatriation is capped at $1 Million USD per financial year.

Can NRIs invest in Indian Mutual Funds without a PIS account?

Yes. Non-Resident Indians can invest directly in Indian mutual funds via regular NRE or NRO savings bank accounts through direct AMC portals or SEBI-registered platforms. A Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS) account is only required for direct secondary market trading in Indian equities on BSE/NSE.

What is the TDS rate on mutual fund redemptions for NRIs in FY 2026-27?

Unlike resident Indians who pay tax when filing returns, NRIs face mandatory Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) at the time of redemption: 20% on Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) from equity funds, 12.5% on Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) from equity funds held over 12 months, and slab rate (30% + cess) on debt mutual funds.

Can US and Canada-based NRIs invest in Indian mutual funds?

Yes, but options are restricted due to US FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) and Canadian CRS compliance. Approximately 10 to 12 major Indian AMCs (including ICICI Prudential, SBI, HDFC, UTI, Nippon India, Sundaram) accept investments from US/Canada NRIs, subject to additional FATCA declarations and offline/in-person verification.

What is the process to repatriate funds from an NRO account abroad?

Under RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) framework for NRIs, up to $1 Million USD per financial year can be repatriated from an NRO account. The process requires submitting Form 15CA (online self-declaration on the income tax portal) and Form 15CB (certification from an Indian Chartered Accountant confirming tax clearance).
