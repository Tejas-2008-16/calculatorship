This guide is intended strictly for international tax and investment awareness under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) and Indian Income Tax Act, 1961. Cross-border tax liabilities and Double Tax Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) depend on your country of tax residency (USA, UAE, UK, Singapore, Canada). Consult a qualified Chartered Accountant or international tax attorney before executing capital transfers.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. FEMA Mandate: Continuing to operate a resident savings bank account after moving abroad violates Section 10(6) of FEMA; you must redesignate existing accounts to NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary).
  • 2. NRE vs NRO: NRE accounts hold foreign income converted into INR with 100% tax-free interest and unlimited repatriation; NRO accounts manage Indian-origin income (rent, dividends) subject to 30% TDS and a $1 Million USD annual repatriation limit.
  • 3. PIS Account Not Required for Mutual Funds: NRIs can invest in Indian mutual funds via regular NRE or NRO accounts directly through AMC portals, MF Central, or SEBI-registered stockbrokers and intermediaries; a Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS) account is mandated only for secondary market direct stock transactions.
  • 4. Mandatory TDS on Redemptions: Unlike resident investors, NRIs face upfront tax withholding: 20% on equity STCG, 12.5% on equity LTCG (above ₹1.25L exemption), and slab rates (30% + cess) on debt funds.
  • 5. US/Canada FATCA Restrictions: Due to complex US SEC and IRS PFIC rules, only ~10 Indian AMCs (including UTI, SBI, ICICI Prudential, and HDFC) accept investments from US and Canada-based NRIs.
  • 6. Repatriation Paperwork: Moving funds from an NRO account abroad requires filing Form 15CA (online portal self-declaration) and Form 15CB (Chartered Accountant tax certificate).

The Resident Account Violation: What Happens When You Move Abroad Without Converting Your Bank

You relocated from Hyderabad or Bengaluru to Dubai, Singapore, or London eight months ago. Your salary lands in your foreign bank account, but you still keep your old resident SBI or HDFC savings account active to pay for mutual fund SIPs, ancestral property maintenance, or insurance premiums. You assume you can update your status "whenever you visit India next."

Under Section 10(6) of the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), holding a resident savings account once your status changes to non-resident is a legal violation carrying penalties of up to three times the amount involved or ₹2,00,000 where the amount is non-quantifiable. The moment you cross 182 days outside India for employment, Indian law mandates converting your resident accounts into NRO accounts or opening fresh NRE accounts.

NRE vs NRO Bank Accounts: The Comparative Rulebook

Every rupee an NRI invests in India must flow through either a Non-Resident External (NRE) or Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) account. Understanding the structural boundaries prevents expensive tax leakage:

Account Feature NRE Account (Non-Resident External) NRO Account (Non-Resident Ordinary)
Source of Funds Foreign earnings remitted inward from abroad Income originating in India (rent, dividends, pension)
Taxability of Interest in India 100% Tax-Free (Section 10(4)) Taxable (Subject to 30% TDS + Cess)
Repatriability Abroad Freely Repatriable (Zero limits or forms) Capped at $1 Million USD per financial year
Can Deposit Indian Rupee Income? Strictly Prohibited Permitted (Rent, business sales, dividends)
Joint Account Holding With another NRI, or resident relative (Former or Survivor) With another NRI or resident Indian relative

NRI Mutual Fund Investing: The PIS Myth and FATCA Realities

A widespread misconception among NRIs is that investing in Indian mutual funds requires opening an expensive Portfolio Investment Scheme (PIS) account with designated banks. This is false:

  • Mutual Funds Do NOT Require PIS: Under Reserve Bank of India (RBI) notifications, NRIs can invest in Indian mutual funds directly using standard NRE or NRO savings accounts linked to mutual fund folios. A PIS account is mandated only for direct buying and selling of individual company shares on the NSE/BSE stock exchanges.
  • The US & Canada FATCA/CRS Constraint: Because of onerous reporting standards mandated by the US Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA) and Passive Foreign Investment Company (PFIC) tax rules, several Indian fund houses refuse investments from NRIs based in the US or Canada. However, top AMCs—including UTI, SBI Mutual Fund, ICICI Prudential, HDFC, and Nippon India—accept investments from US/Canada NRIs, typically requiring an additional FATCA declaration and proof of overseas tax residency.

Capital Gains Tax and Withholding (TDS) for NRIs (FY 2026-27)

Unlike resident Indian investors (who receive full redemption proceeds and self-report capital gains during July ITR filing), NRIs are subjected to mandatory upfront Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) by mutual fund AMCs under Section 195:

Asset Category / Holding Period Holding Period Statutory NRI TDS Rate Effective Tax Rate (with 4% Cess)
Equity Mutual Funds & Stocks (STCG) ≤ 12 Months 20% (Section 111A) 20.80%
Equity Mutual Funds & Stocks (LTCG) > 12 Months 12.5% (Above ₹1.25L exemption) 13.00%
Debt Mutual Funds (Section 50AA) Any duration 30% (Peak slab rate) 31.20%
NRO Savings / FD Interest Accrual 30% (or DTAA treaty rate) 31.20%

How to Recover Excess TDS: AMCs deduct TDS on the gross gain without accounting for your basic tax exemption limit or total income bracket. If your total taxable income in India is below the exemption threshold or your effective tax rate is lower than the withheld TDS, you must file ITR-2 before July 31st to claim a direct income tax refund into your NRO account.

The Repatriation guide: Moving Money from NRO to Your Overseas Bank

If you sell an ancestral property or liquidate Indian mutual funds held in an NRO account, moving that money to your overseas bank in Dubai, London, or New York requires navigating RBI's $1 Million USD remittance scheme:

  1. Obtain Form 15CB from a Chartered Accountant: A practicing Indian CA audits the origin of the funds, verifies that all Indian capital gains taxes have been paid, and uploads a certified Form 15CB to the income tax e-filing portal.
  2. Submit Form 15CA Online: Using the CA's acknowledgment number, log in to incometax.gov.in and generate Form 15CA (Part C). This serves as your statutory self-declaration of foreign remittance.
  3. Submit to Your Authorized Dealer (AD) Bank: Provide Forms 15CA, 15CB, an A2 Form, and source proof (sale deed or AMC redemption statement) to your bank (e.g. SBI, ICICI, HDFC). The bank executes the wire transfer into your foreign account within 24 to 48 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I continue investing in PPF after becoming an NRI?

If you opened a PPF account while you were a resident Indian, you are legally permitted to continue contributing to it on a non-repatriation basis until its original 15-year maturity. However, once the 15-year term expires, NRIs are strictly prohibited from extending the PPF account for further 5-year blocks.

How does Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) benefit me as an NRI?

India has signed DTAA treaties with over 85 countries (including USA, UK, UAE, Singapore, Canada). If tax is deducted on your Indian income (such as 30% TDS on NRO interest), you can submit a Tax Residency Certificate (TRC) and Form 10F to lower your Indian TDS to treaty rates (e.g. 10% to 15%) or claim foreign tax credits in your country of residence to avoid double taxation.

Can an NRI invest in agricultural land or farmhouses in India?

No. Under FEMA Master Directions issued by the Reserve Bank of India, NRIs and OCIs are strictly prohibited from purchasing agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouses in India. You can, however, acquire residential and commercial properties with zero restrictions, or inherit agricultural land from resident relatives.

Why do Indian mutual fund platforms ask for my FATCA declaration every year?

Under Inter-Governmental Agreements signed between India, the US, and OECD Common Reporting Standard (CRS) member countries, Indian financial institutions are legally obligated to report financial accounts held by foreign tax residents to Indian tax authorities, who then share that data with overseas tax agencies like the US IRS.

Can I transfer funds directly from my NRO account to my NRE account?

Yes. RBI permits inter-account transfers from NRO to NRE accounts within the overall annual ceiling of $1 Million USD, provided you submit Form 15CA and Form 15CB from a Chartered Accountant certifying that all applicable Indian taxes on the funds have been settled.

Is an NRI required to file an Indian Income Tax Return if TDS has already been deducted?

If your total taxable income in India consists exclusively of investment income or capital gains on which accurate TDS has already been deducted under Chapter XII-A, Section 115G exempts you from mandatory ITR filing. However, if excess TDS was deducted, or if you wish to carry forward capital losses, filing ITR-2 is mandatory.

Actionable Next Step: Audit Your Indian Bank & Mutual Fund KYC Today

If you have moved abroad within the last 12 months, log in to your Indian bank netbanking portal today and initiate the account conversion from Resident to NRO. Next, visit cvlkra.com or kra.ndml.in to verify your KYC status—submit an updated KYC form with your overseas address and passport copy to redesignate your mutual fund folios as "Non-Resident." Taking these two actions eliminates FEMA non-compliance and ensures direct redemption processing when you decide to repatriate your wealth.

These guidelines reflect statutory provisions under FEMA and the Income Tax Act for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28); consult a registered Chartered Accountant specializing in NRI cross-border taxation for personalized remittance advisory.

How to Think About This Yourself

  • Distinguish FEMA residency from Income Tax residency: FEMA determines bank account legality based on intention to stay abroad, whereas Section 6 of the Income Tax Act determines taxability of global vs Indian-sourced income based on physical days in India.
  • Select between NRE and NRO routes: NRE accounts are funded with foreign currency, offer tax-free interest in India, and provide freely repatriable principal and interest. NRO accounts hold Indian-origin income, are subject to 30% TDS, and have an annual repatriation cap of $1 Million USD under the LRS framework.
  • Factor in TDS on redemptions: Unlike residents, NRIs face mandatory tax deduction at source (20% STCG, 12.5% LTCG on equity; slab rates on debt funds). You must file ITR-2 to claim refunds if your actual tax liability is lower.
  • Examine home-country tax treaties (DTAA): Tax paid in India can generally be claimed as a Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) in your country of residence (e.g., US, UK, UAE, Singapore), preventing double taxation under bilateral treaties.

Where to Get Personalised Help

Cross-border taxation involves compliance under both Indian domestic laws (FEMA, Income Tax Act) and foreign revenue statutes (such as IRS PFIC rules for US residents or HMRC domicile rules for UK residents). For repatriating funds via Form 15CA/15CB or claiming DTAA relief, consult a practicing Chartered Accountant (CA) experienced in international taxation. For investment allocation, consult a fee-only SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA).
