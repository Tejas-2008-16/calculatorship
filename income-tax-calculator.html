What is the Difference Between Old vs New Tax Regime? — A Complete Guide for Indian Taxpayers (FY 2026-27)

In India, taxpayers have the legal choice to compute their personal income tax liability under two distinct regulatory regimes: the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC). The New Tax Regime is the default tax regime in India, engineered by the Ministry of Finance to simplify tax filing by offering significantly lower income tax slab rates in exchange for eliminating most traditional itemized deductions and exemptions.

Under the revised New Tax Regime for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28), salaried individuals receive an enhanced Standard Deduction of ₹75,000 (up from ₹50,000) and a generous tax rebate under Section 87A for taxable incomes up to ₹7,00,000. This means that a salaried employee earning up to ₹7,75,000 pays absolute zero tax (₹0) under the New Regime without investing a single rupee in insurance policies, PPF, or ELSS funds.

Conversely, the Old Tax Regime features higher slab rates (reaching the top 30% rate on income above ₹10 Lakh), but allows taxpayers to claim extensive deductions: Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 Lakh), Section 80D (health insurance up to ₹25,000 to ₹1,00,000), Section 24(b) (home loan interest up to ₹2 Lakh for self-occupied properties), House Rent Allowance (HRA under Section 10(13A)), and Leave Travel Allowance (LTA). For taxpayers who pay substantial home loan EMIs and high rent, the Old Regime can still deliver superior tax savings.

Determining which tax regime is mathematically superior depends entirely on your total aggregate deductions. This calculator computes both systems side-by-side to pinpoint your exact breakeven deduction threshold.

The Math Behind the Calculator — Slabs & Rebates Explained

The mathematical computation follows the official slab structures notified by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) for FY 2026-27:

1. New Tax Regime Slabs (Section 115BAC) — Default

  • Standard Deduction for Salaried / Pensioners: ₹75,000
  • ₹0 to ₹3,00,000: NIL (0%)
  • ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000: 5%
  • ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 10%
  • ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 15%
  • ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000: 20%
  • Above ₹15,00,000: 30%
  • Section 87A Rebate: Full tax rebate up to ₹25,000 if taxable income does not exceed ₹7,00,000.

2. Old Tax Regime Slabs

  • Standard Deduction for Salaried: ₹50,000
  • ₹0 to ₹2,50,000: NIL (0%)
  • ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5%
  • ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 20%
  • Above ₹10,00,000: 30%
  • Section 87A Rebate: Full tax rebate up to ₹12,500 if taxable income does not exceed ₹5,00,000.

Step-by-Step Worked Mathematical Example in Indian Rupees

Let us compare a salaried individual with a Gross Salary of ₹15,00,000 with ₹1.5L (80C), ₹25k (80D), and ₹2.0L (Home Loan Interest Sec 24b):

Under New Tax Regime:

  • Gross Salary = ₹15,00,000 | Standard Deduction = -₹75,000 | Taxable Income = ₹14,25,000
  • ₹0 to ₹3,00,000 = ₹0
  • ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000 (₹4L @ 5%) = ₹20,000
  • ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 (₹3L @ 10%) = ₹30,000
  • ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 (₹2L @ 15%) = ₹30,000
  • ₹12,00,001 to ₹14,25,000 (₹2.25L @ 20%) = ₹45,000
  • Base Tax = ₹1,25,000 | Plus 4% Cess (₹5,000) = ₹1,30,000 Total Tax

Under Old Tax Regime:

  • Gross Salary = ₹15,00,000
  • Total Deductions = Standard Ded (₹50k) + 80C (₹1.5L) + 80D (₹25k) + Sec 24b (₹2.0L) = -₹4,25,000
  • Taxable Income = ₹15,00,000 - ₹4,25,000 = ₹10,75,000
  • ₹0 to ₹2.5L = ₹0 | ₹2.5L to ₹5L (5%) = ₹12,500 | ₹5L to ₹10L (20%) = ₹1,00,000 | ₹10L to ₹10.75L (30%) = ₹22,500
  • Base Tax = ₹1,35,000 | Plus 4% Cess (₹5,400) = ₹1,40,400 Total Tax

Result: Even with ₹4.25 Lakh in deductions, the New Tax Regime saves ₹10,400 in hard cash due to the lower 10%/15%/20% slab rates!

Key Things to Know — Regulations, Deductions & Surcharges for Indian Taxpayers

Important statutory rules to consider when filing your Income Tax Return (ITR):

  • Breakeven Deduction Benchmark: For an income of ₹15 Lakh, you need total deductions exceeding approximately ₹4.25 Lakh to ₹4.50 Lakh (Standard Deduction + 80C + 80D + HRA/Home Loan) for the Old Regime to beat the New Regime. If your deductions are below this threshold, the New Regime is mathematically superior.
  • Employer NPS Benefit (Section 80CCD(2)): Employer contributions to NPS (up to 14% of Basic + DA) can be claimed as a deduction under both the Old and New Tax Regimes. This is one of the very few itemized deductions retained under Section 115BAC.
  • Switching Regimes Rules (Form 10-IEA): Salaried individuals with no business income can switch regimes freely every year directly in their ITR form. Freelancers, consultants, and business owners filing ITR-3 or ITR-4 must file Form 10-IEA to opt out of the New Regime and can switch back to the New Regime only once.
  • Surcharge Rates on High Incomes: For super-high earners (taxable income exceeding ₹5 Crore), the highest surcharge rate under the New Tax Regime is capped at 25% (reducing the maximum effective tax rate from 42.74% down to 39%), whereas under the Old Regime, the surcharge reaches up to 37%.

Common Mistakes Indian Taxpayers Make

Avoid these costly tax filing blunders:

  1. Assuming the New Regime is Automatically Worse: Many taxpayers blindly stick to the Old Regime out of habit, buying unnecessary low-return traditional insurance policies just to save 80C tax, when the New Regime would save them more tax with zero investment lock-in.
  2. Missing Employer HR Investment Proof Deadlines: If you plan to use the Old Regime, failing to submit your rent receipts, home loan provisional interest certificates, or 80C proofs to your employer HR by January/February leads to massive TDS deductions in March. (You can still claim it when filing your ITR).
  3. Failing to Account for Section 87A Marginal Relief: In the New Regime, if taxable income is ₹7,00,100 (just ₹100 over ₹7 Lakh), marginal relief applies so that you do not pay more tax than the income exceeding ₹7 Lakh.
  4. Ignoring Capital Gains Tax Differences: LTCG and STCG on shares and mutual funds are taxed identically under both Old and New Regimes (12.5% LTCG above ₹1.25L, 20% STCG). Deductions like 80C cannot be set off against capital gains income.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Standard Deduction for salaried employees in FY 2026-27?

For salaried employees and pensioners, the Standard Deduction is ₹75,000 under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) and ₹50,000 under the Old Tax Regime. This deduction is applied automatically against gross salary without requiring any expense receipts or investment proofs.

What is the tax rebate threshold under Section 87A in the New Tax Regime?

Under the New Tax Regime, resident individual taxpayers with taxable income up to ₹7,00,000 receive a full tax rebate of up to ₹25,000 under Section 87A. Combined with the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction, salaried individuals earning up to ₹7,75,000 pay zero income tax. Under the Old Regime, the Section 87A rebate is capped at ₹12,500 for taxable income up to ₹5,00,000.

Which deductions can be claimed under the Old Tax Regime but not the New Regime?

The Old Tax Regime permits major deductions including Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 Lakh for EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance, home loan principal), Section 80D (health insurance up to ₹25k/₹50k), Section 24(b) (home loan interest up to ₹2 Lakh for self-occupied property), Section 10(13A) (House Rent Allowance / HRA exemption), Leave Travel Allowance (LTA), and Section 80TTA/80TTB (savings and FD interest). In the New Tax Regime, these deductions are forfeited in exchange for lower slab rates.

Can an individual switch between Old and New Tax Regimes every year?

Salaried individuals who have no income from business or profession can choose between the Old and New Tax Regimes every financial year at the time of filing their ITR return under Section 139(1). However, individuals having income from business or profession can switch out of the New Regime only once in their lifetime.

Is employer NPS contribution deductible under the New Tax Regime?

Yes. Employer contributions to the National Pension System (NPS) under Section 80CCD(2) (up to 14% of Basic Salary + DA for government employees and up to 14% for corporate employees) remain fully deductible under both the New and Old Tax Regimes.

How is the 4% Health and Education Cess calculated?

The 4% Health and Education Cess is calculated on the total income tax payable (after applying slab rates, surcharges, and Section 87A rebates). It is added to your tax liability under both the Old and New Regimes.
