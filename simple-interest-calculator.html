Simple Interest Calculator India

Written by Tejas Waghmare • Last updated: November 2026
Educational content only: This calculator provides mathematical simple interest simulations for loans and notes. Commercial consumer credit often uses reducing balance rates rather than flat simple interest.

Direct Answer: Simple interest is calculated solely on the original principal amount, without adding accrued interest back into the principal. It is predominantly used in India for short-term Treasury Bills (T-Bills), personal promissory notes, inter-corporate deposits, and automobile flat-rate financing.

The Exact Simple Interest Mathematical Formula

Simple interest is calculated using the classical arithmetic formulation:

SI = (P * R * T) / 100

Where:

  • SI: Total Simple Interest generated.
  • P: Principal sum borrowed or deposited.
  • R: Annual interest rate percentage.
  • T: Time duration in years (for example, 6 months = 0.5 years; 73 days = 73/365 = 0.2 years).
  • Total Maturity / Repayment (A): P + SI.

2 Worked Mathematical Examples in Rupees

Example 1: Short-Term Bridge Loan (2 Years)

A business borrower takes a short-term promissory note loan of ₹2,00,000 at 10.5% per annum simple interest for 2 years.

  • P = ₹2,00,000, R = 10.5, T = 2
  • SI = (₹2,00,000 * 10.5 * 2) / 100 = ₹42,000
  • Annual interest = ₹21,000 per year (constant)
  • Total repayment upon maturity = ₹2,00,000 + ₹42,000 = ₹2,42,000

Example 2: 182-Day Government Treasury Bill (RBI T-Bill)

An institutional investor purchases 182-day T-Bills for a face value of ₹10,00,000 issued at a discount equivalent to an annual simple yield of 6.80%.

  • P = ₹10,00,000, R = 6.80, T = 182 / 365 = 0.4986 years
  • SI = (₹10,00,000 * 6.80 * 0.4986) / 100 = ₹33,907
  • Discount issue price = ₹10,00,000 - ₹33,907 = ₹9,66,093
  • On redemption day, the government credits the full ₹10,00,000 face value.

3 Common Mistakes in Simple Interest Calculations

1. Assuming Bank FDs Use Simple Interest

In India, commercial banks compound Fixed Deposit interest on a quarterly basis. Even regular savings accounts calculate interest on daily closing balances and credit it quarterly. Banks almost never apply simple interest for bank term deposits longer than 90 days.

2. Confusing Monthly Flat Rates with Reducing Balance Rates

Vehicle dealerships often quote 'flat interest rates' (for example, 7% flat per annum). Because flat rates calculate interest on the full original principal for the entire loan life without crediting monthly repayments, the true interest rate is nearly 13% reducing balance.

3. Miscalculating Days for T-Bills or Commercial Paper

For 91-day, 182-day, or 364-day Treasury Bills, simple interest requires an exact day-count convention (Actual/365). Rounding to flat 30 days per month creates noticeable pricing discrepancies on institutional sums.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

When is simple interest used instead of compound interest?

Lenders typically apply simple interest to short-duration instruments (under one year) such as Treasury Bills, short-term promissory notes between private parties, and penal interest on delayed statutory payments.

Why is compound interest better than simple interest for investors?

Compound interest produces accelerating wealth growth because your interest begins earning its own interest. Over 10 years at 8%, ₹1 Lakh grows to ₹1.8 Lakh under simple interest, but reaches ~₹2.16 Lakh under compound interest.

How is simple interest taxed in India?

Simple interest received from loans or notes is classified under 'Income from Other Sources' and taxed at your marginal income tax slab rate for the financial year in which it is received or accrued.
