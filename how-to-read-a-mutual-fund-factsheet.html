How to Read a Mutual Fund Factsheet: A Plain-English Guide for Indian Investors

Every AMC publishes a monthly factsheet — but most investors never read it. This guide decodes AUM, portfolio turnover, Sharpe ratio, standard deviation, and top holdings so you know exactly what to look for.

What is a Mutual Fund Factsheet and Why Must You Read It?

Every single month, SEBI mandates that all 44+ Asset Management Companies in India — from HDFC and ICICI Prudential to Parag Parikh and SBI Mutual Fund — publish a detailed document called a Monthly Factsheet. Think of it as a mutual fund's monthly report card, health checkup, and nutritional label all rolled into one PDF file.

Despite being freely downloadable from AMC websites, MF Central, or platforms like Zerodha Coin and Groww, roughly 95% of retail investors never open a factsheet. Instead, they pick funds based on 5-star ratings or recent 1-year return charts on investment apps. Here's the catch: past 1-year returns can easily lie, especially during bull runs. A factsheet reveals what is happening under the hood of your scheme right now.

Whether you are committing a ₹5,000 monthly SIP or investing a ₹2 Lakh lump sum, spending 10 minutes reading a factsheet lets you spot hidden risks before they hurt your portfolio. Let's break down the four critical sections into plain English.

Section 1: Fund Basics — AUM, Fund Manager, and Expense Ratio

When you open the first page of a scheme factsheet, you encounter the fund's identity card. Here are the key data points to inspect:

  • Assets Under Management (AUM): This is the total market value of all investor capital currently managed by the scheme. For large-cap equity funds, an AUM of ₹10,000 Crore to ₹40,000 Crore is common. For small-cap funds, however, a massive AUM above ₹30,000 Crore becomes a double-edged sword — the fund manager struggles to buy liquidity-constrained small stocks without driving up prices.
  • Fund Manager Track Record: Check who is managing your money and how long they have managed this specific scheme. If a fund delivered stellar 10-year returns but its star manager quit 3 months ago, historical performance may no longer apply. Look for fund managers with at least 5 years of stable tenure.
  • Total Expense Ratio (TER): Factsheets break TER into Direct Plan TER (e.g., 0.65%) and Regular Plan TER (e.g., 1.75%). As covered in our guide on understanding expense ratios, choosing the Direct plan saves you distributor commissions every single day.
Key Insight: Check the Benchmark Index

Always verify the scheme's official benchmark (e.g., Nifty 50 TRI or BSE 500 TRI). Make sure the factsheet compares returns against Total Return Indices (TRI), which include reinvested stock dividends — not basic price return indices!

Section 2: Portfolio Composition — Top Holdings and Sector Allocation

The middle pages of a factsheet show exactly where your money is invested. Rather than listing all 60+ stocks, the summary highlights the Top 10 Holdings and Sector Weightages.

Here's the thing most people miss: portfolio overlap. If you invest in three different Large & Mid Cap funds, you might assume your portfolio is well diversified. But when you examine their factsheets side-by-side, you might discover all three hold 8–10% of assets in HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Reliance Industries. In reality, you own the exact same stocks across 3 different schemes — paying 3x the expense ratio for no extra diversification!

Pay close attention to Top 10 Concentration. If the top 10 stocks make up 60% of the fund, it is a high-conviction, concentrated portfolio. If they make up only 25%, the portfolio is broadly spread across 80+ companies. Neither is wrong, but concentrated funds experience wider swings during corrections.

Section 3: Risk Metrics Decoded — Sharpe Ratio, Alpha, Beta, and Standard Deviation

This is where most investors get intimidated by complex jargon. Factsheets include a table labelled "Risk Ratios." Here is how to decode these four numbers like a professional analyst:

Risk Metric What It Measures Ideal Target In Practice
Sharpe Ratio Risk-adjusted excess return per unit of volatility Higher is Better (>1.0) Are returns from skill or excess risk?
Alpha (α) Outperformance vs. the benchmark index Positive (>0.0%) +3.5% Alpha = fund beat index by 3.5% p.a.
Beta (β) Volatility sensitivity vs. the market index ~1.0 (Lower = Defensive) Beta 1.2 = if Nifty drops 10%, fund drops ~12%
Std. Deviation Annual return fluctuation range Lower within peer group Higher deviation = bumpier ride

And this is where it gets interesting. Two funds might both deliver 15% CAGR over 5 years. But if Fund X achieved this with a Sharpe Ratio of 1.2, while Fund Y achieved it with 0.6, Fund X is far superior — it took significantly less risk to deliver the exact same return!

Section 4: Portfolio Turnover Ratio — Active Trading vs Long-Term Conviction

Another crucial metric is the Portfolio Turnover Ratio (PTR), expressed as a percentage. A PTR of 100% means the fund manager completely replaced the entire portfolio over the past year. High turnover (above 80%) incurs brokerage fees, transaction costs, and STT taxes that silently eat into returns. A low turnover ratio (15–30%) indicates a buy-and-hold manager with strong long-term conviction in their stock picks.

If you prefer passive low-cost investing where portfolio turnover is minimal, explore our comparison between active vs passive mutual funds in India.

How Arjun Decoded His Flexi Cap Fund Factsheet on Zerodha

Arjun, a 30-year-old software engineer from Pune, had been running a ₹10,000 monthly SIP in a popular Flexi Cap fund for 2 years. When he finally downloaded the scheme's monthly factsheet from Zerodha Coin, he noticed three critical issues:

  1. Negative Alpha (-1.2%): His active fund was underperforming its Nifty 500 benchmark by 1.2% per year after fees.
  2. High Expense Ratio (1.85%): He had accidentally enrolled in the Regular plan through his bank agent, paying 1.20% extra per year vs. the Direct plan!
  3. Banking Sector Concentration (38%): Over one-third of the fund was in financial stocks, making his portfolio vulnerable to credit sector downturns.

By spending 15 minutes on the factsheet, Arjun switched to a Direct Flexi Cap scheme with positive Alpha (+2.8%), a low TER of 0.55%, and a more balanced sector spread. That single decision will net him over ₹18 Lakhs in extra wealth over his 20-year investment horizon.

Watch Out: Many investors pick Regular plan funds by accident on bank apps and third-party portals. Always double-check the plan type on your account statement or AMFI's MF Central portal. Switching from Regular to Direct requires re-investing but is almost always worth it in the long run.

Put Your Factsheet Knowledge to Work

Reading a mutual fund factsheet might feel like learning a new language at first, but once you master the core indicators — AUM, Direct TER, Sharpe Ratio, Alpha, and Portfolio Turnover — you will never buy a bad fund again.
