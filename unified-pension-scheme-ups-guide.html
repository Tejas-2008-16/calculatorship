Official Central Government Scheme Notice

The Unified Pension Scheme (UPS) was approved by the Union Cabinet and notified by the Department of Financial Services (Notification No. FX-1/3/2024-PR) and Department of Pension and Pensioners' Welfare under the CCS (Implementation of UPS under NPS) Rules, 2025. It operationalized with effect from April 1, 2025 for eligible Central Government employees and state government adopters.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • 50% Assured Pension: Fixed monthly pension equal to 50% of the average basic pay drawn in the last 12 months for 25+ years of qualifying service.
  • Proportional Benefit: Employees with 10 to 24 years of service receive a proportionate assured pension; minimum Fixed pension of ₹10,000/month after 10 years.
  • Dearness Relief (DR) Included: All assured pensions, minimum pensions, and family pensions are indexed to inflation through AICPI-IW Dearness Relief.
  • Family Pension Security: Spouse/dependent family receives 60% of the employee’s pension upon death, along with Dearness Relief.
  • Superannuation Lump Sum: Additional lump sum payout equal to 1/10th of monthly emoluments (Basic + DA) for every completed 6 months of service, over and above retirement gratuity.
  • Contribution Rates: Employee contributes 10% of Basic + DA, while the Central Government contributes 18.5% (an increase from 14% under NPS).

1. What is the Unified Pension Scheme (UPS)?

The Unified Pension Scheme (UPS) is a national pension architecture designed by the Government of India to address longstanding demands by government employees regarding retirement security. For over two decades following the discontinuation of the Old Pension Scheme (OPS) on January 1, 2004, Central Government employees were covered under the market-linked National Pension System (NPS), where pension amounts depend entirely on market returns and commercial annuity rates.

Approved by the Union Cabinet in August 2024 and operationalized from April 1, 2025, the UPS establishes a hybrid contributory pension model. It preserves the contributory discipline of the NPS while restoring the defined-benefit certainty of the Old Pension Scheme: providing a sovereign-Fixed assured pension of 50% of basic pay along with inflation indexation.

2. Background: The Somnathan Committee & NPS Reforms

Following representations from employee associations and divergent pension policies across various Indian states, the Ministry of Finance constituted a high-level committee in April 2023 chaired by then Finance Secretary (Dr. T.V. Somanathan). The committee was tasked with reviewing the pension system for government employees and recommending improvements within macroeconomic fiscal parameters.

The committee evaluated international pension models, actuarial sustainability, and employee welfare. Its recommendations culminated in the Unified Pension Scheme, striking a balance between fiscal prudence for public finances and Fixed post-retirement income for civil servants.

3. Five Core Pillars of the Unified Pension Scheme

The Unified Pension Scheme is anchored on five fundamental statutory commitments:

Pillar Statutory Benefit Eligibility / Condition
1. Assured Pension 50% of Average Basic Pay 25 years of qualifying service (pro-rata for 10–24 years)
2. Assured Family Pension 60% of Employee's Pension Payable immediately upon demise of the employee/pensioner
3. Assured Minimum Pension ₹10,000 per month Minimum 10 years of qualifying service upon superannuation
4. Inflation Indexation Dearness Relief (DR) Applicable on Assured, Family, and Minimum Pensions via AICPI-IW
5. Superannuation Lump Sum (1/10th of Monthly Pay + DA) × Six-Month Periods Payable in addition to Retirement Gratuity; does not reduce pension

4. How the 50% Assured Pension is Calculated (Full vs Pro-Rata Service)

The assured pension calculation depends on the length of qualifying service and the average basic pay received during the final year of employment:

Full Service Formula (Qualifying Service ≥ 25 Years): Monthly Pension = 50% × Average Basic Pay (Last 12 Months) + Applicable DR Proportional Service Formula (10 to 24 Years of Service): Pro-Rata Pension = [ 50% × Average Basic Pay ] × (Qualifying Service in Years / 25)

Subject to a statutory minimum floor of ₹10,000 per month plus Dearness Relief.

5. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Pension Calculation

Case Study: Section Officer Superannuating After 28 Years of Service

Consider Rajesh, an officer retiring under Pay Level 10 with the following career profile:

  • Qualifying Service: 28 Years (Qualifies for full 50% assured tier)
  • Average Basic Pay in Last 12 Months: ₹84,000
  • Dearness Relief (DR) at Superannuation: 53%

Calculations:

  1. Basic Assured Pension: 50% of ₹84,000 = ₹42,000 per month
  2. Dearness Relief Component (53%): ₹42,000 × 53% = ₹22,260 per month
  3. Total Gross Monthly Pension: ₹42,000 + ₹22,260 = ₹64,260 per month
  4. Subsequent Family Pension (if applicable): 60% of ₹42,000 = ₹25,200 per month + applicable DR
Lump Sum Benefit at Retirement:

28 years of service = 56 half-yearly periods. Emoluments (Basic + DA) = ₹84,000 + 53% = ₹1,28,520.
Lump Sum = (1/10 × ₹1,28,520) × 56 = ₹7,19,712 (tax-free, paid in addition to Gratuity).

6. Contribution Structure: Employee 10% vs Government 18.5%

A central design question of the UPS was financing the sovereign guarantee without straining future budgets:

  • Employee Contribution: The employee continues to contribute 10% of Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance each month, exactly identical to their previous NPS deduction.
  • Government Contribution: The Central Government increased its contribution from 14% to 18.5% of Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance.

Under the PFRDA regulatory framework, the employee's 10% and a matching portion of the employer's contribution are credited to the individual's pension fund, while the incremental government contribution builds an actuarially managed Pool / Guarantee Reserve that guarantees the 50% assured payout regardless of market volatility.

7. Superannuation Lump Sum Payout: Formula & Rules

Under the market-linked NPS, an employee is required to utilize at least 40% of their accumulated retirement corpus to purchase a commercial annuity from an insurance company, and can withdraw the remaining 60% as a tax-free lump sum.

Under the Unified Pension Scheme, because the 50% monthly pension is assured by the government, the employee does not need to allocate 40% to a commercial insurer. Instead, they receive:

UPS Superannuation Lump Sum Formula: Lump Sum = [ (Monthly Basic Pay + DA) / 10 ] × Completed 6-Month Blocks of Service

This lump-sum benefit is paid at superannuation over and above standard Retirement Gratuity (which has a statutory limit of up to ₹25 Lakh under the 7th CPC guidelines) and does not reduce the monthly assured pension in any manner.

8. detailed Comparison: UPS vs NPS vs Old Pension Scheme (OPS)

Parameter Old Pension Scheme (OPS) National Pension System (NPS) Unified Pension Scheme (UPS)
Nature of Scheme Defined Benefit (Unfunded) Defined Contribution (Market-linked) Assured Defined Benefit (Funded Hybrid)
Employee Contribution 0% (Nil) 10% of Basic + DA 10% of Basic + DA
Employer Contribution Budgetary Appropriation 14% of Basic + DA 18.5% of Basic + DA
Pension Guarantee 50% of last drawn pay No guarantee (market dependent) 50% of last 12 months average basic
Inflation Protection (DR) Yes (AICPI-IW) No (Fixed Annuity yield) Yes (AICPI-IW Dearness Relief)

9. Who Should Opt for UPS? Practical Decision Framework

Under the official implementation rules, existing Central Government employees covered under NPS were provided a structured window to make their election:

When UPS is Ideal:

  • Risk Aversion: Employees who desire peace of mind and do not want their post-retirement monthly pension exposed to stock market downturns or interest rate fluctuations.
  • Inflation Protection: Those who prioritize Dearness Relief indexation to ensure their purchasing power is not eroded over 20–30 years of retirement.
  • Long Service Record: Employees who will comfortably complete 25+ years of qualifying service before superannuation.

When Staying in NPS Might Appeal:

  • Aggressive Equity Allocators: Younger employees who maintain high equity exposure (Active Choice Scheme E up to 75%) and believe market compounding over 30 years will generate a corpus exceeding the 50% defined annuity threshold.
  • Early Exits: Professionals anticipating leaving government service well before 10–20 years to transition to the private sector.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the Unified Pension Scheme (UPS)?

The Unified Pension Scheme (UPS) is a hybrid retirement framework approved by the Union Cabinet and operational from April 1, 2025 for Central Government employees. It combines the defined-benefit certainty of an assured 50% pension with the funded contributory structure of the National Pension System (NPS).

How is the assured pension calculated under UPS?

Employees with a minimum of 25 years of qualifying service receive an assured pension equal to 50% of the average basic pay drawn in the last 12 months prior to superannuation. For qualifying service between 10 and 25 years, the assured pension is calculated on a proportionate pro-rata basis.

What are the contribution rates under UPS for employee and government?

The employee contribution under UPS remains unchanged at 10% of Basic Pay + Dearness Allowance (matching NPS). The Central Government employer contribution is increased from 14% to 18.5% of Basic Pay + DA to fund the sovereign guarantee.

Is Dearness Relief (DR) provided on the UPS pension?

Yes. Unlike market-linked NPS annuities which do not adjust for inflation, the UPS assured pension, family pension, and minimum pension are indexed to inflation through Dearness Relief (DR), calculated semi-annually based on the All India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW).

What is the family pension benefit under UPS upon death?

In the event of the pensioner's demise, the legally entitled family or spouse receives an Assured Family Pension equal to 60% of the pension amount that the employee was receiving immediately prior to death, along with applicable Dearness Relief.
