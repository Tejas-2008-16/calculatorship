Income Tax Slabs in India (FY 2026-27): Complete Rates, Rebates & Surcharge Guide

Written by: Tejas (Independent Personal Finance Creator • YouTube Channel)
Published: March 2026 • Last Fact-Checked: September 2026 • Reading Time: 12 mins
⚠️ Educational Disclaimer: This article is authored by Tejas for educational purposes only. Tejas is not a Chartered Accountant (CA) or certified tax consultant. Tax provisions and slab structures are statutory rules notified by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), Ministry of Finance, Government of India.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • The New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) is the default tax regime in India with an enhanced ₹75,000 Standard Deduction for salaried taxpayers and pensioners.
  • Salaried employees earning up to ₹7,75,000 gross annual income pay ZERO tax due to the Section 87A rebate.
  • Under the New Tax Regime, the top tax bracket is 30% above ₹15,00,000 taxable income, and surcharges on high net-worth individuals are capped at 25%.
  • The Old Tax Regime slabs range from 5% to 30%, retaining deductions like 80C, 80D, and Section 24b Home Loan Interest.
  • A mandatory 4% Health and Education Cess applies across all computed tax liabilities under both regimes.

Introduction: Official Tax Framework for FY 2026-27

Understanding the statutory income tax slabs for Financial Year 2026-27 (Assessment Year 2027-28) is essential for every salaried professional, business owner, consultant, and senior citizen in India. Accurately projecting your tax liability allows you to optimize TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) deductions by your employer, plan advance tax installments to avoid Section 234B/234C interest penalties, and make informed choices between the Old and New Tax Regimes.

The Union Budget has solidified the New Tax Regime as the primary, default taxation path for individual taxpayers. By offering wide, rationalized slabs, an enhanced ₹75,000 standard deduction, and a full Section 87A rebate up to ₹7,00,000 of taxable income, the tax code allows the vast majority of middle-class Indian families to pay zero or low single-digit effective taxes.

In this master reference guide, we provide complete slab breakdowns for both regimes, detail the Section 87A rebate calculation mechanics, analyze high-net-worth surcharge brackets, explain marginal relief formulas, and provide concrete worked tax tables across salary levels.

Section 87A Rebate Mechanics: Why Gross Salary Up to ₹7.75L is Tax-Free

Section 87A of the Income Tax Act provides relief to lower-middle-class earners. Under the New Tax Regime:

  • Any resident individual whose Net Taxable Income does not exceed ₹7,00,000 is entitled to a rebate equal to 100% of their computed tax (up to a maximum of ₹25,000).
  • For salaried employees and pensioners, the statutory Standard Deduction is ₹75,000.
  • Therefore: Gross Annual Salary of ₹7,75,000 - ₹75,000 Standard Deduction = ₹7,00,000 Taxable Income.
  • Tax on ₹7,00,000 is exactly ₹20,000.
  • Section 87A Rebate = -₹20,000.
  • Net Tax Payable = ₹0 (Zero Rupees)!

Surcharges and Marginal Relief for High-Net-Worth Individuals

For high-income earners, an additional Surcharge is levied on the computed income tax:

  • Taxable Income ₹50 Lakh to ₹1 Crore: 10% Surcharge.
  • Taxable Income ₹1 Crore to ₹2 Crore: 15% Surcharge.
  • Taxable Income Above ₹2 Crore: Capped at 25% under the New Tax Regime (vs 37% under the Old Tax Regime).

Marginal Relief: If your taxable income slightly crosses ₹50 Lakh (e.g. ₹50,50,000), the surcharge cannot exceed the extra income earned above ₹50 Lakh. The tax authority automatically deducts marginal relief during ITR computation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the income tax slab rates for the New Tax Regime in FY 2026-27?

For FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28), the New Tax Regime slab rates are: ₹0 to ₹3,00,000: NIL (0%); ₹3,00,001 to ₹7,00,000: 5%; ₹7,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 10%; ₹10,00,001 to ₹12,00,000: 15%; ₹12,00,001 to ₹15,00,000: 20%; Above ₹15,00,000: 30%.

What is the enhanced Standard Deduction for salaried taxpayers in FY 2026-27?

The Standard Deduction for salaried employees and pensioners under the New Tax Regime is ₹75,000. Under the Old Tax Regime, it remains ₹50,000.

How does the Section 87A rebate work under the New Tax Regime?

Under Section 87A in the New Tax Regime, resident individual taxpayers with taxable income up to ₹7,00,000 are entitled to a full tax rebate of up to ₹25,000. When combined with the ₹75,000 standard deduction, a salaried employee with a gross salary of up to ₹7,75,000 pays zero income tax.

What is the surcharge rate for high-income earners in India?

Under the New Tax Regime, surcharges on taxable income are: ₹50L to ₹1Cr: 10%; ₹1Cr to ₹2Cr: 15%; Above ₹2Cr: capped at 25%. Under the Old Tax Regime, the highest surcharge tier is 37% for incomes exceeding ₹5 Crore.

What is Marginal Relief in Indian income tax?

Marginal relief ensures that the extra tax and surcharge payable on income just above a threshold (e.g. ₹7,00,000 or ₹50,00,000) does not exceed the amount of income earned above that threshold.

What is the mandatory Health and Education Cess in India?

A flat 4% Health and Education Cess is applied on the total sum of computed income tax plus applicable surcharge across both tax regimes.
