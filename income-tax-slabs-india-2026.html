Income Tax Slabs in India (FY 2026-27): New vs Old Regime Rates & Rebates

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Default Regime: The New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) is India's statutory default system for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28), featuring an enhanced ₹75,000 Standard Deduction for salaried taxpayers and pensioners.
  • 2. Zero-Tax Threshold: Salaried individuals earning up to ₹7,75,000 gross annual income pay ₹0 income tax under the New Regime via Section 87A full tax rebate.
  • 3. Slab Expansion: The 30% top tax bracket triggers only above ₹15,00,000 taxable income in the New Regime (compared to just ₹10,00,001 under the Old Regime).
  • 4. Marginal Relief: If your taxable income slightly crosses ₹7,00,000 (e.g. ₹7,10,000), statutory marginal relief caps your additional tax so you never pay more tax than the incremental income earned.
  • 5. High-Earner Surcharge Cap: The highest tax surcharge in the New Regime is permanently capped at 25% for incomes above ₹2 Crore, down from 37% in the Old Regime.
  • 6. Universal 4% Cess: A mandatory 4% Health and Education Cess is added to the computed tax plus surcharge under both regimes.

The ₹7.75 Lakh Salary Reality: How You Pay Zero Tax Without Submitting Any Proofs

You earn ₹64,500 every month in your salary account, bringing your gross annual CTC to ₹7,75,000. Under the Old Tax Regime, you would be forced to lock ₹1,50,000 in PPF or ELSS, submit rent agreements to HR (such as claiming HRA on rent paid to parents), buy health insurance policies, and still end up paying tax if your paperwork fell short. Under the New Tax Regime, you submit zero investment proofs and owe exactly ₹0 in tax.

The math is statutory for FY 2026-27: the enhanced ₹75,000 Standard Deduction brings your net taxable income down to exactly ₹7,00,000. At ₹7 Lakh, Section 87A delivers a full ₹25,000 rebate that completely wipes out your computed tax liability. Over 65% of Indian salaried professionals earning under ₹8 Lakh are entitled to a 100% tax-free income simply by choosing the default New Regime.

Marginal Relief Under Section 87A: The Shock Absorber

What happens if your gross salary increases slightly from ₹7,75,000 to ₹7,85,000? After the ₹75,000 standard deduction, your taxable income becomes ₹7,10,000—exactly ₹10,000 above the ₹7 Lakh rebate ceiling.

Under normal slab calculation, you would owe ₹26,000 in base tax. That means an extra ₹10,000 in earnings would cost you ₹27,040 in tax plus cess—a net loss of ₹17,040! To prevent this absurd outcome, the Income Tax Act provides statutory Marginal Relief:

Incremental Income Above ₹7,00,000: ₹10,000

Normal Computed Tax on ₹7,10,000: ₹26,000

Statutory Tax Ceiling: Tax cannot exceed income above ₹7L = ₹10,000

Marginal Relief Granted: ₹26,000 - ₹10,000 = ₹16,000

Final Payable Tax (with 4% Cess): ₹10,400

Surcharge Rates for High Net-Worth Individuals (HNIs)

For taxpayers with taxable income exceeding ₹50 Lakh, the government levies a graduated surcharge on computed income tax:

  • ₹50 Lakh to ₹1 Crore: 10% surcharge on tax.
  • ₹1 Crore to ₹2 Crore: 15% surcharge on tax.
  • Above ₹2 Crore: Under the New Tax Regime, the surcharge is strictly capped at 25%, keeping the peak effective tax rate at 39.0%. In the Old Tax Regime, incomes above ₹5 Crore face a crushing 37% surcharge, driving the peak effective tax rate to 42.74%.

Frequently Asked Questions

If I earn ₹8 Lakh annual salary, do I pay 10% tax on my entire ₹8 Lakh?

No. Income tax in India operates on a progressive bracket system, never as a flat tax on your whole income. On ₹8 Lakh salary, deduct the ₹75,000 standard deduction first, leaving ₹7,25,000 taxable. The first ₹3 Lakh is taxed at 0%, the next ₹4 Lakh is taxed at 5% (₹20,000), and only the remaining ₹25,000 is taxed at 10% (₹2,500). Your total base tax is just ₹22,500 plus 4% cess.

Does a senior citizen get a higher basic exemption limit in the New Tax Regime?

No. Under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), tax slab rates and the basic exemption limit of ₹3,00,000 are identical for all individual taxpayers, regardless of whether you are 25, 65, or 85 years old. The special exemption limits of ₹3 Lakh for senior citizens (60+) and ₹5 Lakh for super senior citizens (80+) exist only in the Old Tax Regime.

Can I deduct the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction if I am a freelance consultant?

No. The ₹75,000 Standard Deduction under Section 16(ia) is strictly restricted to salaried employees receiving salary income and pensioners receiving pension income. Freelancers and consultants report under "Profits and Gains from Business or Profession" and must claim actual business expenses or opt for Presumptive Taxation under Section 44ADA instead.

Do I have to pay tax on bank savings account interest under the New Tax Regime?

Yes. Section 80TTA (which gives up to ₹10,000 deduction on savings account interest) is unavailable under the New Tax Regime. Every rupee of savings account interest and fixed deposit interest is added to your income and taxed at your applicable slab rate.

Is advance tax mandatory for salaried employees whose companies deduct monthly TDS?

If your employer deducts accurate TDS covering your total salary, you do not need to pay advance tax. However, if you earn substantial outside income—such as capital gains from mutual funds/stocks or interest from FDs—and your net unpaid tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year, you must pay quarterly Advance Tax under Section 208 to avoid penal interest under Sections 234B and 234C.

What happens if I fail to file my ITR before the July 31 deadline?

Filing after July 31 subjects you to a late fee under Section 234F (up to ₹5,000 if total income exceeds ₹5 Lakh; ₹1,000 if below ₹5 Lakh), interest on unpaid tax at 1% per month under Section 234A, and permanent forfeiture of your right to carry forward capital losses or business losses.

How to Think About This Yourself

When assessing income tax liabilities across tax brackets, keep these mathematical principles front of mind:

  • Marginal vs Effective Tax Rate: Moving into the 20% or 30% slab never means your entire salary is taxed at that rate. Only the portion of income falling within that specific slice is assessed at the marginal rate, keeping your overall effective tax rate significantly lower.
  • The Section 87A Rebate Cliff: Under the New Tax Regime, taxable income up to ₹7,00,000 (effectively ₹7,75,000 for salaried employees after standard deduction) qualifies for zero tax via Section 87A rebate. Earning even ₹1,000 above that threshold subjects the income to slab rates, moderated by marginal relief.
  • Cess & Surcharge Additions: Always add the mandatory 4% Health & Education Cess on top of the calculated basic tax. For income above ₹50 Lakh, factor in progressive surcharges (10%, 15%, 25%).
