Income Tax Slabs in India (FY 2026-27): New vs Old Regime Rates & Rebates

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 1 March 2026  •  Last reviewed: 24 November 2026  •  Section 115BAC Slabs, ₹75,000 Standard Deduction & ₹12.75L Zero-Tax Threshold

Educational income tax slab rate guide only. Not certified tax advisory. Income tax provisions governed by the Income-tax Act and annual Finance Acts. Read our full disclaimer.

For FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28), the default New Tax Regime under Section 115BAC provides an enhanced standard deduction of ₹75,000 for salaried employees and pensioners. Combined with the enhanced Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 on taxable income up to ₹12,00,000, resident salaried individuals earning up to ₹12,75,000 pay zero income tax (with marginal relief just above ₹12 Lakh). Above this threshold, concessional progressive slabs from 5% to 30% apply without Chapter VI-A deductions.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Default Regime: The New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) is India's statutory default system for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28), featuring an enhanced ₹75,000 Standard Deduction for salaried taxpayers and pensioners.
  • 2. Zero-Tax Threshold: Salaried individuals earning up to ₹12,75,000 gross annual income pay ₹0 income tax under the New Regime via the ₹60,000 Section 87A rebate.
  • 3. Slab Expansion: The 30% top tax bracket triggers only above ₹24,00,000 taxable income in the New Regime (compared to just ₹10,00,001 under the Old Regime).
  • 4. Marginal Relief: If your taxable income slightly crosses ₹12,00,000 (e.g. ₹12,05,000, where base slab tax ₹60,750 is capped at the ₹5,000 excess income plus 4% cess = ₹5,200), statutory marginal relief caps your additional tax so you never pay more tax than the incremental income earned.
  • 5. High-Earner Surcharge Cap: The highest tax surcharge in the New Regime is permanently capped at 25% for incomes above ₹2 Crore, down from 37% in the Old Regime.
  • 6. Universal 4% Cess: A mandatory 4% Health and Education Cess is added to the computed tax plus surcharge under both regimes.

Zero-Tax Reality up to ₹12.75 Lakh: Paying Nil Tax Without Lock-In Proofs

You earn ₹64,500 every month in your salary account, or earn ₹1,06,250/month for an annual salary of ₹12,75,000. Under the Old Tax Regime, you would be forced to lock ₹1,50,000 in PPF or ELSS, submit rent agreements to HR (such as claiming HRA on rent paid to parents), buy health insurance policies, and still end up paying tax if your paperwork fell short. Under the New Tax Regime, you submit zero investment proofs and owe exactly ₹0 in tax.

Statutory math for FY 2026-27 confirms: deducting the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction from a ₹12,75,000 gross salary leaves ₹12,00,000 net taxable income, on which Section 87A delivers a full ₹60,000 rebate reducing income tax to ₹0.

Marginal Relief Under Section 87A: The Shock Absorber

What happens if your gross salary crosses from ₹12,75,000 to ₹12,80,000? After the ₹75,000 standard deduction, your taxable income becomes ₹12,05,000, just ₹5,000 above the ₹12,00,000 rebate ceiling.

Without relief, normal slabs would charge ₹60,750 base tax plus cess for earning an extra ₹5,000! Statutory Marginal Relief prevents this cliff by capping base income tax at the exact excess income over ₹12,00,000:

Incremental Taxable Income Above ₹12,00,000: ₹5,000

Normal Slab Tax on ₹12,05,000: ₹60,750 (₹20k + ₹40k + ₹750)

Marginal Relief Granted: ₹60,750 − ₹5,000 = ₹55,750

Final Payable Tax (₹5,000 + 4% Cess): ₹5,200

Surcharge Rates for High Net-Worth Individuals (HNIs)

For taxpayers with taxable income exceeding ₹50 Lakh, the government levies a graduated surcharge on computed income tax:

  • ₹50 Lakh to ₹1 Crore: 10% surcharge on tax.
  • ₹1 Crore to ₹2 Crore: 15% surcharge on tax.
  • Above ₹2 Crore: Under the New Tax Regime, the surcharge is strictly capped at 25%, keeping the peak effective tax rate at 39.0%. In the Old Tax Regime, incomes above ₹5 Crore face a crushing 37% surcharge, driving the peak effective tax rate to 42.74%.

Frequently Asked Questions

If I earn ₹15 Lakh annual salary, do I pay 15% tax on my entire ₹15 Lakh?

No. Indian income tax works progressively across brackets, never as a flat percentage of your entire gross income (moreover, salaried incomes up to ₹12.75 Lakh attract zero tax under Section 87A). On a ₹15 Lakh salary after the ₹75,000 standard deduction (₹14.25 Lakh taxable), the first ₹4 Lakh pays 0%, the next ₹4 Lakh pays 5% (₹20,000), ₹8–₹12 Lakh pays 10% (₹40,000), and only the final ₹2.25 Lakh pays 15% (₹33,750), totaling ₹97,500 including 4% cess.

Does a senior citizen get a higher basic exemption limit in the New Tax Regime?

No. Under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), tax slab rates and the basic exemption limit of ₹4,00,000 are identical for all individual taxpayers, regardless of whether you are 25, 65, or 85 years old. Senior citizen exemption limits of ₹3 Lakh (60+) and ₹5 Lakh for super senior citizens (80+) exist only in the Old Tax Regime.

Can I deduct the ₹75,000 Standard Deduction if I am a freelance consultant?

No. The ₹75,000 Standard Deduction under Section 16(ia) is strictly restricted to salaried employees receiving salary income and pensioners receiving pension income. Freelancers and consultants report under "Profits and Gains from Business or Profession" and must claim actual business expenses or opt for Presumptive Taxation under Section 44ADA instead.

Do I have to pay tax on bank savings account interest under the New Tax Regime?

Yes. Section 80TTA (which gives up to ₹10,000 deduction on savings account interest) is unavailable under the New Tax Regime. Every rupee of savings account interest and fixed deposit interest is added to your income and taxed at your applicable slab rate.

Is advance tax mandatory for salaried employees whose companies deduct monthly TDS?

If your employer deducts accurate TDS covering your total salary, you do not need to pay advance tax. However, if you earn substantial outside income, such as capital gains from mutual funds/stocks or interest from FDs, and your net unpaid tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year, you must pay quarterly Advance Tax under Section 208 to avoid penal interest under Sections 234B and 234C.

What happens if I fail to file my ITR before the July 31 deadline?

Filing after July 31 subjects you to a late fee under Section 234F (up to ₹5,000 if total income exceeds ₹5 Lakh; ₹1,000 if below ₹5 Lakh), interest on unpaid tax at 1% per month under Section 234A, and permanent forfeiture of your right to carry forward capital losses or business losses.

Actionable Next Step: Calculate Your Exact Take-Home Pay Today

Download your latest monthly payslip. Check your current monthly TDS deduction. Multiply your gross monthly earnings by 12, subtract ₹75,000, and run your taxable number through the progressive slab table above. If your HR is deducting higher TDS because you were defaulted into the Old Regime without submitting rent or 80C proofs, email your payroll team or log in to your HR portal today to update your declaration to the New Tax Regime. That single adjustment will instantly increase your monthly in-hand salary starting next paycheck.
