Child Higher Education Planning in India: Inflation Math & Investment Roadmap

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 10 August 2026  •  Last reviewed: 23 November 2026  •  Higher Education Inflation Math & 3-Stage Asset Allocation Glide Path

Educational child education planning roadmap only. Not certified financial advisory. Target corpus must be personalized for specific degree courses. Read our full disclaimer.

Planning for your child's higher education in India requires modeling an annual education inflation rate of 10% to 12%—nearly double the general CPI. A premier four-year degree (engineering, MBA, medicine, or design) costing ₹25 Lakh today will inflate to approximately ₹1.04 Crore in 15 years. To accumulate this corpus, start an automated monthly equity mutual fund SIP of ₹20,700 compounding at an assumed 12% CAGR, paired with Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) or PPF. Execute a strict 3-stage glide path, de-risking 100% of the corpus into liquid funds and fixed deposits 2 to 3 years before college admission to eliminate market crash risk.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Higher education inflation in India consistently averages 10% to 12% per year - more than double standard CPI inflation. A professional degree costing ₹15 Lakh today will require approximately ₹47 Lakh in 10 years and ₹75 Lakh in 14 years.
  • Traditional "child endowment insurance policies" and child ULIPs deliver meager 4% to 5.5% annual returns - guaranteeing real purchasing power erosion. Avoid them entirely.
  • The mathematical solution is a disciplined equity-led SIP (Nifty 50 Index + Flexi-Cap) in early years, migrating to debt funds and liquid funds as the matriculation date approaches.
  • Implement a mandatory 3-year pre-goal derisking glidepath: shift 30% of accumulated equity corpus into high-quality debt/arbitrage funds each year from age 15 to 18 to protect against severe market crashes right before tuition payments are due.
  • Both parents must maintain adequate pure term insurance coverage (minimum 15 to 20 times annual expenses) so that the child's educational trajectory is fully funded even in the event of an untimely parental demise.

Every Indian parent shares the ambition of providing their child with top-tier education. Unfortunately, the vast majority fund this dream using instruments Fixed to fail: traditional child insurance plans, bank fixed deposits, and recurring deposits. When college admission arrives 15 years later, parents are stunned to discover that fees have outpaced their conservative investments by millions of rupees, forcing them to liquidate retirement assets or burden the student with high-interest education loans (though interest payments can be claimed under Section 80E).

Planning for your child's education requires unsparing mathematical discipline. You are investing against an inflation rate of 10% to 12%, not the headline retail inflation rate of 5%. Here is the detailed guide to calculate, invest, and derisk your child's education fund.

Asset Allocation & Derisking Glidepath

An equity portfolio that fluctuates wildly is an asset when your child is 5 years old, but a lethal threat when your child is 17 and fee payment is due in six months. Never keep your college fund in 100% equity right until the finish line.

  • Age 0 to 12 (Accumulation Phase): 70% to 80% Equity (Nifty 50 Index Fund + Flexi-Cap Fund) + 20% to 30% Debt (PPF / SSY). Maximize growth and ignore short-term volatility.
  • Age 13 to 15 (Transition Phase): Pause incremental equity additions. Rebalance to 50% Equity and 50% Debt/Arbitrage.
  • Age 16 to 18 (Capital Preservation Phase): Systematically transfer (SWP / STP) from equity into Arbitrage Funds, Liquid Funds, or High-Yield Bank FDs. When the first semester fee is demanded, 100% of the tuition fees for all 4 years should be safely resting in capital-protected debt instruments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I avoid Child Insurance Plans marketed by banks?

Child endowment insurance plans bundle low-cover life insurance with conservative debt investments. They levy heavy upfront policy administration and mortality charges, delivering net annual returns of 4.5%-5.5%. At 10% educational inflation, these products guarantee a severe shortfall. Buy a pure Term Life Insurance plan for yourself (costing ₹15,000-₹25,000/yr for ₹1.5-2 Cr cover) and invest the remaining savings directly into low-cost index and mutual funds.
