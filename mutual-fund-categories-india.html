Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • India's 44 SEBI-registered AMCs collectively manage over ₹68 Lakh Crore in AUM as of 2026—the industry has grown 5x in 10 years, driven by SIP adoption from ₹3,000 Crore monthly SIP flows in 2016 to ₹26,000 Crore in 2026.
  • The Balanced Advantage Fund (BAF) category is the most popular hybrid category because it dynamically shifts between equity (20%–80%) and debt based on valuation signals, offering automatic rebalancing without triggering investor-level capital gains tax.
  • Index funds (ETFs and FOFs tracking Nifty 50, Nifty 100, Sensex) now collectively manage over ₹8 Lakh Crore in India — passive investing has grown from 3% of equity AUM in 2016 to over 22% in 2026.
  • International Fund of Funds (FOFs) have an additional layer of overseas fund expense; SEBI has capped total overseas fund investment limits — check current RBI/SEBI limits before investing.
  • Solution-oriented funds (Retirement Fund and Children's Fund) have a 5-year mandatory lock-in but offer no unique tax advantage over standard equity or hybrid funds—most financial planners do not recommend them.

The mutual fund universe in India can feel overwhelming—44 AMCs, 36 SEBI categories, and hundreds of individual schemes. But the structure is more logical than it appears. (If you need a line-by-line directory of every regulatory bucket, review our companion guide to SEBI's 36 Mutual Fund Categories Explained). Every fund in India slots into one of five super-categories defined by SEBI: Equity, Debt, Hybrid, Solution-Oriented, and Other. Understanding where each fund type lives in this taxonomy—and what drives its risk and return profile—removes the guesswork from fund selection.

The 6 Hybrid Fund Categories Explained

  • Conservative Hybrid: 10%–25% equity, 75%–90% debt. For near-retirees or investors with 2–5 year goals who want equity exposure with significant downside protection.
  • Balanced Hybrid: 40%–60% equity, 40%–60% debt. Strict allocation bands; no dynamic switching. Suitable for moderate-risk investors.
  • Aggressive Hybrid: 65%–80% equity, 20%–35% debt. Taxed as equity funds (LTCG 12.5% after 1 year). Popular alternative to pure equity for conservative long-term investors.
  • Dynamic Asset Allocation / Balanced Advantage Fund (BAF): Equity allocation ranges from 0% to 100% based on proprietary valuation models (P/E, P/B ratios). Internal rebalancing does not trigger investor-level capital gains.
  • Multi-Asset Allocation: Mandatory investment in at least 3 asset classes (equity, debt, gold/real estate/commodities) with minimum 10% in each. True diversification across uncorrelated assets.
  • Arbitrage Fund: Simultaneously buys and sells stocks and futures contracts to capture small price differentials. Very low risk; taxed as equity (LTCG after 1 year). Returns similar to liquid funds but with equity taxation advantage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an arbitrage fund better than a liquid fund for parking short-term money?

For investors in the 30% tax bracket, yes—arbitrage funds are more tax-efficient for money parked 12+ months. Liquid fund gains are taxed at your slab rate (30%); arbitrage fund gains held 12+ months are taxed at 12.5% LTCG. However, arbitrage funds carry marginally higher volatility on a daily basis and are unsuitable for money needed within 30 days.

What is an ETF versus a mutual fund FOF? Which is better?

An ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) trades on NSE/BSE in real-time like a stock; it requires a demat and trading account to purchase. A Fund of Funds (FOF) investing in an ETF uses a conventional mutual fund structure, transacted through AMC portals, MF Central, or intermediaries in Statement of Account (SoA) format without requiring a demat account. FOFs carry a second layer of expense ratio (the underlying ETF TER plus the FOF expense ratio), but offer systematic investment facility (SIP) and fractional units.

Illustrative Portfolio Structures Across Horizons

In financial theory, asset allocation frameworks balance growth assets with capital preservation instruments according to investment horizon and personal volatility tolerance. For instance, long-term models often allocate a majority to broad-market index or diversified equity schemes while pairing them with debt or dynamic asset allocation schemes to moderate drawdowns. Individual asset allocations vary widely based on income stability, age, and financial obligations.

How to Think About This Yourself

  • Match scheme category to time horizon: Goals under 1 year align structurally with ultra-short money market instruments (liquid, overnight), whereas pure equity schemes require a minimum 5-to-7 year horizon to manage market cycle drawdown risks.
  • Understand SEBI mandate restrictions: Under SEBI's 2017 circular, an AMC can offer only one scheme per category (with exceptions like Index funds, ETFs, and Sectoral/Thematic funds), preventing duplicate product proliferation.
  • Watch overlapping holdings: Holding multiple funds in the same category (e.g., three Flexi-Cap funds) often leads to significant stock overlap, increasing expense ratios without providing additional diversification.
  • Examine hybrid fund taxation: Equity-oriented hybrids (≥ 65% equity) qualify for equity capital gains tax rules, while debt-oriented hybrids and multi-asset funds with less than 65% equity are taxed at slab rates or indexation-exempt schedules depending on domestic equity thresholds.

Where to Get Personalised Help

Determining the appropriate asset mix between equity, debt, and commodities depends on personal liabilities, dependants, emergency reserves, and psychological risk tolerance. For a tailored asset allocation plan, consult a fee-only SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) via the SEBI RIA Directory. For tax implications across hybrid rebalancing and capital gains, consult a practicing Chartered Accountant (CA).
