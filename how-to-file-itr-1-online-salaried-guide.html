How to File ITR-1 Online in India: Step-by-Step for Salaried Investors

The Pre-Filled Data Trap: Why Form 16 Is Never Enough

The single costliest mistake salaried employees make on the e-Filing portal is blindly accepting the pre-filled numbers and submitting the return in 5 minutes.

The Income Tax Department's pre-filled utility pulls data from your employer's Form 16, but completely omits or underestimates two critical financial streams: (1) accrued interest on your savings accounts and bank fixed deposits, and (2) share/mutual fund dividend credits. The tax department's automated algorithms cross-reference your filed return with your Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS. If your AIS records ₹32,000 in bank interest but your filed ITR-1 reflects ₹0, an automated scrutiny notice under Section 143(1)(a) for "prima facie adjustment" is practically guaranteed.

Always reconcile your Form 16 against your AIS, Form 26AS, and bank interest certificates before clicking 'Proceed to Verification'.

1. Eligibility Criteria for Form ITR-1 (Sahaj)

Form ITR-1 (Sahaj) is a simplified single-form return designed by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) for straightforward salaried individuals. To file using ITR-1, you must satisfy all of the following criteria:

  • Residential Status: You must be an individual who is Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR) in India. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) individuals are ineligible.
  • Income Threshold: Your Total Gross Income across all sources must not exceed ₹50,00,000 during the financial year.
  • Permissible Income Heads:
    1. Income from Salary or Pension.
    2. Income from One Single House Property (where there is no brought-forward loss).
    3. Income from Other Sources (such as savings bank interest, fixed deposit interest, and family pension).
    4. Agricultural Income up to a maximum of ₹5,000.
  • Ineligibility Triggers: You cannot use ITR-1 if you are a Director in a company, hold unlisted equity shares, own foreign assets/foreign bank accounts, have income from business/profession, or have taxable capital gains.

2. Mandatory Documents Checklist (Form 16, AIS, 26AS)

Before logging in to the Income Tax Department portal (incometax.gov.in), gather and cross-verify the following three primary tax verification documents:

Document Source / Issuer Key Data Points to Verify
Form 16 (Part A & B) Your Employer Total salary paid, Chapter VI-A deductions claimed, Standard Deduction (₹75,000 under New Regime), and Total TDS deducted under Section 192.
Form 26AS (Tax Credit Statement) TRACES / E-Filing Portal Verified credit of TDS deducted by employer, banks (Section 194A), and any advance tax/self-assessment tax paid by you.
Annual Information Statement (AIS / TIS) Income Tax Department Portal Savings bank interest from all bank accounts, FD interest, dividend credits, mutual fund transactions, and high-value expenditures.

3. ITR-1 vs ITR-2: The Mutual Fund Capital Gains Trap

A frequent error among salaried mutual fund investors is attempting to file ITR-1 after redeeming mutual fund units or executing Systematic Transfer Plans (STP) during the year:

  • Holding SIPs without Selling: If you only invested in SIPs and did not sell any mutual fund units, you have 0 realized capital gains. You remain 100% eligible to file ITR-1.
  • Any Mutual Fund Redemption (Equity or Debt): If you redeemed mutual fund units or executed an STP/SWP, the AMC generates a Capital Gains Statement. Even if your capital gain is within the ₹1.25 Lakh LTCG exemption limit, you must file Form ITR-2 to report Schedule CG (Capital Gains). Filing ITR-1 while ignoring mutual fund redemptions triggers an automated defective return notice under Section 139(9).

For complete details on capital gains tax thresholds, review our Capital Gains Tax Guide.

4. Step-by-Step E-Filing Walkthrough on e-Portal 2.0

Follow these sequential operational steps to complete your ITR-1 filing directly on the official portal:

  1. Step 1: Portal Login: Visit incometax.gov.in. Log in using your PAN as User ID, password, and registered Aadhaar-linked OTP.
  2. Step 2: Initiate Filing: Navigate to e-File → Income Tax Returns → File Income Tax Return. Select Assessment Year 2027-28 (for FY 2026-27), select Filing Mode as Online, and Status as Individual.
  3. Step 3: Select ITR-1 (Sahaj): Choose ITR-1. Select the primary reason for filing: Taxable income is more than basic exemption limit.
  4. Step 4: Verify Pre-filled Personal Information: Check your contact details, residential status (Resident), and verify that at least one bank account is marked as Validated & Nominated for Refund.
  5. Step 5: Confirm Gross Total Income:
    • Salary Income: Auto-populated from Form 16. Verify Gross Salary, Standard Deduction (₹75,000 for New Regime / ₹50,000 for Old Regime), and Professional Tax (Section 16(iii)).
    • Income from Other Sources: Add savings bank interest (verify against AIS) and any FD interest or dividend credits.
  6. Step 6: Select Tax Regime (New vs Old): The default regime is the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC). If opting for the Old Tax Regime to claim 80C, 80D, or HRA deductions, select 'Yes' to opt out before the July 31 due date. Use our Income Tax Calculator to compare your exact tax liability under both options.
  7. Step 7: Confirm Tax Paid & Verification: Review Schedule TDS against Form 26AS. If tax liability equals ₹0 or a refund is due, proceed to Preview and submit.
  8. Step 8: Instant e-Verification: Complete Aadhaar OTP verification immediately. An unverified return is legally invalid.

5. Worked Example: Reconciling Salary, Savings Interest & TDS

Consider a salaried professional earning ₹12,00,000 Gross Salary in FY 2026-27 under the default New Tax Regime, with ₹18,000 Savings Bank Interest reported in their AIS:

Gross Salary Form 16 Part B ₹12,00,000
Less: Standard Deduction Section 16(ia) [New Regime] - ₹75,000
Net Taxable Salary Salary Head Total ₹11,25,000
Add: Savings Bank Interest AIS Reconciliation (No 80TTA in New Regime) + ₹18,000
Gross Total Income (Taxable) Total Net Income ₹11,43,000
Tax Calculation on Slabs: ₹0 to ₹4 Lakh @ 0% = ₹0
₹4L to ₹8L @ 5% = ₹20,000
₹8L to ₹11.43L @ 10% = ₹34,300		 ₹54,300
Add: 4% Health & Education Cess 4% on ₹54,300 + ₹2,172
Total Tax Payable Final Tax Obligation ₹56,472
Less: Employer TDS Deducted Form 16 / 26AS Credit - ₹54,600
Self-Assessment Tax to Pay Before Filing Section 140A Challan Deposit ₹1,872

By reconciling the ₹18,000 AIS savings interest before filing and paying the ₹1,872 challan, the taxpayer completely eliminates Section 143(1) tax demand notices.

6. Common Mismatch Errors & Section 143(1) Intimations

The Income Tax Department's Centralized Processing Centre (CPC) runs automated algorithmic cross-checks on every filed return. Common triggers for mismatch notices include:

  1. Omitting Savings Account Interest: Many taxpayers believe savings interest is tax-free. Under the New Tax Regime, Section 80TTA deduction is not available, making every rupee of bank interest fully taxable.
  2. Failure to Report Interest on Income Tax Refunds: If you received an income tax refund in the previous financial year with interest under Section 244A, that interest is taxable under 'Income from Other Sources'.
  3. Multiple Form 16s on Job Switch: If you switched employers during the financial year, both employers likely applied the basic exemption limit. Combining both Form 16s during ITR filing often reveals unpaid tax that must be settled via self-assessment tax.

7. Statutory Deadlines & Penalty Framework for FY 2026-27

Filing Milestone Statutory Due Date Applicable Legal Consequence
Original Due Date 31 July 2027 Standard timely filing; carry forward losses allowed.
Belated Return (Sec 139(4)) 31 December 2027 Late fee up to ₹5,000 (Sec 234F) + 1% monthly interest (Sec 234A).
Updated Return (ITR-U, Sec 139(8A)) Up to 24 Months from AY End Additional tax of 25% to 50% on aggregate tax payable.

Editorial Perspective: Mastering ITR-1 Filing Without Traps

In my view, salaried professionals make two consistent, expensive errors when handling ITR-1: trusting pre-filled data without cross-checking, and postponing filing until the final week of July. The Income Tax Department's automated portal algorithms flag discrepancies between employer Form 16 TDS certificates and AIS bank interest feeds with zero human intervention. Resolving a Section 143(1) intimation notice later costs significantly more mental peace than taking 20 minutes to cross-verify numbers in June.

Always complete e-verification via Aadhaar OTP on the day of submission. An unverified ITR is legally considered invalid after 30 days under Rule 14, exposing you to belated return penalties under Section 234F even if you clicked submit well before July 31.

Edge case & caveat: Even if your gross total income is below the basic exemption threshold (e.g., under ₹2.5 Lakh or ₹3.0 Lakh), filing an ITR is legally mandatory under the seventh proviso to Section 139(1) of the Income Tax Act if you deposited more than ₹1 Crore in aggregate in current bank accounts, incurred foreign travel expenditure exceeding ₹2 Lakh, or paid aggregate electricity bills over ₹1 Lakh during the financial year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is strictly eligible to file ITR-1 (Sahaj) for FY 2026-27?

ITR-1 is applicable strictly for resident individuals having total income up to ₹50 Lakh derived from: (1) Salary or Pension, (2) One single House Property, (3) Other Sources (bank interest, dividends), and (4) Agricultural income up to ₹5,000.

Can I file ITR-1 if I sold equity mutual funds or stocks during the financial year?

No. If you realized Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) or Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) from the sale of mutual funds, shares, or property, you cannot use ITR-1. You are required to file ITR-2 (or ITR-3 if you have business/trading income).

What is the penalty for filing an Income Tax Return after the July 31 deadline?

Under Section 234F of the Income Tax Act: A late fee of ₹5,000 is levied if your total income exceeds ₹5 Lakh and you file a belated return by December 31. If your total income is below ₹5 Lakh, the late fee is capped at ₹1,000. Interest under Section 234A at 1% per month also applies on any unpaid tax balance.

What is the difference between AIS (Annual Information Statement) and Form 26AS?

Form 26AS tracks tax deducted at source (TDS), tax collected at source (TCS), and advance tax payments deposited against your PAN. The Annual Information Statement (AIS) is a broader document capturing comprehensive financial transactions reported by banks, stockbrokers, AMCs, and registrars, including savings bank interest, dividend credits, share purchases, and property transactions.
