How the SGB Calculator Models XIRR, Discount Yield & ETF Cost Drag

Notice three distinct variables driving real post-tax outcomes in this tool: your purchase channel (original RBI subscriber vs exchange demat buyer), your actual acquisition price per gram relative to the original RBI nominal issue price, and your income-tax bracket applied against semi-annual cash flows.

1. Why Nominal Coupon Yield Differs From Headline 2.50%

Semi-annual coupons always compute strictly on the original RBI tranche issue price—for example, ₹6,213/g on the February 2024 Series IV digital tranche equals ₹155.325/g annually before income tax. If you purchase the exact bond at a higher secondary quote of ₹7,900/g on NSE or BSE, your effective pre-tax cash yield shrinks to ₹155.325 / ₹7,900 = 1.97% per year. Entering both fields separately lets you model real current yield instead of overstating cash flows.

2. Internal Rate of Return (XIRR) Across Semi-Annual Cash Flows

Instead of averaging nominal returns linearly, the script solves the discounted cash-flow polynomial numerically across 2 × Tenure half-yearly steps: negative acquisition outlay at t = 0, post-tax semi-annual coupons at t = 0.5, 1.0, ..., Y (discounted by marginal slab plus 4% health & education cess), and net after-tax maturity proceeds at t = Y.

3. Comparing Physical Bullion Front-Load Drag vs Gold ETF Compounding

Physical gold coins and jewellery suffer immediate upfront capital dilution (3% GST plus making charges), reducing working metal grams from Day 1 (Outlay / ((1 + Making%) × 1.03)). Gold ETFs preserve full initial allocation with zero GST but compound annual expense-ratio drag ((1 - TER)^Y) every year. Meanwhile, primary 8-year RBI subscribers under Section 70 owe zero redemption capital gains tax—whereas exchange buyers face 12.5% LTCG (+4% cess = 13.0%) above 12 months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Sovereign Gold Bonds tax-free at maturity in FY 2026-27?

Under Section 70 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026 via Finance Act 2026), 8-year maturity capital gains remain exempt strictly for individual investors who subscribed at the original RBI issue and held continuously to final maturity. Secondary exchange buyers (NSE/BSE) and premature exits pay capital gains tax (12.5% LTCG for listed bonds held over 12 months, or marginal slab if held 12 months or less).

How is the 2.5% interest on SGB calculated if gold prices go up or down?

The fixed 2.50% annual coupon is paid semi-annually strictly on the initial nominal RBI issue price per gram—not on current market gold prices. Even if you buy a bond on NSE/BSE at ₹7,800/g whose original RBI issue price was ₹6,213/g, your annual coupon stays 2.50% of ₹6,213 (₹155.33/g/year), and it is taxable at your slab rate under Income from Other Sources with zero TDS.

When can you exit Sovereign Gold Bonds before 8 years?

RBI permits premature redemption after completing the 5th year on semi-annual interest payout dates, or you can sell anytime on NSE/BSE in demat form using limit orders. Note that Budget 2026 rules restrict blanket capital gains exemption strictly to original RBI subscribers redeeming at final 8-year maturity.

How are SGB issue and redemption prices determined?

Both issue prices (during active tranches up to Series IV in February 2024) and final redemption values are fixed as the simple average of closing 999 purity gold prices published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) for the preceding three working days.
