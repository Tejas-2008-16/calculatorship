Crypto & VDA Taxation in India: 30% Tax, 1% TDS & ITR Schedule VDA

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Flat 30% Tax Rate: Under Section 115BBH, all net gains from the transfer of Virtual Digital Assets (Bitcoin, Ethereum, altcoins, NFTs) are taxed at a flat 30% plus 4% cess (effective rate: 31.2%), regardless of whether held for 2 days or 3 years.
  • 2. Zero Loss Set-Off: Losses from one cryptocurrency trade cannot be set off against gains from another cryptocurrency trade, nor against any other income head. Each winning trade is taxed in isolation.
  • 3. No Business Deductions: Only the direct cost of acquisition can be subtracted. Mining electricity, hardware depreciation, trading commissions, and gas fees are strictly disallowed.
  • 4. 1% TDS Under Section 194S: Withheld on all sell/transfer transactions exceeding ₹50,000 (or ₹10,000 for specified persons) annually, creating an unerasable audit trail in your AIS and Form 26AS.
  • 5. Schedule VDA Filing: Earning or trading crypto legally disqualifies you from ITR-1; you must file ITR-2 or ITR-3 with transaction-by-transaction reporting in Schedule VDA.
  • 6. Airdrops and Staking Rewards: Taxed twice: first as regular income under your income tax slab upon receipt, and subsequently at 30% flat tax on any capital gains when sold.

The Mathematical Asymmetry: How You Pay ₹62,400 in Tax on Net Zero Profit

Consider a taxpayer who deposits ₹5,00,000 across domestic crypto exchanges. Suppose they buy Bitcoin and realize a gain of ₹2,00,000. In the same financial year, they buy another virtual token and lose exactly ₹2,00,000. At the end of the year, their total portfolio value is ₹5,00,000—a net financial profit of zero.

Under Section 115BBH of the Income Tax Act, the tax department ignores the ₹2,00,000 loss entirely. Section 115BBH(2)(b) strictly prohibits setting off a loss from one VDA against the gain from any other VDA. The taxpayer owes a flat 30% tax plus 4% cess on the ₹2,00,000 winning trade—a mandatory cash tax bill of ₹62,400 on an investment where they earned ₹0 net profit. This mathematical asymmetry is a critical legal reality for anyone trading virtual assets in India who assumed crypto operates under standard capital gains set-off rules.

Statutory Rules Under Section 115BBH (FY 2026-27)

The taxation of Virtual Digital Assets under the Finance Act framework is governed by five strict statutory pillars:

  • Flat Rate Without Slab Relief: Gains are taxed at a flat 30%. Unlike equity capital gains, you cannot adjust your basic exemption limit (e.g. ₹3 Lakh) against crypto profits. Even if your salary is ₹0, your crypto profits are taxed at 30% from the very first rupee.
  • Only Acquisition Cost Is Deductible: Under Section 115BBH(2)(a), no deduction in respect of any expenditure or allowance is permitted, other than the actual cost of acquisition. Platform trading fees, Ethereum gas fees, mining rig electricity, and laptop depreciation are totally disallowed.
  • Zero Loss Carry-Forward: Unabsorbed crypto losses cannot be carried forward to subsequent assessment years under Section 115BBH(2)(b). A loss realized in FY 2026-27 dies at the end of the financial year.
  • Crypto-to-Crypto Swaps Are Taxable Events: Swapping Bitcoin for Ethereum or USDT is legally treated as two distinct transactions: selling Bitcoin for Indian rupees, followed by purchasing Ethereum. If the Bitcoin transfer has unrealized gains, 30% tax is triggered instantly at the moment of swap.

Section 194S: The 1% TDS Surveillance Mechanism

Many participants mistakenly assume that trading on offshore virtual asset platforms or using decentralized self-custody wallets keeps their transactions invisible from Indian tax authorities. Section 194S dismantled that anonymity:

1% TDS is mandated on all transfer payments for Virtual Digital Assets. FIU-IND registered domestic exchanges automatically deduct 1% from gross sale proceeds and credit it against the investor's PAN. In peer-to-peer (P2P) transactions, the buyer is legally required to deduct 1% TDS and deposit it via Form 26QE. This 1% TDS reflects directly in the taxpayer's Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS. If an AIS records ₹10 Lakh in crypto sales and the filed ITR reflects ₹0 in Schedule VDA, an automated scrutiny notice for undisclosed income under Section 148 is routinely generated.

Schedule VDA: How to Report Crypto Trades in ITR-2 / ITR-3

Crypto transactions cannot be filed under "Other Sources" or general capital gains. The Income Tax Department enforces a dedicated reporting table named Schedule VDA:

  1. Date of Acquisition & Date of Transfer: You must record the exact date you bought the token and the exact date it was sold.
  2. Cost of Acquisition: Enter the purchase price in Indian Rupees based on the conversion rate prevailing on the date of transaction.
  3. Gross Consideration Received: Enter the gross sale value before platform fee deductions.
  4. Net Income from VDA: The portal software automatically calculates gains per transaction. If a trade results in a negative figure, the system sets it to ₹0 for tax computation, ensuring that losses never reduce your taxable gains.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I set off my crypto losses against stock market profits or my salary?

No. Section 115BBH(2)(b) strictly prohibits setting off VDA losses against any other source of income, including salary, house property, business profits, or capital gains from shares and mutual funds. Crypto losses cannot even be set off against other crypto gains.

How are crypto airdrops and staking rewards taxed in India?

They face double taxation: first, when you receive the airdrop or staking tokens, their fair market value is taxed as "Income from Other Sources" at your regular slab rate (Section 56(2)(x)). Later, when you sell those tokens, the incremental profit above that fair market value is taxed at 30% flat tax under Section 115BBH.

Can I file ITR-1 if I only traded ₹5,000 worth of crypto on an Indian app?

No. The Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) strictly disqualifies any taxpayer with VDA transactions from filing ITR-1 (Sahaj). You must file ITR-2 (or ITR-3 if you have business income), and report the transaction in Schedule VDA. Filing ITR-1 will result in a defective return notice under Section 139(9).

How do I claim back excess 1% TDS deducted on my crypto sales?

The 1% TDS deducted under Section 194S is a tax prepayment, not an additional tax. When filing your ITR-2 or ITR-3, the total 1% TDS appears as a tax credit under the "TDS from Other Sources" schedule. It is adjusted against your final tax liability—if your total tax payable is less than the TDS withheld (for example, if you sold at a loss), the excess TDS is refunded directly to your verified bank account.

Do I have to pay tax if I hold crypto in a hardware wallet and don't sell it?

No. Tax under Section 115BBH is triggered strictly upon the "transfer" (sale, exchange, or relinquishment) of a Virtual Digital Asset. Merely holding tokens in a hardware wallet like Ledger or Trezor creates zero tax liability until you execute a sale or swap.

What happens if I don't report crypto transactions in my ITR?

Because domestic exchanges deposit 1% TDS linked to your PAN, the Income Tax Department's AIS database already records your transaction volume. Non-disclosure triggers reassessment proceedings under Section 147, resulting in a penalty of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded under Section 270A, plus 12% annual penal interest under Section 234B.

Actionable Next Step: Download Your Exchange Tax P&L Report Today

Log in to your domestic reporting crypto exchange and download your official "Tax P&L Report for FY 2026-27." Note down the total gross gains from all winning trades—disregarding the losses entirely. Multiply that winning gross profit by 31.2% (30% tax + 4% cess). Check whether your 1% TDS credits in Form 26AS cover that liability. If not, pay the difference via self-assessment tax before submitting your ITR-2 to avoid compounding penal interest.

These guidelines reflect statutory provisions under Sections 115BBH and 194S of the Income Tax Act for FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28); consult a registered Chartered Accountant specializing in digital asset compliance before filing Schedule VDA.

How to Think About This Yourself

Section 115BBH establishes one of the strictest tax frameworks in the Indian tax code. Because loss set-off and carry-forward are completely barred, high-frequency or multi-token trading carries significant tax friction. Always reconcile every trade against your Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS to confirm that all 1% TDS deductions under Section 194S align before submitting Schedule VDA.
