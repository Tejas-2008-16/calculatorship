Crypto & VDA Taxation in India: 30% Tax, 1% TDS & ITR Schedule VDA

Under Section 115BBH of the Income-tax Act, all profits from the transfer of Virtual Digital Assets (cryptocurrency, NFTs, tokens) are taxed at a flat rate of 30% plus 4% cess (effective 31.2%). No deduction other than the direct cost of acquisition is allowed, and losses cannot be set off against other crypto gains or income, nor carried forward. Domestic transfers also attract 1% TDS under Section 194S.

Flat 30% on every profitable trade. No deductions. No loss set-off. Here is exactly how India's virtual digital asset tax works under Section 115BBH - and the one critical mathematical asymmetry most online guides miss.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Flat 30% Tax Rate: Under Section 115BBH, all net gains from the transfer of Virtual Digital Assets (Bitcoin, Ethereum, altcoins, NFTs) are taxed at a flat 30% plus 4% cess (effective rate: 31.2%), regardless of whether held for 2 days or 3 years.
  • 2. Zero Loss Set-Off: Losses from one cryptocurrency trade cannot be set off against gains from another cryptocurrency trade, nor against any other income head. Each winning trade is taxed in isolation.
  • 3. No Business Deductions: Only the direct cost of acquisition can be subtracted. Mining electricity, hardware depreciation, trading commissions, and gas fees are strictly disallowed.
  • 4. 1% TDS Under Section 194S: Withheld on all sell/transfer transactions exceeding ₹50,000 (or ₹10,000 for specified persons) annually, creating an unerasable audit trail in your AIS and Form 26AS.
  • 5. Schedule VDA Filing: Earning or trading crypto legally disqualifies you from ITR-1; you must file ITR-2 or ITR-3 with transaction-by-transaction reporting in Schedule VDA.
  • 6. Airdrops and Staking Rewards: Taxed twice: first as regular income under your income tax slab upon receipt, and subsequently at 30% flat tax on any capital gains when sold.

The Mathematical Asymmetry: How You Pay ₹62,400 in Tax on Net Zero Profit

Think you broke even? Suppose you deposit Rs 5,00,000 into an Indian crypto exchange. First, trading Bitcoin pockets a Rs 2,00,000 profit. Later that year, you buy Solana and lose Rs 2,00,000. Your year-end balance is exactly Rs 5,00,000. Net gain: zero.

In the stock market, you would pay nothing. In crypto, you owe thousands. Under Section 115BBH, the tax department completely disregards your loss. Statutory provisions in Section 115BBH(2)(b) strictly ban setting off a loss from one crypto token against gains from another. Traders must pay 30% tax plus 4% cess on the full Rs 2,00,000 gain. That creates a mandatory cash tax bill of Rs 62,400 on an investment with Rs 0 net profit. That reality catches many retail traders off guard. Many Indian retail traders discovered this only after receiving automated demand notices.

The taxation of Virtual Digital Assets under the Finance Act framework is governed by five strict statutory pillars:

  • Flat Rate Without Slab Relief: Gains are taxed at a flat 30%. Unlike equity capital gains, you cannot adjust your basic exemption limit (e.g. ₹3 Lakh) against crypto profits. Even if your salary is ₹0, your crypto profits are taxed at 30% from the very first rupee.
  • Only Acquisition Cost Is Deductible: Under Section 115BBH(2)(a), no deduction in respect of any expenditure or allowance is permitted, other than the actual cost of acquisition. Platform trading fees, Ethereum gas fees, mining rig electricity, and laptop depreciation are totally disallowed.
  • Zero Loss Carry-Forward: Unabsorbed crypto losses cannot be carried forward to subsequent assessment years under Section 115BBH(2)(b). A loss realized in FY 2026-27 dies at the end of the financial year.
  • Crypto-to-Crypto Swaps Are Taxable Events: Swapping Bitcoin for Ethereum or USDT is legally treated as two distinct transactions: selling Bitcoin for Indian rupees, followed by purchasing Ethereum. Whenever the transferred Bitcoin carries unrealized gains, 30% tax is triggered instantly at the moment of swap.

Section 194S: The 1% TDS Surveillance Mechanism

Many investors mistakenly believe that trading on foreign exchanges (like Binance or Bybit) or using decentralized self-custody wallets (like MetaMask) keeps their transactions invisible from Indian tax authorities. Enacted in 2022, Section 194S dismantled that anonymity:

1% TDS is mandated on all transfer payments for Virtual Digital Assets. Domestic Indian exchanges automatically deduct 1% from your gross sale proceeds and credit it against your PAN. In P2P (peer-to-peer) transactions, the buyer is legally required to deduct 1% TDS and deposit it via Form 26QE. All 1% TDS entries reflect directly in your Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS. When your AIS records ₹10 Lakh in crypto sales and your filed ITR reflects ₹0 in Schedule VDA, an automated scrutiny notice for undisclosed income under Section 148 is Fixed.

Schedule VDA: How to Report Crypto Trades in ITR-2 / ITR-3

You cannot hide crypto trades under "Other Sources" or general capital gains. CBDT reporting mandates a dedicated table named Schedule VDA:

  1. Date of Acquisition & Date of Transfer: You must record the exact date you bought the token and the exact date it was sold.
  2. Cost of Acquisition: Enter the purchase price in Indian Rupees based on the conversion rate prevailing on the date of transaction.
  3. Gross Consideration Received: Enter the gross sale value before platform fee deductions.
  4. Net Income from VDA: The portal software automatically calculates gains per transaction. Whenever a trade produces a loss, the official tax utility sets it to zero for tax computation, ensuring that losses never reduce your taxable gains.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I set off my crypto losses against stock market profits or my salary?

No. By law, Section 115BBH(2)(b) prohibits setting off VDA losses against any other source of income, including salary, house property, business profits, or capital gains from shares and mutual funds. Crypto losses cannot even be set off against other crypto gains.

How are crypto airdrops and staking rewards taxed in India?

They face double taxation: first, when you receive the airdrop or staking tokens, their fair market value is taxed as "Income from Other Sources" at your regular slab rate (Section 56(2)(x)). Later, when you sell those tokens, the incremental profit above that fair market value is taxed at 30% flat tax under Section 115BBH.

Can I file ITR-1 if I only traded ₹5,000 worth of crypto on an Indian app?

No. Official CBDT instructions disqualify any taxpayer with VDA transactions from filing ITR-1 (Sahaj). You must file ITR-2 (or ITR-3 if you have business income), and report the transaction in Schedule VDA. Filing ITR-1 will result in a defective return notice under Section 139(9).

How do I claim back excess 1% TDS deducted on my crypto sales?

TDS deducted at 1% under Section 194S operates as a tax prepayment, not an additional tax. When filing your ITR-2 or ITR-3, the total 1% TDS appears as a tax credit under the "TDS from Other Sources" schedule. You adjust this credit against your final tax liability - if your total tax payable is less than the TDS withheld (for example, if you sold at a loss), the excess TDS is refunded directly to your verified bank account.

Do I have to pay tax if I hold crypto in a hardware wallet and don't sell it?

No. Liability under Section 115BBH triggers strictly upon the "transfer" (sale, exchange, or relinquishment) of a Virtual Digital Asset. Merely holding tokens in a hardware wallet like Ledger or Trezor creates zero tax liability until you execute a sale or swap.

What happens if I don't report crypto transactions in my ITR?

Because domestic exchanges deposit 1% TDS linked to your PAN, the Income Tax Department's AIS database already records your transaction volume. Non-disclosure triggers reassessment proceedings under Section 147, resulting in a penalty of up to 200% of the tax sought to be evaded under Section 270A, plus 12% annual penal interest under Section 234B.

Actionable Next Step: Download Your Exchange Tax P&L Report Today

Log in to your crypto exchange (CoinDCX, WazirX, CoinSwitch, or Mudrex) and download your "Tax P&L Report for FY 2026-27." Note down the total gross gains from all winning trades - disregarding the losses entirely. Multiply that winning gross profit by 31.2% (30% tax + 4% cess). Check whether your 1% TDS credits in Form 26AS cover that liability. Otherwise, pay the difference via self-assessment tax before submitting your ITR-2 to avoid compounding penal interest.
