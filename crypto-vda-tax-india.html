Crypto & VDA Taxation in India: 30% Tax, 1% TDS & ITR Schedule VDA

Flat 30% on every profitable trade. No deductions. No loss set-off. Here is exactly how India's virtual digital asset tax works under Section 115BBH — and the one critical mathematical asymmetry most online guides miss.

Crypto tax rules in India have evolved multiple times since 2022 — verify all provisions against current Finance Act circulars and CBDT notifications before filing your Income Tax Return.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Flat 30% Tax: All net income from the transfer of any Virtual Digital Asset is taxed at 30% (plus 4% cess and surcharge) under Section 115BBH, regardless of your holding period.
  • Zero Loss Set-Off: Losses from one crypto asset cannot be set off against gains from another crypto asset, nor against salary, capital gains, or business income.
  • No Expense Deductions: Only the direct cost of acquisition can be subtracted from sale proceeds. Mining rigs, electricity, gas fees, and platform commissions are not deductible.
  • 1% TDS (Section 194S): Deducted on all sell transactions exceeding ₹50,000 (or ₹10,000 for non-specified persons) per financial year. Reflected in Form 26AS/AIS.
  • Mandatory ITR-2 or ITR-3: Taxpayers with VDA transactions cannot use the simple ITR-1 (Sahaj). You must report each trade individually in Schedule VDA.

Crypto gains in India are taxed at a flat 30% — full stop. The holding period does not matter. Whether you held Bitcoin for three days or three years, the statutory rate is identical. This surprises investors accustomed to the equity tax framework where long-term gains attract a lower 12.5% rate. The rule was introduced via Section 115BBH, inserted into the Income Tax Act by the Finance Act 2022 (effective FY 2022-23), and it remains the governing standard for FY 2026-27.

But the rate is actually the simpler part. The provision that trips up most Indian crypto investors is this: you cannot set off a loss from one virtual digital asset (VDA) transaction against a gain from another. Not within the same asset class, not against business income, not in future years. Each transaction stands alone.

1. What Counts as a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA)?

Section 2(47A) of the Income Tax Act defines a Virtual Digital Asset as any information, code, number, or token generated through cryptographic means, including non-fungible tokens (NFTs). The Central Government possesses statutory authority to notify additional assets as VDAs — a power it has used to encompass cryptocurrencies broadly. In practice, this covers Bitcoin, Ethereum, all altcoins, NFTs, gaming tokens with monetary value, and decentralized finance (DeFi) protocol tokens.

It does not cover gift cards, customer loyalty points, or Indian-rupee-denominated digital payments (such as UPI, NEFT, or RTGS transactions).

2. The 30% Tax: What Section 115BBH Actually Mandates

The core statutory requirements enacted under Section 115BBH encompass five non-negotiable rules:

  • Statutory Rate: Flat 30% on gross transfer profits (plus 4% health & education cess and applicable surcharge — pushing the effective marginal rate to 35.88% in the highest income tax slab).
  • Exclusive Deduction: Only the direct Cost of Acquisition is deductible. No deductions are permitted for mining hardware depreciation, electricity, broadband bills, exchange fees, or advisory charges.
  • No Loss Set-Off: A loss incurred from the transfer of a VDA cannot be set off against income from the transfer of another VDA, nor against any other head of income (such as salary or house property).
  • No Loss Carry-Forward: Crypto losses cannot be carried forward to subsequent assessment years to reduce future tax liabilities.
  • Zero Basic Exemption Threshold: There is no basic threshold exemption or ₹1.25 Lakh annual relief comparable to Section 112A for equity. The 30% tax applies from the first rupee of profit realized.

3. The Loss That Cannot Help You — A Worked Numerical Illustration

To understand why Section 115BBH is mathematically punitive, let us examine an investor executing two trades during the financial year:

Position Details Cost of Acquisition Sale Consideration Economic Result Taxable Income (Sec 115BBH)
Position A (Bitcoin) ₹3,00,000 ₹5,00,000 + ₹2,00,000 Gain ₹2,00,000 (Taxed @ 30%)
Position B (Altcoin X) ₹4,00,000 ₹1,00,000 - ₹3,00,000 Loss ₹0 (Loss Disallowed)
Total Net Outcome ₹7,00,000 ₹6,00,000 Net Loss of -₹1,00,000 Total Tax Payable: ₹62,400 (30% + 4% cess)

The Arithmetic Reality: Despite losing ₹1,00,000 in real economic cashflow across the two portfolios combined, the taxpayer owes ₹62,400 in direct income tax. The ₹3,00,000 loss from Position B simply ceases to exist for tax purposes.

4. 1% TDS Under Section 194S & Reconciliation

Separately from the 30% tax, every transfer of a virtual digital asset triggers a 1% Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) under Section 194S. This is an advance withholding mechanism designed to create an immutable audit trail on the blockchain for the Income Tax Department:

  • Applicable Threshold: TDS applies when aggregate transfers exceed ₹50,000 in a financial year for specified persons (individuals without business income) or ₹10,000 for corporate and business entities.
  • Indian Crypto Exchanges: Domestic FIU-registered exchanges (such as CoinDCX, WazirX) deduct 1% TDS automatically before transferring the net fiat settlement to your linked bank account.
  • Peer-to-Peer (P2P) & Foreign Exchanges: If trading on decentralized exchanges (DEXs) or overseas exchanges (e.g. Binance), the resident buyer or seller is personally obligated to remit 1% TDS to the Central Government using Challan Form 26QE within 30 days of the end of the month.
  • Claiming Credit in ITR: All deducted TDS reflects in your Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS) under Section 194S. You claim this withholding tax as a direct credit against your final 30% liability.

5. How to Report Crypto in ITR: Schedule VDA Walkthrough

Taxpayers with any VDA transfers are statutorily ineligible for ITR-1 (Sahaj). Salaried professionals must file ITR-2, while individuals with business or professional earnings must file ITR-3. Both forms contain the mandatory Schedule VDA table:

  1. Date of Acquisition: The precise calendar date when the crypto unit or NFT was bought or credited.
  2. Date of Transfer: The date on which the asset was sold, swapped, or redeemed.
  3. Head of Income: Typically categorized under Income from Other Sources for individual investors, or Business Income for high-volume automated traders.
  4. Cost of Acquisition: Purchase cost in Indian Rupees (INR) based on the exchange rate prevailing on the date of transaction.
  5. Consideration Received: Gross sale realization in INR before TDS deductions.
  6. Net Income from Transfer: Calculated automatically. Profitable lines are tallied; loss-making lines are capped at ₹0 and cannot offset neighboring rows.

6. Receiving Crypto: Gifts, Airdrops & Staking Rewards

Non-purchase acquisitions of crypto carry specialized tax implications under Section 56(2)(x):

  • Crypto Gifts: Virtual digital assets received without consideration whose fair market value exceeds ₹50,000 in an aggregate financial year are fully taxed as "Income from Other Sources" at your normal slab rate. (Exemptions apply only for gifts received from specified immediate blood relatives or on occasions of marriage).
  • Airdrops & Staking Yields: Airdrops and proof-of-stake rewards are treated as income at fair market value on the date of credit into your wallet (taxed at personal slab rates). When you subsequently sell those tokens, the 30% tax under Section 115BBH applies to any appreciation above that credited value.
  • Pre-2022 Holdings: VDAs purchased prior to April 1, 2022 enjoy no grandfathering provisions. Any sale executed in FY 2026-27 is fully subject to Section 115BBH.

7. Editorial Perspective & Policy Critique

In my view, the statutory combination of a flat 30% tax rate with a strict prohibition on loss set-off represents the most punitive tax architecture in India's modern fiscal history. An investor who finishes a financial year ₹1 Lakh poorer across their overall portfolio can still face an enforceable tax demand of ₹60,000+ on individual winning trades. No other asset class in India operates under this asymmetric design — equity traders can set off short-term capital losses against capital gains, debt fund holders can offset losses, and even speculative derivative traders enjoy set-off mechanisms. While the policy was clearly engineered by the Ministry of Finance to discourage speculative retail capital flight into unbacked digital currencies, the practical consequence is that retail investors must evaluate crypto solely on an after-tax, gross-gain basis.

Edge case & caveat: Decentralized wallet swaps (e.g. swapping BTC directly for ETH on Uniswap or MetaMask) constitute a taxable transfer under Section 2(47A) even if no fiat Indian Rupee ever enters your bank account. The exchange of one VDA for another triggers Section 115BBH on the gain of the disposed asset, calculated using fair market value in INR at the timestamp of the swap. Taxpayers who assume that "holding everything in on-chain wallets" postpones tax liability risk severe penalties under Section 270A for under-reporting income.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I offset a crypto loss against a crypto gain in India?

No. Section 115BBH explicitly bars setting off any loss from one VDA transaction against gains from another VDA transaction. Each profitable trade is taxed independently at 30%, regardless of other losing trades in the same financial year.

Does the 30% VDA tax apply to NFTs and gaming tokens?

Yes. Section 2(47A) of the Income Tax Act defines a virtual digital asset broadly to include any cryptographic asset, non-fungible token, or tokenized digital representation. The 30% flat tax and no-loss-offset rule apply to all of them.

What if I receive crypto as salary or as a gift?

Crypto received as salary is taxed as perquisite income under your regular slab rates at the time of receipt. Crypto received as a gift is taxable under Section 56(2)(x) if fair market value exceeds ₹50,000 in a year. When you later sell that crypto, the 30% flat tax applies to the incremental gain above that credited value.

Is the 1% TDS deducted by the exchange or by the buyer?

On a registered domestic crypto exchange, the exchange itself withholds 1% TDS before crediting fiat proceeds. In peer-to-peer (P2P) transactions where you are the buyer, you are statutorily responsible for withholding 1% TDS and depositing it via Challan 26QE under Section 194S.
