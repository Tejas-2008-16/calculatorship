What is Inflation and How Does It Affect Your Money? (Simple Guide for Indians)

Have you ever noticed that the same ₹500 grocery bag that felt heavy a decade ago now fits easily into one hand? That is inflation — silently eroding the buying power of idle cash every single day.

August 18, 2026 By Tejas
← Back to Articles

Have you ever noticed that the same &8377;500 grocery bag that felt heavy 10 years ago now fits into your hand? Or that your parents talk about how a movie ticket used to cost &8377;20 when they were young?

That is inflation — and it is happening to your money right now, quietly, every single day.

What Exactly is Inflation?

Inflation simply means that the prices of goods and services go up over time. When prices rise, each rupee in your pocket can buy a little less than it could before. In other words, the real purchasing power of your money falls.

India's average inflation rate has hovered around 5% to 6% per year over the last decade. That sounds modest, but compounded over time, its effects are dramatic.

If inflation averages 6% per year:

  • What costs &8377;100 today will cost &8377;134 in 5 years
  • What costs &8377;100 today will cost &8377;179 in 10 years
  • What costs &8377;100 today will cost &8377;321 in 20 years

So if your savings sit idle in a standard bank account earning 3.5% interest while inflation runs at 6%, your wealth is eroding by approximately 2.5% every year in real terms.

Why Does Inflation Happen?

  • Demand-Pull (More money chasing goods): As incomes and spending power rise, demand exceeds production capacity, prompting sellers to raise prices.
  • Cost-Push (Rising producer costs): When fuel, raw materials, or transport become expensive, businesses pass increased costs directly to consumer retail prices.
  • Global Macro Factors: Import costs for commodities like crude oil and currency exchange shifts impact domestic pricing.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) manages interest rates to keep domestic inflation within a target band of 2% to 6%.

The Inflation Trap: Your Savings Account Is Losing Value

Most individuals keep substantial emergency funds in savings accounts yielding 3% to 3.5% interest. But if consumer price inflation is 5.5%, you are moving backwards:

The Math of Idle Cash

You keep &8377;5 lakhs in a savings account. At 3.5% interest, after 10 years it grows to &8377;7.05 lakhs. But at 5.5% annual inflation, you would need &8377;8.56 lakhs just to purchase what &8377;5 lakhs bought originally. You saved diligently, yet ended up with lower purchasing power.

Fixed Deposits Are Not Much Better

Bank FDs offer ~6.5% to 7.5% gross interest, but interest is taxed at your income slab. For someone in the 20% slab:

  • FD Gross Interest: 7.0%
  • Taxes (20%): -1.4%
  • Post-Tax Return: 5.6%
  • Inflation: ~5.5%
  • Net Real Return: ~0.1%

The Only Real Way to Beat Inflation: Growth Assets

To preserve and expand your wealth against inflation, your portfolio requires assets that historically outpace consumer price increases:

  • Equity Mutual Funds: Indian stock markets (Nifty 50) have historically delivered 12% to 14% CAGR over 10+ year periods. Even after 12.5% LTCG tax, real returns comfortably clear inflation by 5% to 6% annually.
  • Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs): Gold matches inflation over multi-decade cycles, and SGBs pay an additional 2.5% annual interest.
  • Real Estate: Commercial and residential property often appreciates alongside inflation, though it requires high capital outlay and lacks liquidity.

How Much More Will You Need at Retirement?

If your household currently requires &8377;50,000 per month for living expenses, future inflation (at 5.5%) shifts the requirements:

  • In 10 years: &8377;85,000 per month
  • In 20 years: &8377;1.45 lakh per month
  • In 30 years: &8377;2.47 lakh per month

This is why calculators recommend retirement targets of &8377;3 crore to &8377;5 crore — to sustain future inflated living expenses.

Practical Steps to Protect Yourself from Inflation

  1. Start a disciplined monthly SIP: Even &8377;1,000/month in an index fund compounds at rates that outpace inflation over a decade.
  2. Keep only 3–6 months of expenses in cash/FD: Retain cash purely for liquidity and emergencies, not long-term investment.
  3. Use Annual Step-Up SIPs: Increase your investment amount by 10% each year as your income grows to combat lifestyle inflation.
