How to Read Your Mutual Fund Statement (CAMS/KFintech CAS Statement Decoded for Indian Investors)

By tejascopywriter Published: June 2025 Last Updated: July 2026
← Back to Articles

Every month, millions of Indian retail investors receive a password-protected PDF document in their email inbox titled "Consolidated Account Statement" (CAS). It comes from Registrar and Transfer Agents (RTAs) like CAMS or KFintech, or directly from NSDL and CDSL depositories.

If you open this document, you are greeted with dense financial grids, cryptic jargon like NAV, Folio, ISIN, STT, Stamp Duty, and two completely different performance percentages: Absolute Return and XIRR. Most investors skim through the bold total balance figure at the bottom and close the PDF. But here is the thing: ignoring the fine print on your statement means missing crucial data about expense ratios, tax harvesting timelines, and unit allocation accuracy.

In this comprehensive guide, we will break down every section of your mutual fund CAS statement into plain, actionable language so you can audit your portfolio like a seasoned financial professional.

1. What Is a CAS Statement and Who Sends It?

A Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) is a single unified document generated under SEBI guidelines that combines all your mutual fund holdings across different Asset Management Companies (AMCs) into one master view. Whether you hold SIPs with HDFC Mutual Fund, Parag Parikh, SBI, or ICICI Prudential, your CAS brings them together under your Permanent Account Number (PAN).

In India, two primary Registrar and Transfer Agents (RTAs) process mutual fund transactions:

  • CAMS (Computer Age Management Services): Handles fund houses such as HDFC, ICICI Prudential, SBI, DSP, Kotak, and PPFAS.
  • KFintech (formerly Karvy Fintech): Serves fund houses like Axis, Nippon India, Mirae Asset, UTI, and Motilal Oswal.

In addition, if you hold mutual funds in Demat form (through platforms like Zerodha Coin), NSDL or CDSL issues a Depository CAS combining your equity shares, ETFs, and mutual fund holdings.

2. How to Request Your Official CAS Statement Online

If you are not receiving your statement automatically by email, you can request a consolidated statement free of charge in less than two minutes:

  1. Visit the official CAMS Online CAS portal (camsonline.com) or the myCAMS app.
  2. Select "CAS - Portfolio Statement".
  3. Choose your statement type: Detailed Statement (shows all transactions) or Summary Statement.
  4. Enter your registered Email ID, PAN card number, and set a password for the PDF file (usually your PAN in lowercase or a custom combination).
  5. Select the date range (e.g., Specific Period, Current Financial Year, or Inception to Date) and click Submit.

Within 5 to 10 minutes, an encrypted PDF will arrive in your email inbox.

3. The 5 Key Sections of Your Mutual Fund CAS Statement Explained

Section 1: Folio Number and Fund House Details

A Folio Number is a unique account number assigned by an AMC to group your investments in their fund house. Think of it like a bank account number for a specific mutual fund company. Your statement will list your full name, PAN, KYC status (VERIFIED), registered bank account details, and nominee details. Always verify that your bank IFSC code and nominee name are listed correctly under each folio.

Section 2: Net Asset Value (NAV)

Net Asset Value (NAV) represents the market price of one single unit of a mutual fund scheme on a given business day. Your statement displays two NAV figures:

  • Purchase NAV: The NAV at which your units were purchased on your SIP auto-debit dates.
  • Current Market NAV: The official closing NAV published by the AMC at the end of the statement period.

Section 3: Units Held vs Units Redeemed

This section tracks your transaction history down to four decimal places. When your monthly SIP of ₹5,000 is processed, the AMC deducts applicable government stamp duty (0.005%) and divides your net invested amount by that day's NAV to calculate your unit allocation. If you switch funds or redeem capital, the deducted units appear as negative entries.

Section 4: Invested Value vs Current Valuation

Your statement presents two critical monetary figures at the bottom of each scheme header:

  • Cost / Total Invested Amount: The cumulative sum of all monthly SIP deposits or lumpsum capital you have transferred into the fund.
  • Current Market Value: Total Units Held multiplied by the Latest Available NAV. The difference between Current Market Value and Invested Amount is your total un-realized capital gain or loss.

Section 5: Absolute Returns vs XIRR

Here is where most investors get confused. Your statement displays two distinct percentage figures. Let me explain the vital distinction between them.

Critical Insight: Absolute returns only measure simple price growth from start to finish without accounting for cash flow timing. For regular SIP investments made every month, XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return) is the only accurate measure of your annualized compounding return.

4. Absolute Return vs XIRR: The Math Indian Investors Miss

Let's look at why relying on absolute return figures on your statement can lead to false conclusions about fund performance.

Suppose you start a monthly SIP of ₹10,000 for 3 years (36 months). Your total out-of-pocket investment is ₹3,600,000. After 3 years, your CAS shows a current market value of ₹4,50,000.

If you calculate simple absolute return:

Absolute Return = ((₹4,50,000 - ₹3,60,000) / ₹3,60,000) × 100 = 25.00%

Looking at 25%, you might think your money grew at 25% per year. But that is mathematically incorrect! You did not deposit ₹3,60,000 on Day 1. You deposited ₹10,000 every single month. The last ₹10,000 deposit was in the market for only 30 days, while the first deposit was in the market for 36 months.

When calculated using XIRR (which accounts for the exact transaction date of every installment), your actual annualized return is approximately 14.8% CAGR. When evaluating equity fund performance, always look at the XIRR column on your CAS statement.

5. Practical Example: Walkthrough of a ₹5,000/Month SIP CAS Entry

Let's examine how a 6-month transaction ledger appears in a standard CAMS CAS statement for a retail investor running a ₹5,000 monthly SIP in a Flexi Cap Fund:

Date Transaction Description Amount (₹) NAV (₹) Units Purchased Total Balance Units
05-Jan-2026 SIP Auto Debit Purchase ₹5,000 ₹100.00 49.9750 49.9750
05-Feb-2026 SIP Auto Debit Purchase ₹5,000 ₹95.00 52.6052 102.5802
05-Mar-2026 SIP Auto Debit Purchase ₹5,000 ₹105.00 47.5952 150.1754
05-Apr-2026 SIP Auto Debit Purchase ₹5,000 ₹110.00 45.4318 195.6072
05-May-2026 SIP Auto Debit Purchase ₹5,000 ₹102.00 48.9950 244.6022
05-Jun-2026 SIP Auto Debit Purchase ₹5,000 ₹115.00 43.4565 288.0587

Statement Summary at End of Period:

  • Total Invested Amount: ₹30,000
  • Total Units Accumulated: 288.0587 units
  • Latest Closing NAV (05-Jun-2026): ₹115.00
  • Current Portfolio Value: 288.0587 × ₹115.00 = ₹33,126.75
  • Unrealized Profit: ₹3,126.75 (+10.42% Absolute, ~21.4% XIRR)

Notice how unit allocation varies each month based on NAV fluctuations. When NAV fell to ₹95 in February, the investor automatically accumulated 52.6 units (Rupee Cost Averaging in action).

6. Statement Red Flags: What to Check Every Quarter

Whenever you open a fresh CAS statement, run this quick 3-point security checklist:

  1. Plan Type Check: Ensure your schemes are labeled as "Direct Plan - Growth" rather than "Regular Plan". Regular plans carry higher expense ratios that pay agent commissions out of your money.
  2. KYC & Contact Audit: Verify that your Mobile Number, Email, and Bank Account number are up to date. If an old bank account is listed under a folio, your future redemption payouts could bounce.
  3. Unrecognized Folio Alert: If you see a folio or scheme you never invested in, contact CAMS or KFintech customer support immediately to verify if a transaction error occurred.

If you hold tax-saving funds, your CAS also lists the exact lock-in expiry date for each installment, making it easy to plan tax harvesting under Section 80C. For details on optimizing tax savings, read our guide on ELSS Funds Tax Saving Strategy.

Key Takeaways

  • A CAS statement consolidates all your mutual fund holdings across CAMS and KFintech RTAs into one master view.
  • For SIP investments, ignore absolute returns and always evaluate performance using XIRR.
  • Check that all your schemes are under Direct Plan - Growth to avoid hidden commission deductions.
  • Verify your bank details, mobile number, and nominee assignment across all folios annually.
  • Request a fresh CAS statement free of charge anytime from the CAMS or KFintech web portals.
