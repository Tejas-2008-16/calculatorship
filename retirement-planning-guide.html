A Practical Retirement Planning Guide for Indian Investors

Retirement planning in India requires tackling two compounding forces: rising life expectancy and medical inflation. Relying solely on traditional savings accounts or fixed deposits guarantees purchasing power erosion. Here is a step-by-step framework for building a retirement corpus that outlasts you.

July 13, 2026 By tejascopywriter
← Back to Articles

Accounting for Post-Retirement Inflation

When planning for retirement in India, you have to fight two distinct economic realities: rising life expectancy and relentless medical inflation.

If your household spends ₹60,000 per month today, and general inflation averages 6% per year, maintaining that exact same lifestyle in 20 years will require **₹1.92 Lakhs per month**. If you retire at 60 and expect to live until 85 or 90, your retirement corpus must fund 25 to 30 years of inflation-adjusted monthly income!

The 25x Rule for India

Target Corpus = (Annual Expenses at Retirement Age) × 25 to 30 years.
For example: If your annual living expenses at age 60 will be ₹20 Lakhs/year, your baseline target retirement corpus should be **₹5 Crore to ₹6 Crore**.

How SWP (Systematic Withdrawal Plan) Replaces Pension

During your working years, you build your corpus using monthly SIPs. When you retire, you flip the switch from accumulation to income distribution using a **Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP)**.

An SWP acts as a self-made pension: it automatically redeems a fixed rupee amount from your mutual fund portfolio on a set date each month and deposits it directly into your savings account. Unlike traditional bank FDs where 100% of interest is taxed at your income slab rate, an SWP only taxes the *capital gains portion* of each withdrawal — making it vastly more tax-efficient!

Building Your Retirement Arsenal (NPS + Equity SIP)

Here are the core building blocks of a robust Indian retirement plan:

  1. National Pension System (NPS): Gives you low-cost equity exposure (up to 75% under Active Choice) and an extra ₹50,000 tax deduction under Section 80CCD(1B). At age 60, 60% of the corpus can be withdrawn 100% tax-free!
  2. Equity Mutual Fund SIPs: Provide complete liquidity, tax-efficient LTCG rules (up to ₹1.25L/yr tax-free), and high long-term compounding growth.
  3. Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS) / RBI Bonds: Post-60, park a portion in government-backed guaranteed yield options to cover essential month-to-month living expenses.
