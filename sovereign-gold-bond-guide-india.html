Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) pay 2.5% annual interest on the issue price in addition to full gold price appreciation — making them the only gold investment instrument in India that simultaneously earns income and tracks gold prices.
  • The most tax-significant feature of SGBs: capital gains on redemption at maturity (8 years) are completely tax-free under Section 47 of the Income Tax Act — no LTCG, no TDS.
  • The RBI Government has suspended new SGB issuances since late 2024; fresh tranches have not been announced for FY 2025-26 or 2026-27. Existing SGBs can be traded on NSE/BSE before maturity via secondary market.
  • SGBs are denominated in grams of gold (minimum 1 gram, maximum 4 kg per financial year for individuals); the issue/redemption price is linked to the IBJA (India Bullion and Jewellers Association) average gold rate.
  • The 2.5% semi-annual interest payments are taxable as "income from other sources" at your personal slab rate — only the capital gains component at maturity is tax-free.

Among all methods of investing in gold in India — physical coins and bars, gold ETFs, gold mutual funds, digital gold, and Sovereign Gold Bonds — SGBs are uniquely superior for long-term investors who can commit to the 8-year maturity window. No physical storage risk, no GST on purchase, a 2.5% annual interest income, and zero capital gains tax at maturity. No other gold instrument in India matches this combination.

The SGB Maturity Tax Math: Why 8 Years Matters

Assume you invested ₹50,000 in an SGB in 2018 when gold was priced at ₹3,200/gram. In 2026, you redeem at maturity at ₹7,200/gram — a 125% capital gain (₹62,500 profit on ₹50,000 invested).

  • SGB (maturity redemption): ₹62,500 in capital gains = ₹0 tax.
  • Gold ETF (same holding period): ₹62,500 × 12.5% LTCG = ₹7,812 in tax.
  • Physical gold: ₹62,500 × 12.5% LTCG = ₹7,812 + making charges + locker fees.

Additionally, the 2.5% annual interest on ₹50,000 = ₹1,250/year × 8 years = ₹10,000 additional income (taxable at your slab, but still real cash income no other gold format provides).

Buying SGBs on the Secondary Market (Since New Issuances Are Paused)

As of FY 2026-27, the RBI has not announced fresh SGB tranches. Investors wanting SGB exposure must purchase existing bonds in the secondary market via NSE/BSE. Key considerations:

  • Liquidity is thin: Daily trading volumes in the SGB secondary market are low. Large purchase orders may move prices significantly. Use limit orders, not market orders.
  • Maturity date matters: Secondary market SGB purchases do not guarantee the full 8-year maturity tax-exemption for secondary buyers — only the original RBI issue price and the RBI redemption price qualify for the zero-tax treatment. Secondary market transaction gains may be taxed differently; consult a CA before large purchases.
  • Premium to intrinsic value: Popular SGB tranches sometimes trade at a 2%–5% premium to current gold price due to the tax advantage. Check the premium before purchasing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I exit an SGB before the 8-year maturity?

Yes — via two routes. First, the RBI allows "premature redemption" at the end of years 5, 6, or 7 (on coupon payment dates), with capital gains tax exemption applying for redemption through RBI's premature exit window. Second, you can sell in the secondary market on NSE/BSE at any time, but secondary market capital gains are taxed as normal LTCG (12.5% after 12 months holding). The full tax exemption applies only to RBI direct redemption at maturity or premature redemption windows.

How Much Gold Allocation Is Appropriate?

Most financial planners recommend 5%–15% of total portfolio in gold as a hedge against currency devaluation and equity market crashes. Gold and equity have historically low correlation — gold often rises when equities fall, providing portfolio stability during crises (2008, 2020 COVID crash). SGBs (or gold ETFs for more liquid access) are the appropriate vehicle for this allocation. Avoid physical gold beyond jewelry with emotional value — the GST cost, making charges, and locker expenses make it a poor investment vehicle.
