How to Start Your First SIP: A Step-by-Step Walkthrough for Absolute Beginners

This guide assumes you have never opened an investment app in your life. Zero experience needed. By the time you finish reading, you will know exactly which platform to use, which documents to keep ready, how to pick your first fund, and what the first three months will feel like.

Meet Aditi, a 26-year-old marketing executive in Bengaluru earning ₹48,000 take-home. She has heard “SIP” and “mutual fund” a hundred times in the last year — from colleagues, LinkedIn posts, and well-meaning uncles at family gatherings. Every time she tries to research it, she ends up 40 tabs deep and more confused than when she started.

If that sounds familiar, this guide is for you. No jargon, no assumptions, no detours. Just the actual steps to start your first SIP this week.

What You Need Before You Start (Checklist)

The documentation requirements for starting a mutual fund SIP in India are minimal. Keep these ready:

  • PAN Card: Mandatory for any investment above ₹50,000 per year. If you don’t have one, apply at incometaxindia.gov.in or through NSDL/UTITSL — it is now issued digitally.
  • Aadhaar Card: Used for address verification and Aadhaar-based OTP KYC. Your Aadhaar-linked mobile number must be active.
  • Savings Bank Account: Net banking or UPI must be enabled. This is where your SIP debit happens and where redemptions get credited.
  • Selfie or Video KYC: A short live video or photograph taken within the app itself. No physical submission required on any modern platform.
Important: Your PAN and Aadhaar should be linked. If they are not, your KYC application will be rejected. Check link status at incometax.gov.in before you start.

Step 1: Choosing the Right Platform

The honest answer: the platform matters less than starting. But there are meaningful differences to understand.

Option A: Direct Through the AMC Website or MF Central

If you already know which fund you want (say, a Nifty 50 Index Fund from HDFC or SBI), you can invest directly on the AMC’s website or the industry-wide MF Central platform (mfcentral.com). This automatically puts you in a Direct Plan — no distributor commission, lower expense ratio, slightly higher NAV growth over time. The downside: managing multiple funds from different AMCs means logging into multiple websites.

Option B: Third-Party Broker Platforms

Platforms like Groww, Zerodha Coin, and Kuvera aggregate all AMCs in one place. You complete KYC once and can invest in any fund from a single dashboard. All three offer Direct Plans — this is important, because some platforms route you into Regular Plans. Always check the plan type before confirming any order.

  • Groww: Very beginner-friendly interface. Good for complete first-timers who want simplicity.
  • Zerodha Coin: Ideal if you already use Zerodha for stocks. Mutual funds alongside your equity portfolio in one app.
  • Kuvera: Strong goal-based planning features and excellent Direct Plan access. Slightly more feature-rich than Groww.
Direct vs Regular Plans: Regular plans pay distributor commissions of roughly 0.5–1% per year, deducted from your returns. On a 20-year SIP, a 1% annual drag can cost you ₹15–20 lakh on a modest portfolio. Always select “Direct Plan — Growth Option.”

Step 2: Completing Your KYC

KYC is a one-time regulatory requirement mandated by SEBI. Once approved, you can invest with any SEBI-registered mutual fund in India without redoing it. Here is the process:

  1. Download the app and create an account with your mobile number and email ID.
  2. Enter your PAN number. The system will auto-fetch your name from the PAN record.
  3. Aadhaar verification: Enter your Aadhaar number. An OTP is sent to your Aadhaar-linked mobile.
  4. Bank account linking: Enter your account number and IFSC code. The platform may send a penny-drop verification (₹1 credited to confirm).
  5. Video or photo KYC: Hold your PAN card in front of the camera and take a live selfie. This step takes about 2–3 minutes.

Most platforms approve KYC within a few hours. Some take up to 24 hours. You will receive an email or SMS confirmation once verified.

Step 3: Selecting Your First Mutual Fund

There are over 2,500 mutual fund schemes in India. The secret to picking a first fund: match it to your time horizon and keep it simple.

For a Time Horizon of 7+ Years (Most Beginners)

A Nifty 50 Index Fund (Direct Plan) is the textbook starting point. It tracks India’s 50 largest companies, has an expense ratio of roughly 0.1–0.2%, and requires zero fund manager judgment on your part. Examples: UTI Nifty 50 Index Fund Direct Growth, SBI Nifty Index Fund Direct Growth, HDFC Index Fund Nifty 50 Plan Direct Growth.

If you want slightly more active management, a Flexi Cap Fund (Direct Plan) can invest across large, mid, and small-cap companies. Higher expense ratios (typically 0.7–1.2% for Direct) but potentially better risk-adjusted returns over 10–15 years depending on the fund manager.

For a Time Horizon of 3–5 Years

Equity funds are too volatile for a 3-year goal. Consider a Hybrid Aggressive Fund or a Short Duration Debt Fund for money you will need within 3 years. Do not put 3-year goal money into a pure equity fund — a market correction in year 2 could mean you redeem at a loss.

Step 4: Setting the SIP Date and the NACH Auto-Debit Mandate

Once you have selected your fund, pick:

  • SIP amount: Start with whatever you are comfortable with — even ₹500/month is fine. A good guideline: invest at least 20% of your take-home income, but start smaller if needed. You can always increase later.
  • SIP date: Pick a date 4–6 days after your salary credit date. This ensures funds are available when the debit hits.
  • Duration: Most platforms default to “perpetual” — the SIP continues until you cancel. This is the recommended setting for long-term investing. You can stop it anytime with 1–3 business days’ notice.

After confirming your SIP, the platform sets up a NACH (National Automated Clearing House) mandate with your bank. You will receive an SMS asking you to approve the NACH mandate through net banking — approve it within the deadline (usually 30 days) or the first SIP instalment will fail.

Aditi’s Moment: She chose a Nifty 50 Index Fund Direct Growth, set a ₹3,000/month SIP on the 7th of every month (salary arrives on the 1st), approved the NACH mandate the same evening, and her first SIP debit happened 6 days later. Total time from downloading the app to placing the first order: 22 minutes.

What to Expect in the First 3 Months

Most SIP beginners get discouraged in the first 90 days. Here is what will actually happen so you are not caught off guard:

  • Month 1: Your first ₹3,000 gets invested. The portfolio value might be ₹2,980 or ₹3,030. This is normal. Do not read anything into it.
  • Month 2: Total invested: ₹6,000. Portfolio value: probably ₹5,900–₹6,300. The variation feels large in percentage terms because the absolute amounts are small.
  • Month 3: Total invested: ₹9,000. At a 12% annual return, expected gains after 3 months are roughly ₹160–₹270. It looks disappointing — that is correct.

Here is what is actually happening: you are building the habit. You are buying units at different prices each month — more units when the market dips, fewer when it rises. This is rupee cost averaging at work. After 5 years, the portfolio will look very different. Use our SIP Calculator to see what ₹3,000/month looks like after 10 or 20 years at 12% CAGR — the number will surprise you.

The Most Important Rule of the First 3 Months: Do not check your portfolio daily. Open the app once a month, glance at the statement, and close it. Your job for the first year is simply not to stop the SIP. The market will do the rest.

After Your First SIP Is Running: What Next?

  • Set up a Step-Up SIP: Every year, when your salary increases, increase your SIP by 10%. This single habit can double your final corpus over a 20-year horizon compared to a flat SIP. Our Step-Up SIP Calculator shows you the exact impact.
  • Add a second fund (after 6+ months): Once your first fund feels comfortable, consider adding one mid-cap or flexi-cap fund. Keep the total to 2–3 funds maximum for the first few years.
  • Set a goal: Assign your SIP to a purpose — retirement at 55, home down payment in 8 years, child’s education in 12 years. Use our Goal Planner to calculate exactly how much to invest monthly for each target.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need to start a SIP in India?
You need four things: PAN card, Aadhaar card (with linked mobile number active), a savings bank account with net banking or UPI enabled, and a selfie or short video KYC done within the app. No physical paperwork is required for any SEBI-registered online platform.
What is the minimum amount to start a SIP?
Most mutual funds allow SIPs starting at ₹100–₹500/month. There is no maximum. Start with whatever amount you can commit to consistently every month — ₹500 invested for 20 years beats ₹5,000 invested for 3 years and then stopped.
Should I start SIP through Groww, Zerodha Coin, or directly with the AMC?
All three routes are legitimate. Direct AMC websites and MF Central give you Direct Plans. Broker platforms like Groww, Zerodha Coin, and Kuvera also offer Direct Plans with the convenience of a single dashboard. Always select Direct Plan Growth Option and avoid Regular Plans, which add 0.5–1% in distributor commissions per year.
What should I do if my SIP does not show returns in the first 3 months?
Nothing — that is completely normal. After 3 months of a ₹5,000/month SIP, your total invested is ₹15,000. Even with a 12% annual return, gains are roughly ₹600–₹800. The compounding effect becomes visible after 3–5 years, not 3 months.
