FD vs Mutual Funds in India: Returns, Tax Rules (FY 2026-27) & When to Pick Each

Written by Tejas Waghmare Published: 25 June 2026 Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Educational guide only, not personalized investment or tax advice. Rules and rates can change; verify official sources and see our disclaimer.

Choose Bank FDs for capital certainty and goals under 2 years (up to ₹5 Lakh DICGC cover per bank; TDS exempt up to ₹50,000/yr or ₹1,00,000 for seniors). Choose Equity Mutual Funds for 5+ year inflation-beating wealth (12.5% LTCG >1 yr), or Arbitrage Funds for 30%-slab short-term tax efficiency.

2. Long-Term Compounding: ₹1,00,000 Over 15 Years

Comparing a cumulative Bank FD at 7.0% p.a. compounded quarterly (standard Indian bank convention: A = P(1 + r/4)4t) versus a diversified equity mutual fund growing at an illustrative 12.0% CAGR before tax:

  • Bank FD at 7.0% p.a. (quarterly compounded, 15 years): Grows from ₹1,00,000 to ₹2,83,182 pre-tax (note: if interest is taxed annually in the 30% bracket, annual net compounding reduces effective after-tax growth to 4.9% pre-cess, yielding ~₹2,06,271).
  • Equity Mutual Fund at 12.0% CAGR (15 years, illustrative): Grows from ₹1,00,000 to ₹5,47,357 pre-tax, with tax deferred until sale and ₹1,25,000 annual LTCG exemption available at redemption.

3. Debt Mutual Funds (Section 50AA) & Tax Deferral vs Bank FDs

After Finance Act 2023 introduced Section 50AA, specified debt mutual funds holding ≤35% domestic equity lost indexation benefits and are now taxed at your marginal income slab rate just like bank FDs. However, two practical structural differences remain:

  • Tax Deferral in Growth Debt Funds: Banks tax accrued FD interest annually (deducting 10% TDS under revised Section 194A / IT Act 2025 Section 393 if total bank interest exceeds ₹50,000 for general citizens or ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens from FY 2025-26 onwards). Growth debt funds defer tax payment until actual redemption. For a ₹10,00,000 sum compounded over 3 years at 7.0% in the 30% slab before cess, deferring tax produces ₹11,57,530 versus ₹11,57,320 in a quarterly FD taxed yearly—plus instant partial redemption without breaking the full deposit.
  • Arbitrage Funds for 30% Slab Investors: Arbitrage funds maintain ≥65% hedged equity exposure (buying spot stock and selling futures simultaneously), qualifying for equity taxation (12.5% LTCG after 12 months with ₹1.25L annual exemption) while behaving similarly to liquid money-market returns.

4. How to Choose Between FDs and Mutual Funds

  • Choose Bank FDs when: Your goal is under 1–2 years, capital preservation is non-negotiable, or you are a senior citizen/taxpayer below taxable thresholds utilizing Form 15G/15H and the ₹1,00,000 TDS ceiling across DICGC-insured limits (₹5 Lakh per bank).
  • Choose Arbitrage or Liquid Funds when: You hold tactical short-term reserves in the 20%–30% tax bracket and want partial redemption flexibility without FD premature penalties (typically 0.5%–1.0%).
  • Choose Equity Mutual Funds when: Your investment horizon exceeds 5–7 years and you need post-tax returns capable of outpacing CPI inflation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are debt mutual funds taxed in FY 2026-27 under Section 50AA?

Capital gains on specified debt mutual funds (holding up to 35% domestic equity) are treated as short-term capital gains regardless of holding duration and taxed at your normal income tax slab rate.

What is the Bank FD TDS limit in FY 2026-27?

Effective April 1, 2025 (covering FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27), banks deduct 10% TDS under Section 194A (Section 393 of Income-tax Act 2025) when aggregate FD/RD interest at a bank exceeds ₹50,000 in a financial year for non-seniors, or ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens (60+).

Are Bank Fixed Deposits completely risk-free?

Scheduled commercial bank deposits (principal plus accrued interest combined) are insured up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC). Amounts above ₹5 Lakh carry bank-specific credit exposure.

Can I redeem part of a mutual fund without breaking everything?

Yes. Mutual funds allow redeeming any specific rupee amount or unit count anytime (exit load may apply if redeemed early, such as <7 days on liquid funds or <30–365 days on hybrid/equity funds), whereas FDs often require premature breaking of the deposit receipt unless an overdraft/sweep facility is active.
