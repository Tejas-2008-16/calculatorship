FD vs Mutual Funds: Which Is Better for Your Money in India?

If you have money sitting in your savings account and are trying to decide what to do with it, you have probably heard two options more than anything else: Fixed Deposits and Mutual Funds. Here is a clear, jargon-free breakdown.

August 18, 2026 By Tejas
← Back to Articles

If you have some money sitting in your savings account and you are trying to decide what to do with it, you have probably heard two options more than anything else — Fixed Deposits and Mutual Funds. Your parents probably swear by FDs. Your friends in the office talk about SIPs. So which one is actually better for you?

Let's break it down in simple language — no jargon, no confusion.

What is a Fixed Deposit?

A Fixed Deposit (FD) is one of the oldest and simplest investment options in India. You give your money to a bank for a fixed period of time — say 1 year, 3 years, or 5 years. The bank promises to give it back with interest at a fixed rate, no matter what happens in the economy.

Right now (2026), most major Indian banks are offering FD rates between 6.5% and 7.5% per year for regular customers. Senior citizens get a little more — usually 0.25% to 0.5% extra.

The big attraction of an FD is that it is Fixed. You know exactly how much you will get back. There is no risk of losing principal.

What is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund pools money from thousands of investors and uses it to buy a basket of stocks, bonds, or other assets. A professional fund manager handles the buying and selling. When the value of those assets goes up, your investment grows too.

The returns are not fixed or Fixed. They depend on how the market performs. But historically, equity mutual funds in India have delivered 10% to 14% CAGR (Compounded Annual Growth Rate) over long periods of 10 years or more.

The Real Difference: Returns Over Time

This is where things get interesting. Let's say you invest &8377;1 lakh and leave it alone for 15 years:

  • In an FD at 7% per year, your &8377;1 lakh grows to about &8377;2.76 lakhs.
  • In an equity mutual fund at 12% per year, your &8377;1 lakh grows to about &8377;5.47 lakhs.

That is almost double the FD result — just by choosing a different product. This is the power of higher compounding over a long period.

But Wait — What About Risk?

Here is the honest truth: mutual funds carry market risk. In bad years, your investment can go down temporarily. For example, in 2020 when COVID hit, many equity funds fell 30% to 40% in a few months. People who panicked and withdrew locked in their losses. But investors who stayed the course saw their portfolios rebound to new highs.

FDs carry virtually zero market volatility. Your principal is safe. The returns are predictable. That is exactly why FDs are great for short-term goals (1 to 3 years) or for money you cannot afford to risk.

Decision Framework

Need money in 1 to 2 years? → FD is safer.
Investing for 5 years or longer? → Mutual funds will build significantly more real wealth.

Tax: The Hidden Difference Nobody Talks About

This is where FDs quietly hurt your net returns:

Interest earned on FDs is added to your total annual income and taxed at your applicable slab rate. If you are in the 30% tax bracket, you pay 30% tax on your FD interest every single year — even if you have not withdrawn the money.

Mutual funds work differently. You only pay tax when you redeem units. If you hold equity funds for more than 1 year, the gains are taxed at 12.5% (Long Term Capital Gains tax) — with the first &8377;1.25 lakh of gains fully tax-exempt each year.

Example: You earn &8377;1 lakh in interest from an FD. If you are in the 30% slab, you pay &8377;30,000 in tax. If you earn &8377;1 lakh in LTCG from an equity mutual fund, you pay zero tax (it falls inside the &8377;1.25 lakh annual exemption).

Liquidity: Which One Can You Access Faster?

FDs are reasonably liquid but carry penalties. If you break your FD before maturity, banks typically charge a premature withdrawal penalty of 0.5% to 1% on the interest rate. Tax-saving 5-year FDs cannot be broken at all before maturity.

Most open-ended mutual funds (except 3-year ELSS) allow redemption on any business day. Funds credit to your bank account within 1 to 3 working days with no penalty after the standard 1-year exit load period.

Quick Side-by-Side Comparison

Feature Fixed Deposit Equity Mutual Fund
Returns 6.5%–7.5% (fixed) 10%–14% (market-linked)
Risk Very low Moderate to high
Tax Taxed as income (up to 30%) 12.5% LTCG after 1 year (&8377;1.25L exempt)
Liquidity 1–3 days (penalty applies) 1–3 days (no penalty after 1 yr)
Ideal for Short-term, capital safety Long-term wealth building
Fixed Yes No
Inflation Beat Weak (~0% real return) Strong (+5% to +7% real return)

So Which One Should You Choose?

The honest answer: you do not have to pick just one. A balanced financial strategy leverages both instruments:

  1. Emergency Fund in FD: Keep 3 to 6 months of living expenses in an FD or high-yield liquid fund for complete peace of mind.
  2. Long-Term Wealth via SIP: Invest monthly surpluses you won't need for 5+ years into equity mutual funds.

A Note on Debt Mutual Funds

There is a solid middle ground. Debt mutual funds invest in government bonds and high-quality corporate papers. They carry much less volatility than equity funds and offer better operational convenience than locking fixed sums into individual bank deposits.

Final Word

FDs provide stability and predictability for short-term goals. But if your goal is beating inflation and building wealth for retirement or milestones 5 to 15 years away, equity mutual funds through systematic SIPs are far superior.
