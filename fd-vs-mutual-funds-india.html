Fixed Deposits vs Mutual Funds in India: Arithmetic, Taxation, and Risk Differences

Comparing Fixed Deposits and Mutual Funds involves evaluating contractual fixed returns versus market-linked growth, post-tax yields across income brackets, and liquidity terms. Here is a breakdown of how the mechanisms differ.

August 18, 2026 By Tejas
← Back to Articles

If you have some money sitting in your savings account and you are trying to decide what to do with it, you have probably heard two options more than anything else — Fixed Deposits and Mutual Funds. Your parents probably swear by FDs. Your friends in the office talk about SIPs. So which one is actually better for you?

Let's break it down in simple language — no jargon, no confusion.

What is a Fixed Deposit?

A Fixed Deposit (FD) is one of the oldest and simplest investment options in India. You give your money to a bank for a fixed period of time — say 1 year, 3 years, or 5 years. The bank promises to give it back with interest at a fixed rate, no matter what happens in the economy.

Right now (2026), most major Indian banks are offering FD rates between 6.5% and 7.5% per year for regular customers. Senior citizens get a little more — usually 0.25% to 0.5% extra.

The big attraction of an FD is that it is Fixed. You know exactly how much you will get back. There is no risk of losing principal.

What is a Mutual Fund?

A mutual fund pools money from thousands of investors and uses it to buy a basket of stocks, bonds, or other assets. A professional fund manager handles the buying and selling. When the value of those assets goes up, your investment grows too.

The returns are not fixed or Fixed. They depend on how the market performs. But historically, equity mutual funds in India have delivered 10% to 14% CAGR (Compounded Annual Growth Rate) over long periods of 10 years or more.

Understanding Risk and Volatility

Mutual funds carry market risk and daily Net Asset Value (NAV) fluctuations. During economic contractions or market corrections, equity portfolios experience mark-to-market declines.

Fixed Deposits carry low market volatility because banks guarantee principal and contractual interest upon maturity, backed by DICGC insurance coverage up to ₹5 Lakh per depositor per bank.

Tax Treatment Across Slabs

Interest from bank fixed deposits is categorized as "Income from Other Sources" and taxed at an individual's applicable slab rate. For someone in the 30% tax bracket, an annual FD interest return of 7% yields approximately 4.9% post-tax.

In contrast, gains from listed equity mutual funds held for more than 12 months qualify as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) under Section 112A, taxed at 12.5% plus cess on aggregate net gains exceeding ₹1,25,000 per financial year.

Liquidity and Premature Exit Rules

Bank FDs generally allow premature liquidation subject to a penal interest deduction (often 0.5% to 1.0%), while 5-year tax-saving FDs are completely locked. Open-ended mutual fund schemes allow redemptions on any business day, subject to applicable scheme exit loads (typically 1% if redeemed within 1 year for equity funds).

How to think about this yourself

  • Post-Tax Yield Assessment: Calculate your post-tax return. In lower tax slabs (such as 5% or 10%), the tax friction on FD interest is relatively small, making guaranteed returns attractive. In the 30% slab, equity capital gains tax rules create a notable post-tax difference.
  • Horizon and Risk Capacity: If funds are needed within 1–3 years for a planned milestone, market fluctuations make equity funds risky. For long-term goals (10+ years), compounding across asset classes becomes a primary focus.
  • Sovereign & DICGC Limits: If holding substantial fixed deposits, remember the ₹5 Lakh DICGC protection limit applies across all accounts within a single commercial or co-operative bank.

Where to get personalised help

If you are planning an overall asset allocation across fixed income, debt securities, and equities, consider consulting a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA). Verified advisers can be found on the official SEBI directory (sebi.gov.in). For tax planning regarding interest accrual and TDS deductions, speak with a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA).
