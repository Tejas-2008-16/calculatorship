What is Net Asset Value (NAV) in Mutual Funds? Mathematical Accounting & Pricing Mechanics

Hi, I'm Tejas — an engineering student researching personal finance. This guide explores how Net Asset Value (NAV) is computed under SEBI regulations, how unit allotment mathematics works, and why NAV magnitude does not indicate valuation cheapness. It is written with AI assistance and checked against regulatory standards. It is purely educational and does not constitute investment advice.

A common retail misunderstanding equates mutual fund NAV with stock prices, assuming a ₹10 NAV fund is cheaper or offers greater growth than a ₹500 NAV fund. Here is the mathematical accounting behind NAV calculation, SEBI cut-off rules, and unit allocation.

What is Net Asset Value (NAV)? The Basic Accounting Formula

In mutual fund accounting, Net Asset Value (NAV) represents the per-unit book value of a scheme's net assets. When an Asset Management Company (AMC) pools investor capital to purchase underlying securities (equities, debt instruments, or money market instruments), the total portfolio is divided into units.

Under SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996, every fund house must compute and publish the daily NAV of each scheme after the close of trading hours by 11:00 PM IST on AMFI's central portal. The accounting formula is defined as:

Daily Net Asset Value (NAV) Formula:

NAV = (Market Value of Portfolio Investments + Current Assets & Accrued Income − Current Liabilities & Daily Accrued Expenses) ÷ Total Outstanding Scheme Units

Unlike a publicly traded company where market share prices fluctuate continuously based on order book bid-ask spreads, an open-ended mutual fund's NAV is an end-of-day mathematical accounting tally reflecting portfolio asset valuation divided by units in circulation.

The NFO Perception: ₹10 Par Value vs Seasoned Scheme NAV

During New Fund Offers (NFOs), schemes are typically issued at an initial par value of ₹10 per unit. In contrast, long-running schemes that have compounded over 15 to 20 years may have NAVs exceeding ₹500 or ₹1,000.

A frequent beginner misconception is assuming that buying a ₹10 NFO is comparable to buying an undervalued stock at a low price-to-earnings ratio. However, a mutual fund unit is not an operating company; it represents a fractional claim on a basket of underlying assets.

Mathematical Perspective: Unit Slicing

If two funds hold the exact same basket of underlying securities, whether that basket is divided into 5,000 units of ₹10 each or 100 units of ₹500 each does not alter the underlying asset ownership or percentage growth rate. Capital appreciation is governed entirely by the percentage movement of the underlying portfolio.

Mathematical Proof: NAV Price Does Not Determine Percentage Returns

Consider an investment of ₹50,000 made simultaneously into two hypothetical funds holding identical underlying equity portfolios, where both portfolios appreciate by 15% over the subsequent 12 months:

Metric Scheme A (Initial NAV ₹10) Scheme B (Initial NAV ₹500)
Capital Invested ₹50,000 ₹50,000
Initial NAV per Unit ₹10.00 ₹500.00
Units Allotted 5,000.00 Units 100.00 Units
Portfolio Gain +15.0% +15.0%
NAV at Year-End ₹11.50 ₹575.00
Final Valuation (Units × NAV) ₹57,500 ₹57,500

Both holdings terminate at an identical valuation of ₹57,500. The numerical value of the NAV serves purely as an internal accounting divisor to determine unit quantity upon allocation.

SEBI Cut-off Timings for NAV Allocation

While NAV magnitude does not impact performance, the cut-off timing governing which day's NAV applies to a transaction is strictly regulated by SEBI circulars:

  • Equity & Debt Schemes (Subscription / Purchase): Standard cut-off time is 3:00 PM IST on business days.
  • Liquid & Overnight Schemes: Cut-off time is 1:30 PM IST.
  • Fund Realisation Mandate: Under prevailing SEBI rules, same-day NAV allotment occurs only when investor funds are credited to the AMC's bank account before the cut-off time, regardless of when the purchase order was submitted.

In recurring Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs), monthly installments are executed at the NAV applicable on the scheduled debit realization date, applying rupee cost averaging across fluctuating market levels.

Core Objective Metrics for Scheme Evaluation

Rather than reviewing absolute NAV figures, factsheets provide objective analytical data:

  1. Rolling Return Consistency: Evaluating 3-year and 5-year rolling returns against the scheme's statutory benchmark filters out cyclical distortions.
  2. Total Expense Ratio (TER): Direct plans carry a lower TER than regular plans, retaining daily NAV advantages because no intermediary distribution commissions are subtracted. Read our analysis of how expense ratios impact compounding.
  3. Portfolio Structure & Turnover: Reviewing asset allocation, credit rating profile (in debt schemes), and portfolio turnover ratios.

How to Think About This Yourself

  • NAV is a unit price, not a valuation multiple: A high NAV simply indicates a fund has compounded over multiple market cycles; it does not indicate overvaluation.
  • Realisation rules determine pricing: Electronic fund transfers must hit the AMC collection account before 3:00 PM to secure that day's closing NAV.
  • Track performance in percentages: Mutual fund compounding depends entirely on percentage growth (CAGR / XIRR) net of expenses, not the physical quantity of units owned.

Where to Get Personalised Help

Daily historical NAV data and scheme factsheets are published transparently on the AMFI India portal. If you require assistance aligning fund categories with your risk tolerance and investment time horizon, consult a fee-only SEBI-Registered Investment Adviser (RIA) via the official SEBI directory.
