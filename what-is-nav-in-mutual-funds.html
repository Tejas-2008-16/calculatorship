What is NAV in Mutual Funds? Why It Does Not Determine a Fund's Value

Common misconception busted — a ₹10 NAV fund is NOT cheaper than a ₹500 NAV fund. Learn how NAV actually works, what it means, and why it should never influence your fund selection.

What is Net Asset Value (NAV)? The Basic Formula

Imagine going to a neighbourhood bakery with three friends to buy a substantial 1 kg custom chocolate cake costing ₹1,000. None of you wants to eat the whole thing alone. So the baker cuts it into 10 equal slices at ₹100 each. You pay ₹100, walk away with 1 slice — and that slice represents exactly 10% ownership of that delicious cake.

In the Indian mutual fund industry, Net Asset Value (NAV) is simply the price of one single unit of a mutual fund scheme. When an Asset Management Company (AMC) like SBI Mutual Fund, ICICI Prudential, or Nippon India pools thousands of crores to buy underlying stocks like Reliance, TCS, and Infosys, it divides the entire portfolio into equal units. NAV measures the exact market value of one such unit after deducting fund operational expenses.

Under SEBI guidelines, every fund house must calculate and publish the NAV of all its schemes at the close of every business day by 11:00 PM IST on AMFI's official portal. The formula is straightforward:

Daily Net Asset Value (NAV) Formula:

NAV = (Total Market Value of Securities + Cash & Receivables − Liabilities & Daily Expenses) ÷ Total Outstanding Scheme Units

The NFO Myth: Is a ₹10 NAV Fund Cheaper Than a ₹500 NAV Fund?

Here is the single biggest trap beginner Indian investors fall into on platforms like Zerodha Coin, Groww, or Paytm Money. They see a newly launched New Fund Offer (NFO) selling units at ₹10 each. Right next to it sits a 15-year-old established blue-chip equity fund at a NAV of ₹500.

Their stock-market intuition kicks in: "If I invest ₹50,000 in the ₹10 NFO, I get 5,000 units! In the ₹500 fund, I only get 100 units. More units must mean more wealth, right?"

Quick Fact: The Pizza Slicing Reality

NFOs do not offer underlying stocks at a discount. A ₹10 NAV simply slices your investment pie into smaller pieces. Having 500 slices of a 1-foot pizza gives you the exact same amount of food as 5 slices of the same pizza. Unit quantity has zero impact on total capital appreciation!

This reasoning is fundamentally flawed. When you buy shares of a company like Tata Motors, price multiples like P/E and P/B tell you if it's cheap or expensive. But a mutual fund is not an individual company — it is a basket of securities. A high NAV simply indicates the fund has been running for a long time and its underlying holdings have appreciated significantly.

Mathematical Proof: NAV Price Does Not Impact Your Returns

Let's prove this with a real example. Meet two friends from Bengaluru — Ramesh and Priya. Both saved ₹50,000 from their annual bonus and invest it on the exact same day in two different Flexi Cap equity funds.

Ramesh falls for the NFO hype and buys Fund A at a fresh NAV of ₹10 — he gets 5,000 units. Priya ignores NAV numbers and invests in Fund B, a veteran fund at ₹500 per unit — she gets 100 units. Both funds hold identical Nifty 50 stocks and grow exactly 15% over the next 12 months:

Metric Ramesh — Fund A (Low NAV) Priya — Fund B (High NAV)
Initial Investment ₹50,000 ₹50,000
Starting NAV ₹10 ₹500
Units Allotted 5,000 Units 100 Units
Portfolio Growth +15% +15%
New NAV After 1 Year ₹11.50 ₹575.00
Final Portfolio Value ₹57,500 ₹57,500

And this is where it gets interesting. Both Ramesh and Priya ended up with the exact same ₹57,500! Choosing a fund based on whether its NAV is ₹10 or ₹500 is like choosing a gold coin based on the size of its box. What matters is the weight and purity of the gold inside — not the packaging.

SEBI Cut-off Timings for NAV Allocation

While the absolute NAV number does not determine performance, the timing of when your NAV is locked in does matter — especially during sharp market volatility. SEBI has strict cut-off timing rules across all Indian AMCs:

  • Equity & Debt Schemes (Purchase/SIP): Standard cut-off is 3:00 PM IST. If your transaction and funds reach the AMC pool account before 3:00 PM, you get that day's closing NAV. After 3:00 PM, you receive the next business day's NAV.
  • Liquid & Overnight Schemes: Cut-off is earlier at 1:30 PM IST, and allotment relies on funds reaching the AMC by that deadline.
  • Realization Rule: Since February 2021, SEBI mandates same-day NAV only when money actually reaches the AMC's bank account before cut-off — regardless of when you authorized the UPI or net banking transfer.

If you invest through a SIP, your monthly installment is processed at the NAV of the debit date, benefiting from rupee cost averaging over the long term.

What Actually Matters When Choosing a Mutual Fund

Now that the NAV myth is busted, what should you actually evaluate when picking a scheme? Here are four fundamental factors that drive real long-term returns:

  1. Consistent Risk-Adjusted Returns: Look at 3-year, 5-year, and 10-year rolling returns rather than trailing 1-year spikes. A fund that consistently beats its benchmark index across market cycles is far superior to a newly launched ₹10 NFO with zero track record.
  2. Total Expense Ratio (TER): Every scheme charges an annual management fee. A fund at 0.50% TER leaves significantly more in your compounding basket than an identical fund at 1.80%. Read our full guide on how expense ratios erode long-term wealth.
  3. Direct vs Regular Plans: Always select the "Direct-Growth" variant. Direct plans eliminate agent distributor commissions, boosting your net NAV growth every day. See our breakdown of Direct vs Regular mutual funds.
  4. Fund Manager & Portfolio Turnover: Examine the factsheet to understand top holdings, sector concentration, and how frequently the manager trades stocks.
Did You Know? Under SEBI's AMFI data, there are over 2,500 mutual fund schemes registered in India as of 2026. Using NAV as your filter would be meaningless — scheme age determines NAV levels, not future potential!
