Section 80GG Rent Deduction Guide: Claim Rent Without HRA

Millions of Indian taxpayers pay significant monthly house rent but receive zero House Rent Allowance (HRA) in their pay slip—including freelance developers, consultants, gig workers, small business owners, and employees of firms offering consolidated pay packages. Section 80GG of the Income Tax Act provides a targeted deduction of up to ₹60,000 annually. Discover how the statutory formula works, mandatory Form 10BA compliance, and key restrictions.

Statutory Compliance Notice: Section 80GG deductions are governed by Section 80GG of the Income Tax Act, 1961, and Rule 11B of the Income Tax Rules, 1962. Section 80GG is an Old Tax Regime deduction and is not admissible under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC). Online submission of Form 10BA prior to filing ITR is a mandatory prerequisite.

Key Takeaways & Executive Summary

  • Maximum Deduction: ₹5,000 per month, translating to an annual ceiling of ₹60,000 (enhanced from ₹2,000/month by Finance Act 2016).
  • Who Can Claim: Self-employed individuals, professionals, freelancers, and salaried employees who do not receive HRA from any employer during the financial year.
  • The 3-Way Test: The deduction is strictly the lowest of: (1) ₹5,000/month, (2) 25% of Adjusted Total Income, or (3) Actual Rent Paid minus 10% of Adjusted Total Income.
  • Mandatory E-Form: Taxpayers must furnish Form 10BA online on the Income Tax e-filing portal before submitting their tax return.
  • Property Ownership Restriction: You, your spouse, your minor child, or your HUF must not own residential accommodation in the city of residence or employment.

1. What is Section 80GG & Who Can Benefit?

Under the Indian tax code, salaried employees receiving House Rent Allowance (HRA) as part of their cost-to-company (CTC) can claim tax exemptions under Section 10(13A). However, hundreds of thousands of productive economic participants do not receive HRA. This includes:

  • Freelancers, Consultants & Independent Professionals: Software developers, legal counsels, designers, and medical professionals billing clients directly.
  • Sole Proprietors & Traders: Small business owners living in rented residential apartments.
  • Salaried Individuals Without HRA Component: Professionals whose employers provide a consolidated gross salary or stipend without structured allowances.
  • Employees in Startups: Early-stage hires receiving lump-sum compensation structures.

Section 80GG bridges this exact gap. Inserted into Chapter VI-A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, Section 80GG grants tax deductions on actual expenditure incurred towards rent paid for furnished or unfurnished residential accommodation occupied for personal residence.

2. Statutory Eligibility Criteria: 4 Essential Checkpoints

To substantiate a valid deduction under Section 80GG during assessment, the taxpayer must satisfy all four of the following legal conditions simultaneously:

Condition Statutory Requirement Verification Protocol
1. Taxpayer Category Applicable only to Individual taxpayers (both Resident and Non-Resident). Companies, LLPs, and Partnership Firms cannot claim. PAN entity status code must be “P” (Individual).
2. No HRA Received The individual must not have received any HRA from any employer at any point during the entire financial year. Form 16 Part B / Salary slip check for allowance code Section 10(13A).
3. No Local Property No residential house owned by the individual, spouse, minor child, or HUF at the place where the individual resides, works, or carries on business. Self-declaration in Form 10BA and municipal registry check.
4. No Self-Occupied Claim Elsewhere If the assessee owns residential property at any other location, no concession/deduction under Section 24(b) (self-occupied status) must be claimed for it. Schedule HP (Income from House Property) in ITR.

3. How Section 80GG is Calculated: The 3-Way Formula

Section 80GG does not provide a blanket ₹60,000 exemption. By statutory design, the deduction is determined as the least of the following three calculations:

Section 80GG Permitted Deduction = Least of:

  1. Statutory Monetary Ceiling: ₹5,000 per month (₹60,000 per annum).
  2. 25% of Adjusted Total Income: 25% of the taxpayer's total income computed after specific adjustments.
  3. Excess Rent Formula: Actual Rent Paid minus 10% of Adjusted Total Income.

If any of these three values computes to zero or negative, the permissible deduction becomes zero.

4. Understanding “Adjusted Total Income”

The term “Total Income” in Section 80GG has a precise statutory definition under the Income Tax Act. It is not your Gross Receipts or Gross Total Income. To compute Adjusted Total Income:

Adjustment Component Income Tax Section Mathematical Impact
Gross Total Income (GTI) Aggregate of all heads of income Starting Benchmark
Less: Long-Term Capital Gains Section 112 & Section 112A Subtracted in full
Less: Short-Term Capital Gains (Equity) Section 111A Subtracted in full
Less: Incomes under Sec 115A to 115D Non-resident special tax provisions Subtracted if applicable
Less: All Chapter VI-A Deductions (except 80GG) Sec 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B), 80E, 80TTA, etc. Subtracted in full

5. Practical Numerical Scenarios: Freelancer vs Salaried

Let us analyze two real-world case studies demonstrating how the three-way rule operates under actual financial numbers.

Case Study A: Rohit (Independent Tech Consultant in Bengaluru)

  • Gross Professional Receipts: ₹12,00,000 (Net taxable business profit: ₹8,50,000)
  • Deductions under Section 80C + 80D: ₹1,50,000 + ₹25,000 = ₹1,75,000
  • Adjusted Total Income: ₹8,50,000 − ₹1,75,000 = ₹6,75,000
  • Actual House Rent Paid: ₹18,000/month (× 12 = ₹2,16,000/year)

Applying the 3-Way Test:

  1. Statutory Ceiling: ₹5,000 × 12 = ₹60,000
  2. 25% of Adjusted Income: 25% of ₹6,75,000 = ₹1,68,750
  3. Rent minus 10% Adjusted Income: ₹2,16,000 − (10% of ₹6,75,000) = ₹2,16,000 − ₹67,500 = ₹1,48,500

Result: Rohit claims the least of (₹60,000, ₹1,68,750, ₹1,48,500) = ₹60,000 deduction under Section 80GG.

Case Study B: Meera (Salaried Executive with Consolidated Pay in Pune)

  • Consolidated Salary (No HRA): ₹5,00,000
  • Section 80C Deduction (PPF + ELSS): ₹1,00,000
  • Standard Deduction: ₹50,000
  • Adjusted Total Income: ₹5,00,000 − ₹50,000 − ₹1,00,000 = ₹3,50,000
  • Actual House Rent Paid: ₹6,000/month (× 12 = ₹72,000/year)

Applying the 3-Way Test:

  1. Statutory Ceiling: ₹60,000
  2. 25% of Adjusted Income: 25% of ₹3,50,000 = ₹87,500
  3. Rent minus 10% Adjusted Income: ₹72,000 − ₹35,000 = ₹37,000

Result: Meera claims ₹37,000 because the excess rent calculation (Criterion 3) is lower than the ₹60,000 cap.

6. Form 10BA: Online E-Filing Procedure Step-by-Step

Unlike Section 80C or 80D which can simply be entered in the respective ITR schedules, Section 80GG legally mandates a separate electronic statutory declaration called Form 10BA. Under Rule 11B, filing Form 10BA before submitting your ITR is required to prevent immediate CPC processing defects.

  1. Login to Portal: Access incometax.gov.in with your PAN credentials and password.
  2. Navigate to Statutory Forms: Click on e-File → Income Tax Forms → File Income Tax Forms.
  3. Select Form 10BA: Search for “Form 10BA” (Declaration for Deduction under Section 80GG).
  4. Choose Assessment Year: Select the relevant Assessment Year (e.g., AY 2027-28 for FY 2026-27).
  5. Fill Rental Premises Details: Enter full residential address, tenure of stay, monthly rental amount, payment method (cheque/NEFT/UPI), and landlord details.
  6. Landlord PAN Requirement: Enter the Landlord’s PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000. If the landlord does not have a PAN, a formal declaration to that effect must be noted.
  7. Verification & Submission: E-verify using Aadhaar OTP, Net Banking, or Digital Signature Certificate (DSC). Save the acknowledgement receipt.

7. Crucial Disqualifications & Property Ownership Traps

Many taxpayers mistakenly claim Section 80GG and subsequently face demand notices during scrutiny. Review these major disqualification traps:

  • Spouse or Minor Child Owns a Flat in the Same City: Even if the property is registered solely in your spouse’s name, you cannot claim Section 80GG if that flat is in the municipal limits of your work city.
  • Owning a House in Another City Claimed as “Self-Occupied”: If you own a house in your hometown and claim interest deduction under Section 24(b) asserting it is “self-occupied” (annual value taken as Nil), Section 80GG specifically bars you from claiming rent deduction in your work city. You can only claim 80GG if your hometown property is treated as deemed let-out or let-out with rental income properly offered to tax.
  • Paying Rent to Spouse: Unlike HRA where rent paid to parents is widely accepted with valid rental agreements and bank records, claiming Section 80GG for rent paid to a spouse is invalid because husband and wife are legally co-habitants of matrimonial residence.
  • Rent Paid in Cash Without Receipts: Without registered agreements, rent receipts, and clear bank transaction trails, claims are routinely disallowed during computerized risk-management assessments.

8. Section 80GG under Old vs New Tax Regime

Under Section 115BAC (the New Tax Regime), virtually all Chapter VI-A deductions are removed in exchange for lower slab rates. Section 80GG is explicitly unavailable under the New Tax Regime.

Feature Old Tax Regime New Tax Regime (Sec 115BAC)
Section 80GG Eligibility Fully Eligible (up to ₹60,000/yr) Not Allowed (Nil Deduction)
Form 10BA Requirement Mandatory before ITR filing Not Applicable
Maximum Annual Tax Saved ₹18,720 (at 30% slab + 4% cess) ₹0

9. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Can I claim both HRA and Section 80GG in the same year?

You cannot claim both for the same period. However, if you were salaried and received HRA for part of the financial year (e.g., 4 months) and then became self-employed or moved to a company without HRA for the remaining 8 months, you can claim HRA for the first 4 months and Section 80GG proportionately for the remaining 8 months.

Can I pay rent to my parents and claim Section 80GG?

Yes, provided you do not own residential property in that city, receive zero HRA, and your parents are the legal owners of the house. You must execute a formal rent agreement, transfer rent via banking channels, and your parents must report this rental income in their respective Income Tax Returns. (If you DO receive HRA from your employer, refer to our dedicated guide on HRA on Rent Paid to Parents (FY 2026-27): Rules, Form 124 & Tax Saving Math instead, which permits significantly higher deductions).

What if my monthly rent is ₹40,000? Can I claim more than ₹5,000/month?

No. Even if you pay ₹50,000 or ₹1,00,000 per month in rent, the statutory ceiling under Section 80GG is strictly capped at ₹5,000 per month (₹60,000 annually).

What happens if I forget to submit Form 10BA?

If you enter a deduction under Section 80GG in your ITR without an e-filed Form 10BA on record, the Centralized Processing Centre (CPC) will typically issue an intimation under Section 143(1) disallowing the deduction and adding the amount back to your taxable income along with interest.
