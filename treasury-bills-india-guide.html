Zero Default Risk Sovereign Money Market Instrument

Treasury Bills are direct short-term obligations of the Central Government of India, issued under Section 16 of the Government Securities Act, 2006. They carry zero credit risk and are issued at a discount and redeemed at par (₹100). Returns depend dynamically on competitive cut-off prices determined during weekly Wednesday auctions.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Sovereign Instrument: Issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Central Government with zero default risk.
  • Zero-Coupon Securities: No periodic coupon payments; issued at a discount and redeemed at par (₹100).
  • Standard Maturities: Three tenures available: 91 days, 182 days, and 364 days.
  • Minimum Investment: ₹10,000 (and in multiples of ₹10,000 thereafter).
  • Commission-Free Access: Retail investors bid at weighted average cut-off yield via the RBI Retail Direct platform with zero account maintenance fees.
  • Tax Treatment: Discount earned on redemption is taxed as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) at your slab rate; no TDS deducted at source.

1. What Are Treasury Bills (T-Bills)?

Treasury Bills (commonly known as T-Bills) are short-term promissory notes issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the Government of India to finance temporary mismatches between governmental receipts and expenditure. They represent the benchmark risk-free rate for money markets in India.

Unlike conventional government bonds (dated G-Secs) or corporate debentures, Treasury Bills are zero-coupon securities. This means they do not carry a stated coupon rate and do not disburse periodic interest payments. Instead, they are issued at a price lower than their face value (at a discount) and redeemed by the Central Government at full face value (par, ₹100) upon maturity.

Because they are unconditional sovereign liabilities of the Central Government, Treasury Bills carry the highest credit rating possible in India, representing zero credit or default risk for investors.

2. Tenures & Issuance Schedule (91, 182 & 364 Days)

T-Bill Tenure Approximate Duration Auction Frequency Primary Usage
91-Day T-Bills 3 Months (13 Weeks) Every Wednesday Ultra-short cash parking & quarterly advance tax funds
182-Day T-Bills 6 Months (26 Weeks) Every Wednesday Medium-term liquidity bridging across two financial quarters
364-Day T-Bills 12 Months (52 Weeks) Every Wednesday One-year money market benchmark & risk-free asset allocation

Auction Settlement: Auctions take place on Wednesday via RBI's e-Kuber portal. Settlement is on T+1 (Thursday), when securities are credited directly to your gilt or demat account.

3. Discount Pricing Mechanics & Yield Calculation Formula

Because T-Bills do not pay periodic interest, their yield is implicit in the difference between what you pay at auction (the discounted issue price) and what the government pays you back at maturity (the face value of ₹100).

Annualized T-Bill Yield Formula: Yield (%) = [ (F - P) / P ] × (365 / D) × 100

Where F = Face value (₹100), P = Purchase cut-off price (e.g., ₹98.35), and D = Days to maturity (91, 182, or 364).

4. Step-by-Step Worked Yield Example

Practical Case: ₹1,00,000 Allocation in a 91-Day T-Bill

  • Nominal Face Value: ₹1,00,000 (1,000 units of ₹100 face value)
  • Auction Cut-off Price: ₹98.35 per unit
  • Actual Cash Outlay: 1,000 × ₹98.35 = ₹98,350
  • Maturity Redemption: ₹1,00,000 credited on Day 91

Absolute Return: ₹1,00,000 – ₹98,350 = ₹1,650 (1.6776% in 91 days).
Annualized Yield: 1.6776% × (365 ÷ 91) = 6.73% per annum.

5. RBI Auction Mechanism & Non-Competitive Bidding

RBI conducts Treasury Bill auctions through a two-tier mechanism:

  • Competitive Bidding: Reserved for institutional players (banks, primary dealers, mutual funds). They specify the precise price or yield they demand.
  • Non-Competitive Bidding Scheme (NCBS): Reserved for retail individuals, trusts, and small regional banks. Retail bidders do not quote prices. Instead, you specify how much money you want to invest (minimum ₹10,000), and you receive allotment at the weighted average price of accepted competitive bids.

6. How to Buy T-Bills: RBI Retail Direct & Stockbrokers

  1. RBI Retail Direct: Visit rbiretaildirect.org.in. Open a free Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account. Bids open on Monday and close Tuesday. Pay via UPI or Net Banking with zero brokerage and zero holding fees.
  2. Stockbrokers (Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, ICICI Direct): Access the G-Sec / T-Bills auction window on NSE goBID or BSE Direct. Allocated T-bills are delivered directly to your standard Demat account.

7. Tax Treatment: Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) & TDS

Under the Income-tax Act, 1961, Treasury Bills are capital assets with a tenure under 12 months. Therefore, the discount earned on maturity is classified as Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG) and taxed at your marginal slab rate.

Crucially, the Reserve Bank of India does NOT deduct any TDS upon redemption at maturity. The full ₹100 face value per unit is credited to your bank account without deductions.

9. Who Should Invest in Treasury Bills?

  • High-Net-Worth Individuals (> ₹5 Lakhs): Investors seeking absolute sovereign risk mitigation beyond the ₹5 Lakh DICGC bank deposit insurance ceiling.
  • Advance Tax Planners: Corporates and self-employed professionals aligning 91-day T-bills with quarterly advance tax schedules.
  • Demat Cash Managers: Active market investors keeping dry powder in Demat-held T-bills that can be liquidated or pledged as trading margin.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the minimum amount required to invest in T-Bills?

The minimum investment amount is ₹10,000 nominal face value, and in multiples of ₹10,000 thereafter.

Can State Governments issue Treasury Bills in India?

No. Only the Central Government of India issues Treasury Bills. State Governments raise short-term funds through Ways and Means Advances (WMA) and issue State Development Loans (SDLs).

Can I sell my Treasury Bills before maturity?

Yes. T-Bills held in Demat form can be sold on the secondary debt market of NSE/BSE through your broker, or on the NDS-OM platform via your RBI Retail Direct account.
