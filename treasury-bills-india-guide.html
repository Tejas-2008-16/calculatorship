Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Sovereign Instrument: Issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Central Government with zero default risk.
  • Zero-Coupon Securities: No periodic coupon payments; issued at a discount and redeemed at par (₹100).
  • Standard Maturities: Three tenures available: 91 days, 182 days, and 364 days.
  • Minimum Investment: ₹10,000 (and in multiples of ₹10,000 thereafter).
  • Commission-Free Access: Retail investors bid at weighted average cut-off yield via the RBI Retail Direct platform with zero account maintenance fees.
  • Tax Treatment: Discount earned on redemption is taxed as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) at your slab rate; no TDS deducted at source.

1. What Are Treasury Bills (T-Bills)?

Treasury Bills (commonly known as T-Bills) are short-term promissory notes issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the Government of India to finance temporary mismatches between governmental receipts and expenditure. They represent the benchmark risk-free rate for money markets in India.

Unlike conventional government bonds (dated G-Secs) or corporate debentures, Treasury Bills are zero-coupon securities. This means they do not carry a stated coupon rate and do not disburse periodic interest payments. Instead, they are issued at a price lower than their face value (at a discount) and redeemed by the Central Government at full face value (par, ₹100) upon maturity.

Because they are unconditional sovereign liabilities of the Central Government, Treasury Bills carry the highest credit rating possible in India, representing zero credit or default risk for investors.

2. Tenures & Issuance Schedule (91, 182 & 364 Days)

The Reserve Bank of India currently conducts regular auctions for three standard maturities of Treasury Bills:

91-Day T-Bills

Matures in approximately 3 months (13 weeks). Auctioned weekly on Wednesdays. Highly liquid money market instrument ideal for parking cash earmarked for quarterly tax payments or short-term commitments.

182-Day T-Bills

Matures in approximately 6 months (26 weeks). Auctioned weekly on Wednesdays. Suitable for mid-term liquidity management spanning two quarters.

364-Day T-Bills

Matures in exactly 52 weeks (1 year). Auctioned weekly on Wednesdays. Serves as the one-year sovereign benchmark yield against which bank lending and deposit rates are frequently referenced.

Cash Management Bills (CMBs)

Non-standard short-term bills issued for tenures below 91 days (e.g., 14 to 84 days) during urgent temporary cash flow deficits. Issued on-demand rather than on a pre-notified auction calendar.

Auction Day & Settlement: T-Bill auctions take place every Wednesday on RBI’s e-Kuber electronic platform. Payment settlement occurs on the subsequent working day (T+1, Thursday), at which point the securities are credited electronically to the investor’s gilt or demat account.

3. Discount Pricing Mechanics & Yield Calculation Formula

Because T-Bills do not pay periodic interest, their yield is implicit in the difference between what you pay at auction (the discounted issue price) and what the government pays you back at maturity (the face value of ₹100).

The Annualized Yield Formula

Financial analysts and institutional treasuries compute the annualized yield on a Treasury Bill using the standard money market formula:

Annualized T-Bill Yield Formula: Yield (%) = [ (F - P) / P ] × (365 / D) × 100

Where:
F = Face value of the T-Bill (₹100)
P = Purchase price or cut-off price at auction (e.g., ₹98.30)
D = Number of days to maturity (91, 182, or 364)

Discount vs Yield: A Crucial Distinction

Investors often confuse the rate of discount with the rate of return (yield):

  • Discount Rate: Computed as [(F - P) / F] × (365 / D) × 100. It calculates the discount relative to the face value (₹100).
  • Investment Yield: Computed as [(F - P) / P] × (365 / D) × 100. Because your actual cash outlay is P (which is less than 100), the effective yield is always higher than the discount rate.

4. Step-by-Step Worked Yield Example

Let us walk through a practical scenario to illustrate how a retail investor purchases a 91-day T-Bill and realizes their sovereign return:

Scenario: Bidding for ₹1,00,000 Nominal Value in a 91-Day T-Bill

  • Face Value per unit: ₹100
  • Total units: 1,000 units (Total Face Value = ₹1,00,000)
  • Auction Cut-off Price: ₹98.35 per unit
  • Actual Capital Paid: 1,000 × ₹98.35 = ₹98,350
  • Tenure: 91 days

Calculation of Returns:

  1. Absolute Capital Gain: ₹1,00,000 (Maturity Redemption) – ₹98,350 (Purchase Outlay) = ₹1,650
  2. Absolute Return over 91 Days: (₹1,650 ÷ ₹98,350) × 100 = 1.6776%
  3. Annualized Return (Yield): 1.6776% × (365 ÷ 91) = 6.729% per annum

Upon maturity on Day 91, the Reserve Bank of India credits ₹1,00,000 directly into the investor’s registered bank account, locking in a clean ₹1,650 return without any credit risk or intermediary deduction.

5. RBI Auction Mechanism & Non-Competitive Bidding

Understanding how T-Bills are auctioned enables retail investors to appreciate how their purchase prices are settled.

Competitive vs Non-Competitive Bidding

RBI T-Bill auctions operate on a two-tier bidding architecture:

  • Competitive Bidding: Conducted exclusively for commercial banks, primary dealers, mutual funds, insurance companies, and provident funds. These institutional participants state the specific price or yield at which they wish to purchase.
  • Non-Competitive Bidding Scheme (NCBS): Reserved specifically for retail individual investors, trusts, and small regional rural banks. As a retail investor, you do not have to guess or quote an auction price. You simply specify the nominal amount you want to invest (minimum ₹10,000). You are automatically allotted T-Bills at the weighted average price of all accepted competitive institutional bids.

The RBI reserves up to 5% of the notified auction amount specifically for retail participants under the non-competitive scheme, ensuring that individual bids are almost always fulfilled in full.

6. How to Buy T-Bills: RBI Retail Direct & Stockbrokers

Individual investors have two seamless digital gateways to purchase Treasury Bills in India:

Method 1: RBI Retail Direct (Zero Fees, Direct Holding)

The Reserve Bank of India’s Retail Direct Scheme is the most direct, commission-free platform for retail participants:

  1. Open an account on the RBI Retail Direct portal (rbiretaildirect.org.in). You will need your PAN, Aadhaar, bank details, and an active mobile number.
  2. Complete electronic KYC through the video-verification process. A free Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) Account is created.
  3. Log in on Monday or Tuesday prior to the Wednesday auction. Under the Primary Market > Treasury Bills section, select the desired tenure (91, 182, or 364 days).
  4. Enter your bid amount (minimum ₹10,000, in multiples of ₹10,000).
  5. Block funds using UPI or Net Banking. Upon auction completion on Wednesday, the exact allotment price is calculated, excess funds (if any) are refunded to your bank account, and the T-Bills are credited to your RDG account.

Method 2: Through Stockbrokers (Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, ICICI Direct)

Leading stockbrokers facilitate non-competitive bidding for government securities through the NSE goBID or BSE Direct portals:

  • Navigate to the “Govt Securities” or “G-Sec/T-Bills” section in your broker’s application.
  • Bids open typically every Monday and close on Tuesday evening.
  • Approve the UPI mandate to block the required investment sum.
  • The allocated T-Bills are credited directly into your standard Demat account (CDSL/NSDL) within 1 to 2 business days after auction settlement.

7. Tax Treatment: Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) & TDS

Understanding the exact tax treatment of Treasury Bills ensures there are no surprises when filing your annual Income Tax Return (ITR).

Classification as Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG)

Under the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961, Treasury Bills are defined as capital assets. Because the maximum tenure of any T-Bill does not exceed 364 days (which is well within the 12-month holding threshold for listed securities), the difference between the redemption face value (₹100) and the discounted acquisition price is categorized as Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG).

Short-Term Capital Gains on debt instruments are added to your gross total income under the head “Capital Gains” and taxed at your applicable marginal income tax slab rate under either the Old Tax Regime or the default New Tax Regime.

No Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)

Crucial Benefit: Unlike bank fixed deposits where banks deduct 10% TDS if interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens), the Reserve Bank of India does NOT deduct any TDS when redeeming Treasury Bills at maturity. The full face value of ₹100 per unit is credited to your bank account. However, investors remain legally responsible for declaring the gain in their ITR and paying advance tax where applicable.

9. Who Should Invest in Treasury Bills?

Treasury Bills are not universally superior to bank deposits, but they offer distinct strategic advantages for specific investor profiles:

  • Large Cash Deployers (> ₹5 Lakhs): Investors holding large cash reserves from property sales, business dividends, or bonuses who want absolute sovereign safety beyond the ₹5 Lakh DICGC bank deposit insurance ceiling.
  • Quarterly Advance Tax Planners: Corporates and self-employed professionals parking advance tax funds in 91-day T-Bills matched exactly to advance tax deadlines.
  • Active Demat Cash Managers: Stock market traders keeping cash dry in demat-held T-Bills that can be pledged as margin or liquidated swiftly when market opportunities emerge.
  • Investors in Lower Tax Brackets: Individuals whose taxable income falls under the rebate limits (e.g., up to ₹7 Lakhs under the New Tax Regime) who can enjoy ~6.7%–7.1% risk-free returns without TDS hassles.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the minimum amount required to invest in T-Bills?

The minimum investment amount for Treasury Bills under the non-competitive bidding scheme is ₹10,000 (nominal face value), and in multiples of ₹10,000 thereafter. For example, you can invest ₹10,000, ₹20,000, or ₹1,00,000, but not ₹15,000.

Can State Governments issue Treasury Bills in India?

No. Under Indian financial statutes, only the Central Government of India is authorized to issue Treasury Bills. State Governments manage their short-term cash flow gaps through Ways and Means Advances (WMA) and Overdraft facilities from the RBI, and issue State Development Loans (SDLs) for longer-term market borrowings.

Can I sell my Treasury Bills before maturity?

Yes. T-Bills held in Demat form can be sold on the secondary debt segment of the National Stock Exchange (NSE) or Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) through your stockbroker. T-Bills held in your RBI Retail Direct account can be sold on the secondary market via the NDS-OM (Negotiated Dealing System - Order Matching) platform. However, retail secondary market liquidity can be thin compared to primary auctions.

Are Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) eligible to buy T-Bills?

Yes. Under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) guidelines and RBI regulations, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) are permitted to invest in Government of India Treasury Bills on a non-repatriation or repatriation basis through their NRO/NRE accounts via primary dealer or broker channels.

Do T-Bills provide section 80C income tax deductions?

No. Investments in Treasury Bills do not qualify for any tax deductions under Section 80C or any other provision of Chapter VI-A of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

What happens on the maturity date of a Treasury Bill?

On the scheduled maturity date, the full face value (₹100 per unit) is automatically redeemed by the RBI. The redemption proceeds are directly credited to your registered bank account via NACH/NEFT without requiring any manual claim submission or redemption request.
