How Mutual Fund Categories and Metrics Work in India: An Educational Guide

I'm Tejas, a student. This is what I learned about SEBI mutual fund categorisation, direct plans, and factsheet metrics, written with AI assistance and checked against official SEBI and AMFI documentation. It is not advice, a recommendation to buy or sell any security, or a personalised financial plan. I am not a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser or Research Analyst. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks; past performance is not indicative of future returns.

With dozens of mutual fund classifications available in India, understanding how SEBI defines categories, how expense ratios erode returns, and how to read factsheets helps demystify the investment landscape.

July 25, 2026 By Tejas
← Back to Articles

Opening an investment portal or intermediary app presents a wide array of options: hundreds of mutual fund schemes across large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap, flexi-cap, index, debt, and liquid categories. For anyone new to personal finance, distinguishing between these labels can feel complicated.

Rather than evaluating hundreds of individual schemes, it is helpful to understand the underlying framework: how SEBI classifies funds, how direct and regular plans differ mathematically in costs, and which quantitative metrics appear on an official fund factsheet.

1. How SEBI Defines Mutual Fund Categories

In October 2017, SEBI issued a landmark circular on the Categorization and Rationalization of Mutual Fund Schemes. The objective was to ensure that schemes in the same category have uniform asset allocation mandates, preventing confusion among retail investors.

Broad Category SEBI Mandated Asset Allocation Primary Asset Class Typical Risk Profile
Liquid / Overnight Debt securities with residual maturity up to 91 days (Liquid) or 1 day (Overnight) Short-term money market instruments Lower volatility, interest rate sensitive
Conservative Hybrid 75%–90% in debt instruments, 10%–25% in equity instruments Fixed income with modest equity kicker Moderate risk
Large Cap Fund Minimum 80% investment in top 100 companies by market capitalisation Established blue-chip equity Market volatility, equity risk
Nifty 50 / Sensex Index Minimum 95% replication of the underlying benchmark index Passive blue-chip equity replication Tracks benchmark volatility
Flexi Cap Fund Minimum 65% in equity across large, mid, and small cap without rigid sub-limits Dynamic equity allocation High equity volatility

Different categories serve different liquidity and risk constraints. Debt and liquid funds focus on capital preservation and short-term liquidity, whereas equity funds are designed for long-term compounding subject to market fluctuations.

2. Direct Plans vs. Regular Plans: The Arithmetic of Costs

Under SEBI regulations introduced in January 2013, every mutual fund scheme offers two operational options:

  • Regular Plan: Involves a mutual fund distributor or intermediary. The asset management company (AMC) pays ongoing trail commissions to the distributor, which are deducted from the scheme's assets. This increases the scheme's Total Expense Ratio (TER).
  • Direct Plan: Purchased directly from the AMC or through non-commission execution platforms (like MFCentral or direct AMC portals). No distributor commissions are deducted, resulting in a lower TER.
Arithmetic illustration of cost drag:
Suppose an equity scheme has an annualized gross portfolio growth rate before expenses. If the Regular Plan has a TER of 1.75% and the Direct Plan has a TER of 0.65%, the net return difference is 1.10% per year.

On a hypothetical ₹10,000 monthly investment over 20 years, an illustrative net difference of 1.10% per annum compounds into a noticeable variance in accumulated wealth over long horizons. You can run mathematical comparisons across custom amounts using our free SIP Calculator.

3. Reading Factsheet Metrics: TER, Tracking Error, and Rolling Returns

Asset management companies publish monthly fund factsheets mandated by SEBI. When examining a factsheet, three quantitative metrics provide objective insights:

  1. Total Expense Ratio (TER): SEBI caps the maximum TER an AMC can charge based on scheme Assets Under Management (AUM). A lower TER means a smaller proportion of your investment is deducted annually for fund management and administration.
  2. Tracking Error (for Index Funds & ETFs): Measures the annualized standard deviation of the return difference between the index fund and its benchmark index. A lower tracking error indicates that the fund replicates the target index more closely after accounting for cash holdings and transaction costs.
  3. Rolling Returns: Rather than looking at point-to-point trailing returns (e.g., "1-year return as of today"), rolling returns measure performance across multiple overlapping holding periods (such as every 3-year or 5-year block over a 10-year period). This illustrates consistency across differing market cycles without point-to-point recency bias.

How to Think About This Yourself

  • Clarify Time Horizon First: Money needed in the next 1–3 years has different liquidity requirements than funds allocated for a decade-long objective.
  • Examine Structural Costs: Check the scheme information document (SID) and monthly factsheet for the direct plan's expense ratio and exit load schedule.
  • Avoid Point-to-Point Past Performance: Past top performers in a single bull year frequently change ranks. Rely on regulatory categories and broad market diversification rather than chasing short-term charts.
  • Evaluate Execution Channels: Investors can invest through direct AMC portals, MFCentral, or registered intermediaries, depending on whether they require guidance or self-directed execution.

Where to Get Personalised Help

If you need tailored financial planning or specific scheme recommendations suited to your income, risk appetite, and liabilities, consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA). You can verify RIA credentials directly on the official SEBI Intermediary Directory. For direct execution and official scheme records, visit MFCentral or the respective AMC portal.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does SEBI categorise mutual fund schemes in India?
Under SEBI's 2017 categorisation circular, schemes are classified into distinct groups including Equity (such as Large Cap, Mid Cap, Flexi Cap), Debt (such as Liquid, Short Duration), Hybrid, and Solution Oriented, each with defined asset allocation rules.
What is the structural difference between Direct and Regular mutual fund plans?
Regular plans include distributor commissions built into the annual Total Expense Ratio (TER), which lowers the scheme's net asset value (NAV). Direct plans have no intermediary commissions, resulting in a lower TER and proportionately higher NAV over time.
Why are rolling returns preferred over trailing returns?
Trailing returns measure performance only between two fixed dates, which can be heavily skewed by market highs or lows on either endpoint. Rolling returns compute annualized returns across overlapping windows over several years, providing a clearer picture of historical consistency.
