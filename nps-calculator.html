NPS Calculator (National Pension System)

Written by Tejas Waghmare • Last updated: November 2026
Educational content only: This calculator provides mathematical projections of NPS Tier 1 wealth accumulation. Annuity rates and equity returns vary over time and are regulated by PFRDA and IRDAI.

Direct Answer: The National Pension System (NPS) is a voluntary, market-linked retirement scheme regulated by PFRDA. At retirement age 60, up to 60% of your corpus can be withdrawn completely tax-free, while at least 40% must be deployed into a life annuity to generate monthly pension.

NPS Contribution Parameters

Retirement Corpus at Age 60

Projected Total Corpus ₹1,13,96,000

Lump sum is 100% tax-free under Section 10(12A); monthly annuity is taxable.

How the NPS Corpus & Annuity Formula Works

Retirement requires planning. Start investing early. Compounding needs decades. When structuring your long-term retirement planning through the National Pension System, beginning regular monthly contributions in your twenties rather than your late thirties creates an enormous compounding advantage due to decades of uninterrupted market returns. The National Pension System (NPS) Tier 1 is an investment vehicle regulated by PFRDA. The maturity corpus calculation follows compound monthly accumulation:

  • Monthly Accumulation: Monthly contributions compound at your asset allocation weighted return across Equity (E), Corporate Debt (C), and Government Bonds (G).
  • Mandatory 40% Annuity at Age 60: Under PFRDA exit regulations, at least 40% of the accumulated corpus must be utilized to purchase an immediate annuity from an IRDAI-registered life insurance company.
  • 60% Lump Sum Withdrawal: Up to 60% of the accumulated corpus can be withdrawn as a tax-free lump sum under Section 10(12A).

2 Worked Mathematical Examples in Rupees

Example 1: Starting at Age 30 (₹5,000/Month)

  • Total Invested (30 Years): ₹5,000 * 360 months = ₹18,00,000.
  • Assumed Return: 10.0% p.a. (moderate equity & debt mix).
  • Projected Total Corpus at 60: ~₹1.14 Crore.
  • Tax-Free Lump Sum (60%): ~₹68.4 Lakh.
  • Annuity (40% at 6% yield): ~₹45.6 Lakh corpus yielding ~₹22,800 monthly pension.

Example 2: Starting at Age 40 (₹15,000/Month)

  • Total Invested (20 Years): ₹15,000 * 240 months = ₹36,00,000.
  • Assumed Return: 9.5% p.a.
  • Projected Total Corpus at 60: ~₹1.08 Crore.
  • Tax-Free Lump Sum (60%): ~₹64.8 Lakh.
  • Annuity (40% at 6% yield): ~₹43.2 Lakh corpus yielding ~₹21,600 monthly pension.

3 Common Mistakes When Planning NPS Returns

Annuities provide pensions. Equity drives growth. PFRDA regulates rules. Lump sums are tax-free. Pensions face income tax. Choose fund managers wisely. Tier 1 enforces discipline. Every contribution counts. Because annuity income received from life insurance service providers after retirement age sixty remains fully taxable under your applicable income tax slab rates, calculating your net post-tax monthly pension helps you build adequate supplementary retirement savings.

1. Monthly Annuity Is Fully Taxable

While the 60% lump sum exit is 100% tax-free under Section 10(12A), monthly annuity pension payments are added to your taxable income in retirement and taxed at your applicable slab rates.

2. Over-Allocating to Government Bonds in Your 20s

NPS allows up to 75% equity exposure (Scheme E) under Active Choice up to age 50. Sticking to an ultra-conservative 100% debt portfolio early in your career limits long-term compounding against inflation.

3. Ignoring Corporate NPS under Section 80CCD(2)

Employer contributions up to 14% of (Basic + DA) for government and 10% for private sector employees are tax-deductible under both the Old and New Tax Regimes, above and beyond standard limits.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Can I withdraw 100% of my NPS corpus without annuity?

Under PFRDA exit regulations, if your total accumulated corpus at retirement age 60 is ₹5 Lakh or less, you can withdraw the entire 100% as a lump sum without purchasing an annuity.

What happens if I exit NPS before age 60?

If you exit NPS prematurely before age 60, at least 80% of your accumulated corpus must be deployed into an annuity, leaving only 20% available as a lump sum.

Is NPS Tier 2 also eligible for tax deduction?

No. NPS Tier 2 is an open-ended voluntary investment account with zero withdrawal restrictions, but contributions do not qualify for tax deductions (except for central government employees under specific 3-year lock-in rules).
