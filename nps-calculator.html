The National Pension System (NPS): Complete Indian Investor Guide

The National Pension System (NPS) is a voluntary, long-term retirement savings scheme regulated by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) under the Government of India. With fund management charges capped at under 0.09% per annum, NPS is one of the world's most cost-effective retirement accumulation tools.

Any Indian citizen aged between 18 and 70 years can open an NPS Tier-1 account and allocate savings across Equity (E), Corporate Bonds (C), Government Securities (G), and Alternative Assets (A) through Auto Choice (Lifecycle Fund) or Active Choice.

PFRDA Superannuation & Exit Mandates

Upon reaching the superannuation age of 60:

  • 60% Tax-Free Lump Sum: You can withdraw up to 60% of the accumulated corpus completely tax-free under Section 10(12A) of the Income-tax Act.
  • Mandatory 40% Annuity: You must allocate at least 40% of the corpus to purchase an annuity from an approved Life Insurance company (like LIC, HDFC Life, SBI Life) providing a guaranteed monthly pension for life.
  • Small Corpus Exemption: If your total accumulated corpus is ₹5,00,000 or less, PFRDA permits a 100% lump-sum withdrawal without purchasing an annuity.

Frequently Asked Questions About NPS

Is the monthly annuity pension from NPS taxable?

Yes. While the 60% lump-sum withdrawal is 100% tax-free under Section 10(12A), the monthly pension received from the remaining 40% annuity is treated as income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates in the financial year of receipt.

Can I choose 100% equity allocation in NPS?

Under current PFRDA rules for private sector citizens, the maximum equity allocation (Asset Class E) under Active Choice is capped at 75% up to age 50, after which it automatically tapers down by 2.5% each year until it stabilizes at 50% at age 60 to protect the corpus from market drawdowns.

What is the difference between Tier-1 and Tier-2 NPS accounts?

Tier-1 is the primary, non-withdrawable retirement pension account that qualifies for tax deductions with lock-in until age 60. Tier-2 is an optional voluntary investment account with unrestricted liquidity where you can deposit and withdraw at any time, but contributions carry no tax deductions.

Strategic Retirement Portfolio Rule

To build an inflation-proof retirement nest egg in India:

  1. Leverage Section 80CCD(2) Corporate NPS: Ask your employer to route 10% of your Basic Wage directly into NPS. This deduction directly reduces your taxable income even under the New Tax Regime.
  2. Combine NPS with Equity Mutual Funds: While NPS locks in 40% into mandatory annuities, maintaining parallel diversified mutual fund SIPs provides unrestricted liquidity at retirement.
  3. Choose Active Choice (75% Equity) in Your 20s and 30s: Young professionals with a 25-30 year horizon should maximize equity exposure to benefit from compounding equity risk premia.
