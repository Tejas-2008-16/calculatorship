Muhurat Trading 2026: Date, Timings & How the Diwali Trading Session Works

Hi, I'm Tejas — an engineering student researching personal finance. This guide outlines the operational timetable, settlement rules, Closing Auction Session (CAS) mechanics, and tax structure of Diwali Muhurat Trading. Every Diwali, Indian stock markets open for a special one-hour session to ring in the new Hindu financial year. Here is everything you need to know about Samvat 2083 timings, settlement mechanics, Closing Auction Session rules, capital gains taxes, and practical guidance.

Quick Answer: What You Need to Know

Diwali Muhurat Trading for Samvat 2083 takes place on Sunday, 8 November 2026. Continuous trading runs from 2:20 PM to 3:30 PM. It is the first Muhurat session incorporating the Closing Auction Session (CAS). Trades are real, binding contracts settling on T+1, taxed at 20% STCG or 12.5% LTCG above ₹1.25 Lakh.

1. What Is Muhurat Trading and What Does Samvat Mean?

Imagine your neighborhood merchant closing their traditional red-bound ledger (Chopda) on Diwali evening, lighting an oil lamp, and opening a brand-new ledger with prayers for prosperity. For centuries in India, this marked the commercial fresh start of the traditional mercantile calendar: Vikram Samvat.

The Vikram Samvat calendar is a historical Hindu lunar calendar that is roughly 56.7 years ahead of the Gregorian solar calendar. The Diwali session on 8 November 2026 rings in Samvat 2083. For Indian businesses and investors, this hour-long trading window represents an auspicious initiation of wealth-building for the coming year.

While the cultural backdrop is rich with diyas and celebratory rituals, the market operations are fully digital and regulated. Every order matched on exchange servers creates legally binding settlement rights and obligations.

2. History: BSE in 1957 to NSE in 1992

The tradition of opening the stock exchange during Diwali evening began on the floor of the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) in 1957. Floor traders, jobbers, and brokers in Mumbai gathered in traditional attire with their families to conduct ceremonial trades, distribute sweets, and bless their order books.

When the National Stock Exchange (NSE) commenced operations in 1992 as India's first fully electronic screen-based nationwide exchange, it embraced the custom. Electronic trading allowed investors from small towns and metro cities alike to log into their broker accounts simultaneously, opening access to what was once an exclusive Dalal Street gathering.

Over the decades, Muhurat trading has evolved from physical open-outcry bidding into a nationwide digital event involving millions of retail demat account holders.

3. Official 2026 Schedule & The Closing Auction Session (CAS)

The National Stock Exchange (NSE) and BSE circulars define the detailed timetable for the Diwali Muhurat Trading session for Samvat 2083. The session is scheduled for the afternoon of Sunday, 8 November 2026.

A notable structural change in 2026 is that this session is the first Muhurat event conducted following the full rollout of the Closing Auction Session (CAS) across Indian exchanges. Under CAS rules, eligible securities transition from continuous trading to a specialized call auction mechanism to discover the official closing price.

4. Settlement Obligations & Balipratipada Holiday (10 Nov 2026)

A common point of confusion among first-time participants is whether Sunday trades settle immediately. Under SEBI's standardized T+1 rolling settlement cycle, equity trades settle on the next clearing business day.

Here is how the calendar functions around the 2026 session:

  • Sunday, 8 November 2026: Muhurat Trading session takes place. Trades are executed and marked for settlement.
  • Monday, 9 November 2026: Regular trading day for NSE and BSE. Shares bought on Sunday are processed through the clearing corporations (NSCCL / ICCL).
  • Tuesday, 10 November 2026: Official Market Holiday on account of Diwali Balipratipada. Both equity trading and clearing settlement desks are completely closed.
  • Wednesday, 11 November 2026: Markets reopen and regular clearing cycles resume.

Because trades generate actual settlement obligations, if you sell shares you do not own or default on fund debits, you face auction penalties and square-off charges just like any regular Tuesday or Friday.

5. Available Segments and Eligible Order Types

According to the official circulars issued by NSE and BSE, the following segments are operational during the Muhurat window:

  • Equity Cash Segment (Capital Market): All listed equity shares, Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs), and Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) traded on secondary markets.
  • Equity Derivatives Segment: Futures and options contracts on indices (Nifty, Bank Nifty, Sensex) and approved single stocks.
  • Commodity Derivatives & Currency: Gold and silver futures, crude oil, and notified currency pairs.

Eligible vs Ineligible Order Types

Standard order types—including Limit Orders and Market Orders—are accepted. However, certain automated order facilities behave differently:

  • Good-Till-Triggered (GTT) Orders: Most discount brokerages disable GTT triggers during the 70-minute session to prevent order queue jams.
  • After-Market Orders (AMO): AMOs placed over the weekend are pushed into the pre-open auction between 2:05 PM and 2:15 PM.
  • Unexecuted Orders: All open, unexecuted limit orders are automatically cancelled by the exchange at session close (3:30 PM / 3:35 PM). They do not carry forward to Monday morning.

6. How Muhurat Trading Differs from a Normal Trading Session

While the matching engine is identical, the market microstructure during Muhurat trading has four distinct characteristics:

  1. Compressed Window: At just 70 minutes of continuous trading, participants have very little time to adjust positions or react to breaking global cues.
  2. Predominance of Buyers: Because retail participants view the session as an auspicious ritual to buy token shares, order books lean heavily toward buy orders. This often produces an initial pop in broad indices.
  3. Thin Institutional Volume: Large institutional desks and foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) rarely execute large rebalancing orders on a Sunday afternoon, resulting in lower aggregate institutional liquidity.
  4. Wider Bid-Ask Spreads: In less liquid midcap and smallcap counters, the spread between the best buy price and best sell price can widen, making market orders vulnerable to price slippage.

7. Myths vs Facts Comparison Table

Let us dispel the most frequent misconceptions surrounding Diwali market sessions:

Common Myth Factual Regulatory Reality Practical Impact
"Trades are merely symbolic or demo orders." Every trade is legally binding, settled via clearing houses, and debited from bank accounts. Failed trades result in auction penalties.
"The market is open for full normal trading hours." Continuous trading runs for only 70 minutes (2:20 PM to 3:30 PM), not 6.25 hours. Strict cut-off times apply for squaring off intraday positions.
"A green Muhurat close predicts a bull year." Historical data reveals zero statistical correlation between Diwali gains and 12-month returns. Short-term festive pops do not guarantee full-year compounding.
"Diwali trades are exempt from capital gains tax." Standard Income-tax Act provisions apply: STT is charged, and STCG/LTCG apply on disposal. All realized gains must be reported in your annual income tax return.

8. Tax Treatment (STT, STCG 20%, LTCG 12.5%) & Worked Example

Profits made on shares purchased during Muhurat Trading follow standard Indian capital gains taxation rules updated under recent Finance Act revisions:

  • Securities Transaction Tax (STT): Levied at 0.1% on delivery purchases and sales of listed equities, deducted automatically on the broker's contract note.
  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG - Section 111A): If equity shares are held for 12 months or less, gains are taxed at a flat rate of 20% (plus applicable 4% cess).
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG - Section 112A): If held for more than 12 months, gains exceeding the statutory threshold of ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year are taxed at 12.5% without indexation benefits. Gains below ₹1.25 Lakh remain tax-free.

Worked Numerical Example: A Festive Purchase of ₹5,000

Consider an illustrative scenario: Priya, a salaried professional in Bengaluru, participates in Muhurat Trading for the first time by purchasing 10 shares of a leading Nifty 50 constituent at an illustrative price of ₹500 per share.

Purchase Invoice Breakdown (Sunday, 8 Nov 2026):

  • Gross Share Value: 10 shares × ₹500 = ₹5,000.00
  • Brokerage (typical discount broker delivery rate): ₹0.00 (or flat ₹20 depending on broker plan)
  • Securities Transaction Tax (STT @ 0.1% on buy): ₹5.00
  • Exchange Transaction Charges (~0.00345%): ₹0.17
  • SEBI Turnover Fee: ₹0.01
  • State Stamp Duty (0.015% on equity delivery buy): ₹0.75
  • GST on Brokerage & Exchange fees (18%): ₹0.03
  • Total Transaction Costs: ₹5.96
  • Total Bank Debit: ₹5,005.96

Scenario A (Long-Term Compounding): Priya holds her shares for 3 years and redeems them at ₹800 per share (proceeds ₹8,000). Her capital gain is ₹2,994.04. Assuming her total equity LTCG across all investments in that financial year is under the ₹1.25 Lakh threshold, her tax liability is ₹0.00.

Scenario B (Short-Term Flip): If Priya sells her shares 4 months later for ₹620 (gain of ₹1,194.04), the gain is classified as STCG under Section 111A. Her tax at 20% plus 4% cess (effective 20.8%) equals ₹248.36, leaving a net post-tax gain of ₹945.68.

9. Key Takeaways for Indian Investors

  • Exact Timings: Continuous trading runs from 2:20 PM to 3:30 PM on Sunday, 8 November 2026. Pre-open is 2:05 PM to 2:20 PM.
  • Closing Auction Active: This is the first Muhurat session incorporating the Closing Auction Session (CAS) for price settlement.
  • Settlement Obligations: Every executed order is a legally binding trade settling on T+1. Tuesday 10 November is a market holiday.
  • Use Limit Orders: Given the brief 70-minute window and potential spread volatility, avoid unpriced market orders.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the date and timing for Muhurat Trading 2026?

Diwali Muhurat Trading 2026 for Samvat 2083 is scheduled for Sunday, 8 November 2026. Continuous regular trading runs from 2:20 PM to 3:30 PM, preceded by a pre-open session from 2:05 PM to 2:20 PM as confirmed in official exchange circulars.

Are trades executed during Muhurat Trading real or mock?

Every trade executed during Muhurat Trading is a real, legally binding transaction resulting in standard settlement obligations. Shares purchased will be debited or credited to your Demat account under standard T+1 rolling settlement.

Is the stock market open on Monday, 9 November or Tuesday, 10 November 2026?

Monday, 9 November 2026 is a regular working trading day. Tuesday, 10 November 2026 is an official clearing and trading holiday on account of Diwali Balipratipada.

How does the Closing Auction Session (CAS) operate during Muhurat Trading?

2026 marks the first Muhurat session featuring the Closing Auction Session. For eligible securities, normal trading closes at 3:15 PM, order entry in CAS runs from 3:20 PM to 3:30 PM, and order matching concludes by 3:35 PM to ensure transparent closing price discovery.

What are the tax implications on shares bought during Muhurat Trading?

Capital gains follow standard equity rules: short-term capital gains (held under 12 months) are taxed at flat 20% under Section 111A. Long-term gains (held over 12 months) are taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year under Section 112A.

Can retail investors trade in F&O and Commodity derivatives during the session?

Yes, exchanges open Equity Derivatives and Commodity Derivatives alongside the cash segment from 2:20 PM. However, wide bid-ask spreads and liquidity distortions during a brief 70-minute window make derivative trading risky for retail participants.

Does a positive return on Muhurat day predict a bullish market year?

No. Historical market data shows zero statistical correlation between indices closing green during the 1-hour Muhurat session and broader market performance over the subsequent Samvat year. Muhurat trading is a cultural tradition, not a financial forecasting tool.

How much money do people commonly allocate during Muhurat Trading?

People commonly treat Muhurat Trading as a symbolic ritual, allocating modest token amounts of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 to broad index funds or bluechip shares, keeping their primary wealth creation anchored in disciplined monthly SIPs.

How to Think About This Yourself

Muhurat Trading is a symbolic tradition rather than an opportunity for aggressive speculative day-trading. When considering participation in the session:

  • Liquidity and Spread Awareness: Because continuous trading lasts only ~70 minutes, thin market depth and wider bid-ask spreads can trigger unexpected slippage on market orders; prefer limit orders.
  • Real Settlement Obligations: Every executed order is a legally binding contract subject to standard T+1 clearing and statutory taxes (STT, stamp duty, GST, and SEBI turnover fees).
  • Long-Term Symbolism: If participating for auspicious continuity, consider token long-term equity or index fund allocations rather than high-leverage intraday or derivatives bets.
