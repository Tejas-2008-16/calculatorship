Section 80G Donations Tax Deduction Guide: 100% vs 50% Limits & Form 10BE

Donating to charitable organizations and national disaster relief funds not only creates social impact but also qualifies for valuable tax deductions under Section 80G of the Income Tax Act. However, claiming Section 80G is among the most audited deductions in India. Learn the 4 distinct categories (100% vs 50% deduction, with and without qualifying limits), the 10% Adjusted Gross Total Income cap, mandatory electronic Form 10BE certificates, and the ₹2,000 cash restriction for FY 2026-27.

Statutory Compliance Notice: Section 80G deductions are governed by Section 80G of the Income Tax Act, 1961, and Rule 18AB of the Income Tax Rules, 1962. A valid Certificate of Donation in Form 10BE issued by the donee organization is a mandatory legal prerequisite for claiming deduction. Section 80G is an Old Tax Regime deduction and is not admissible under Section 115BAC (New Tax Regime).

Key Takeaways & Executive Summary

  • Four Statutory Tiers: Deductions range from 100% without qualifying limits (e.g. PM National Relief Fund) to 50% subject to qualifying limits (ordinary registered trusts).
  • The 10% AGTI Cap: For categories with a qualifying limit, total eligible donations cannot exceed 10% of Adjusted Gross Total Income.
  • Mandatory Form 10BE: Tax receipts alone are no longer sufficient; the trust must e-file Form 10BD and issue an electronic Form 10BE with a Unique Registration Number (URN).
  • Strict Cash Ban: Donations made in cash exceeding ₹2,000 are 100% disallowed. Donations in kind (food, clothing, goods) do not qualify.
  • Old Regime Only: Section 80G cannot be claimed under the default New Tax Regime.

1. What is Section 80G & Who Can Claim It?

Section 80G of the Income Tax Act, 1961, was introduced into Chapter VI-A to encourage philanthropic giving towards relief funds, charitable trusts, universities, and approved non-governmental institutions. Any taxpayer in India—including individuals, salaried employees, sole proprietors, partnership firms, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), companies, and Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)—can claim a deduction from their Gross Total Income for eligible contributions.

However, Section 80G does not offer a uniform deduction. The tax law categorizes donee institutions into four distinct legal groups, each governed by different percentages and mathematical ceiling thresholds.

2. The 4 Statutory Deduction Categories: 100% vs 50% Matrix

Eligible funds and institutions under Section 80G are categorized across two dimensions: the percentage of deduction allowed (100% or 50%) and whether the deduction is capped by a qualifying limit:

Category Deduction Rate Qualifying Limit Prominent Qualifying Institutions
Category A 100% Deduction No Qualifying Limit National Defence Fund, PM CARES Fund, PM National Relief Fund, Clean Ganga Fund, National Children’s Fund
Category B 50% Deduction No Qualifying Limit Jawaharlal Nehru Memorial Fund, Prime Minister’s Drought Relief Fund, Indira Gandhi Memorial Trust
Category C 100% Deduction Subject to 10% AGTI Limit Donations to Government or approved local authority/association for promoting family planning
Category D 50% Deduction Subject to 10% AGTI Limit Approved charitable NGOs, religious trusts (for renovation), educational trusts, and hospitals registered under Section 80G(5)

The vast majority of donations made by Indian citizens to non-profit organizations, food drives, and community education programs fall under Category D, meaning donors receive a 50% deduction subject to the qualifying income limit.

3. Understanding the 10% Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI) Limit

For donations made under Categories C and D, Section 80G(4) establishes a strict ceiling: the total eligible donations cannot exceed 10% of Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI).

Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI) Formula:

AGTI = Gross Total Income (GTI) minus:

  • Long-Term Capital Gains taxable under Section 112 or Section 112A.
  • Short-Term Capital Gains on listed equities taxable under Section 111A.
  • All Chapter VI-A deductions (Section 80C, 80D, 80E, 80TTA, etc.) excluding Section 80G itself.
  • Income referred to in Sections 115A, 115AB, 115AC, 115AD (special non-resident provisions).

The Maximum Permissible Donation across Categories C and D combined is capped at 10% of this calculated AGTI. Category C donations are accommodated first; any remaining balance within the 10% limit is applied to Category D donations at 50%.

4. Mandatory Form 10BE & Form 10BD Verification Protocol

Historically, taxpayers claimed Section 80G deductions simply by retaining paper donation receipts. This loophole led to rampant fake donation syndicates. To establish total transparency, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) introduced Rule 18AB via the Finance Act:

  1. Donee e-Files Form 10BD: Every charitable trust, society, or institution registered under Section 80G is legally required to file an annual digital statement of donations in Form 10BD by May 31 immediately following the financial year.
  2. Generation of Form 10BE: Once Form 10BD is submitted, the Income Tax e-filing portal automatically generates Form 10BE (Certificate of Donation) for each individual donor.
  3. Mandatory URN: Form 10BE contains a Unique Registration Number (URN), the donor's PAN, address, donation mode, and the exact amount contributed.

Critical Rule: A paper receipt or cancelled cheque is legally invalid for claiming Section 80G. If the donee trust fails to upload Form 10BD and issue you a downloadable Form 10BE, the Centralized Processing Centre (CPC) will disallow your deduction during ITR processing.

5. The ₹2,000 Cash Donation Restriction & Banking Modes

Under Section 80G(5D) of the Income Tax Act, no deduction is allowed for any donation exceeding ₹2,000 if paid in cash. This rule was tightened from the former ₹10,000 limit to curtail unaccounted cash flows.

To ensure 100% tax eligibility for donations of any size:

  • Approved Payment Modes: Account payee cheque, demand draft, NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, or UPI.
  • Cash Donations up to ₹2,000: Eligible for deduction, but must still be substantiated with an official receipt and donor PAN.
  • Cash Donations Exceeding ₹2,000: If you donate ₹5,000 in cash, the entire ₹5,000 is disallowed—not just the ₹3,000 excess.

6. Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Case Study

Let us review an end-to-end mathematical example illustrating how the 10% qualifying limit and deduction tiers operate in practice.

Donor Financial Profile: Vikram

  • Gross Salary Income: ₹14,00,000
  • Interest Income from FDs: ₹1,00,000
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (Section 112A): ₹2,00,000
  • Gross Total Income (GTI): ₹17,00,000
  • Section 80C Deductions (EPF + PPF): ₹1,50,000
  • Section 80D Health Insurance: ₹25,00,0
  • Standard Deduction on Salary: ₹50,000 (Old Regime)

Donations Made During the Financial Year

  • Donation to PM National Relief Fund (Category A — 100% no limit): ₹50,000
  • Donation to Approved Child Welfare NGO (Category D — 50% with limit): ₹1,80,000

Step 1: Compute Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI)

GTI: ₹17,00,000
Less: LTCG Sec 112A: −₹2,00,000
Less: Sec 80C + 80D: −₹1,75,000
Less: Standard Deduction: −₹50,000
AGTI = ₹12,75,000

Step 2: Determine the Qualifying Ceiling

Maximum Qualifying Limit for Categories C & D = 10% of ₹12,75,000 = ₹1,27,500.

Step 3: Calculate Permissible Deductions

  1. PM National Relief Fund (Category A): 100% of ₹50,000 = ₹50,000 (No ceiling applied).
  2. Child Welfare NGO (Category D): Actual donation is ₹1,80,000, but qualifying ceiling is ₹1,27,500. The excess ₹52,500 is ignored. Eligible deduction = 50% of ₹1,27,500 = ₹63,750.

Total Section 80G Deduction = ₹50,000 + ₹63,750 = ₹1,13,750

At a 30% marginal tax slab (+ 4% cess), Vikram saves ₹35,490 in direct income tax.

7. Section 80G under Old vs New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC)

With the default adoption of Section 115BAC (New Tax Regime), taxpayers must carefully assess which regime maximizes their net post-tax savings:

Parameter Old Tax Regime New Tax Regime (Sec 115BAC)
Section 80G Deductions Fully Admissible (Subject to tiers & limits) Not Allowed (Nil Deduction)
Form 10BE Requirement Mandatory before filing Not Applicable
Slab Rates 5%, 20%, 30% slabs Concessional progressive slabs

If you make substantial charitable donations, test your numbers on our Income Tax Calculator to determine if the Old Regime yields greater net tax savings than the New Regime.

8. Donations Ineligible for Deduction (In-Kind, Foreign, Political)

Many well-intentioned donors are dismayed when claims are rejected during assessment. Review these non-deductible contributions:

  • Donations in Kind: Distributing blankets, medicine, school books, or ration kits does not qualify under Section 80G. Only cash (up to ₹2,000) or electronic monetary transfers are recognized.
  • Donations to Foreign Charities: Contributions to overseas trusts, international NGOs not registered in India, or foreign universities do not qualify.
  • Donations to Political Parties: Contributions to registered political parties or electoral trusts are governed by Section 80GGB (for companies) and Section 80GGC (for individuals), not Section 80G.
  • Religious Trusts Without Specified Renovation Status: General donations to temples, mosques, or churches for daily maintenance are non-deductible unless the government has notified the site under Section 80G(2)(b) for historic preservation or renovation.

9. How to Report Section 80G in ITR-1 and ITR-2

To successfully claim Section 80G, the information in your return must precisely match the e-filed Form 10BD records. In your ITR form:

  1. Navigate to Schedule 80G (or the Section 80G table in ITR-1).
  2. Select the correct statutory table:
    • Table A: 100% deduction without qualifying limit.
    • Table B: 50% deduction without qualifying limit.
    • Table C: 100% deduction subject to qualifying limit.
    • Table D: 50% deduction subject to qualifying limit.
  3. Enter the Donee's PAN, full legal name, registered address, and the Unique Registration Number (URN) from your Form 10BE.
  4. Enter the split between cash and non-cash (banking) amounts.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What should I do if a charity does not give me Form 10BE?

Contact the institution immediately and ensure they have your correct PAN. Remind them to file Form 10BD on the income tax portal. Without Form 10BD filing, you cannot generate Form 10BE, and claiming the deduction in your ITR will result in an automated defect notice from the CPC.

Can I claim Section 80G for donations made through salary deductions?

Yes. If your employer runs a payroll giving program or collects contributions for disaster relief (e.g. PM CARES Fund), the employer reflects the donation in Form 16 Part B, enabling a direct claim.

Is there an overall monetary ceiling on Section 80G like the ₹1.5 Lakh under 80C?

No. Section 80G does not have a flat monetary cap like Section 80C. Category A donations are uncapped, while Category C and D donations are limited only by the 10% Adjusted Gross Total Income formula.

Can a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) claim Section 80G?

Yes. NRIs can claim Section 80G deductions for donations made to eligible Indian funds and charitable institutions against their taxable Indian income.

11. Official Statutory References & Regulatory Framework

  • Income Tax Act, 1961 — Section 80G

    Statutory provisions governing deductions in respect of donations to certain funds, charitable institutions, and causes

  • Income Tax Rules, 1962 — Rule 18AB, Form 10BD & Form 10BE

    Mandatory procedures for furnishing statement of donations and issuing electronic certificate of donation to donors

  • Finance Act, 2017 & Finance Act, 2020 Amendments

    Statutory reduction of cash donation threshold to ₹2,000 and introduction of the electronic Form 10BD/10BE regime
