What is NAV in Mutual Funds? Why It Does NOT Matter for SIP Investors

One of the most persistent misconceptions among Indian retail investors is equating a mutual fund's Net Asset Value (NAV) to a stock price. Many believe a ₹10 NAV fund is "cheap" and a ₹500 NAV fund is "expensive." Here is the rigorous mathematical proof of why NAV is irrelevant to your returns, how daily pricing works, and how SIP investors harness NAV fluctuations to build multi-crore wealth.

1. What is NAV? The Mathematical Formula

Net Asset Value (NAV) represents the per-unit book value of a mutual fund scheme. When you invest ₹10,000 in a mutual fund, you do not buy shares of a single company; you buy a collective slice of a diversified portfolio of equities, debt instruments, cash, and corporate bonds managed by an Asset Management Company (AMC).

Unlike listed equity shares where prices fluctuate second-by-second on the NSE or BSE based on supply and demand, a mutual fund's NAV is calculated exactly once per day at the close of the trading session by the fund accountant.

The NAV Calculation Formula

NAV = [ (Total Value of Portfolio Securities + Cash & Receivables) - (Total Liabilities + Daily Accrued Expenses) ] / Total Number of Outstanding Units

Worked Example: Suppose an equity scheme holds ₹1,050 Crore worth of shares across 45 companies, holds ₹50 Crore in liquid cash reserves, owes ₹10 Crore in management fees and operational liabilities, and has 10 Crore outstanding units issued to investors.

Net Assets = ₹1,050 Cr + ₹50 Cr - ₹10 Cr = ₹1,090 Crore.
NAV per Unit = ₹1,090 Crore / 10 Crore Units = ₹109.00 per unit.

2. The Big Indian NAV Myth: ₹10 vs ₹500 NAV Compared

The most common error beginner investors make is treating NAV like a company's share price. In the stock market, if Company A trades at ₹50 and Company B trades at ₹5,000, Company A might appear to have a lower nominal price (though valuation depends on P/E ratio and earnings). However, in mutual funds, a low NAV does NOT indicate cheapness, and a high NAV does NOT indicate that a fund is expensive or saturated.

A mutual fund with a NAV of ₹1,000 simply indicates that the fund has been running successfully for 20–25 years, compounding capital and accumulating wealth. A fund with a NAV of ₹10 is either a brand new fund or an NFO that launched yesterday.

Mathematical Proof: ₹10 NAV vs ₹500 NAV

Let us take two investors, Pooja and Rahul. Both invest ₹1,00,000 on the exact same day in two different large-cap funds (Fund Alpha and Fund Beta) that hold identical underlying Nifty 50 stocks. Over the next 5 years, the underlying index grows by 60%.

Metric / Parameter Fund Alpha (Low NAV) Fund Beta (High NAV)
Initial NAV per Unit ₹10.00 ₹500.00
Amount Invested ₹1,00,000 ₹1,00,000
Units Allotted 10,000.000 units 200.000 units
Portfolio Growth +60% +60%
New NAV after 5 Years ₹16.00 ₹800.00
Final Value of Holding 10,000 × ₹16 = ₹1,60,000 200 × ₹800 = ₹1,60,000
Net Wealth Created ₹60,000 (+60%) ₹60,000 (+60%)

The Takeaway: Despite Pooja owning 10,000 units and Rahul owning only 200 units, both generated the exact same ₹1,60,000 corpus and ₹60,000 profit. In mutual fund investing, your wealth grows in direct proportion to the percentage capital appreciation of the underlying assets, not the number of units you hold.

3. How NAV Moves Daily & SEBI Cut-Off Rules

Every business day, after the Indian equity markets shut at 3:30 PM IST, AMCs value their entire portfolio based on closing stock prices on the BSE and NSE. The daily NAV is published on the AMFI portal and AMC websites by 11:00 PM IST.

SEBI Cut-off Timings for NAV Allotment (India FY 2026-27)

To ensure fair pricing and prevent arbitrage, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) mandates strict cut-off timings. If you execute a purchase or redemption, the NAV allotted to your transaction depends on your order timestamp and fund realization:

Mutual Fund Scheme Type SEBI Cut-off Time NAV Allotment Rule
Equity & Hybrid Funds (Purchase) 3:00 PM IST Same-day NAV if funds are credited to AMC account before 3:00 PM; otherwise Next Business Day NAV.
Debt & Conservative Funds (Purchase) 3:00 PM IST Same-day NAV subject to fund realization before 3:00 PM.
Liquid & Overnight Funds (Purchase) 1:30 PM IST Previous day's NAV if funds realized before 1:30 PM.
All Schemes (Redemption / Exit) 3:00 PM IST Same-day closing NAV if submitted before 3:00 PM; next day closing NAV if after 3:00 PM.

Note: Since SEBI's NAV realization rule implementation, UPI or Net Banking speed directly affects your NAV allotment date for lumpsum investments. For automated monthly SIPs, banks execute debits early morning, ensuring same-day NAV on your scheduled date.

4. Why the ₹10 NFO (New Fund Offer) is a Psychological Trap

Many distributors and marketing campaigns pitch New Fund Offers (NFOs) by highlighting: "Get in on the ground floor at only ₹10 per unit!"

This is a psychological illusion that exploits equity IPO thinking. In an IPO of an unlisted company, buying at a lower valuation multiple can provide listing gains if the business is fundamentally underpriced. However, in a Mutual Fund NFO:

  • No Discount on Stocks: If an NFO raises ₹1,000 Crore at ₹10 per unit, the fund manager takes that ₹1,000 Crore into the open market and buys HDFC Bank, Infosys, and TCS at their full prevailing market prices. You receive zero discount on the underlying shares.
  • Zero Track Record: An established fund with a ₹250 NAV has a 10-year audited track record demonstrating how the fund manager navigated bull runs, market crashes, and interest rate cycles. An NFO has no historical performance proof.
  • Cash Drag Risk: When a new fund raises hundreds of crores, it takes weeks or months to deploy cash into equities without inflating market prices, leading to cash drag during a rising market.

Never choose an NFO merely for its ₹10 NAV. Choose funds based on strategy uniqueness, investment mandate, AMC pedigree, and risk-adjusted track record.

5. How SIP Investors Profit from NAV Fluctuations

While nominal NAV value is irrelevant, NAV volatility is the greatest ally of a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) investor. Through the mechanism of Rupee Cost Averaging, market dips allow you to accumulate more units automatically without timing the market.

Consider a ₹5,000 monthly SIP running over a 6-month volatile market cycle:

Month Monthly SIP Amount Fund NAV (₹) Units Purchased Market Condition
Month 1 ₹5,000 ₹100 50.00 units Market Baseline
Month 2 ₹5,000 ₹80 62.50 units Correction (-20%)
Month 3 ₹5,000 ₹75 66.67 units Market Bottom (-25%)
Month 4 ₹5,000 ₹90 55.56 units Recovery Phase
Month 5 ₹5,000 ₹100 50.00 units Back to Baseline
Month 6 ₹5,000 ₹110 45.45 units New High (+10%)
Total / Avg ₹30,000 Invested Avg NAV: ₹92.50 330.18 Total Units Portfolio Value: ₹36,320

Even though the fund ended at ₹110 (only 10% above Month 1 NAV), the investor's portfolio generated a gain of +21.07% (₹36,320) because the drop in NAV in Months 2 and 3 allowed them to accumulate surplus units at bargain valuations. This mathematical engine is why staying invested through market corrections creates immense multi-decade wealth.

6. What Metrics Actually Matter Instead of NAV

When selecting or reviewing your mutual fund portfolio, ignore the nominal NAV. Instead, focus on these essential quantitative and qualitative indicators:

  • Rolling Returns (3Y & 5Y): Point-to-point trailing returns can be skewed by recent market rallies. Rolling returns measure consistency across hundreds of historical market cycles.
  • Sharpe & Sortino Ratios: Measure excess return generated per unit of risk taken. A fund with a higher Sharpe ratio delivers superior returns without exposing you to gut-wrenching volatility.
  • Total Expense Ratio (TER): Every basis point saved in annual management fees compounds directly into your pocket over 15–20 years. Always opt for Direct Plans over Regular Plans to save 0.5% to 1.2% annually (see our guide on Direct vs Regular Mutual Funds).
  • Portfolio Turnover Ratio: High turnover indicates frequent trading, which incurs brokerage costs and short-term taxation, whereas low turnover reflects a high-conviction buy-and-hold strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy a fund with low NAV because it is cheaper?

No. A low NAV does not mean a mutual fund is cheaper. Unlike individual stock valuation where price-to-earnings (P/E) ratios matter, mutual fund NAV simply reflects the net per-unit market value of the fund's underlying assets. A ₹10 NAV fund and a ₹1,000 NAV fund holding the same underlying portfolio will deliver identical rupee wealth on identical percentage gains.

Does NAV affect SIP returns in India?

NAV does not dictate the compounding growth percentage of your SIP. However, daily and monthly NAV fluctuations are precisely what power Rupee Cost Averaging: when the market dips and NAV falls, your fixed monthly SIP instalment automatically purchases more units, lowering your average acquisition cost per unit over time.

What is the NAV cut-off time in India?

Under SEBI regulations, the cut-off time for equity and debt mutual funds is 3:00 PM IST. For liquid and overnight funds, the cut-off time is 1:30 PM IST. Furthermore, under SEBI rules, the NAV applicable to your purchase depends on the realization of funds in the AMC's bank account before the cut-off time, regardless of transaction submission time.

Why is investing in an NFO at ₹10 NAV a trap?

Many retail investors treat an NFO with a base price of ₹10 per unit as equivalent to an IPO priced cheaply. In reality, a ₹10 NFO unit has zero track record, identical expense structures, and buys underlying stocks at their current full market prices. You are not buying discounted stocks; you are merely buying a new basket of full-priced securities divided into ₹10 chunks.

How is mutual fund NAV calculated daily?

NAV is calculated daily after market closing (by 11:00 PM IST across AMCs and AMFI) using the formula: NAV = (Total Market Value of Securities + Cash & Accrued Income - Total Liabilities & Daily Accrued Expenses) / Total Number of Outstanding Units.
