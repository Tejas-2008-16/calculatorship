What is NAV in Mutual Funds? Why It Does NOT Matter for SIP Investors

One of the most persistent misconceptions among Indian retail investors is equating a mutual fund's Net Asset Value (NAV) to a stock price. Many believe a ₹10 NAV fund is "cheap" and a ₹500 NAV fund is "expensive." Here is the rigorous mathematical proof of why NAV is irrelevant to your returns, how daily pricing works, and how SIP investors harness NAV fluctuations to build multi-crore wealth.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • NAV (Net Asset Value) is simply the per-unit price of a mutual fund, calculated daily as: (Total Assets – Total Liabilities) ÷ Total Units Outstanding. A ₹200 NAV fund isn't more expensive or "better" than a ₹20 NAV fund—what matters is percentage returns, not the absolute NAV number.
  • For a standard SIP or purchase order placed before 3 PM on business days, you receive the same-day NAV. Orders placed after 3 PM (or on market holidays) receive the next business day's NAV.
  • The misconception that a "lower NAV fund is cheaper to buy" is mathematically wrong: buying 500 units at ₹20 NAV or 50 units at ₹200 NAV with the same ₹10,000 produces identical returns if both funds deliver the same percentage gain.
  • Direct Plans consistently have higher NAVs than Regular Plans of the same fund, precisely because they don't deduct distributor commissions—a higher NAV here is the sign of better investor returns, not worse value.
  • SEBI mandates that all mutual fund NAVs are published daily by 11 PM on the AMC website, AMFI website (amfiindia.com), and via registrar portals (CAMS, KFintech).

Ask ten investors to explain what NAV means and seven will say "it's the price of one unit of a mutual fund." That is technically correct but dangerously incomplete. The more common follow-up mistake is assuming a lower NAV means a "cheaper" or "better time to buy" fund. It does not. NAV is simply the daily accounting of what one unit of a fund is worth—nothing more, nothing less.

Understanding how NAV is calculated, why it changes daily, what cut-off times determine which NAV you receive, and how Direct and Regular plans differ in their NAV levels is essential before you place your first mutual fund order.

The NAV Formula: What Drives the Daily Number

Every evening, after NSE and BSE market hours close at 3:30 PM, each mutual fund house calculates the day's NAV using this formula:

NAV = (Market Value of All Securities Held + Accrued Income + Other Assets – Liabilities – Expenses) ÷ Total Units Outstanding

Expenses include the Total Expense Ratio (TER), AMC management fee, custodian fee, and registrar charges deducted daily. SEBI mandates publication by 11 PM IST.

For an equity mutual fund holding 50 stocks, the NAV reflects the closing market price of every share weighted by quantity held, minus accrued daily management expenses. On a day when the Nifty 50 rises 1.5%, a Nifty 50 index fund's NAV also rises approximately 1.5% (minus a fraction for daily TER deduction).

Which NAV Do You Actually Get? Cut-off Time Rules

SEBI's cut-off time regulation determines which day's NAV applies to your transaction:

Transaction Type Order Time NAV You Receive
Equity / Hybrid Fund Purchase Before 3:00 PM on business day Same-day NAV
Equity / Hybrid Fund Purchase After 3:00 PM or on market holiday Next business day NAV
Liquid / Overnight Fund Purchase Before 1:30 PM Same-day NAV
Equity Fund Redemption Before 3:00 PM Same-day NAV; proceeds credited T+2 or T+3
SIP Auto-Debit Processed on mandate date (automated) Same-day NAV if debit clears before cut-off

The Low-NAV Myth: Why a ₹10 NAV Isn't "Cheaper"

This is the most damaging NAV misconception in Indian retail investing. Investors frequently prefer NFOs (New Fund Offers) launching at ₹10 NAV, believing they are "buying at a discount." This is completely wrong.

Consider two equity funds run by equally skilled managers with identical 50-stock portfolios:

  • Fund A NAV: ₹200 (5-year-old fund) — You invest ₹10,000 → receive 50 units.
  • Fund B NAV: ₹20 (new fund) — You invest ₹10,000 → receive 500 units.

If both funds generate 15% returns next year:

  • Fund A: 50 units × ₹230 NAV = ₹11,500
  • Fund B: 500 units × ₹23 NAV = ₹11,500

Identical outcome. The NAV level is irrelevant. Returns depend on percentage gain in NAV, which depends on the fund manager's portfolio decisions—not the unit price.

Why Direct Plan NAVs Are Always Higher Than Regular Plan NAVs

Both Direct and Regular plans of the same fund hold identical portfolios. The NAV divergence occurs because:

  • Regular Plans deduct distributor trail commissions (0.60%–1.40% annually) from the fund corpus daily before calculating NAV.
  • Direct Plans incur no commission deductions, so less is deducted from the corpus daily.

Over 10+ years, this daily NAV divergence compounds significantly. A fund launched in 2012 at ₹10 NAV might have a 2026 Direct Plan NAV of ₹180 and a Regular Plan NAV of ₹152. The ₹28 difference per unit represents real wealth transferred from investors to distributors—not fund performance. Always verify that your fund name includes the word "Direct" before investing.

Frequently Asked Questions About Mutual Fund NAV

Should I time my SIP purchase to a specific market time to get a better NAV?

For a long-term SIP investor with a 10+ year horizon, the daily NAV variation is economically negligible over thousands of future installments. The SEBI cut-off matters operationally (ensure your SIP order processes before 3 PM), but do not obsess over intraday NAV timing. Over 20 years, your compounded CAGR will be determined by asset allocation and market returns—not by whether you bought at the 10 AM NAV or the 2 PM NAV.

Why does a mutual fund's NAV sometimes not move on days when the Sensex rises?

A fund's NAV reflects only the specific shares it holds, weighted by their portfolio allocation. If a fund has significant exposure to banking stocks that underperformed on a day the IT sector drove the Sensex higher, the fund's NAV can remain flat or fall while the index rises. This is normal and reflects the fund's specific portfolio composition, not a pricing error.

Where can I check the official NAV of any mutual fund in India?

AMFI India's website (amfiindia.com) publishes NAVs for all funds across all AMCs daily by 11 PM IST. Your investment app (Groww, Kuvera, Zerodha Coin) shows your personal portfolio at current NAV. CAMS and KFintech portals also provide official NAV data along with your complete transaction history and account statements.

What to Focus on Instead of NAV

Rather than evaluating funds by NAV level, focus on what actually drives returns: 3-year and 5-year rolling CAGR versus benchmark, Total Expense Ratio (Direct Plan), fund manager tenure and consistency, and portfolio overlap with your existing holdings. Use MFCentral or Value Research Online to compare funds on these metrics. NAV is an output of fund performance—not an input that predicts it.
