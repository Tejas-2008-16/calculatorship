Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • A 30-year-old non-smoker can purchase ₹1 Crore of term life insurance cover for approximately ₹8,000–₹10,000 per year — less than ₹700 per month for protection that replaces 10–15 years of income for dependents.
  • The standard rule for cover amount: Human Life Value = Annual Income × Years to Retirement × Inflation-adjusted multiplier. Simplified rule: minimum 10× to 15× your annual gross income.
  • Online term plans purchased directly from insurers (LIC eTerm, HDFC Life Click2Protect, ICICI Pru iProtect Smart, Max Life Smart Secure Plus) are 30%–40% cheaper than offline agent-sold plans for identical cover.
  • The IRDAI-mandated 30-day free-look period allows full policy cancellation and premium refund if you are not satisfied with policy terms after receiving the policy document.
  • Term insurance premiums qualify for Section 80C deduction up to ₹1.5 Lakh annually; death benefit received by nominees is completely tax-free under Section 10(10D) with no upper limit.

Term insurance is the only life insurance product worth buying for most salaried Indians with financial dependents. Full stop. It provides maximum death benefit at minimum cost — no investment component, no surrender value, no bonus structure. If you die within the policy term, your nominee receives the full sum assured. If you survive, you pay a small annual premium and receive nothing back. That simplicity is the product's greatest strength, not a flaw.

How Much Cover Do You Actually Need?

The most common mistake is buying ₹25–50 Lakh of cover based on what "sounds like a lot." Here is the correct framework:

  • Income replacement: Your family needs to replace your after-tax take-home income for 10–15 years. On ₹10 LPA take-home, that means ₹1.0–1.5 Crore minimum.
  • Liability clearance: Add outstanding home loan balance (e.g., ₹50 Lakh) + car loan + personal loans.
  • Future obligations: Children's education corpus (e.g., ₹25 Lakh per child), daughter's wedding fund, spouse's retirement fund.

A 32-year-old earning ₹12 LPA with a ₹45 Lakh home loan, two children, and a non-earning spouse should realistically carry ₹2 Crore+ in term cover — not ₹50 Lakh.

3 Riders Worth Considering — and 2 You Should Skip

Worth buying:

  • Accidental Death Benefit Rider: Doubles (or adds fixed amount to) death benefit if death is accidental. Cost: ₹300–₹800/year. High utility for high-risk occupations or frequent travelers.
  • Waiver of Premium Rider: Future premiums are waived if you suffer permanent total disability. Ensures the policy stays active even when you can no longer earn or pay premiums.
  • Critical Illness Rider: Pays a lump-sum on diagnosis of cancer, heart attack, stroke, or kidney failure. Useful if your employer health insurance has a low coverage limit.

Skip these:

  • Return of Premium (ROP) Rider: Gets all premiums back if you survive the term. Sounds great — but the extra premium charged is 2.5x–3x a plain term plan. The math consistently shows buying a standard term plan and investing the premium difference in a Nifty 50 SIP delivers far more terminal wealth than the ROP payout.
  • Whole Life Rider: Extends cover to age 99. Premiums are dramatically higher. For most salaried Indians, financial dependence ends before age 70 when children are independent and retirement corpus is built. Lifetime cover beyond 65–70 is unnecessary for most profiles.

Frequently Asked Questions About Term Insurance

Should I buy term insurance if I'm single with no financial dependents?

Not necessarily. Term insurance protects financial dependents — children, non-earning spouses, elderly parents who rely on your income. If you have zero financial dependents, the case for term insurance is weak. However, if you plan to have dependents within 5 years and have any health risks (family history of diabetes, hypertension), buy term insurance now while you can qualify at standard rates without exclusions.

Can the insurance company reject my nominee's claim after I've been paying premiums for 10 years?

Under IRDAI regulations, any policy in force for 3+ continuous years cannot be disputed on the grounds of material misrepresentation in the original proposal (Section 45 of the Insurance Act). After 3 years, the insurer cannot repudiate (reject) a claim based on non-disclosure unless they can prove deliberate fraud. This makes honest, complete disclosure in the proposal form absolutely critical — declare all pre-existing conditions, smoking history, and family medical history accurately.

Buy Term Insurance Before Your 35th Birthday

Term insurance premiums increase 8%–15% per year of age as you get older. A 30-year-old paying ₹8,500/year for ₹1 Crore cover would pay ₹12,000–₹14,000/year if they wait until 36. Over a 30-year policy, that premium difference compounds into lakhs of additional cost. Compare quotes on Ditto Insurance or PolicyBazaar today, pick the plan with the highest claim settlement ratio at the lowest premium, and buy it online directly. The entire process takes 20 minutes.
