Insurance & Protection Updated for FY 2026-27

Term Insurance in India (2026): Cover Calculation Math, Riders, and Pure Protection

Written by Tejas Waghmare Published: 06 September 2026 Last reviewed: 23 November 2026 Statutory Framework: Insurance Act, 1938 & IRDAI Regulations

Educational insurance guide only. Not personal financial, legal, or insurance advice. Life insurance coverage is subject to individual medical underwriting and policy terms. Read our full disclaimer.

Pure term insurance in India is the only financial product designed exclusively for income replacement: you pay a small fixed annual premium to guarantee a death benefit of 15 to 20 times your annual income plus outstanding liabilities. Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938, no life insurer can repudiate a death claim on any ground after three continuous years, and all death proceeds are 100% tax-free under Section 10(10D).

Key Summary: Term Life Insurance in India

  • Pure Risk Protection Only: A level term life plan pays your nominee the full Sum Assured if death occurs during the policy term, with no savings or surrender component keeping premiums low.
  • Coverage Baseline: Size cover around 10×–15× annual net income plus outstanding loans (home/car/education) and major family milestones, minus existing liquid assets.
  • IRDAI Industry Claim Record (Annual Report 2024–25): Across 10,34,455 individual death claims totaling ₹34,995 crore, Indian life insurers settled 97.82% by policy count and 96.29% by claim value, while repudiating only 1.01% by count.
  • 3-Year Section 45 Incontestability: Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, 1938, no life policy can be called into question after 3 years from issuance/revival on any ground including misstatement.

1. How Much Term Insurance Cover Do You Need?

Buying an arbitrary ₹25 Lakh or ₹50 Lakh policy often leaves dependents severely under-insured once inflation and debts are counted. Use a structured sum-assured checklist:

  • Income Replacement (10×–15×): Illustrative example, not a real person: For an earner taking home ₹10,00,000/year supporting a family, 12× annual income establishes ₹1.20 Crore base replacement capital.
  • Add Outstanding Debts: Add remaining mortgage principal (say ₹45,00,000) and vehicle/personal liabilities so family assets remain unencumbered.
  • Subtract Existing Liquid Investments: Deduct EPF, PPF, and unencumbered mutual fund savings earmarked for goals, plus check existing employer group cover separately (remember employer group life cover ends when you resign).

3. Riders Worth Considering vs Costly Add-Ons

  • Waiver of Premium Rider (Useful): Waives all remaining base premiums if permanent total disability or listed critical illness stops income, keeping death cover active.
  • Critical Illness Rider (Evaluative): Pays a fixed lump sum on first diagnosis of covered severe illnesses; compare against buying a standalone comprehensive health & critical illness cover.
  • Return of Premium (TROP) Option (Usually Avoid): Charges substantially higher premiums simply to return nominal premiums at policy end; investing the premium savings into PPF or index mutual funds typically leaves more terminal liquidity with full inflation flexibility.
  • Whole-Life Extension to Age 99 (Usually Unnecessary): Financial dependency rarely lasts past ages 65–70 once retirement corpus is built and children are independent.

4. Mandatory Disclosures, Section 45 & Tax Rules

IRDAI 30-Day Free-Look & Section 45: Every retail life policy gives a 30-day free-look review period (per IRDAI Protection of Policyholders' Interests regulations) to cancel for a refund (less proportionate medical/stamp/mortality cost). Under Section 45 of the Insurance Act, after 3 continuous years from policy issuance or revival, insurers cannot challenge the policy for misstatement or non-disclosure. Always declare exact tobacco usage, family medical history, occupation risks, and prior policies truthfully during underwriting so claims stay clean from Day 1.

Taxes: Death benefits received by nominees are tax-free under Section 10(10D). Pure term premiums qualify for Old-Regime Section 80C deduction (capped within ₹1.5 Lakh), with zero deduction under the default New Tax Regime.

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need term insurance if I am single with zero dependents?

Generally no, because term insurance replaces income for financial dependents. Only consider buying earlier if aging parents rely on your earnings or if emerging health risks might raise future premiums or exclusions.

How long should my term insurance policy run?

Until your planned retirement age or when dependents become financially independent and major liabilities (like a home loan) are cleared—typically around age 60 to 65 for most salaried earners.
