Term Insurance in India (2026): Complete Buyer's Guide — How Much Cover Do You Need?

Written by: Tejas (Independent Personal Finance Creator • YouTube Channel)
Published: September 2026 • Last Fact-Checked: September 2026 • Reading Time: 15 mins
⚠️ Educational Disclaimer: This article is authored by Tejas for educational purposes only. Tejas is an independent personal finance researcher and is not an IRDAI-registered insurance broker or agent. All insurance policies are subject to insurer underwriting terms, medical checkups, and policy wording governed by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI).

Key Takeaways: Essential Rules Before Buying

  • Pure Term Insurance is mandatory: Never mix insurance with investment. Avoid endowment plans and ULIPs that offer meager 5% returns and inadequate coverage.
  • Target 15x to 25x Annual Income: Use the Human Life Value (HLV) method to account for inflation, outstanding home loans, and critical life milestones like children's higher education.
  • Buy early to lock in cheap rates: A 25-year-old non-smoker can secure ₹1 Crore cover for ~₹8,000/year for 35 years. Waiting until age 35 increases your lifetime outlay by over 100%.
  • Avoid Return of Premium (TROP): Paying 2.5x more just to get your nominal money back after 30 years destroys purchasing power due to long-term inflation.
  • Strict Honest Disclosure: Section 45 of the Insurance Act protects claims after 3 years, but deliberate fraud or non-disclosure of smoking/medical conditions will cause immediate claim rejection.

1. What Is Term Insurance and Why Indians Are Severely Underinsured

When I speak with young Indian professionals who just landed their first software job or banking role, I always ask one foundational question: If you were to pass away tomorrow, how many months could your parents, spouse, or children maintain their current standard of living without your salary? In the vast majority of cases, the answer is terrifying—barely 6 to 12 months.

According to comprehensive data published in the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) Annual Report and Swiss Re sigma studies, India suffers from an alarming protection gap of over 83%. This means that for every ₹100 of financial protection an Indian breadwinner's family requires to survive in their absence, less than ₹17 of life cover actually exists.

A pure term life insurance plan is the simplest, cleanest, and most cost-effective financial instrument on earth. You pay a small, fixed annual premium to a life insurer. If you pass away during the policy term, the insurer pays your family a lump sum (e.g., ₹1 Crore or ₹2 Crore) completely tax-free under Section 10(10D). If you survive the term, the policy simply expires and you get no refund—exactly like car or health insurance. By eliminating expensive investment gimmicks, term insurance lets you buy massive financial security for the price of a couple of pizzas a month.

2. How Much Term Cover Do You Actually Need — The HLV & Income Replacement Methods

The most catastrophic mistake middle-class Indian families make is picking a random, round number like "₹50 Lakh" because it sounded huge to their parents in 1995. In today's economy with 7% real urban inflation, ₹50 Lakh invested in a bank FD generating 6.5% interest yields barely ₹27,000 per month after taxes—hardly enough to pay rent in Bengaluru or Mumbai, let alone fund school fees.

To calculate your exact required sum assured, professional financial planners use two proven mathematical approaches:

A. The Income Replacement Method

As a baseline rule of thumb, your term insurance sum assured should equal 15x to 20x your gross annual income if you are under age 35, and 10x to 15x your annual income if you are between ages 35 and 50:

  • Earning ₹8 LPA: Minimum Cover = ₹8,00,000 × 15 = ₹1.20 Crore to ₹1.50 Crore
  • Earning ₹15 LPA: Minimum Cover = ₹15,00,000 × 18 = ₹2.70 Crore to ₹3.00 Crore
  • Earning ₹25 LPA: Minimum Cover = ₹25,00,000 × 20 = ₹5.00 Crore

B. The Human Life Value (HLV) Comprehensive Formula

The Human Life Value (HLV) method is the gold standard because it adds your family's living expenses and specific liabilities together, then subtracts existing liquid assets:

Total Required Cover = [Annual Living Expenses × Years to Retirement] + [All Outstanding Debts (Home Loans, Personal Loans)] + [Future Milestone Goals (Kids Education, Marriage)] - [Existing Liquid Investments (Mutual Funds, EPF, PPF)]

Let us look at a real-world numerical calculation for a 32-year-old earning ₹15 Lakh per year with a spouse and 3-year-old child:

1. Family Living Expense Corpus (₹50k/mo × 12 × 25 years with inflation): ₹1,80,00,000

2. Outstanding Home Loan Balance: + ₹45,00,000

3. Child Higher Education Fund (15 yrs away): + ₹35,00,000

4. Child Marriage Buffer: + ₹15,00,000

5. Total Gross Financial Need: = ₹2,75,00,000

6. Less Existing Liquid Assets (EPF + MF + PPF): - ₹35,00,000

Net Recommended Term Cover: ₹2.40 Crore (Round up to ₹2.50 Crore)

4. Term Insurance vs ULIP vs Endowment: Why Mixing Insurance and Investment Is a Trap

In India, thousands of well-meaning parents and young earners are sold traditional endowment policies (like "Jeevan Labh") or Unit Linked Insurance Plans (ULIPs) by bank relationship managers and neighborhood agents. Agents love selling these because they pocket commissions as high as 25% to 35% on the first year's premium.

Let us look at the mathematical reality of what happens when you invest ₹1,00,000 per year into an Endowment Plan versus executing the classic "Buy Term and Invest the Rest in Mutual Funds" strategy over 20 years:

5. Riders You Should (and Shouldn't) Add to Your Policy

When buying a term policy online, insurers present a barrage of optional add-ons known as riders. Some are indispensable, while others are expensive marketing gimmicks:

  • Critical Illness Rider (RECOMMENDED): Pays out a lump sum if you are diagnosed with major specified illnesses (cancer, stroke, kidney failure, heart attack). This provides vital replacement income during recovery when you may be unable to work.
  • Waiver of Premium on Disability (HIGHLY RECOMMENDED): If an accident causes permanent total disability, all your future insurance premiums are waived, while your full life cover continues active till tenure end. This rider is extremely cheap (often a few hundred rupees) and provides priceless peace of mind.
  • Accidental Death Benefit (OPTIONAL): Pays an extra sum if death occurs via an accident. While helpful, remember that your family needs financial protection regardless of whether death occurs from illness, heart attack, or an accident. Focusing on a higher base term cover is generally superior.
  • Return of Premium / TROP Rider (AVOID COMPLETELY): As demonstrated earlier, paying double to get nominal money back 35 years later is a massive loss in real purchasing power due to inflation's compounding impact.

6. How Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR) Works and Which Numbers to Actually Trust

Every life insurance advertisement proudly displays a "99.2% Claim Settlement Ratio." But how is this metric calculated, and can you trust it blindly?

Under IRDAI regulations, life insurers report their annual claims statistics in the IRDAI Annual Handbook. To properly evaluate an insurer, analyze these three distinct parameters:

  1. Claim Settlement Ratio by Number: The percentage of total death claims received that were paid out. Look for insurers consistently above 98.0% for at least 5 consecutive years (e.g., HDFC Life, ICICI Prudential, Max Life, Tata AIA, SBI Life).
  2. Claim Settlement Ratio by Amount / Value: This reveals whether the insurer settles high-ticket claims (e.g., ₹2 Cr policies) with the same diligence as small rural policies. An amount ratio above 95.0% indicates high institutional integrity.
  3. Solvency Ratio: Measures the insurer's financial buffer to pay claims during catastrophic events. IRDAI mandates a minimum of 1.50 (150%). Top Indian life insurers maintain solvency buffers between 1.80 and 2.10.

7. Common Mistakes When Buying Term Insurance in India

  • Hiding Smoking, Vaping, or Tobacco Habits: If you smoke even 1 cigarette occasionally or use nicotine pouches, declare yourself as a smoker. If an insurer detects nicotine traces during medical underwriting or discovers a smoking history during post-claim hospital records investigation, they will reject the entire ₹2 Crore claim under Section 45.
  • Not Opting for the Married Women's Property Act (MWPA): When purchasing a policy, married men can endorse the policy under Section 6 of the MWPA, 1874. This creates a statutory trust for the wife and children, ensuring that the insurance proceeds can never be attached by bank creditors, tax authorities, or business debt recovery tribunals.
  • Choosing Coverage Beyond Retirement Age (e.g. up to Age 85 or 100): Term insurance is designed to replace income while your family depends on your salary. Once you retire at age 60 or 65 with a substantial corpus built through PPF, EPF, and equity mutual funds, your family is already financially independent. Buying coverage up to age 85 or 100 turns term insurance into an unnecessarily expensive estate legacy tool.

8. Step-by-Step: How to Buy a Direct Online Term Plan

Purchasing directly through the insurer's official website or official aggregator platforms eliminates middleman commissions and secures direct-to-consumer discounts:

  1. Step 1 — Calculate Exact Need: Determine your sum assured (15x–20x income + loans) and tenure (up to planned retirement age, e.g., 60 or 65).
  2. Step 2 — Compare 3 Top Tier-1 Insurers: Shortlist leading insurers with >98% CSR and >1.8 solvency ratios.
  3. Step 3 — Fill the Proposal Form Personally: Never let an agent fill your medical declaration. Truthfully disclose personal medical history, pre-existing conditions, parental illnesses (cancer/diabetes), height/weight, and exact income.
  4. Step 4 — Upload Income Proof: Provide your last 3 months' salary slips, 6 months' bank statements, and latest Form 16 / ITR V (insurers strictly require income verification to approve high sum assured policies).
  5. Step 5 — Complete the Tele-Medical / Home Medical Test: Insurers arrange a free home visit by a phlebotomist for blood tests (fasting blood sugar, lipid profile, liver/kidney function) and ECG. Honest medical records permanently safeguard your family against future claim disputes.

9. Tax Benefits of Term Insurance Under Section 80C and Section 10(10D)

Term insurance offers twin tax incentives under Indian income tax statutes:

  • Section 80C Deduction: Annual premiums paid for yourself, your spouse, or dependent children can be deducted up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year under the Old Tax Regime (see our 80C deductions guide).
  • Section 80D Deduction (For Critical Illness Riders): Premiums paid towards critical illness health riders qualify for additional deductions up to ₹25,000 under Section 80D.
  • Section 10(10D) Full Exemption: The entire death benefit payout received by the nominee is 100% exempt from income tax, with zero TDS deductions.

11. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is the ideal term insurance cover for someone earning ₹15 Lakh per year?

For an individual earning ₹15 Lakh per annum, standard income replacement guidelines recommend a base cover of 15x to 20x annual income (₹2.25 Crore to ₹3.00 Crore). If you have an active home loan or young children, adding those liabilities through the Human Life Value formula typically brings the ideal sum assured to ₹3.50 Crore.

2. Should I choose Return of Premium (TROP) term plans?

No. Return of Premium plans charge 2x to 3x higher premiums simply to refund your nominal principal after 30 or 40 years. Because of long-term inflation, the purchasing power of that refund will be negligible. You will build far more wealth by buying a pure term plan and investing the savings into index mutual funds.

3. At what age should I buy term insurance in India?

The best time to buy term insurance is in your mid-20s as soon as you have income and financial dependents. Applying at age 25 locks in low annual rates of ₹7,800 to ₹9,200 for ₹1 Crore cover for 35 to 40 years, whereas waiting until age 35 more than doubles your annual premium for the same coverage period.

4. Is Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR) alone enough to pick an insurer?

No. In addition to a CSR above 98% by number of claims, you should verify the insurer's Claim Settlement Ratio by Amount (should be >95%), check that their Solvency Ratio exceeds 1.80, and ensure they have a substantial Asset Under Management (AUM) base and strong claims turnaround track record.

5. Are term insurance payouts taxable in India?

Under Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act, the death benefit sum assured received by the nominee is 100% tax-free. Furthermore, annual premiums paid qualify for tax deductions up to ₹1.5 Lakh under Section 80C in the Old Tax Regime.

6. Can non-working homemakers buy term insurance in India?

Yes. Leading Indian life insurers now offer independent term insurance plans for non-working spouses and homemakers under special eligibility guidelines, typically linked to the working spouse's income and existing life coverage up to ₹50 Lakh to ₹1 Crore.
