PMJJBY & PMSBY Guide: India's ₹436/yr Life Insurance and ₹20/yr Accident Cover: Eligibility, Claims & Tax Rules Explained

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: October 2026  •  Last reviewed: 23 November 2026  •  Social Security Insurance: Ministry of Finance DFS Guidelines & Section 10(10D) Exemption

Educational government social security guide only. Not personal insurance advisory. Verify scheme terms with your bank branch or the official Jan Suraksha portal. Read our full disclaimer.

India's Jan Suraksha initiative provides universal government-backed social security insurance through PMJJBY and PMSBY at a combined annual cost of ₹456 (just ₹1.25 per day). PMJJBY delivers ₹2 Lakh life insurance cover for death due to any cause at ₹436/year for individuals aged 18 to 50, subject to an initial 30-day lien period. PMSBY provides ₹2 Lakh accidental death and permanent total disability cover (₹1 Lakh for partial disability) at ₹20/year for individuals aged 18 to 70. Both policies feature automatic annual bank debit on 31st May, and all claim payouts are 100% tax-free under Section 10(10D).

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • PMJJBY Premium: ₹436 per year for ₹2 Lakh life cover, auto-debited every June 1 from your linked savings account.
  • PMSBY Premium: ₹20 per year for ₹2 Lakh accidental death or permanent total disability cover, also auto-debited every June 1.
  • Combined Cover: Enroll in both for just ₹456 per year total, one of the world's most affordable dual-protection insurance packages.
  • Section 80C Eligibility: PMJJBY premium qualifies for Section 80C deduction (Old Regime only). Death benefit is tax-free under Section 10(10D).
  • Enrollment: Available through any participating bank branch, net banking, or mobile banking app in under 5 minutes.

1. What is the Jan Suraksha Initiative?

In May 2015, the Government of India launched the Pradhan Mantri Jan Suraksha initiative, a cluster of affordable, mass-market social security schemes designed to bring insurance and pension coverage to India's vast uninsured population. Jan Suraksha rests on three pillars:

  • Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY): Renewable term life insurance covering death from any cause.
  • Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY): Accident insurance covering accidental death and disability.
  • Atal Pension Yojana (APY): A retirement pension scheme for unorganised sector workers.

Policymakers designed this program around a fundamental gap in India's financial system. At the time of its launch, less than 20% of Indian adults had any form of life or accident insurance. By making premiums minimal (less than ₹1.20 per day for life cover) and routing everything through the banking system, the government eliminated the friction that historically kept insurance out of reach for most households.

As of March 2026, over 23 crore individuals are enrolled in PMJJBY and over 44 crore in PMSBY. Yet a significant segment of urban and semi-urban salaried Indians remain unenrolled in both schemes, often simply because they are unaware these options exist at this price point.

2. PMJJBY: Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana Explained

PMJJBY is a one-year, annually renewable term life insurance policy. Life policies under this banner operate strictly as a pure risk product, not a savings or investment plan. Should the insured person die during the policy year for any reason (illness, accident, natural causes, or any other cause), the registered nominee receives ₹2,00,000. No payment is made if the insured person survives to the end of the policy year.

Core Features of PMJJBY

Feature Details
Annual Premium ₹436 per year (inclusive of GST and bank service charges)
Life Cover Amount ₹2,00,000 (₹2 Lakh) lump sum to nominee upon death from any cause
Entry Age 18 years (completed) to 50 years (age at time of joining)
Maximum Cover Age 55 years (policy terminates at the June 1 renewal) following the insured's 55th birthday
Policy Period 1 June to 31 May each year (annually renewable)
Underwriter Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) or other empanelled life insurers
Survival Benefit None (pure term insurance with zero maturity or savings component
Premium Deduction Auto-debited from the linked savings account on or before June 1 each year

PMJJBY functions best as a complement, not a replacement, to a private term insurance policy. A ₹2 Lakh cover is modest relative to a household's full income replacement needs, but at ₹436 per year, it represents an extraordinarily cheap incremental layer of protection. For the self-employed, gig workers, and informal sector workers who may not afford large commercial premiums, PMJJBY is often the only life insurance they will ever have access to at this price.

3. PMSBY: Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana Explained

PMSBY is a one-year, annually renewable personal accident insurance policy. Unlike PMJJBY, it does not cover death from illness or natural causes. Accident protection specifically covers death or disability that is the direct result of an accident.

Core Features of PMSBY

Feature Details
Annual Premium ₹20 per year (inclusive of applicable charges)
Accidental Death Benefit ₹2,00,000 lump sum to nominee
Permanent Total Disability ₹2,00,000 for loss of both eyes, both hands/feet, or one eye + one hand/foot
Permanent Partial Disability ₹1,00,000 for loss of sight in one eye, or use of one hand or foot
Entry Age 18 years to 70 years
Policy Period 1 June to 31 May each year (annually renewable)
Underwriter Public/private sector general insurance companies empanelled with the bank

The term "accident" under PMSBY means a sudden, unforeseen, and involuntary external event like road accidents, falls, drowning, electrocution, and similar causes. It does not cover disability arising from medical conditions, pre-existing diseases, substance abuse, self-inflicted injury, or war-related perils.

Priced at ₹20 per year, PMSBY ranks among of the least expensive insurance products available globally. Even for someone already holding a private term insurance policy (which typically does not pay out for disability short of death), PMSBY adds a ₹2 Lakh accident-specific disability layer for under ₹2 per month. There is practically no financial reason for an eligible person aged 18 to 70 not to be enrolled.

4. Eligibility Criteria & How to Enroll

Eligibility Requirements

To subscribe to either scheme, you must satisfy the following conditions:

  • Savings Account: An active savings bank account with a participating bank or post office savings account is mandatory.
  • Age: 18 to 50 years for PMJJBY (at the time of first enrollment). 18 to 70 years for PMSBY.
  • Aadhaar Linkage: Your Aadhaar card must be linked to your savings account, mandatory per revised government guidelines.
  • Sufficient Balance: Account must maintain enough balance to allow the annual premium auto-debit on June 1 each year.
  • Written Consent: A signed auto-debit mandate/consent form must be submitted to the bank (or confirmed digitally via net banking).

When holding savings accounts at multiple banks, you may enroll in each scheme through only one bank account. Duplicate enrollment through multiple banks is not permitted, and premiums wrongly debited due to duplicate enrollment may be forfeited rather than refunded.

How to Enroll: Step by Step

Enrollment is available through multiple convenient channels:

  • Bank Branch: Request the PMJJBY/PMSBY enrollment form, fill in nominee details, sign the auto-debit consent, and submit with an Aadhaar copy.
  • Net Banking / Mobile App: Most major banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Bank of Baroda, PNB, Union Bank, etc.) allow enrollment via net banking or their mobile app by looking under "Government Schemes" or "Insurance" sections.
  • SMS Banking: Certain banks offer enrollment via SMS commands to a shortcode. Refer to your bank's official SMS banking guide for the specific keyword.
  • Jan Suraksha Portal: The official government portal at the official Jan Suraksha government portal hosts scheme brochures, enrollment forms, and a participating bank directory.
Practical Tip: Enroll Before April 30 Each Year

The annual policy cycle runs June 1 to May 31. Enrolling before April 30 ensures direct processing before the June 1 auto-debit date and avoids any coverage gap. Also set a reminder to verify your account has sufficient balance before May 25 each year so the auto-debit does not fail.

5. Coverage Details, Policy Period & Key Exclusions

What PMJJBY Covers

PMJJBY covers death from any cause whatsoever (natural, accidental, illness-related, or otherwise) as long as the policy is active and the insured is within the eligible age band. Such breadth of coverage at ₹436 per year is genuinely exceptional. One structural limitation involves a 45-day initial waiting period for non-accidental deaths when you first enroll. After the 45-day window, full any-cause coverage is active. Importantly, this waiting period only applies to your very first enrollment; it does not apply on annual renewals.

What PMSBY Covers

  • Accidental death → ₹2,00,000 paid to nominee
  • Permanent total disability (loss of both eyes, both hands/feet, or one eye and one hand/foot) → ₹2,00,000 paid to insured person
  • Permanent partial disability (loss of one eye or one hand/foot) → ₹1,00,000 paid to insured person

There is no waiting period under PMSBY; accident cover begins from the date of enrollment.

Key Exclusions Under PMSBY

  • Death or disability arising from illness or medical conditions (not accident-related).
  • Self-inflicted injury or suicide attempts.
  • Disability arising from consumption of alcohol, intoxicants, or drugs.
  • War, civil war, rebellion, nuclear risk, or acts of terrorism.
  • Participation in criminal or unlawful activities.
  • Claims arising solely from pregnancy or childbirth-related medical complications.

6. Premium Auto-Debit Mechanics & Renewal Rules

Both PMJJBY and PMSBY operate on a fully automated annual renewal model. Once you enroll and authorize auto-debit, the annual premium is automatically deducted from your linked savings account on or before June 1 every year. No manual action (no branch visit and no renewal form) is required as long as your account has sufficient balance.

What Happens if the Auto-Debit Fails?

If your savings account has insufficient balance on the June 1 debit date, the auto-debit will fail and your policy will lapse. From the moment of lapse, you have no coverage under the scheme. To revive the lapsed policy, you would need to re-enroll, which may involve:

  • A fresh 45-day waiting period for non-accidental death claims under PMJJBY.
  • Submission of a new health declaration in certain cases.

Practical prevention stays simple: set a recurring calendar reminder for May 25 each year to verify that your linked savings account holds at least ₹460 (₹436 for PMJJBY + ₹20 for PMSBY + buffer). A lapse due to a momentarily empty account is one of the most avoidable and most common reasons claims get rejected.

7. How to File a Claim: Step-by-Step Process

Claims under both schemes are initiated through the bank branch where the insured person maintained the savings account linked to the policy. Beneficiaries (for death/disability claims under PMJJBY) or the insured person (for disability claims under PMSBY) approaches the bank to start the process.

PMJJBY Death Claim: Documents Required

  • Completed PMJJBY claim form (available at the bank branch or via the Jan Suraksha portal).
  • Original or certified copy of the death certificate issued by municipal authority or gram panchayat.
  • Nominee's Aadhaar card copy (for identity verification).
  • Nominee's bank account details (cancelled cheque or passbook copy) for NEFT credit.
  • Insured person's savings account passbook copy confirming active scheme enrollment at the time of death.

PMSBY Accident Claim: Documents Required

  • A duly signed and completed PMSBY claim-cum-discharge form from the designated bank.
  • For accidental death: Police FIR copy, post-mortem report, death certificate, and nominee's identity and bank details.
  • For permanent disability: Police FIR or incident report (where applicable), disability certificate from a civil surgeon or equivalent government-authorized medical authority, and the insured person's bank details.

Claim Timeline and Escalation

Claims are typically settled within 30 days of submission of complete documentation. Underwriting insurers transfer the amount directly to the beneficiary's bank account via NEFT. If a claim is wrongly delayed beyond 30 days without a valid reason, you may file a grievance through the IRDAI Integrated Grievance Management System (IGMS) through the IRDAI IGMS grievance portal, or escalate to the Insurance Ombudsman in your region.

8. Tax Treatment: Section 80C, Section 10(10D) & Deductibility

PMJJBY Premium: Section 80C Deduction

The ₹436 annual premium paid for PMJJBY constitutes a life insurance premium paid to a recognized insurer and qualifies for deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961, subject to the aggregate ₹1,50,000 annual ceiling. This deduction is only available under the Old Tax Regime. Under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC), Section 80C deductions are not available.

Absolute tax savings remain modest: at the 30% tax bracket, the saving amounts to approximately ₹131 per year on the ₹436 premium. However, the deduction is legitimate and should be claimed if you are on the Old Regime with headroom under the ₹1.5 Lakh ceiling. Ensure this premium is included in Schedule VI-A under 80C when filing your ITR.

PMJJBY Death Benefit: Section 10(10D) Exemption

The ₹2,00,000 death benefit received by the nominee under PMJJBY is fully exempt from income tax under Section 10(10D) of the Income Tax Act, which exempts any sum received under a life insurance policy on the death of the insured. Nominees carry zero tax obligation on this amount and does not need to report it as income in their ITR.

PMSBY: Tax Treatment of Premium and Benefit

PMSBY is a personal accident insurance policy. Paying the ₹20 annual premium generally does not qualify under Section 80D (which is specifically for health insurance premiums as classified by IRDAI). However, the accident benefit received (whether for accidental death or disability) is generally treated as a non-taxable capital receipt rather than income under ordinary circumstances. Since PMSBY is a government social security scheme, its benefit payouts are not typically characterized as taxable income. Consult a qualified Chartered Accountant for the precise tax treatment applicable to your specific situation.

Regime Comparison: Old vs New Regime
  • Old Tax Regime: PMJJBY ₹436 premium → deductible under Section 80C (within ₹1.5L ceiling). Death benefit → tax-free under Section 10(10D).
  • New Tax Regime: PMJJBY ₹436 premium → no deduction available. Death benefit → still fully tax-free under Section 10(10D).
  • PMSBY (both regimes): ₹20 premium → deductibility uncertain; verify with your CA. Accident benefit → generally non-taxable capital receipt.

10. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I enroll in PMJJBY if I am already covered under a private term insurance policy?

Absolutely yes. There is no restriction on holding PMJJBY alongside private term insurance. In fact, the combination makes strategic sense: your private term policy provides the large income-replacement cover (₹50 Lakh or more), while PMJJBY adds an additional ₹2 Lakh for just ₹436 per year. The nominee receives both benefits independently upon the insured's death.

What happens to my PMJJBY coverage when I turn 55?

PMJJBY coverage automatically terminates on the June 1 renewal following your 55th birthday, even if you enrolled at age 18. The scheme cannot be renewed beyond this ceiling, and your bank should stop debiting the premium. If a premium is incorrectly debited after you cross 55, contact your bank for a refund. At 55, you would need to explore private term insurance or other life insurance options, though coverage becomes significantly more expensive at this age.

Is there an income limit to join PMJJBY or PMSBY?

No. There is absolutely no income ceiling for either scheme. A software professional earning ₹25 Lakh per year is equally eligible as a daily-wage laborer. These schemes were designed as universal social security coverage programs open to all income levels. The only requirements are an eligible savings bank account, the correct age, and Aadhaar linkage.

Does PMJJBY cover COVID-19 or pandemic-related deaths?

Yes. Since PMJJBY covers death from any cause, deaths due to COVID-19 and similar infectious diseases are fully covered, provided the policy was active at the time of death and the 45-day initial waiting period had elapsed. This broad "any cause" coverage was one of the reasons PMJJBY claim volumes increased dramatically during 2020 to 2022, and the scheme delivered on its promise to millions of families.

Can NRIs enroll in PMJJBY or PMSBY?

PMJJBY and PMSBY are designed for Indian residents. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are generally not eligible, as the schemes require a resident savings account (not an NRE or NRO account). NRIs who return to India and open a resident savings account become eligible to enroll. Always confirm the current eligibility norms with your bank, as government guidelines may be updated periodically.

11. Official Government Sources & References

Verify the most current premium amounts, scheme terms, and claim procedures through these official government and regulatory sources:

  • Jan Suraksha Portal (Official Portal)

    Official Ministry of Finance portal for PMJJBY, PMSBY, and APY. Contains scheme documents, claim forms, and a participating bank directory.

  • Department of Financial Services (DFS Official Website)

    Ministry of Finance portal for government insurance and pension scheme notifications, circulars, and press releases.

  • Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC India Website)

    LIC is the primary underwriter for PMJJBY claims through most participating banks. Their portal contains master policy terms and claim procedures.

  • IRDAI IGMS Grievance Portal (IRDAI Grievance Portal)

    For escalating delayed or wrongly rejected claims under PMJJBY and PMSBY to the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India.
