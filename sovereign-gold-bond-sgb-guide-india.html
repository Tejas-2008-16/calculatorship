Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) Guide India: Returns, Tax Rules & Secondary Market

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 15 June 2026  •  Last reviewed: 26 November 2026

Educational guide only, not personalized investment or tax advice. Rules and rates can change; verify official sources and see our disclaimer.

Sovereign Gold Bonds pay 2.50% annual interest on your initial issue price plus full IBJA gold price appreciation over 8 years. Under Budget 2026 rules (effective 1 April 2026), maturity capital gains remain exempt only for original individual RBI subscribers; NSE/BSE secondary buyers pay 12.5% LTCG.

How Sovereign Gold Bonds Work: Interest Plus Gold Price

Issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the Government of India, each Sovereign Gold Bond unit represents 1 gram of 999 purity gold. An individual investor can hold between 1 gram and a maximum of 4 kg per financial year (4 kg for HUFs; 20 kg for trusts). Issue and redemption prices follow the simple average of India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) 999 closing prices over the preceding 3 working days.

Alongside tracking gold prices, SGBs pay a fixed 2.50% per annum simple interest paid semi-annually directly to the investor's bank account, calculated strictly on the initial nominal issue price. When primary RBI tranches were active (up to 2023-24 Series IV in February 2024 at ₹6,263/g offline and ₹6,213/g online), online subscribers received a ₹50 per gram discount.

What Changed Recently: Budget 2026 Secondary Market SGB Tax Rule

Critical Budget 2026 amendment (effective 1 April 2026): Under the Finance Act 2026 alignment of Section 70 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (corresponding to Section 47(viic) of the 1961 Act), the complete capital gains tax exemption at 8-year maturity is restricted exclusively to individual taxpayers who originally subscribed at RBI issuance and held continuously until final maturity.

  • Secondary market buyers (NSE/BSE): Individuals who purchase existing SGB tranches from the exchange do not receive capital gains tax exemption at RBI maturity or premature exit. Long-term gains (>12 months listed bond) attract 12.5% LTCG without indexation; ≤12 months are taxed at marginal slab rate.
  • Premature RBI exit (Year 5–7): Under Budget 2026 clarifications, premature exit with the RBI before full 8-year maturity also no longer retains automatic blanket exemption under the amended wording; confirm your holding status with a tax professional before tendering early.
  • No fresh primary tranches: After 2023-24 Series IV (February 2024), the Government has not issued fresh SGB tranches in FY 2024-25, FY 2025-26, or FY 2026-27.

How Secondary Market SGB Trading Works Now

Investors looking to buy existing SGB tranches on NSE/BSE demat platforms should always place limit orders rather than market orders because daily order books are thin and bid-ask spreads can exceed 2%–4%. Compare the quoted clean bond price against the daily IBJA 999 per-gram rate plus accrued semi-annual interest before placing an order, and price in 12.5% LTCG at redemption since secondary purchases are taxable from April 2026 onwards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are Sovereign Gold Bonds completely tax-free at maturity in FY 2026-27?

Only for individual investors who originally subscribed at the RBI issue window and held the bonds continuously until full 8-year maturity. Under the Finance Act 2026 rules effective 1 April 2026 (Section 70 of Income-tax Act 2025), buyers purchasing SGBs on NSE/BSE exchanges pay 12.5% LTCG if held over 12 months.

Is the 2.5% annual SGB interest taxable?

Yes. The 2.50% annual coupon paid semi-annually on the initial issue price is taxable under 'Income from Other Sources' at your slab rate. No TDS is deducted on SGB interest.

Are new RBI SGB tranches expected in 2026?

No fresh primary SGB tranche has been announced by the RBI or Ministry of Finance since 2023-24 Series IV (February 2024).

Can I exit an SGB before 8 years?

Yes. RBI permits premature redemption after completing the 5th year on scheduled semi-annual coupon dates, or you can sell anytime on NSE/BSE in demat form. However, capital gains exemption no longer protects exchange sales or Budget-2026 secondary purchases.

How much portfolio weight should go into gold?

Diversified asset-allocation frameworks typically keep gold between 5% and 10% of total long-term financial assets as a currency and crisis hedge rather than a core compounding engine.
