How IPO Applications Work in India via UPI: ASBA Mechanics, Allotment Math, and Capital Gains Tax

Educational content only. I'm Tejas, a student. This is what I learned about how the UPI ASBA mandate mechanism works for Initial Public Offerings, SEBI allocation rules, and capital gains tax, written with AI assistance and checked against official sources. It's not advice. I am not a SEBI-registered Research Analyst or broker. IPOs carry market and business risks.

The introduction of UPI ASBA made applying for public offerings a digital process. Here is an educational walkthrough of how the ASBA lien mechanism operates, how lot size formulas are set by merchant bankers, how oversubscribed allotment lottery math functions, and how capital gains tax applies upon listing.

By Tejas, Calculatorship | Published: 21 Oct 2026 | Last updated: 21 Oct 2026 | 14 min read

Quick Summary: The UPI ASBA Mechanism

When applying for an IPO via UPI in India, a bidder enters their UPI ID on their SEBI-registered stock broker's portal and confirms an electronic mandate request in their UPI application. Rather than transferring cash upfront, funds remain in the investor's bank account under a temporary hold (lien) until allotment is decided. SEBI mandates listing within T+3 working days following issue closure.

1. Prerequisites: Demat, Bank Account & UPI ID

Before submitting an IPO application in India, three basic components must be linked under the same primary identifier:

  • An Active Demat Account: Maintained with an authorized Depository Participant (DP) registered under NSDL or CDSL via any SEBI-registered stock broker or banking depository service.
  • A Bank Account with UPI Enabled: An Indian savings bank account supporting UPI 2.0 mandate functionalities.
  • PAN Matching: Your Demat account, bank account, and UPI handle must carry the exact same Permanent Account Number (PAN). Third-party bank accounts or bids submitted on another individual's PAN using your bank UPI will be rejected during registrar validation.

2. What Is ASBA and How the UPI Mandate Block Works

In the pre-digital era of Indian markets, applying for an IPO meant writing physical demand drafts or cheques. If you were not allotted shares, you spent weeks waiting for refund cheques to arrive in the post.

SEBI eliminated that inefficiency by introducing ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount). When you apply for an IPO today:

Your money never leaves your bank account at the time of application.

Instead, a temporary lien (hold) is placed on your bank balance. If you apply for one lot costing ₹15,000, your available balance drops by ₹15,000, but the funds remain inside your savings account and continue to earn daily savings bank interest. If you are allotted shares, the exact amount is debited on allotment day. If you receive no shares, the lien is released immediately without any refund hassle.

3. Step-by-Step Overview: How the Application Flow Functions

The operational sequence for placing an IPO bid through any SEBI-registered broker involves the following milestones:

  1. Access the Intermediary's IPO Portal: Log in to your SEBI-registered broker's portal or app and locate the active IPO bidding window.
  2. Select the Active IPO: Choose the open public issue to view the issue dates and price band.
  3. Select Your Investor Category: Choose Retail Individual Investor (RII) (for bids up to ₹2,00,000) or Non-Institutional Investor (NII) for larger bids.
  4. Enter Number of Lots & Bid Price: Enter the quantity (must be an integer multiple of the official lot size). The Cut-Off Price option allows retail investors to bid at the final price determined by the book-building process.
  5. Enter Your Valid UPI ID: Type your exact UPI Virtual Payment Address (VPA) registered to your bank account.
  6. Authorize the UPI Mandate: Open your UPI mobile app upon receiving the mandate request from the clearing corporation and verify the details before entering your UPI PIN to place the hold.
  7. Confirm Verification: Once approved, the exchange receives confirmation that the lien has been established, validating the bid on the electronic book.

4. Understanding Lot Size, Price Band & Cut-Off Price (Worked Example)

Companies do not sell single shares during an IPO. SEBI requires merchant bankers to define a minimum "Lot Size" so that the minimum application value for a retail investor sits close to ₹14,000 to ₹15,000.

Let us look at a realistic worked example: Aditya, a first-time investor in Bengaluru, wants to apply for "Apex Energy Tech Ltd".

Worked Math for Apex Energy Tech IPO:

  • Price Band: ₹480 to ₹500 per share (Floor price: ₹480, Cap price: ₹500)
  • Minimum Lot Size: 30 shares
  • 1 Lot Bid Value (at Cap Price): 30 shares × ₹500 = ₹15,000.00
  • 2 Lots Bid Value: 60 shares × ₹500 = ₹30,000.00
  • Maximum Retail Bid (under ₹2 Lakh): 13 lots (390 shares) × ₹500 = ₹1,95,000.00
  • Note: If Aditya bids for 14 lots (₹2,10,000), his bid automatically crosses ₹2 Lakh and gets bumped into the High-Net-Worth (sNII) category!

Why You Must Always Check "Cut-Off Price": If an IPO price band is ₹480 to ₹500 and you manually bid ₹485 to save money, and institutional demand pushes the final issue price to ₹500, your application is rejected immediately without entering the allotment pool. Checking "Cut-Off" guarantees that you agree to buy at whatever final price is discovered within the band.

5. Investment Limits: Retail (₹2 Lakh) vs NII & UPI ₹5 Lakh Cap

Under SEBI regulations, public issue applications are segmented into strict investor categories with dedicated reservation quotas:

Category Bid Range (₹) Typical Quota Allotment Method
Retail Individual (RII) Up to ₹2,00,000 Minimum 35% Lottery system if oversubscribed (1 lot per winner)
Small HNI (sNII) ₹2,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 15% (one-third of NII) Draw of lots for minimum NII lot
Big HNI (bNII) Above ₹10,00,000 15% (two-thirds of NII) Proportionate allotment subject to minimum lot size

The ₹5 Lakh UPI Limit: In 2022, SEBI revised the UPI mandate ceiling from ₹2 Lakh to ₹5 Lakh. This allows retail investors to apply for both the retail portion (up to ₹2 Lakh) and small HNI bids (between ₹2 Lakh and ₹5 Lakh) directly from their smartphone without needing net banking net-banking ASBA.

6. How IPO Allotment Works: The Proportional vs Lottery System

A substantial misunderstanding among beginner investors is assuming that applying for 10 lots in the retail category increases their probability of getting allotment 10 times.

In the retail category, applying for multiple lots in an oversubscribed IPO does NOT increase your chances.

Under SEBI's retail allotment rules:

  • Every valid applicant is first allotted exactly one minimum lot.
  • If the retail category is oversubscribed by 15 times, there are not enough lots to give even one lot to every applicant.
  • The registrar runs an automated computerized lottery (draw of lots). Each valid PAN has exactly one ticket in this lottery, whether they applied for 1 lot (₹15,000) or 13 lots (₹1,95,000).

Therefore, if an IPO is heavily subscribed, applying for maximum lots simply ties up ₹1.9 Lakh of your capital without improving your allotment odds by a single percentage point. A smarter approach is having eligible family members apply for 1 lot each from their own separate PAN Demat accounts.

7. The SEBI T+3 Listing Timeline Explained

Effective December 2023, SEBI mandated the accelerated T+3 listing timeline for all Indian mainboard public issues, slashing the traditional T+6 cycle in half. Here is the operational schedule where 'T' represents the issue closing date:

  • T Day (Issue Closes): Bidding shuts at 5:00 PM. UPI mandate approval cutoff is typically 5:00 PM on the same day.
  • T+1 Day (Allotment Finalization): The registrar audits bids, removes duplicate PANs, runs the lottery, and publishes the basis of allotment.
  • T+2 Day (Credit & Unblocking): Depository credits allotted shares to Demat accounts. Banks unblock funds for non-allottees and debit funds for successful bidders.
  • T+3 Day (Exchange Listing): Trading commences on NSE and BSE at 10:00 AM (Pre-open price discovery from 9:00 AM to 9:45 AM).

8. Reading an IPO: Fresh Issue vs OFS & Valuation Sanity Checks

Before putting your hard-earned money into an IPO, spend 15 minutes skimming the Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) available on the SEBI or exchange websites. Pay special attention to the issue structure:

  • Fresh Issue: New shares created by the company. The money raised enters the company's bank accounts to fund capital expenditure, debt reduction, or factory expansion. This creates productive future value.
  • Offer for Sale (OFS): Existing promoters, founders, or private equity venture capitalists selling their shares to the public. Not a single rupee from an OFS enters the company's treasury. It goes straight into the departing investors' pockets.

If an IPO is 90% OFS and priced at a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 85x when established listed peers trade at 30x, retail investors are essentially offering an expensive exit liquidity cushion to private equity funds.

9. Why Grey Market Premium (GMP) Is Unreliable

Social media and YouTube are flooded with daily "GMP" updates claiming an IPO will list at an 80% premium. What is the Grey Market?

The grey market is an unofficial, completely unregulated cash-based OTC market operating outside the purview of SEBI, NSE, or BSE. Operator syndicates quote arbitrary rates to create artificial frenzy. If global markets correct sharply on T+2 day, grey market premiums can completely evaporate before the 10:00 AM opening bell on T+3 day, turning an expected 30% listing gain into a 10% listing loss.

Never base an investment decision purely on GMP figures. Treat GMP as speculative noise rather than a Fixed profit indicator.

10. Tax on IPO Listing Gains (STCG 20% & LTCG 12.5%)

If you get lucky in the allotment lottery and decide to sell your shares on listing day to pocket quick gains, remember the tax department takes its share:

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): Allotted equity shares sold within 12 months are subject to flat 20% tax plus 4% cess (effective rate: 20.8%) under Section 111A.
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): If you hold the shares for over 12 months, gains above ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year across your equity holdings are taxed at 12.5% plus 4% cess (effective rate: 13.0%) under Section 112A.

Example: You are allotted 1 lot of 30 shares at ₹500 (₹15,000 cost). The stock lists at ₹650 on T+3 day, and you sell immediately for ₹19,500. Your gross profit is ₹4,500. STCG tax (20.8%) is ₹936. Your actual take-home profit is ₹3,564.

For more details on setting off short-term capital losses against future gains, refer to our detailed guide on mutual fund and equity taxation in India.

11. Six Common Mistakes Beginners Make When Applying

  1. Missing the 5:00 PM UPI Mandate Cutoff: Submitting your bid on your broker app at 4:55 PM but approving the UPI mandate at 5:15 PM results in an automatic system drop.
  2. Submitting Multiple Bids Under the Same PAN: Applying from Zerodha, Groww, and your bank portal under your own PAN causes all three bids to be rejected as duplicates.
  3. Third-Party UPI Payments: Using your father's or spouse's UPI ID for an application filed under your PAN results in an automated mismatch rejection.
  4. Bidding at the Floor Price: Forgetting to tick the "Cut-off Price" checkbox frequently disqualifies retail bids during oversubscribed issues.
  5. Ignoring Lock-In Expiry for Anchor Investors: Anchor investors have a 30-day and 90-day lock-in period. High-flying IPOs often face heavy selling pressure exactly when anchor lock-ins expire.
  6. Treating IPOs as Replacements for Systematic Wealth Creation: Chasing one IPO per month does not build retirement wealth. A consistent monthly allocation in low-cost index funds outperforms opportunistic IPO flipping over 15-year horizons.

12. Should You Apply? The 5-Minute Checklist

Before approving any UPI block mandate, ask yourself these three basic questions:

  • Is the company genuinely profitable and generating positive operating cash flows? Avoid loss-making tech startups asking for astronomical price-to-sales valuations unless you understand the business thoroughly.
  • Is the issue primarily Fresh Issue rather than OFS? Fresh capital grows the business; excessive OFS simply enriches private equity sellers.
  • Does the stock trade at a discount or reasonable multiple compared to listed industry leaders? If market leaders trade at 35x P/E, paying 70x for a smaller competitor is a high-risk proposition.

13. Key Takeaways for Retail Investors

  • low risk of Stolen Funds: Money remains in your own bank account under ASBA lien until allotment is confirmed.
  • Limits: Retail bids are capped at ₹2,00,000; UPI mandate processing allows up to ₹5,00,000 per application.
  • Always Use Cut-Off: Tick the "Cut-off Price" box to avoid getting eliminated if the final issue price is fixed at the upper band.
  • Fast T+3 Listing: Allotment is decided by T+1, shares credit by T+2, and trading begins on T+3.
  • Tax Factor: 20% STCG applies on listing gains sold within 12 months. Factor taxes into your profit calculations.

14. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum investment limit for retail investors in an IPO?

For the Retail Individual Investor (RII) category, the maximum total bid amount is ₹2,00,000. If your bid exceeds ₹2,00,000, your application is automatically classified as Non-Institutional Investor (sNII for ₹2 Lakh to ₹10 Lakh).

What is the per-transaction UPI limit for IPO applications?

SEBI and NPCI permit individual investors to block up to ₹5,00,000 per IPO application using UPI-based ASBA, covering both the retail category (up to ₹2 Lakh) and small high-net-worth bids (sNII up to ₹5 Lakh).

What is the T+3 listing timeline mandated by SEBI?

SEBI mandates a T+3 listing timeline for all mainboard public issues, where 'T' is the IPO closure date. Allotment is finalized by T+1, funds are unblocked or debited by T+2, and trading commences on the stock exchanges on T+3.

Does money leave my bank account immediately when I apply for an IPO?

No. Under ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount), the bid amount remains in your savings account and continues to earn bank interest. Funds are debited only if and to the extent shares are actually allotted to you.

Can I submit multiple IPO applications from different brokers using the same PAN?

No. Submitting multiple bids under the same PAN within the retail category causes registrar systems to automatically reject all your applications as duplicate bids. However, family members with distinct PANs can each submit independent bids.

How are listing gains on IPO shares taxed in India?

If you sell allotted shares on listing day or within 12 months, profits are classified as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and taxed at flat 20% plus 4% cess under Section 111A. If held beyond 12 months, LTCG is taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per year.

Why is Grey Market Premium (GMP) unreliable for retail investors?

Grey Market Premium represents unregulated, cash-based unofficial bets that are easily manipulated by operators with circular quotes. GMP frequently evaporates overnight during market corrections prior to official listing.

How to Think About Public Offerings Yourself

Core Prospectus Evaluation Framework:
  • Examine Objects of the Issue in the RHP: Determine what fraction represents a "Fresh Issue" (capital retained by the company for capital expenditure or debt reduction) versus an "Offer for Sale" (OFS, where existing promoters or private equity investors sell their stakes and proceeds leave the firm).
  • Analyze operating cash flow fundamentals: Review Section V (Financial Information) of the Red Herring Prospectus filed with SEBI to evaluate whether the business generates positive cash flows from operations rather than accounting profit alone.
  • Assess relative peer valuation: Compare the Price-to-Earnings (P/E) and Price-to-Book (P/B) ratios implied by the upper price band against established, listed peer companies with audited operating track records.

Where to Get Personalised Help

Official Regulatory and Advisory Channels:

For formal investment advice, stock valuation opinions, or personal portfolio suitability:

  • SEBI-Registered Research Analysts (RA) & Investment Advisers (RIA): Consult the SEBI Intermediary Directory for licensed market analysts subject to statutory code-of-conduct regulations.
  • SEBI SCORES Portal: If you face unresolved delays in ASBA mandate unblocking or allotment issues with registrars, file a complaint on SEBI SCORES.
  • Official Stock Exchange Portals: Track official public issue bidding data, subscription figures, and Red Herring Prospectuses directly on BSE India and NSE India.
