EMI vs SIP: Where Should Your Extra ₹5,000 Go?

By Tejas Published: August 2026
← Back to Articles

₹5,000
The common "extra" amount most salaried Indians get after a raise

You just got a raise. Or you cut a subscription. Or your bonus arrived smaller than expected, but still something. After covering your expenses and existing EMIs, you find yourself with an extra ₹5,000 every month. The question hits you almost immediately: should I put this towards my loan, or start a SIP?

It sounds like a simple choice. It is not. The answer depends on your interest rate, your investment horizon, your tax slab, and quite frankly, your personality. This article gives you a clear, math-backed framework — not a one-size-fits-all answer — to figure out what is right for your situation.

The Math of Loan Prepayment: A Guaranteed Return

Here is something most people do not realise: when you prepay a loan, you earn a guaranteed, risk-free return equal to your loan's interest rate. If your home loan charges 8.5% per annum, every rupee you use to prepay saves you 8.5% in interest — with zero risk, zero market dependency, and zero paperwork.

Let us put real numbers to this. Say you have a ₹30 lakh home loan at 8.5% interest, with 20 years remaining. Your EMI is roughly ₹26,000. If you make a lump-sum prepayment of ₹60,000 today (₹5,000 × 12 months), here is what happens:

  • Your loan tenure reduces by approximately 10–12 months
  • Your total interest saving over the life of the loan: approximately ₹1.1–1.3 lakh
  • Effective return on your ₹60,000: 8.5% compounded, tax-free (interest savings are not taxed)

That last point matters. If you are in the 30% tax bracket, a 8.5% tax-free return is equivalent to earning approximately 12.1% in a taxable investment. This is the number that changes the conversation.

The Math of SIP Investing: Compounding Over Time

On the other side, investing ₹5,000 per month in a diversified equity mutual fund over 20 years — assuming a 12% CAGR — grows to approximately ₹49.96 lakh. Your total investment: ₹12 lakh. Your returns: almost ₹38 lakh from compounding alone.

That is the power of time. And this is where SIP wins in the long run — because the effective CAGR from equities (12%) is higher than most loan interest rates (8–9%), especially after accounting for the tax-free nature of long-term capital gains up to ₹1.25 lakh per year.

But here is the key word: expected. SIP returns are not guaranteed. Markets go up and down. In any given 5-year window, equity funds may deliver 6%, 15%, or even negative returns. Loan prepayment gives you 8.5% every single time, without exception.

The Break-Even Formula: When Does SIP Win?

The break-even point is simple to understand: SIP wins when its expected post-tax return exceeds your loan's effective post-tax interest cost.

The Quick Rule of Thumb:
If your loan interest rate is below 8% → Invest in SIP
If your loan interest rate is above 9% → Lean towards prepayment
If your loan interest rate is 8–9% → Use the hybrid approach (split 50/50)

For a home loan specifically, there is another layer: Section 24 of the Income Tax Act allows you to deduct up to ₹2 lakh of home loan interest every year in the Old Tax Regime. If you are claiming this deduction, your effective interest cost is lower — making the loan cheaper and SIP more attractive.

Over 20 years, the SIP wins clearly. Over 10 years, the gap is smaller — and if the market delivers only 9–10% CAGR in that period, prepayment might actually come out ahead on a risk-adjusted basis.

Three Decision Rules by Life Stage

Early Career (Age 22–30): Invest in SIP First

Time is your biggest asset. At 25, ₹5,000/month invested for 35 years at 12% becomes ₹3.24 crore. You have decades to ride out market volatility. Unless your loan interest rate is above 10%, prioritise starting your SIP early over aggressive prepayment.

Mid-Career (Age 30–45): Use the Hybrid Approach

Your income is higher, your loan balance has peaked, and your risk tolerance is moderate. Split your extra money: ₹2,500 for EMI prepayment, ₹2,500 for SIP. This gives you both guaranteed interest savings and compounding wealth growth simultaneously. Near Retirement (Age 45+): Shift Towards Prepayment

With a shorter investment horizon and approaching retirement, becoming debt-free takes priority. Market volatility over a 5-10 year period can significantly impact your SIP corpus. Clearing your home loan also reduces your monthly expense need in retirement — which is a form of inflation protection.

The Hybrid Approach: Split ₹5,000 as ₹2,500 + ₹2,500

If you are genuinely uncertain, the hybrid approach is not a cop-out — it is actually the smartest mathematical choice for most mid-career Indians. Here is why:

  • You are diversifying between guaranteed 8.5% return (prepayment) and expected 12% return (SIP)
  • Your blended expected return is approximately 10.25% — better than either a pure savings account or a pure FD
  • You maintain liquidity (SIP corpus is accessible), while also reducing your total debt burden
  • Psychologically, you feel progress on both fronts — which is underrated but important for staying the course
