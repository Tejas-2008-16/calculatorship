Section 80C Deductions in India: Eligible Options, Limits & Rules

Section 80C of the Income Tax Act is the most popular tax deduction section for Indian individual taxpayers, allowing a deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 from gross total income. Understanding the risk, returns, lock-in, and taxability of every eligible instrument ensures optimal tax planning under the Old Tax Regime.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • 1. Old Regime Exclusive: Section 80C deductions (capped at ₹1,50,000 per financial year) are strictly disallowed under the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC).
  • 2. Aggregate Ceiling: The ₹1.5 Lakh limit is a shared total across all eligible instruments—EPF, PPF, ELSS, Home Loan principal, SSY, NSC, and school tuition fees combined.
  • 3. Salaried Default Fullness: If your monthly Basic Salary is ₹1,05,000 or higher, your mandatory 12% employee EPF contribution (₹12,600/month) already exhausts your entire ₹1.5 Lakh 80C limit without investing an extra rupee.
  • 4. Fastest Liquidity: ELSS mutual funds have the shortest lock-in at 3 years, compared to 5 years for Tax-Saving FDs/NSC and 15 years for PPF.
  • 5. Non-Investment Deductions: Stamp duty, registration charges, and principal repayment on a home loan qualify under 80C in the year of purchase.
  • 6. Tuition Fee Relief: Full-time tuition fees paid for up to two children to recognized Indian schools or universities qualify, excluding coaching and capitation fees.

The ₹12,500 Monthly Trap: Why Your 80C May Already Be 100% Full

Every January, salaried Indians earning ₹15 Lakh to ₹20 Lakh panic and rush to buy ₹1.5 Lakh worth of tax-saving mutual funds or insurance policies before HR closes the proof submission window. Over 60% of them do not realize that their Section 80C bucket was already completely filled on day one of the financial year.

Under statutory EPFO rules, your employer deducts 12% of your Basic Salary every month as your employee provident fund contribution. If your Basic Salary is ₹1,05,000 per month, your EPF deduction is exactly ₹12,600 monthly, totaling ₹1,51,200 annually. That single line item on your payslip fully absorbs your statutory ₹1,50,000 Section 80C ceiling. Throwing another ₹1.5 Lakh into ELSS, PPF, or an endowment policy yields exactly ₹0 in additional tax savings under the Old Regime—and absolutely nothing under the New Regime.

Non-Investment Expenses You Can Claim Under 80C

Many taxpayers mistakenly believe Section 80C is strictly for financial investments. You can claim routine life expenses that you are already paying:

  1. School and College Tuition Fees: Paid for the full-time education of up to two dependent children in India. Covers recognized playschools, nursery, CBSE/ICSE schools, and accredited universities. Does not cover coaching classes, bus fees, or overseas institutions.
  2. Stamp Duty and Property Registration Charges: When purchasing a residential house property, the statutory stamp duty and registration fee paid to state governments can be claimed under 80C in the financial year the purchase is completed. For a ₹60 Lakh flat with 6% stamp duty (₹3,60,000), this expense alone maxes out the ₹1,50,000 limit instantly.
  3. Home Loan Principal Repayment: The principal portion of your monthly home loan EMI qualifies under 80C, provided you do not sell the property within 5 years of taking possession (selling earlier reverses all claimed deductions).

The Traditional Life Insurance Policy Pitfall

The single most destructive financial habit in India is buying traditional endowment or money-back life insurance policies to claim Section 80C deductions. Insurance agents pitch these as "Fixed risk-free savings with tax benefits."

Here is the reality of the math: a typical endowment plan requiring a ₹1,50,000 annual premium delivers an internal rate of return (IRR) of just 4.8% to 5.4% upon maturity after 15 to 20 years. Committing ₹1.5 Lakh annually into a 5% endowment plan for 15 years yields approximately ₹32.8 Lakh. If you separate insurance from investment by buying a pure Term Life Insurance plan (₹15,000/year for ₹1.5 Crore cover) and investing the remaining ₹1,35,000 into a PPF at 7.1% or an ELSS/index fund compounding at 12%, your accumulated wealth reaches ₹36.6 Lakh (in PPF) or ₹56.4 Lakh (in equity). Never buy traditional insurance for tax savings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does each 80C instrument get its own separate ₹1.5 Lakh limit?

No. The statutory ceiling of ₹1,50,000 under Section 80C is an aggregate cumulative cap. All your qualifying contributions across EPF, PPF, ELSS mutual funds, home loan principal, children's tuition fees, and NSC are summed together and capped at exactly ₹1.5 Lakh total per financial year.

Can I claim 80C deductions if I opt for the New Tax Regime in FY 2026-27?

No. Chapter VI-A deductions—including Section 80C, 80D, and 80TTA—are completely eliminated under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC). Under the New Regime, you receive an unconditional ₹75,000 Standard Deduction instead of itemized investment deductions.

What happens if my employee EPF contribution alone is ₹1.8 Lakh in a year?

Your Section 80C deduction will be capped at the maximum permissible ₹1,50,000. The excess ₹30,000 EPF contribution yields no tax deduction. However, the interest earned on employee EPF contributions remains completely tax-free as long as annual contributions stay below ₹2,50,000.

Can I claim coaching class fees for my children under Section 80C?

No. Section 80C applies strictly to tuition fees paid for full-time education to a school, college, or university recognized by government authorities in India. Private tuition classes, entrance coaching institutes (e.g. JEE/NEET prep), and online skill courses are totally ineligible.

Is interest earned on Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) taxable?

No. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana enjoys full EEE status under Section 10(11A). Annual deposits qualify for Section 80C deductions, annual accrued interest (currently 8.2%) is 100% tax-free, and the entire maturity proceeds paid to the girl child are completely exempt from tax.

What happens if I sell my house within 5 years of claiming 80C principal deduction?

Under Section 80C(5), if you transfer or sell the residential property within 5 years from the end of the financial year in which possession was obtained, all aggregate deductions claimed on home loan principal in prior years are deemed income of the year of sale and fully taxed at your slab rate.

Actionable Next Step: Check Your January Payslip Today

Open your latest monthly salary payslip right now. Multiply the "EPF Employee Contribution" amount by 12. If that figure is ₹1,50,000 or more, do not invest a single rupee into tax-saving schemes—your 80C limit is already saturated. If it is less than ₹1.5 Lakh, calculate the exact remaining gap and fill it using an automated monthly SIP in a direct ELSS fund (such as Parag Parikh ELSS Tax Saver Direct Plan) or a PPF transfer via net banking before March 15th.
