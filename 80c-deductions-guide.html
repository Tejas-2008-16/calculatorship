Section 80C Deductions in India: Eligible Options, Limits & Statutory Rules

I'm Tejas, a student. Section 80C allows an aggregate deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 from gross total income for individual taxpayers under the Old Tax Regime. Understanding the statutory lock-in, tax treatment (EEE vs EET), and non-investment claims clarifies how deductions function.

Key Takeaways: Statutory Framework

  • 1. Old Regime Exclusive: Section 80C deductions (capped at ₹1,50,000 per financial year) are strictly disallowed under the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC).
  • 2. Aggregate Ceiling: The ₹1.5 Lakh limit is a shared total across all eligible instruments—EPF, PPF, ELSS, Home Loan principal, SSY, NSC, and school tuition fees combined.
  • 3. Salaried EPF Absorption: If an employee's monthly Basic Salary is ₹1,05,000 or higher, the mandatory 12% employee EPF contribution (₹12,600/month) already fully absorbs the statutory ₹1.5 Lakh ceiling.
  • 4. Lock-in Variations: ELSS mutual funds have a statutory lock-in of 3 years, compared to 5 years for Tax-Saving FDs/NSC and 15 years for PPF.
  • 5. Non-Investment Deductions: Stamp duty, registration charges, and principal repayment on a home loan qualify under Section 80C subject to statutory conditions.
  • 6. Tuition Fee Relief: Full-time tuition fees paid for up to two children to recognized Indian schools or universities qualify, excluding coaching and capitation fees.

1. Why Mandatory EPF Often Saturates Section 80C

Under statutory EPFO rules, employers deduct 12% of an employee's Basic Salary each month as the employee provident fund contribution. If an individual's Basic Salary is ₹1,05,000 per month, the EPF deduction is ₹12,600 monthly, totaling ₹1,51,200 across the financial year.

Because Section 80C imposes an aggregate statutory cap of ₹1,50,000 across all eligible items, this mandatory EPF deduction alone exhausts the entire ceiling. In this scenario, allocating additional money to other 80C instruments yields no further tax deduction under either regime.

3. Non-Investment Expenses Allowable Under Section 80C

Section 80C encompasses certain mandatory life and household expenditures beyond financial investments:

  1. School and College Tuition Fees: Paid for full-time education for up to two dependent children in India. Covers recognized playschools, elementary/high schools, and recognized degree colleges. Does not cover private coaching classes, admission/capitation fees, or foreign institutions.
  2. Stamp Duty and Property Registration Charges: Paid to state governments upon purchasing or constructing a new residential property. Claimable only in the financial year in which payment is made.
  3. Home Loan Principal Repayment: The principal component of equated monthly instalments (EMIs) paid toward a residential property loan from a recognized financial institution, subject to Section 80C(5) holding requirements.

How to Think About This Yourself

  • Verify Regime Superiority First: With the default New Tax Regime offering lower tax slabs and a ₹75,000 standard deduction, calculate your overall tax liability under both regimes before locking money away solely for Section 80C.
  • Audit Existing Outflows: Check your payslip for EPF deductions and collect receipts for home loan principal and tuition fees to identify your true remaining headroom.
  • Consider Statutory Lock-In: Funds committed to PPF (15 years) or SSY (21 years) have distinct liquidity limitations compared to 3-year ELSS funds or 5-year FDs.
  • Separate Insurance from Investment: Financial commentators emphasize evaluating insurance for risk cover and mutual funds/PF for wealth accumulation, rather than bundling them into low-yielding endowment policies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does each 80C instrument get its own separate ₹1.5 Lakh limit?

No. The statutory ceiling of ₹1,50,000 under Section 80C is an aggregate cumulative cap. All qualifying contributions across EPF, PPF, ELSS mutual funds, home loan principal, children's tuition fees, and NSC are summed together and capped at exactly ₹1.5 Lakh total per financial year.

Can I claim 80C deductions if I opt for the New Tax Regime in FY 2026-27?

No. Chapter VI-A deductions—including Section 80C, 80D, and 80TTA—are not available under the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC). Under the New Regime, salaried individuals receive an unconditional ₹75,000 Standard Deduction instead of itemized investment deductions.

What happens if my employee EPF contribution alone is ₹1.8 Lakh in a year?

Your Section 80C deduction will be capped at the maximum permissible ₹1,50,000. The excess ₹30,000 EPF contribution yields no tax deduction. However, interest earned on employee EPF contributions remains exempt from tax provided total annual contributions do not exceed ₹2,50,000.

Can I claim coaching class fees for my children under Section 80C?

No. Section 80C applies strictly to tuition fees paid for full-time education to a school, college, or university recognized by government authorities in India. Private tuition classes, entrance coaching institutes, and online courses are ineligible.

Is interest earned on Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY) taxable?

No. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana enjoys EEE status under Section 10(11A). Annual deposits qualify for Section 80C deductions, annual accrued interest is tax-free, and maturity proceeds paid to the girl child are completely exempt from tax.

What happens if I sell my house within 5 years of claiming 80C principal deduction?

Under Section 80C(5), if you transfer or sell the residential property within 5 years from the end of the financial year in which possession was obtained, all aggregate deductions claimed on home loan principal in prior years are deemed income of the year of sale and fully taxed at your slab rate.
