Section 80C Deductions in India: Eligible Options, Limits & Rules

Written by Tejas Waghmare  •  Published: 07 July 2026  •  Last reviewed: 23 November 2026  •  Tax Law: Sections 80C & 80CCE (Old Tax Regime)

Educational tax guide only. Not personal chartered accountancy or tax advice. Section 80C deductions apply exclusively under the Old Tax Regime and are unavailable under the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC). Read our full disclaimer.

Section 80C of the Income-tax Act allows individual taxpayers and HUFs to deduct up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year from their taxable income by investing in eligible instruments like EPF, PPF, ELSS, and Life Insurance, or paying children tuition fees and home loan principal. Note that Section 80C is available exclusively under the Old Tax Regime; the default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) disallows all 80C deductions.

Key Summary: Section 80C in FY 2026-27

  • Old Regime Exclusive: Section 80C deductions (capped at ₹1,50,000 total per financial year across Section 80CCE) apply solely under the Old Tax Regime. The default New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC) disallows Chapter VI-A deductions.
  • Mandatory EPF Check First: At a Basic Salary of ₹1,05,000/month, statutory 12% employee EPF deductions total ₹1,51,200 annually—fully exhausting your ₹1.5 Lakh limit without extra investments.
  • Fastest Liquidity: ELSS mutual funds carry a 3-year lock-in per installment compared with 5 years for Tax-Saving FDs and NSC, or 15 years for PPF.
  • Direct Expenses Also Qualify: Children's full-time tuition fees in India (up to two children), home loan principal, and stamp duty/registration fees qualify within the shared ₹1.5 Lakh ceiling.

1. Why Salaried EPF May Already Fill Your ₹1,50,000 Limit

Before buying new tax-saving products each January, check your monthly payslip first. Under statutory EPFO rules, employees contribute 12% of Basic Salary plus Dearness Allowance into Employee Provident Fund (EPF). Whenever monthly Basic pay reaches ₹1,05,000 or more, mandatory employee EPF equals ₹12,600/month (₹1,51,200/year), leaving ₹0 extra Section 80C deduction room regardless of whether you invest in ELSS or PPF.

3. Non-Investment Expenses Qualifying Under Section 80C

  • Full-Time Children Tuition Fees: Fees paid for up to two children to recognized schools, colleges, or universities located in India qualify. Private coaching classes, transport charges, capitation/development fees, and foreign universities do not qualify.
  • Stamp Duty & Registration Charges: Statutory stamp duty and property registration fees paid in the financial year of house purchase count toward the same ₹1.5 Lakh cap.
  • Term Life Insurance Premiums: Pure protection policy premiums qualify (subject to premium ≤10% of sum assured for policies issued from April 2012 onwards), keeping death proceeds tax-free under Section 10(10D).

5. Frequently Asked Questions

Does each Section 80C option get a separate ₹1.5 Lakh cap?

No. Under Section 80CCE, ₹1,50,000 is the shared combined cap across all Section 80C, 80CCC, and employee 80CCD(1) items together.

Can I claim Section 80C under the New Tax Regime in FY 2026-27?

No. Chapter VI-A deductions including 80C and 80D are disallowed under the default New Tax Regime, which instead gives salaried earners a ₹75,000 standard deduction and zero tax up to ₹12.75 Lakh gross salary via Section 87A rebate.

What if employee EPF alone exceeds ₹1.5 Lakh (say ₹1.8 Lakh)?

Only ₹1,50,000 qualifies for Section 80C deduction under the Old Regime. Interest on employee EPF deposits remains tax-free up to ₹2,50,000 annual contribution where employer contributes too.

What happens if I sell my house within 5 years of claiming 80C home loan principal?

Under Section 80C(5), all prior-year principal deductions claimed become taxable as income in the year of sale.
