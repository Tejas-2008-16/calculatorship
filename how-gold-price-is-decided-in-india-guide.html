How Gold Price Is Decided in India: 22K vs 24K, GST, Making Charges & Hallmark

Spot gold prices and retail making charges fluctuate daily; check official IBJA rates and verify hallmarked jewellery using the BIS Care app before purchasing.

Ever wondered why the gold rate flashed on TV news differs from your jeweller's bill? Here is how global bullion prices turn into Indian retail rates, how 22K and 24K math works, how GST and making charges are added, and how to read your invoice line by line.

By Tejas, Calculatorship | Published: 22 Oct 2026 | Last updated: 22 Oct 2026 | 12 min read

Quick Answer: How Gold Pricing Works

Gold pricing in India starts with the international price (USD per troy ounce), converted to INR, loaded with customs duty and local bullion premiums. Retail 22K gold costs roughly 22/24 (91.6%) of 24K bullion. Your final jewellery bill adds making charges (8% to 25% or flat per-gram), 3% GST on metal, 5% GST on making charges, and mandatory 6-digit BIS HUID verification.

1. The Chain: From London Spot Price to Indian Rupee Rate

When you read a headline stating gold reached a new high, where does that number originate? India imports over 80% to 90% of its annual gold requirement. Consequently, domestic retail prices are tied directly to international bullion trading hubs, primarily the London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) and the COMEX futures exchange in the United States.

Here is how that price flows across continents and currencies into a local jewellery showroom:

  1. International Spot Price (USD/oz): Overseas bullion trades in US Dollars per troy ounce. One troy ounce equals exactly 31.1034768 grams (often rounded to 31.1035 grams).
  2. Currency Conversion (USD to INR): The dollar spot price is multiplied by the prevailing USD/INR foreign exchange rate. If the Rupee weakens against the US Dollar, domestic gold prices rise even if global gold prices stay completely flat.
  3. Unit Conversion (Per Gram / Per 10 Grams): The converted rupee cost is divided by 31.1035 to establish the baseline cost of raw 24K gold per gram, and multiplied by 10 to establish the familiar 10-gram benchmark.
  4. Customs Import Duty & Cess: Raw gold imported into India attracts customs duties levied by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC). Over recent budgets, the effective duty structure has seen strategic adjustments, combining Basic Customs Duty (BCD) and Agriculture Infrastructure and Development Cess (AIDC).
  5. Local Logistics, Refining & Premium/Discount: Authorized importing banks and nominated agencies add freight, vaulting insurance, assaying costs, and a regional trading margin (or discount, depending on local demand and supply).

The mathematical chain can be summarized as:

Landed 24K Gold Rate (₹/10g) = [(International Price in USD/oz × USD/INR) ÷ 31.1035 × 10] × (1 + Customs Duty Rate) + Local Bullion Premium

Because exchange rates and global spot quotes change every second, domestic wholesale bullion rates shift throughout the trading day.

2. Who Publishes the "Gold Rate Today" in India?

Unlike listed shares that trade at a single centralized National Stock Exchange price, India does not have a single statutory gold price enforced by law. Instead, benchmark rates are published twice daily by trade associations.

The most prominent institutional benchmark is set by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA). Founded in 1919, IBJA surveys major bullion dealers across the country at opening and closing sessions to determine the national benchmark rate for 999 (24K), 995, and 916 (22K) purity gold. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) officially utilizes IBJA closing rates to calculate the issue and redemption values of Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB).

However, city jewellers do not strictly sell at the IBJA rate. Regional jewellers' associations—such as the Madras Jewellers & Diamond Merchants Association in Chennai, the Mumbai Jewellers Association, or local gold merchants' bodies in Delhi and Kolkata—meet each morning to declare their recommended city retail rate. Large national jewellery retail chains often set their own corporate card rates, which might be unified nationwide or calibrated by territory.

3. Why Gold Rates Differ Slightly Between Cities

If you compare gold prices in Mumbai, Chennai, Delhi, and Jaipur on the same morning, you will notice a difference of ₹20 to ₹80 per gram. People often ask: why isn't the gold rate uniform across the country?

Here are the primary factors driving inter-city price variations:

  • Port Proximity & Freight: Most physical gold arrives via international airports or sea ports in Mumbai, Chennai, Ahmedabad, and Kochi. Transporting secure bullion inland to cities like Lucknow or Jaipur involves armored vehicle logistics, armed security escort, and transit insurance costs that get factored into local rates.
  • Regional Trade Associations: Local jeweller bodies factor in local operating costs, regional banking surcharges, and wholesale order volumes when setting their daily indicative opening rate.
  • Local Supply-Demand Dynamics: During peak regional festivals or wedding seasons (e.g., Akshaya Tritiya in South India or regional harvest festivals), localized demand can create modest spot premiums over national benchmark rates.

4. Purity Demystified: 24K, 22K, 18K and the 22/24 Math

Purity in gold is measured in karats (spelled "carat" internationally, denoted as K) on a scale of 24 parts. 24 Karat gold represents 100% pure gold in theory (technically 99.9% in commercial practice, denoted as 999 fineness). Because 24K gold is soft, ductile, and pliable, it cannot hold delicate stone settings or intricate jewellery shapes without bending.

To craft durable necklaces, bangles, and rings, artisans alloy pure gold with metals such as copper, silver, zinc, or palladium. The proportion of gold defines the purity grade.

Carat Rating Purity % BIS Fineness Mark Common Use Cases Price Calculation Ratio
24K 99.9% 999 Investment bars, minted coins, SGB redemption benchmark 100% of Base Rate (1.000)
22K 91.6% 916 Traditional Indian wedding jewellery, chains, solid bangles 22/24 ≈ 91.67% of 24K Rate
18K 75.0% 750 Diamond-studded jewellery, engagement rings, modern watches 18/24 = 75.00% of 24K Rate
14K 58.5% 585 Daily-wear fashion accessories, lightweight budget studs 14/24 ≈ 58.33% of 24K Rate

The 22/24 Mathematical Relationship

Why is the 22K rate directly derived from the 24K rate? Because 22 karats means 22 parts pure gold out of 24 parts total weight. The remaining 2 parts consist of alloy metals whose market value is negligible compared to gold.

If an illustrative 24K gold rate is ₹78,000 per 10 grams (₹7,800 per gram), the theoretical 22K gold rate is:

22K Rate = (22 ÷ 24) × ₹78,000 = 0.91667 × ₹78,000 = ₹71,500 per 10 grams (₹7,150 per gram)

Some jewellers calculate 22K at exactly 91.6% (giving ₹71,448), while others use 91.67%. When comparing jewellers, check whether their advertised 22K rate adheres closely to this ratio.

5. Making Charges: Percentage vs Per-Gram Mechanics

Making charges represent the craftsmanship fee charged by the jeweller and the artisan (karigar) to transform raw bullion into finished ornaments. Making charges vary significantly depending on design complexity:

  • Machine-Made Plain Jewellery (Chains, Plain Bangles): Typically attracts lower making charges, ranging from 8% to 14% of the gold value or a flat ₹400 to ₹650 per gram.
  • Handcrafted & Filigree Ornaments: Involves intensive manual labor; making charges often range between 14% and 20%.
  • Antique, Temple, or Kundan Jadau Jewellery: These intricate pieces require specialized carving, setting, and finishing. Making charges can range from 20% to over 28% of the gold value.

Percentage vs Flat Per-Gram: Which Favors You?

When gold prices rise, a percentage-based making charge automatically increases in rupee terms even if the labor required has not changed. For example, a 15% making charge on a ₹70,000 item is ₹10,500, but on an ₹85,000 item it rises to ₹12,750. In contrast, a flat per-gram charge (say ₹650/g) remains constant regardless of gold rate swings.

6. GST on Gold vs Making Charges: The CBIC Tax Rules

Before the Goods and Services Tax (GST) regime, gold purchases carried a confusing mix of Value Added Tax (VAT) and excise duties. Under the CBIC framework, gold taxation is standardized into two clear categories:

  1. 3% GST on Intrinsic Gold Value: This comprises 1.5% Central GST (CGST) and 1.5% State GST (SGST), or 3% Integrated GST (IGST) for interstate transactions. This 3% applies to 18K, 22K, or 24K gold coins, bars, and the base metal component of jewellery.
  2. 5% GST on Making Charges: Craftsmanship and fabrication are classified as services (job work / supply of service). As notified by the GST Council, making charges attract a 5% GST rate (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST).

Many jewellers historically simplified their billing software by charging a blanket 3% across the entire subtotal (gold value plus making charges). However, transparent GST-compliant invoices itemize the gold metal value at 3% and the making service at 5%.

7. BIS Hallmarking and 6-Digit HUID: How to Verify on BIS Care

Under regulations enforced by the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), selling un-hallmarked gold jewellery is prohibited in notified districts across India. Hallmarking protects buyers from receiving lower purity (such as getting 20K gold while paying for 22K).

A legitimate BIS hallmark consists of three distinct laser-engraved marks:

1. The BIS Logo

A triangular mark representing certification by the national standards body.

2. Purity & Fineness

Clear purity stamp, such as 22K916, 18K750, or 14K585.

3. 6-Digit HUID Code

A unique alphanumeric code (e.g., AB12CD) specific to that single piece of jewellery.

Verifying HUID Using the Official BIS Care App

You do not need to rely on the jeweller's word. The central government offers the free BIS Care app (available on Google Play Store and Apple App Store) for instant verification:

  1. Download and launch the BIS Care app on your smartphone.
  2. Tap on "Verify HUID" on the main dashboard.
  3. Type in the 6-digit alphanumeric code stamped on your jewellery item.
  4. The app fetches real-time data from the central BIS registry, displaying the jeweller's registration number, the Assaying and Hallmarking Centre (AHC) that tested the piece, the exact purity grade, and the article type.

If the app returns an error or says "Record Not Found", the item is not properly registered with BIS, and you should halt the transaction immediately.

8. Worked Invoice Example: Calculating a 10 g 22K Ornament

Let us walk through a practical scenario to see how all these components add up on an actual bill. Consider a buyer in Pune purchasing a 10-gram 22K gold chain.

Assumptions for this illustrative calculation:

  • Illustrative 24K Benchmark Rate: ₹78,000 per 10 grams (₹7,800/g)
  • Illustrative 22K Rate (22/24 ratio): ₹71,500 per 10 grams (₹7,150/g)
  • Gross Weight of Item: 10.000 grams (no studded stones)
  • Making Charges: 12% of the intrinsic gold value
  • GST: 3% on gold value, 5% on making charges
  • BIS Hallmarking Fee: ₹45 + 18% GST (nominal fee capped by BIS)
Invoice Line Item Formula / Rate Applied Amount (₹)
1. Pure Gold Metal Value 10.000 g × ₹7,150 per gram ₹71,500.00
2. Making Charges (12%) 12% × ₹71,500 ₹8,580.00
3. GST on Gold (3%) 3% of ₹71,500 (1.5% CGST + 1.5% SGST) ₹2,145.00
4. GST on Making Charges (5%) 5% of ₹8,580 (2.5% CGST + 2.5% SGST) ₹429.00
5. BIS Hallmarking Charge + GST ₹45.00 + 18% GST (₹8.10) ₹53.10
Total Final Invoice Amount Sum of Lines 1 through 5 ₹82,707.10

Notice the spread: while the raw metal value was ₹71,500, the customer pays ₹82,707.10. Making charges and statutory taxes added approximately 15.67% over the intrinsic gold value. This difference illustrates why jewellery is primarily a consumer purchase rather than a frictionless investment asset.

9. Festive Context: Dhanteras 2026 Buying Dynamics

In 2026, Dhanteras (Dhantrayodashi) falls on Friday, 6 November 2026 per the traditional Hindu lunar calendar (verified via Drik Panchang). This day marks one of the highest single-day retail gold consumption windows across India.

During festive peak days like Dhanteras and Diwali, high showroom footfall can sometimes lead buyers to overlook invoice line items. Jewellers frequently offer promotional discounts on making charges (such as "flat 25% off making charges"). When evaluating such offers, verify that the jeweller has not quietly inflated the underlying base gold rate to offset the making charge discount.

10. Common Myths and Expensive Buying Mistakes

Navigating the gold market requires separating promotional claims from mathematical facts. Here are four widespread misconceptions:

Myth 1: "22K and 24K cost roughly the same."

Fact: 22K gold has exactly 8.33% less pure gold by weight than 24K gold. A retailer who quotes you a 22K price close to the 24K rate is overcharging by pocketing an inflated margin.

Myth 2: "Gross weight and net weight are the same for studded jewellery."

Fact: If your ornament contains semi-precious stones, cubic zirconia, or pearls, the jeweller must deduct the weight of the stones before applying the gold rate. Paying gold prices for the weight of embedded stones or lac/wax filling leads to substantial financial losses upon resale.

Myth 3: "Making charges are fixed by government regulations."

Fact: Making charges are entirely unregulated and determined by individual jewellers. Always negotiate making charges, especially on standard machine-crafted items.

Myth 4: "You get full value back when you resell jewellery."

Fact: Making charges (10% to 25%) and GST (3% and 5%) paid during purchase are permanently lost on resale. When selling old gold, you only receive the value of the pure melted gold weight at current buyback rates.

11. Key Takeaways for Gold Buyers

  • Check the 22/24 Formula: Ensure the 22K rate is calculated as roughly 91.67% of the prevailing 24K benchmark rate.
  • Insist on 6-Digit HUID: Every gold ornament must carry the triangular BIS mark, purity grade (e.g., 22K916), and a distinct 6-digit HUID code verified on the BIS Care app.
  • Verify Net Weight: Ensure the weight of stones, enamel, and lac is deducted from the gross weight so you only pay the gold rate for actual gold.
  • Scrutinize the Invoice: Confirm that GST is itemized properly (3% on metal, 5% on making charges) and that the jeweller's GSTIN and HUID are clearly printed.
  • Compare Investment Alternatives: If your objective is pure financial investment rather than personal wear, paper or electronic gold instruments like Sovereign Gold Bonds or Gold ETFs eliminate making charges, storage risks, and resale deductions. 12. Frequently Asked Questions

How is the daily retail gold price calculated from international markets?

The international benchmark is quoted in USD per troy ounce (31.1035 grams). Importers convert this price to Indian Rupees using current forex rates, divide by 31.1035 to get per-gram value, add customs import duties (basic customs duty plus cess), and incorporate local logistics and bullion association premiums.

Why is 22K gold cheaper than 24K gold?

24 Karat gold is 99.9% pure raw gold, whereas 22 Karat gold contains 91.6% pure gold alloyed with 8.4% other metals like copper, silver, or zinc for structural strength. Mathematically, 22K gold rate is calculated roughly as 22 divided by 24 (or 91.6%) of the prevailing 24K gold rate.

Why do gold rates differ slightly between Indian cities?

Gold rates vary across cities due to local bullion association price-setting, interstate transportation logistics and security insurance costs, regional supply-demand dynamics, and varying local trade association margins between major port hubs (like Mumbai or Chennai) and inland cities.

What is the GST rate on gold jewellery in India?

GST on the intrinsic value of gold metal is 3% (1.5% CGST plus 1.5% SGST). Making charges or craftsmanship fees attract a separate 5% GST rate as a service under CBIC regulations. Both items are itemized separately on compliant jewellery invoices.

What is an HUID and how do I verify it using the BIS Care app?

Hallmark Unique Identification (HUID) is a mandatory 6-digit alphanumeric code laser-engraved onto hallmarked gold jewellery by BIS-recognized assaying centres. You can enter this 6-digit code in the free BIS Care mobile app under the 'Verify HUID' tab to view registration, purity, and assaying details.

How are jewellery making charges calculated?

Jewellers calculate making charges either as a flat rupee fee per gram (e.g., ₹450 to ₹900 per gram) or as a percentage of the gross gold value (typically 8% to 25% depending on whether the ornament is machine-cast, handcrafted, or antique temple jewellery).

When is Dhanteras 2026 celebrated in India?

Dhanteras (Dhantrayodashi) in 2026 is observed on Friday, 6 November 2026, marking the customary festive occasion when Indian families traditionally purchase precious metals, coins, utensils, and household assets ahead of Diwali.

Do making charges apply when you sell or exchange old gold jewellery?

No. When you sell or melt old jewellery, jewellers only compensate you for the net weight of pure gold melted down at current melting rate. Making charges and GST paid at the time of purchase are sunk costs and are not recovered during resale.

How to Think About This Yourself

When assessing gold purchases either for ornamentation or investment, dissect the price equation into three clean variables:

  • Jewellery vs Paper Gold: Physical jewellery involves 8% to 25% in making charges and 3% non-recoverable GST. If your objective is pure financial allocation or gold price tracking, instruments like Gold ETFs or Sovereign Gold Bonds (when available in secondary markets) avoid making friction entirely.
  • The Karat Purity Equation: 22K gold rate is mathematically `24K Gold Rate × (22 / 24)`. Ensure the jeweller has priced the metal on 91.6% purity rather than 24K baseline when computing making charges.
  • Mandatory 6-Digit HUID Verification: Never accept un-hallmarked jewellery. Every hallmarked piece in India must carry the BIS triangular mark, purity grade (e.g., 916), and a 6-digit laser-engraved alphanumeric HUID that can be authenticated immediately on the BIS Care app.

Where to Get Personalised Help

For gold consumer rights, hallmarking disputes, and portfolio asset allocation:

  • If a jeweller refuses to issue a compliant tax invoice with HUID or if purity fails an assay test, file a consumer grievance via the official Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) Portal at BIS Official Website or National Consumer Helpline.
  • For allocating gold as a hedge within a diversified portfolio, consult a fee-only SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA) via the SEBI RIA Directory.
