Tax Loss Harvesting in India: How to Legally Offset Capital Gains & Cut Your Tax Bill

When equity markets fluctuate, paper losses in your mutual funds and stocks do not have to be dead weight. Through Tax Loss Harvesting under Sections 70 and 74 of the Income Tax Act, you can realize strategic losses to offset real taxable gains, protect your returns, and reset your cost basis.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Asymmetric Set-off: Short-Term Capital Losses (STCL) can offset both STCG and LTCG. However, Long-Term Capital Losses (LTCL) can strictly offset only LTCG.
  • Intra-Head Only: Capital losses can never be set off against Salary, House Property, or Other Sources income.
  • 8-Year Carry Forward: Unadjusted losses can be carried forward for 8 consecutive Assessment Years under Section 74, provided your ITR is filed before the Section 139(1) due date.
  • Reinvestment Nuance: While India does not have a statutory 30-day wash-sale rule, immediate round-trip same-day trades may be treated as speculative or scrutinized under GAAR. Switching to equivalent index funds or waiting for settlement avoids friction.

1. What is Tax Loss Harvesting in India?

Every active investor's portfolio inevitably holds positions that are trading below their acquisition cost. While nobody sets out to make loss-making investments, unrealized (paper) losses represent an unrecognized tax asset. Tax Loss Harvesting (TLH) is the systematic practice of selling securities at a loss before the close of the financial year (March 31) to convert paper losses into realized capital losses on your tax ledger.

By pairing these realized losses against taxable capital gains generated during the same financial year, you legally lower your net capital gains, reducing the final cheque payable to the Income Tax Department. You can then immediately deploy those proceeds back into similar assets, preserving your intended asset allocation and long-term compounding trajectory.

2. The Capital Loss Set-Off Matrix (Sections 70 & 71)

The Income Tax Act, 1961 establishes strict boundary walls governing how losses under the head "Capital Gains" can be adjusted. You cannot freely deduct stock market losses against your salaried paycheck or rental income.

Type of Realized Loss Can Offset STCG? Can Offset LTCG? Can Offset Salary/Other? Governing Section
Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL) YES YES NO Section 70(2)
Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL) NO YES NO Section 70(3)
Inter-Head Set-off NO NO NO Section 71(3)

The Core Rule: A loss from a source where income is taxed at a concession or specific rule cannot be set off against higher-taxed income unless the statute explicitly permits it. Since LTCL arises from assets enjoying lower tax rates (e.g. 12.5% under Section 112A), Parliament specifically barred LTCL from wiping out STCG (taxed at 20% under Section 111A).

3. Harvesting Gains vs Losses: The ₹1.25 Lakh Exemption Play

In India, Section 112A provides an annual statutory exemption of up to ₹1.25 Lakh on aggregate Long-Term Capital Gains from listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds. Any unused portion of this ₹1.25 Lakh threshold lapses at midnight on March 31—it does not roll forward.

Smart wealth management therefore pairs tax loss harvesting with tax gain harvesting:

  • Gain Harvesting: If you have ₹1,00,000 of unrealized LTCG, you can redeem units, book the profit tax-free under the ₹1.25 Lakh threshold, and repurchase the fund next morning. Your new purchase price becomes your reset cost basis, saving tax when you exit years later.
  • Loss Harvesting: If your LTCG exceeds ₹1.25 Lakh, or if you generated Short-Term Capital Gains taxed at 20%, you harvest losses to drag net taxable profit back down toward zero.

4. detailed Worked Portfolio Simulation

Consider an investor, Rohit, who conducted a detailed portfolio review on 15 March 2027. During FY 2026-27, he had already realized ₹2,50,000 of Long-Term Capital Gains and ₹60,000 of Short-Term Capital Gains.

Scenario A: Doing Nothing (No Loss Harvesting)

  • LTCG Realized: ₹2,50,000 | Less Section 112A Exemption: ₹1,25,000 → Taxable LTCG = ₹1,25,000
  • LTCG Tax @ 12.5% = ₹15,625
  • STCG Realized: ₹60,000 | STCG Tax @ 20% = ₹12,000
  • Total Base Capital Gains Tax = ₹27,625 (+ 4% Health & Education Cess = ₹28,730)

Scenario B: Harvesting Unrealized Paper Losses

Rohit identifies two underperforming holdings in his portfolio:

  • Fund X (Held 8 months, Short-Term): Unrealized loss of ₹40,000
  • Fund Y (Held 2 years, Long-Term): Unrealized loss of ₹50,000

Rohit redeems both funds before 31 March, realizing ₹40,000 STCL and ₹50,000 LTCL:

  1. Offsetting STCG: ₹60,000 STCG − ₹40,000 STCL = ₹20,000 net taxable STCG. Tax @ 20% = ₹4,000 (down from ₹12,000).
  2. Offsetting LTCG: Gross LTCG ₹2,50,000 − ₹50,000 LTCL = ₹2,00,000 net LTCG.
  3. Applying Statutory Exemption: ₹2,00,000 net LTCG − ₹1,25,000 Section 112A exemption = ₹75,000 taxable LTCG.
  4. LTCG Tax @ 12.5%: ₹75,000 × 12.5% = ₹9,375 (down from ₹15,625).
  5. New Base Tax: ₹4,000 + ₹9,375 = ₹13,375 (+ 4% Cess = ₹13,910).

Net Direct Cash Tax Savings: ₹14,820 in a single financial year.

5. Carry-Forward Rules & Crucial Deadlines (Section 74)

What happens if your harvested losses exceed all realized gains in the current financial year? Section 74 of the Income Tax Act provides significant relief:

  • 8-Year Window: Unabsorbed capital losses can be carried forward for up to 8 Assessment Years immediately succeeding the year of loss.
  • Strict Bucketing in Future Years: When carried forward, a brought-forward STCL can offset future STCG and LTCG. A brought-forward LTCL can strictly offset only future LTCG.
  • The Non-Negotiable ITR Filing Deadline (Section 80): To carry forward capital losses, you must file your Income Tax Return on or before the due date specified under Section 139(1) (usually July 31 for non-audit individuals). If you file a belated return under Section 139(4), your right to carry forward capital losses for that year is permanently extinguished.

6. Wash Sales, Re-entry Strategies & GAAR Traps

Unlike the United States IRS code, which imposes a mandatory 30-day "wash sale rule" disallowing losses if you buy substantially identical securities within 30 days before or after the sale, the Indian Income Tax Act has no explicit general 30-day wash-sale statute for equities. (Note: if you invest directly in American equities, review our detailed handbook on Tax on US Stocks in India (FY 2026-27): 24-Month Rule & Schedule FA for foreign tax credit and capital loss set-off mechanics).

However, investors must exercise tactical care:

  • Same-Day Intra-day Risk: If you sell and buy back identical shares on the same trading day within the same exchange account, the broker and tax system will treat the transaction as an intraday speculative trade rather than a delivery-based capital loss. Always wait until the trade settles (T+1) before repurchasing.
  • Substitutable Re-entry: Rather than repurchasing the identical stock or fund and taking settlement risk, redeploy the capital immediately into an equivalent broad index fund (e.g., selling a Large Cap active fund at a loss and immediately buying a Nifty 50 Index Fund). This keeps your market exposure intact with zero timing risk.
  • Section 94 Anti-Avoidance: Section 94(7) and 94(8) prohibit "dividend stripping" and "bonus stripping"—buying shortly before a record date and selling at a loss shortly after. Such artificial losses are strictly disallowed by tax authorities.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I set off mutual fund losses against salary income?

No. Under Section 71(3), capital losses can only be set off against capital gains under the same head. They cannot be adjusted against salary, business profits, rental income, or interest earnings.

Does tax loss harvesting apply to debt mutual funds?

Under the Finance Act amendments to Section 50AA, capital gains from specified debt mutual funds acquired on or after April 1, 2023 are deemed short-term capital gains and taxed at slab rates. Realized losses on debt funds can be offset against other capital gains according to capital loss rules.

What documents do I need to claim loss harvesting in my ITR?

You need your Capital Gains Statement from your broker or CAMS/KFintech, your Annual Information Statement (AIS), and Form 26AS. You must report these transactions in ITR-2 or ITR-3 under Schedule CG.
