Atal Pension Yojana (APY) Complete Guide: Eligibility, Contributions & Benefits

Atal Pension Yojana (APY) is a government-guaranteed pension scheme for Indian citizens between 18 and 40 years of age, administered by PFRDA. It offers a guaranteed monthly pension of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 after age 60, funded by monthly auto-debit contributions from your savings account. Since October 2022, income-tax payers cannot enrol. This guide explains eligibility, contribution charts, spouse benefits, exit rules, and how APY compares to NPS.

Important Notice for New Applicants

Since 1 October 2022, income-tax payers are not eligible to join Atal Pension Yojana (Gazette Notification, August 2022). Applicants who are income-tax payers will have their APY accounts closed and corpus refunded. Existing subscribers who enrolled before that date may continue their accounts regardless of their current income-tax status.

Key Takeaways

  • For non-income-tax payers aged 18–40 with a savings bank or post office account.
  • Guaranteed pension of ₹1,000, ₹2,000, ₹3,000, ₹4,000, or ₹5,000 per month from age 60.
  • Minimum contribution period: 20 years (join by 40, receive pension from 60).
  • Government guarantee: If investment returns fall short, the Government of India covers the difference.
  • Auto-debit mode: Contributions debited monthly, quarterly, or half-yearly from linked savings account.

1. What is Atal Pension Yojana?

Atal Pension Yojana (APY) was launched by the Government of India on 9 May 2015 under the National Pension System architecture administered by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). It was designed for India's unorganised sector workforce — domestic workers, construction labourers, daily wage earners, and small traders — who have no employer-provided retirement benefit.

Unlike EPF, which is mandatory for formal-sector employees, APY is a voluntary subscriber-funded scheme with a government guarantee on the pension amount. If actual investment returns fall short of what is needed to pay the guaranteed pension, the shortfall is borne by the Government of India.

APY is not a market-linked scheme. Your contribution is professionally managed by PFRDA-appointed pension fund managers, but what you receive at age 60 is a fixed, predetermined monthly pension amount — not a variable amount based on market returns. This makes it fundamentally different from NPS Tier I or mutual fund SIPs.

2. Eligibility Rules & the Income-Tax Payer Exclusion

Criterion Requirement
Nationality Indian citizen (resident)
Age at Entry 18 to 40 years
Bank Account Active savings bank or post office savings account
Mobile Number Mobile number linked to savings account for OTP and communications
Income-Tax Payer Status Must NOT be an income-tax payer. Effective 1 October 2022, income-tax payers who apply will have their accounts closed and corpus refunded.

The income-tax payer restriction was introduced by the Ministry of Finance via a Gazette Notification on 10 August 2022, effective from 1 October 2022. An "income-tax payer" for this purpose is someone who is or was liable to pay income tax under the Income Tax Act, 1961. If you filed an ITR with any taxable income, or if your income exceeds the basic exemption limit, you are considered an income-tax payer for this restriction. Existing APY subscribers who later become income-tax payers can continue their existing accounts — the restriction applies to new enrolments only.

3. Guaranteed Pension Slabs: ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 Per Month

At enrolment, you choose one of five guaranteed monthly pension slabs. Your choice determines your required monthly contribution based on your age. The pension starts at age 60 and continues for your lifetime, then for your spouse's lifetime, and finally the accumulated corpus goes to the nominee.

Pension Slab Monthly Pension After 60 Indicative Corpus at 60 Nominee Receives
Slab 1 ₹1,000 ~₹1.7 Lakh ~₹1.7 Lakh
Slab 2 ₹2,000 ~₹3.4 Lakh ~₹3.4 Lakh
Slab 3 ₹3,000 ~₹5.1 Lakh ~₹5.1 Lakh
Slab 4 ₹4,000 ~₹6.8 Lakh ~₹6.8 Lakh
Slab 5 ₹5,000 ~₹8.5 Lakh ~₹8.5 Lakh

Corpus figures are indicative; actual amounts may vary. The guaranteed element is the monthly pension, not the corpus.

4. Age-Wise Monthly Contribution Table & Worked Example

The monthly contribution depends on the pension slab chosen and the age at entry. Younger subscribers pay less because their money has more time to accumulate. The table below shows representative monthly contribution amounts from PFRDA's official subscriber contribution chart:

Age at Entry ₹1,000/mo ₹2,000/mo ₹3,000/mo ₹4,000/mo ₹5,000/mo
18 years ₹42 ₹84 ₹126 ₹168 ₹210
20 years ₹50 ₹100 ₹150 ₹198 ₹248
25 years ₹76 ₹151 ₹226 ₹301 ₹376
30 years ₹116 ₹231 ₹347 ₹462 ₹577
35 years ₹181 ₹362 ₹543 ₹722 ₹902
40 years ₹291 ₹582 ₹873 ₹1,164 ₹1,454

Source: PFRDA official subscriber contribution chart (monthly frequency). Figures per official PFRDA/NPS CRA publication and may be updated periodically. Verify exact amounts at the official PFRDA portal before subscribing.

Worked Example: Joining APY at Age 25 for ₹5,000/Month Pension

  • Monthly contribution: ₹376
  • Contribution period: 35 years (age 25 to 60)
  • Total contributions paid: ₹376 × 12 × 35 = ₹1,57,920
  • Guaranteed pension from age 60: ₹5,000/month for subscriber's lifetime, then for spouse's lifetime
  • Nominee corpus (indicative): ~₹8.5 Lakh
  • Break-even: If subscriber receives ₹5,000/month, they recover their total contribution in about 31.6 months (roughly 2.5 years) after turning 60.

5. How to Enrol in APY

APY accounts can be opened through multiple channels:

  • Bank branch: Visit any participating bank (most nationalised and private banks participate). Submit the APY subscriber registration form with your Aadhaar card, savings account details, and linked mobile number.
  • Internet banking: Several banks including SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, PNB, and Axis Bank allow APY enrolment through their net banking portals under 'Social Security Schemes'.
  • Mobile banking apps: YONO SBI, HDFC MobileBanking, and similar apps support APY enrolment directly.
  • Post office: India Post customers can enrol through their post office branch if they have a post office savings account.

At enrolment you must choose: (a) a pension slab, (b) contribution frequency (monthly/quarterly/half-yearly), (c) a nominee, and (d) optionally register your spouse's details for survivor pension. You must also provide a self-declaration that you are not an income-tax payer. Once enrolled, you receive a Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN) to track your account on the NPS CRA portal.

6. Spouse & Nominee Benefits

Event Benefit Payable
Subscriber reaches age 60 Guaranteed monthly pension (chosen slab) for subscriber's lifetime
Subscriber dies after 60 Spouse receives the same monthly pension for their lifetime
Both subscriber and spouse die Nominee receives the accumulated pension corpus
Subscriber dies before 60 Spouse may continue the APY account until original retirement date and then receive pension, OR close the account and receive the accumulated corpus

To receive survivor pension benefits, your spouse must be registered in the APY system with their name, Aadhaar, and date of birth. If these details are not registered at enrolment, update them promptly through your bank.

7. Exit Rules: Before 60 and At 60

Normal Exit at Age 60

At age 60, apply to your bank to formally close the APY account and commence the guaranteed monthly pension. The bank coordinates with NPS CRA to initiate pension payouts. The subscriber cannot withdraw the corpus as a lump sum at age 60 — the corpus is annuitised and you receive only the monthly pension.

Voluntary Exit Before Age 60

Voluntary early exit for healthy subscribers is technically permitted under PFRDA's guidelines, but on premature exit you receive only your own contributions and the net investment returns on them. Government co-contributions (if any) and returns on them are not refunded. This makes premature exit financially unattractive.

Penalty for Auto-Debit Failure

If your account has insufficient funds and auto-debit fails, PFRDA levies a monthly penalty:

  • ₹1 per month per ₹100 of contribution overdue (for contributions up to ₹100/month)
  • ₹2 per month (contributions ₹101–₹500/month)
  • ₹5 per month (contributions ₹501–₹1,000/month)
  • ₹10 per month (contributions above ₹1,000/month)

Failure for 6 months freezes the account; 12 months deactivates it; 24 months results in closure with corpus returned.

8. Tax Treatment of APY Contributions

APY contributions qualify for the income-tax deduction under Section 80CCD(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, within the overall Section 80C family ceiling of ₹1,50,000 per financial year. However, since income-tax payers cannot newly enrol in APY from October 2022, this deduction primarily benefits existing subscribers who enrolled before that date and continue their accounts.

The monthly pension received from APY after age 60 is taxable as income at the subscriber's (or spouse's) applicable slab rate. Many APY recipients, being in lower income brackets, may fall below the basic exemption limit post-retirement and may have no actual tax liability on the pension.

9. APY vs NPS: Key Differences

Feature APY NPS (Tier I)
Target Audience Non-income-tax payers in unorganised sector All Indian citizens aged 18–70
Returns Guaranteed fixed amount Market-linked, not guaranteed
Government Guarantee Yes — GoI backs shortfall No
Minimum Contribution ₹42/month (age 18, ₹1,000 slab) ₹500/month or ₹6,000/year
Withdrawal at 60 Monthly pension only (no lump-sum) 60% lump-sum + 40% compulsory annuity
Extra Tax Deduction No additional deduction beyond ₹1.5L ceiling Additional ₹50,000 under Sec 80CCD(1B)

APY is a safety-first scheme for those needing certainty. NPS is a wealth-creation tool for those comfortable with market risk and higher potential returns. Read our EPF vs PPF vs NPS retirement account comparison for a broader analysis.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Enrolling despite being an income-tax payer: Your account will be closed and you will receive only your own contributions back, without any guaranteed pension. Check your income-tax status before applying.
  • Not maintaining sufficient balance for auto-debit: A single failed debit triggers a penalty and repeated failures lead to account freezing. Keep a buffer balance in your APY-linked account.
  • Forgetting to register spouse details: The survivor pension benefit requires your spouse's name, Aadhaar, and date of birth to be registered. Do this at enrolment, not later.
  • Treating APY as sufficient retirement income: ₹5,000/month is unlikely to cover post-retirement expenses given 20–40 years of inflation. Use APY as a guaranteed floor and supplement it with NPS, PPF, or SIP investments.
  • Choosing a slab without checking if you can sustain contributions: Missing payments creates penalties. Pick a slab whose contribution you can comfortably auto-debit every month for the next 20–42 years.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I hold both an APY account and an NPS Tier I account?

Yes, there is no restriction on holding both. However, since income-tax payers cannot enrol in APY from October 2022, most individuals who have active NPS accounts (income-tax filers) would not be eligible to newly enrol in APY.

What happens if I become an income-tax payer after enrolling in APY?

If you enrolled in APY before 1 October 2022, your existing account continues without any change even if you subsequently become an income-tax payer. The restriction applies only to new enrolments after 1 October 2022.

How safe is APY? Is my pension guaranteed?

APY is backed by the Government of India. The pension fund is managed by PFRDA-approved pension fund managers investing in government securities and bonds. If returns fall short of what is needed to pay the guaranteed pension, the Government of India funds the difference. APY is considered one of the safest retirement instruments from a counterparty risk perspective.

Can I contribute more to get a higher pension than ₹5,000/month in APY?

No. APY is a defined-benefit scheme limited to ₹5,000/month as the highest slab. You cannot top up contributions to get more. For higher retirement income, consider NPS, PPF, or SIP-based mutual fund investments alongside APY.

Is the APY pension received after age 60 taxable?

Yes. The monthly pension received from APY is taxable as income at the recipient's applicable slab rate. However, many APY pensioners, being in lower income brackets, may fall below the basic exemption limit post-retirement and effectively have no tax liability on the pension.
