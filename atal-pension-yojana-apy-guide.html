Atal Pension Yojana (APY) Complete Guide: Eligibility, Contributions & Benefits

Atal Pension Yojana (APY) is a sovereign-guaranteed pension scheme providing Indian citizens aged 18 to 40 a fixed monthly pension of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 starting from age 60. An 18-year-old subscriber contributes just ₹210 per month (totaling ₹1.06 Lakh over 42 years) to secure ₹5,000 every month for life, with identical pension rights for their spouse and an ₹8.50 Lakh corpus returned to the nominee.

Atal Pension Yojana (APY) is a government-Fixed pension scheme for Indian citizens between 18 and 40 years of age, administered by PFRDA. It offers a Fixed monthly pension of ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 after age 60, funded by monthly auto-debit contributions from your savings account. Since October 2022, income-tax payers cannot enrol - making it exclusively a scheme for non-income-tax-paying individuals. Below, we examine eligibility, contribution charts, benefits, exit rules, and how APY compares to NPS.

Key Takeaways

  • For non-income-tax payers aged 18 to 40 with a savings bank or post office account.
  • Fixed pension of ₹1,000, ₹2,000, ₹3,000, ₹4,000, or ₹5,000 per month from age 60.
  • Minimum contribution period: 20 years (join by age 40, receive from age 60).
  • Government co-contribution: Available only to subscribers who joined before 31 March 2016 and were not income-tax payers or covered under any statutory social security scheme.
  • Auto-debit mode: Contributions are debited monthly, quarterly, or half-yearly from your linked savings account.

1. What is Atal Pension Yojana?

Atal Pension Yojana (APY) was launched by the Government of India on 9 May 2015, administered by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). Central authorities introduced the scheme to provide a reliable safety net for unorganized sector workers, including drivers, domestic help, construction workers, and small shop owners who have zero corporate pension benefits.

Unlike the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), which is mandatory for formal-sector employees, APY is a voluntary, subscriber-funded scheme with a government guarantee on the pension amount. Sovereign guarantees backstop the pension obligation: if actual returns from the APY corpus fall short of what is needed to pay the Fixed pension, the shortfall is borne by the Government of India.

Unlike conventional mutual funds, APY does not operate as a market-linked scheme. Your contribution is professionally managed by PFRDA-appointed pension fund managers, but what you receive at age 60 is a fixed, predetermined monthly pension amount - not a variable amount linked to investment returns.

2. Eligibility Rules & the Income-Tax Payer Exclusion

The eligibility criteria for APY are straightforward, but there is one significant rule change from October 2022 that many people are unaware of:

Criterion Requirement
Nationality Indian citizen (resident)
Age at Entry 18 to 40 years (minimum contribution period: 20 years)
Bank Account Active savings bank or post office savings account
Mobile Number Mobile number linked to savings account for OTP and communications
Income-Tax Payer Status Must NOT be an income-tax payer. Effective 1 October 2022, income-tax payers who apply will have their accounts closed and corpus refunded without government co-contribution or penalty fee waiver.

What Does "Income-Tax Payer" Mean Here?

An "income-tax payer" for APY exclusion purposes is someone who is or was liable to pay income tax under the Income Tax Act, 1961. If you filed an ITR (even with zero tax payable), or if your income exceeds the basic exemption limit, you would be considered an income-tax payer. Gazette notifications issued by the Ministry of Finance formalized this restriction on 10 August 2022, effective from 1 October 2022. Existing APY subscribers who later become income-tax payers can continue their existing accounts - the restriction applies to new enrolments only.

3. Fixed Pension Slabs: ₹1,000 to ₹5,000 Per Month

At the time of enrolment, you choose one of five Fixed monthly pension slabs. Your choice determines your required monthly contribution, which is based on your age at entry. Pension payouts commence at age 60 and continue throughout your lifetime. After your death, your spouse receives the same pension amount, and after the spouse's death, the nominee receives the accumulated corpus.

Pension Slab Monthly Pension After 60 Indicative Corpus at 60 Nominee Receives
Slab 1 ₹1,000/month ~₹1.7 Lakh ₹1.7 Lakh
Slab 2 ₹2,000/month ~₹3.4 Lakh ₹3.4 Lakh
Slab 3 ₹3,000/month ~₹5.1 Lakh ₹5.1 Lakh
Slab 4 ₹4,000/month ~₹6.8 Lakh ₹6.8 Lakh
Slab 5 Rs 5,000 a month ~₹8.5 Lakh ₹8.5 Lakh

Note: Corpus figures are indicative. Actual accumulated corpus may vary. The Fixed component is the monthly pension amount, not the corpus value.

The monthly contribution required under APY depends on the pension slab you choose and the age at which you join. Younger subscribers pay less because their money has more time to accumulate. Worked Example: Joining APY at Age 28 for Rs 5,000 a month Pension

Suppose Rekha joins APY at age 28 and opts for the Rs 5,000 a month pension slab.

  • Monthly contribution: approximately ₹478 (for entry age 28 - between 25 and 30 on the chart)
  • Contribution period: 32 years (until age 60)
  • Total contributions: ₹478 × 12 × 32 ≈ ₹1,83,552
  • Fixed pension from age 60: Rs 5,000 a month for Rekha's lifetime, then Rs 5,000 a month for her spouse's lifetime
  • Nominee corpus: approximately ₹8.5 Lakh (indicative)

Exact monthly amount for age 28 should be verified from the official PFRDA contribution chart linked in the sources section below.

5. How to Enrol in APY

APY accounts can be opened through multiple channels:

  • Bank branch: Visit any participating bank (most nationalised and private banks participate) and submit the APY subscriber registration form. You will need your Aadhaar card, savings account details, and a linked mobile number.
  • Internet banking: Several banks including SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, PNB, and others allow APY enrolment through their net banking portals under the 'Social Security Schemes' section.
  • Mobile banking apps: YONO SBI, HDFC MobileBanking, and similar apps support APY enrolment.
  • Post office savings account: India Post customers can enrol through their post office branch.

At the time of enrolment, you must:

  • Choose a pension slab (₹1,000, ₹2,000, ₹3,000, ₹4,000, or ₹5,000).
  • Choose your contribution frequency (monthly, quarterly, or half-yearly).
  • Nominate a beneficiary (nominee) and, optionally, register your spouse's details for survivor pension benefits.
  • Provide a self-declaration that you are not an income-tax payer (applicable from October 2022).

Once enrolled, your contributions are auto-debited from your savings account on the chosen frequency date. You receive an acknowledgement and a Permanent Retirement Account Number (PRAN) which you can use to track your APY account on the NPS CRA portal.

6. Spouse & Nominee Benefits

APY provides a tiered benefit structure that extends beyond the subscriber's own lifetime:

Event Benefit
Subscriber reaches 60 Receives Fixed monthly pension (chosen slab) for lifetime
Subscriber dies after 60 Spouse receives the same monthly pension for their lifetime
Both subscriber and spouse die Nominee receives the accumulated pension corpus (indicative amounts in table above)
Subscriber dies before 60 Spouse may continue the APY account until original subscriber would have turned 60, then receive pension - OR - choose to close the account and receive the accumulated corpus

This multi-generational benefit design makes APY a genuine lifelong income instrument, not just a retirement corpus accumulation product.

7. Exit Rules: Before 60 and At 60

At Age 60 (Normal Exit)

At age 60, the subscriber must apply to their bank to formally close the APY account and commence the Fixed monthly pension. Your bank coordinates directly with the NPS CRA to initiate pension payouts. Subscribers cannot withdraw the lump-sum corpus at age 60 - the corpus is annuitised by the scheme, and you receive only the monthly pension.

Before Age 60 (Voluntary Exit)

Voluntary exit before age 60 is permitted only in exceptional cases: upon the death of the subscriber or due to a terminal illness. For healthy subscribers wishing to exit early, this option was not available until PFRDA introduced a voluntary exit provision. Whenever a voluntary early exit occurs, the subscriber receives only their own contributions and the returns earned on them - the government co-contribution (if any) and returns on it are not returned.

Penalty for Non-Payment

If your savings account has insufficient funds and auto-debit fails, you face an overdue penalty:

  • ₹1 per month per ₹100 of contribution overdue for contributions up to ₹100/month
  • ₹2 per month for contributions between ₹101 and ₹500/month
  • ₹5 per month for contributions between ₹501 and ₹1,000/month
  • ₹10 per month for contributions above ₹1,000/month

If contributions remain unpaid for 6 months, the account is frozen. After 12 months, it is deactivated. After 24 months, it is closed, and the corpus is returned to the subscriber.

8. Tax Treatment of APY Contributions

APY contributions made by the subscriber qualify for the income-tax deduction under Section 80CCD(1) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. This deduction falls under the broader Section 80C family and is subject to the overall ceiling of ₹1,50,000 per financial year shared among Section 80C, 80CCC, and 80CCD(1) investments.

Yet a practical complication exists: since 1 October 2022, income-tax payers cannot enrol in APY. Existing subscribers who enrolled before that cutoff and later became income-tax payers, you can continue your APY account and claim the deduction under Section 80CCD(1).

Monthly pension disbursements received at age 60 are taxable as income from salary/pension in the hands of the subscriber, at their applicable income-tax slab rate at that time.

9. APY vs NPS: Key Differences

APY and NPS are both administered by PFRDA and both create a retirement corpus using pension fund managers. In practice, both frameworks serve fundamentally different purposes:

Feature APY NPS (Tier I)
Target Group Unorganised sector workers (non-IT payers) All Indian citizens 18 to 70
Pension Type Fixed fixed amount Market-linked, not Fixed
Government Guarantee Yes, Government backstops shortfall No
Minimum Contribution ₹42/month (age 18, ₹1,000 slab) ₹500/month or ₹6,000/year
Withdrawal at 60 Only monthly pension (no lump-sum) 60% lump-sum + 40% annuity
Tax Deduction Section 80CCD(1), up to ₹1.5L ceiling 80CCD(1) + 80CCD(1B) up to ₹50,000 extra

APY is a safety-first scheme designed for low-income workers who need certainty. NPS is a wealth-creation tool for those comfortable with market-linked returns. See our EPF vs PPF vs NPS comparison guide for a deeper analysis of retirement account options.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Enrolling despite being an income-tax payer: Your account will be closed and you will receive only your contributions back - without any government co-contribution or any annuity benefit. Check your income tax filing status before applying.
  • Not maintaining sufficient balance for auto-debit: A single failed auto-debit triggers a penalty. Set up a reminder or maintain a minimum balance in your APY-linked savings account consistently.
  • Choosing the wrong slab and not adjusting: You can change your pension slab once per year in April. Many subscribers choose a low slab thinking they will upgrade later, but forget to do so for years.
  • Not registering spouse details: The survivor pension benefit is contingent on your spouse being registered in the APY system. Ensure spouse details (name, Aadhaar, date of birth) are correctly entered at enrolment.
  • Expecting APY to be sufficient as the only retirement plan: Even at the highest slab, Rs 5,000 a month is unlikely to cover post-retirement expenses given inflation over a 20-40 year horizon. Treat APY as a baseline floor guarantee rather than your entire retirement strategy.

11. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I have both an APY account and an NPS Tier I account?

Yes, there is no restriction on holding both APY and NPS accounts simultaneously. They use the same PRAN (Permanent Retirement Account Number) architecture. Because income-tax payers cannot enrol in APY after October 2022, most people who have NPS accounts (which require filing returns) would not be eligible to newly enrol in APY.

What happens if I become an income-tax payer after already enrolling in APY?

Subscribers who enrolled in APY prior to 1 October 2022 retain active accounts without any change, even if you subsequently become an income-tax payer. The restriction applies only to new enrolments after 1 October 2022. You can continue contributing and will receive the Fixed pension at age 60.

How is the APY pension funded and is it safe?

Pooled contributions are managed professionally by PFRDA-appointed pension fund managers who invest in a conservative mix of government securities and corporate bonds. The guarantee of the fixed pension amount is backed by the Government of India - meaning if the investment returns fall short of what is required to pay the Fixed pension, the Government meets the difference. Backing by the sovereign makes APY one of India's safest pension vehicles from a counterparty risk perspective.

Can I contribute more than the mandatory amount to get a higher pension in APY?

No. APY is a defined benefit scheme - the pension amount is fixed at the chosen slab, and you cannot "top up" contributions to get more than Rs 5,000 a month. If you want higher retirement income, consider combining APY with NPS, PPF, or SIP investments in mutual funds, as appropriate to your situation.

Is the monthly pension received from APY after age 60 taxable?

Yes. The monthly pension received from APY after age 60 is taxable as income in the hands of the subscriber (or spouse, as applicable) at their applicable income-tax slab rate. At the same time, because APY targets individuals in lower income brackets, many recipients may fall below the basic income-tax exemption limit after retirement and may have no actual tax liability on the pension received.
