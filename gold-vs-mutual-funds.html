Gold vs Mutual Funds in India: Returns, Inflation Defense & Asset Allocation

While gold acts as a proven crisis hedge and inflation defense delivering 9–10% historical annualized returns in rupees, equity mutual funds generate superior long-term wealth compounding at 12–14% CAGR by participating in corporate earnings growth. A ₹10,000 monthly SIP over 15 years yields ₹55.57 Lakh in equity mutual funds versus ₹38.12 Lakh in gold, making a 5–10% gold allocation ideal strictly as a portfolio shock absorber.

Physical bullion holds immense cultural and emotional significance in Indian households. However, from an investment standpoint, evaluating gold against equity and debt mutual funds across long-term returns, volatility defense, liquidity, and post-Budget taxation is essential.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Over the 20-year period ending 2025, the Nifty 50 TRI delivered a 14.2% CAGR versus gold's 11.8% CAGR , equity outperformed gold significantly over long horizons, with the gap widening to 3%+ annually on extended timelines.
  • Precious metals and equity mutual funds exhibit a near-zero correlation over 15+ year periods , when equities crash, gold frequently rallies, making 10% to 15% gold allocation an effective portfolio diversifier and volatility hedge.
  • For a 30-year retirement horizon, ₹10,000 monthly in Nifty 50 grows to approximately ₹3.53 Crore at 12% CAGR; the same ₹10,000 in a gold SIP grows to approximately ₹2.09 Crore at 9% CAGR , a ₹1.44 Crore difference.
  • The yellow metal generates no operating income (dividends, interest, rent); its entire return is purely capital appreciation driven by global demand, currency weakness, and crisis sentiment.
  • Gold's best performance periods are high-inflation, currency-crisis, and geopolitical stress environments , specifically when real interest rates are negative. In normal economic cycles, equity significantly outperforms gold.

Bullion has been India's most emotionally invested asset class for centuries , cultural, ceremonial, and now financial. Equity mutual funds are the new challenger, arriving at scale in Indian households only in the 2010s. Both assets have genuine merit. Our core question is not which is better, but which belongs in what proportion of a thoughtfully constructed portfolio , and why.

When Gold Outperforms Equity

Gold has specific conditions under which it significantly outperforms equity. Understanding these helps you use it tactically rather than emotionally:

  • Geopolitical crises and wars: Russia-Ukraine (2022), COVID-19 (2020), and geopolitical shocks — domestic gold prices have frequently rallied sharply during acute risk-off periods.
  • Negative real interest rates: When RBI repo rates are below inflation (real rate negative), gold rallies. When real rates are high (+3%+), gold underperforms.
  • Rupee depreciation: Gold is priced in USD globally. A weakening rupee inflates gold's INR returns even when USD gold prices are flat.
  • Global central bank buying: RBI and other central banks have been net buyers of gold since 2022, supporting sustained demand and prices.

Recommended Allocation: 85/15 for Long-Term Investors

For a 30-year-old with a 25-year investment horizon, the optimal allocation based on long-term data is approximately 85% equity mutual funds + 15% gold (via SGBs or gold ETFs). Such an allocation delivers:

  • Full participation in India's equity growth story (primary wealth generator)
  • Inflation hedge and crisis buffer through gold's negative equity correlation
  • Reduced portfolio volatility during equity bear markets (2008, 2020)

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I sell my physical gold jewelry to invest in equity mutual funds?

Physical gold jewelry used for wear is a personal asset, not purely an investment. Selling jewelry triggers making charges, market discount on sale, and capital gains tax (12.5% LTCG after 24 months). If you want to understand how your local jeweller computes pricing, GST, and 22K vs 24K rates, read our detailed guide on how gold price is decided in India. If you have surplus physical gold sitting unused in a bank locker consuming annual locker fees, monetizing it via Sovereign Gold Bond exchange schemes (RBI's Gold Monetisation Scheme) or selling to buy gold ETFs may make financial sense. Do not sell emotionally significant or religiously important jewelry.

Strategic Takeaway

Gold and equity mutual funds are not competitors , they are complementary portfolio components with different risk-return profiles and different roles. Holding bullion provides stability during equity market crises. Investors who hold both in appropriate proportions sleep better during market turbulence and accumulates more wealth over 20+ years than the investor who goes 100% into either. Add gold exposure through SGBs (for buy-and-hold investors) or gold ETFs on Zerodha Coin (for tactical rebalancing) , never through jewellery or digital gold platforms. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the historical return difference between Gold and Equity Mutual Funds in India?

Over rolling 15-year holding horizons, domestic gold prices in INR have historically compounded at 9% to 10% CAGR (bolstered by rupee depreciation), while diversified equity mutual funds (such as Nifty 50 or Flexi-Cap funds) have generated 12% to 14% CAGR, driven by India's underlying corporate earnings growth.

How is gold taxed after the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024?

Under the revised rules, the holding period for Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) on physical gold, Gold ETFs, and Gold Mutual Funds has been reduced to 24 months. LTCG is taxed at a flat rate of 12.5% without indexation benefit. Short-Term Capital Gains (held for 24 months or less) are added to income and taxed at applicable slab rates.

What is the ideal percentage of gold to hold in a long-term investment portfolio?

Most institutional financial planners recommend allocating 5% to 10% of your total investable net worth to gold (via Gold ETFs or Sovereign Gold Bonds). This provides a portfolio shock absorber during geopolitical conflicts and equity bear markets without dragging down long-term compounding.

Are Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) still tax-free upon maturity?

Yes. Under Section 47(viic) of the Income-tax Act, capital gains arising on redemption of Sovereign Gold Bonds by resident individual investors upon full maturity (8 years) with the RBI remain 100% exempt from income tax.

Why does gold have a low or negative correlation with equities?

Gold is viewed globally as a safe-haven monetary reserve. During periods of severe economic recessions, market crashes, or geopolitical turmoil, investors liquidate risky assets (stocks) and purchase gold, causing gold prices to surge when stock indices plunge.
