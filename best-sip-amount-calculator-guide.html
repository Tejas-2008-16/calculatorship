Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • The correct SIP amount is derived from your goal corpus, timeline, and expected CAGR — not from a vague percentage of salary. Use the reverse SIP formula: Monthly SIP = Goal ÷ FVIFA(r, n), where r = monthly rate and n = months.
  • To accumulate ₹1 Crore in 15 years at 12% CAGR, you need to invest approximately ₹20,000 per month. At 10 years, you need ₹43,500 per month for the same corpus. Starting earlier reduces monthly burden by more than half.
  • Financial planners recommend committing 20%–30% of take-home monthly salary to SIP investments; the 50-30-20 rule suggests 20% for savings/investments as a minimum floor.
  • Always size your SIP so that even a 3-month income interruption doesn't force a panic pause — maintain a 6-month emergency fund in a liquid fund before launching any equity SIP.
  • Never size your SIP by looking at what "others invest" — the right amount is the one that covers your specific financial goals with a comfortable monthly surplus remaining after deducting EMIs, rent, and living expenses.

The question "how much should I invest in SIP?" has a mathematically precise answer — and it is not "10% of salary" or "whatever you can afford." Your SIP amount should be derived backward from the specific corpus you need for a specific goal by a specific date. Anything less is guesswork that either leaves you underfunded at retirement or over-invests into equity when you should be sleeping peacefully.

The Reverse SIP Calculation Formula

Instead of asking "how much will my SIP grow to?", ask "how much SIP do I need to reach my goal?"

Monthly SIP Required = Goal Amount × r / [(1+r)^n - 1]

Where: r = monthly interest rate (Annual Rate ÷ 12) | n = total months of investment

The Salary Allocation Rule for SIP Sizing

A practical thumb rule for sizing your monthly SIP based on take-home salary:

  • Essential expenses (rent, food, EMIs, utilities): 50% of take-home
  • Discretionary (lifestyle, dining, entertainment): 30% of take-home
  • Savings + Investments (SIP, PPF, emergency fund): Minimum 20% of take-home

On a ₹70,000/month take-home salary, the minimum SIP budget is ₹14,000. Allocate this across goal-specific SIPs: ₹5,000 into a liquid fund (emergency top-up), ₹5,000 into mid-cap equity (retirement), and ₹4,000 into ELSS (tax saving).

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I prioritize paying off my home loan EMI or investing in SIP?

Do both simultaneously. Home loan interest is pre-tax deductible under Section 24(b) up to ₹2 Lakh annually on a self-occupied property — your effective after-tax home loan interest rate is typically 6%–7.5%. An equity SIP historically returns 11%–12% CAGR. The mathematical case for investing in parallel with your EMI is strong for anyone with a 10+ year SIP horizon. Only prepay your home loan aggressively if your interest rate exceeds 8.5% and you have no tax deduction headroom remaining.
