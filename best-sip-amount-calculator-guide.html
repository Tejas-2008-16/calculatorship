Introduction: Goal-Based Sizing vs Random Investing

The single most frequent question from Indian salaried professionals and new mutual fund investors is: "How much SIP should I start each month?"

Too often, investors pick an arbitrary round number—such as ₹1,000, ₹5,000, or ₹10,000—without linking it to a concrete financial objective or their real savings potential. This ad-hoc approach often leads to under-investing during high-earning years or taking mismatched risks. The solution is goal-based reverse sizing: calculating your exact monthly investment requirement based on your target corpus, expected time horizon, and compounding CAGR.

1. The Golden Formula: Reverse-Engineering Your Monthly SIP

The Future Value of an annuity due (monthly SIP) is governed by the standard compounding equation:

FV = PMT × [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r] × (1 + r)

Where PMT is your monthly SIP installment, r is the periodic monthly rate of return (Annual CAGR ÷ 12), and n is the total number of months. By solving for PMT, we can determine the exact monthly contribution needed to achieve any target corpus.

2. Salary-Based SIP Sizing: What % of Income Should You Invest?

According to the classic 50-30-20 Budgeting Rule, salaried individuals in India should allocate a minimum of 20% of net take-home salary to long-term wealth building, with 25% to 30% being ideal for early financial independence.

3. Life-Stage SIP Sizing Guide (Ages 22 to 55)

Your capacity to save and your risk tolerance change dramatically across life stages:

Age 22–28 (Early Career): Start Small, Start Now

When starting your career, time is your greatest financial superpower. Investing ₹3,000 per month starting at age 22 beats someone investing ₹15,000 per month starting at age 35 due to 13 extra years of compounding runway. Prioritize broad-market index funds and flexi-cap schemes.

Age 28–35 (Career Acceleration & Family): Scale with Appraisals

During your late 20s and early 30s, career promotions and income growth accelerate. Commit to a 10% annual Step-Up SIP. Aim for ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 monthly contributions aligned with long-term home purchase down payments and retirement foundations.

Age 35–45 (Peak Earning Years): Maximize Goal Allocations

Earnings peak during this decade. Target ₹40,000 to ₹80,000+ monthly SIPs across segregated folios dedicated to children's higher education, marriage, and retirement corpus accumulation.

Age 45–55 (Pre-Retirement & De-risking): Strategic Asset Shift

With retirement on the horizon, gradually shift equity profits toward conservative debt instruments and prepare for post-retirement Systematic Withdrawal Plans (SWP Guide).

4. The Step-Up SIP Multiplier Effect

A flat monthly SIP ignores annual salary increments. By stepping up your monthly contribution by just 10% each year, your wealth accumulation potential surges by 40% to 60% over long horizons.

5. Common SIP Sizing Mistakes to Avoid

  • The Token ₹500 Trap: Investing ₹500 per month while spending ₹10,000 on weekend dining and lifestyle inflation creates zero meaningful retirement security.
  • Over-Diversification of Tiny Amounts: Splitting a ₹5,000 monthly SIP into 5 different funds (₹1,000 each) creates administrative chaos and high portfolio overlap. Two well-chosen funds are sufficient.
  • Stagnant SIP Amounts: Keeping the same ₹5,000 monthly SIP for 10 years despite receiving multiple salary promotions.
  • Ignoring Inflation: Failing to account for lifestyle and education inflation (typically 7%–10% in India) when sizing target goal numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is ₹1,000 per month SIP enough to build wealth in India?

Yes. Starting with ₹1,000 per month creates an early investing habit. Compounded at 12% CAGR over 25 years, a ₹1,000 monthly SIP grows to over ₹18.9 Lakh. Combined with a 10% annual Step-Up SIP, it grows to over ₹58 Lakh.

What is the minimum SIP amount in Indian mutual funds?

The minimum SIP amount for most Indian mutual funds is ₹500 per month, with several leading index funds and micro-SIP options allowing investments starting from as low as ₹100 per month.

Should I split a ₹20,000 monthly SIP across 5 funds or 2 funds?

It is far better to split ₹20,000 across 2 to 3 well-chosen funds (e.g. ₹10,000 in a Nifty 50 Index Fund and ₹10,000 in a Flexi Cap Fund) rather than spreading it thinly across 5+ funds which causes heavy portfolio overlap and administrative clutter.

How often should I increase my monthly SIP amount?

You should step up your SIP contribution annually by 10% to 15% immediately following your annual salary appraisal or bonus cycle.

Can I invest ₹5 Lakh as a lumpsum instead of an SIP?

Yes. If you have a lump sum of ₹5 Lakh, you can either invest it directly in equity funds for a 7+ year horizon or park it in a liquid fund and deploy it systematically via a 6 to 12-month Systematic Transfer Plan (STP) to average entry valuations.
