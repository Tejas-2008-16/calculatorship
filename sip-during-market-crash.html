How SIPs Work During Market Downturns: Rupee Cost Averaging and Unit Math

1. The Mathematics of Rupee Cost Averaging

In a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP), an investor commits a fixed financial amount at scheduled intervals (such as monthly). Because the contribution remains constant in rupee terms, the number of units allotted varies inversely with the scheme's Net Asset Value (NAV):

Units Allotted = Fixed Monthly Installment ÷ Applicable NAV

When market valuations decline and the scheme's NAV decreases, each fixed rupee installment purchases a mathematically higher number of units. Conversely, when valuations appreciate, each installment buys fewer units.

3. Historical Market Drawdowns in India

Indian equity markets have historically experienced cyclical corrections. For instance, during the 2008 Global Financial Crisis and the March 2020 pandemic onset, broad benchmark indices such as the Nifty 50 and BSE Sensex experienced notable peak-to-trough drawdowns before eventual recoveries. However, market timing remains mathematically challenging because recovery phases often occur rapidly, making individual entry and exit forecasting uncertain.

4. How to Think About This Yourself

When assessing your portfolio during market corrections, consider:

  • What is the remaining investment horizon for your financial goals? Capital required in the near term (e.g., under 3 years) should generally not be exposed to equity market volatility.
  • Do you have an adequate emergency fund (typically 3 to 6 months of living expenses in liquid instruments) so that you are not forced to liquidate equity units at depressed prices?
  • Does your overall asset allocation match your personal risk tolerance, or is your equity exposure causing undue stress?
Where to get personalised help: For individualized advice on asset allocation, rebalancing, or risk assessment during market downturns, consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (RIA). The official registry is available at sebi.gov.in. For tax implications regarding redemptions and capital loss set-offs, consult a Chartered Accountant.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the primary difference between a SIP pause and a redemption?

Pausing an SIP temporarily halts future monthly debits from your bank account without selling any existing units. A redemption, on the other hand, sells your accumulated units back to the fund house at the prevailing NAV, which realizes capital gains or losses and triggers tax obligations.

Does continuing an SIP eliminate investment risk?

No. An SIP averages purchase costs over time but does not protect against prolonged bear markets or permanent capital loss in underperforming assets. Equity investments remain subject to general market risks.
