Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS): Interest, Rules & Math

Hi, I'm Tejas — an engineering student researching personal finance. This guide breaks down the statutory rules, deposit caps (₹9L single, ₹15L joint), monthly interest payout arithmetic (7.4% p.a.), premature exit penalties, and tax treatment for POMIS. It is written with AI assistance and checked against official Ministry of Finance circulars and India Post rules. It is purely educational and does not constitute financial, investment, or tax advice. Always verify current interest rates at your local post office branch before investing.

For retirees, conservative savers, and households seeking Fixed monthly income without equity market volatility, the Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS) remains one of India's premier sovereign-backed fixed-income instruments. With an attractive 7.4% interest rate, revised ₹9 Lakh single and ₹15 Lakh joint deposit ceilings, and monthly automated payouts, it provides stable predictable cash flow.

Key Takeaways at a Glance

  • Sovereign Safety: Backed directly by the Government of India with 100% capital protection and Fixed non-fluctuating interest across the entire 5-year tenure.
  • Substantial Deposit Ceilings: Single accounts allow up to ₹9,00,000, while joint accounts accommodate up to ₹15,00,000.
  • Fixed Monthly Cash Flow: At 7.4% per annum, an individual account generates ₹5,550 per month, while a ₹15 Lakh joint account delivers ₹9,250 per month.
  • Zero TDS at Source: The post office deducts no tax at source on monthly interest, but the income must be declared in your tax return under Other Sources.
  • Lock-in & Penalties: 5-year tenure. No exit allowed in Year 1. A 2% penalty applies between Years 1 to 3, and a 1% penalty applies between Years 3 to 5.

1. What is the Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS)?

The National Savings Monthly Income Account (commonly known as POMIS) is a government-Fixed savings scheme administered by the Department of Posts under the Ministry of Communications. Designed primarily to provide steady recurring cash flows, the scheme allows Indian citizens to park a lump sum amount for a fixed tenure of 5 years and receive a Fixed monthly interest payout.

Unlike commercial bank fixed deposits where monthly interest options often come at discounted payout rates, POMIS calculates interest at the full annual rate (currently 7.4% p.a.) divided strictly into 12 equal monthly disbursements.

2. Eligibility, Deposit Limits & Joint Account Architecture

POMIS is accessible to all resident Indian citizens subject to statutory guidelines:

  • Eligible Entities: Resident Indian adults. Accounts can also be opened on behalf of a minor (aged below 10 through a guardian) or by a minor aged 10 and above in their own name. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) are not eligible.
  • Single Account Limit: A single individual can deposit up to ₹9,00,000 in POMIS across all post office branches combined.
  • Joint Account Limit: Up to three adults can hold a joint account with a combined deposit cap of ₹15,00,000. In a joint account, all joint holders have an equal share in the investment and resulting monthly payout.
  • Multiple Account Holding Rule: An individual can operate multiple POMIS accounts across different post offices, provided their aggregate share across all single and joint accounts does not breach the individual statutory limit of ₹9,00,000.

3. Monthly Payout Math: Single vs Joint Payout Tables

The monthly interest formula governing POMIS is straightforward:

Monthly Payout = (Principal Amount × Annual Interest Rate) ÷ 12

The interest is credited automatically into the investor's Post Office Savings Account (POSA) on the monthly anniversary of the opening deposit date, from which it can be auto-transferred to an ECS-linked bank account.

4. Premature Withdrawal Penalties & 5-Year Maturity

POMIS carries a statutory maturity period of 5 years from the deposit date. While liquidity is provided, premature closure incurs statutory penalties:

  • Within 1 Year: No premature closure is allowed under any circumstances (except on the unfortunate death of the account holder).
  • Between 1 Year and 3 Years: The account can be closed prematurely, but an amount equal to 2% of the principal deposit is deducted as a penalty, and the balance is refunded.
  • Between 3 Years and 5 Years: The account can be closed prematurely with a deduction of 1% of the principal deposit.
  • Maturity at 5 Years: Upon completion of 5 years, the entire original principal is returned with zero deductions. Investors can choose to close the account or reinvest the principal under prevailing new rates.

5. Tax Treatment, TDS Rules & Section 80TTB Relief

Investors frequently harbor misconceptions regarding the tax-saving credentials of post office schemes. Here is the exact statutory tax positioning of POMIS:

  • No Section 80C Benefit: The initial investment in POMIS is not deductible under Section 80C. Unlike PPF, NSC, or 5-year Post Office Time Deposits, POMIS offers zero entry tax deduction.
  • Zero TDS Deducted: The post office does not deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on monthly interest payouts under Section 194A. However, "No TDS" does not mean "Tax-Free".
  • Taxed at Slab Rates: Monthly interest receipts must be reported under "Income from Other Sources" in your Income Tax Return (ITR-1 or ITR-2) and are taxed at your applicable personal income tax slab rate.
  • Section 80TTB for Senior Citizens: If the account holder is a senior citizen (aged 60+), interest earned from POMIS qualifies for the ₹50,000 annual interest deduction under Section 80TTB (Old Tax Regime), offering substantial tax sheltering. 7. The POMIS-to-RD Auto-Compounding Strategy

While POMIS is designed for monthly consumption, non-retired savers often use it for Fixed capital accumulation through the POMIS-to-RD Loophole Strategy:

  1. Deposit the maximum permissible ₹9,00,000 into a POMIS account.
  2. Simultaneously open a 5-year Post Office Recurring Deposit (RD) account.
  3. Set an automated ECS standing instruction to divert the monthly POMIS interest of ₹5,550 directly into the RD account every month.

Because Post Office RD interest compounds quarterly at competitive sovereign rates (currently 6.7% p.a.), this converts a simple non-compounding monthly payout scheme into a compounding sovereign wealth-building vehicle, yielding an effective return exceeding 8.1% on original capital.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if I don't withdraw the monthly interest from my POMIS account?

If monthly interest is not withdrawn, it sits in your post office savings account without earning any additional interest over and above basic savings account rates (4% p.a.). The unclaimed interest does NOT compound within the POMIS deposit itself.

Can I transfer my POMIS account from one post office to another?

Yes. You can submit an SB-10(b) account transfer application along with your passbook to either your current or new post office branch. Account transfers are permitted across any CBS-enabled post office nationwide without fee or loss of accumulated interest.

Is nomination mandatory for POMIS accounts?

Yes, nominating at least one nominee is mandatory at the time of opening under the Government Savings Promotion Act. In the event of the depositor's demise, the account is closed and principal plus interest up to the month of death is paid to the nominee.

Can two family members hold a joint account with ₹15 Lakh and also separate single accounts of ₹9 Lakh each?

No. The statutory rule dictates that an individual's total share in all POMIS accounts cannot exceed ₹9 Lakh. In a ₹15 Lakh joint account with two equal holders, each holder has consumed ₹7.5 Lakh of their personal limit. Therefore, each holder can only invest an additional ₹1.5 Lakh in a separate single account (₹7.5L + ₹1.5L = ₹9L).

How to Think About This Yourself

  • Cash Flow Certainty: POMIS locks in a fixed 7.4% sovereign payout for 5 years without market volatility, making it an effective fixed cash-flow component for retired individuals or conservative portfolios.
  • Interest Non-Compounding: Monthly payouts must be actively withdrawn or swept via standing instruction into a savings or recurring deposit account; otherwise, idle balances do not earn compounded return.
  • Deposit Cap Architecture: Keep track of your aggregate share across all post offices to ensure it stays within the statutory ₹9 Lakh individual ceiling (or ₹15 Lakh joint allocation).
  • Taxability: Monthly payouts are fully taxable at slab rates under "Income from Other Sources". Senior citizens can claim up to ₹50,000 deduction on total interest income under Section 80TTB.
