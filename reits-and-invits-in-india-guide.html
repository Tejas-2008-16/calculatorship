REITs and InvITs in India: Complete Investor's Guide 2026

1. What are REITs and InvITs in India?

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) and Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) are investment vehicles regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) under the SEBI (Real Estate Investment Trusts) Regulations, 2014 and SEBI (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) Regulations, 2014.

A REIT functions by pooling capital from retail and institutional investors to acquire, manage, and operate income-generating commercial real estate assets (such as Grade-A IT parks, corporate towers, and regional retail malls). InvITs operate under a similar legal structure but invest in long-gestation infrastructure projects such as toll roads, interstate power transmission corridors, and renewable energy grids.

By statutory mandate, both REITs and InvITs must distribute at least 90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unitholders at least semi-annually (most Indian REITs pay quarterly distributions). Furthermore, a minimum of 80% of total asset value must be invested in operational, revenue-generating assets, capping speculative development risk at 20%.

2. SEBI Regulations, Lot Sizes & Exchange Trading

Historically, commercial real estate in India was accessible only to high-net-worth individuals and corporate funds due to high ticket sizes running into tens of crores. Following progressive reforms by SEBI, the barrier to entry has been lowered to make these trusts accessible to everyday retail earners:

  • Lot Size: The minimum trading lot size on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) is fixed at 1 unit.
  • Dematerialized Trading: Units trade during standard market hours (09:15 to 15:30 IST) just like listed common equity shares, directly settled in your demat account on a T+1 settlement cycle.
  • Current Listed REITs in India: Embassy Office Parks REIT, Mindspace Business Parks REIT, Brookfield India Real Estate Trust, and Nexus Select Trust (India's first pure-play retail mall REIT).
  • Current Publicly Traded InvITs: PowerGrid Infrastructure Investment Trust (PGInvIT), India Grid Trust (IndiGrid), and IRB InvIT Fund.

3. The 3-Tier Distribution Taxation Framework (FY 2026-27)

When you receive quarterly cash payouts from a REIT, the distribution is not treated as a single uniform income source. Because the REIT trust holds properties through Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs), the trust passes through various income streams to unitholders under Section 115UA and Section 10(23FD) of the Income Tax Act.

Distribution Component Legal Nature Tax Status for Unitholders (FY 2026-27)
Interest Income Interest earned by trust on SPV loans Taxable at Slab Rates: Added to your taxable income under 'Income from Other Sources' and taxed at your marginal slab rate. TDS @ 10% deducted if aggregate interest exceeds ₹5,000.
Dividend Income Dividends declared by underlying SPVs Dual Regime Dependent: If the underlying SPV chose the old corporate tax regime (concession-free), the dividend is 100% Tax-Exempt in your hands. If the SPV opted for Section 115BAA (22% corporate tax), the dividend is taxable at your income tax slab rate.
Repayment of Debt / Capital Reduction Principal capital returned by SPVs to the Trust Taxable under Section 56(2)(xii): Taxable as income from other sources, after deducting the issue price adjustment. The taxable portion is taxed at your applicable slab rate.
Rental / Other Income Direct rent earned by trust without SPV layer Taxable at Slab Rates: Fully taxable under 'Income from Other Sources'.

For capital gains realized upon selling REIT units in the secondary stock market, check our detailed Capital Gains Tax Guide: Long-Term Capital Gains (>12 months holding) are taxed at 12.5% above the ₹1.25 Lakh annual exemption threshold, while Short-Term Capital Gains (≤12 months) are taxed at 20% flat.

Let us analyze the exact annual cashflow and net post-tax return for an investor allocating ₹1,00,000 across Indian commercial REIT units yielding an aggregate gross pre-tax distribution of 7.20% per annum (₹7,200 annual cash distribution). Suppose the investor falls in the 30% Income Tax Slab (effective 31.20% including 4% Health and Education Cess).

Interest Income 40% ₹2,880 31.20% Slab Tax = ₹898.56 ₹1,981.44

6. Structural Risks, Tenant Vacancy & Liquidity Constraints

While REITs and InvITs offer regular cashflows, investors must evaluate several risk factors before committing capital:

  1. Interest Rate Sensitivity: REIT unit prices demonstrate an inverse relationship with benchmark bond yields. When the Reserve Bank of India raises the repo rate, sovereign bond yields increase, making REIT distribution yields comparatively less attractive, which leads to temporary unit price corrections on stock exchanges.
  2. Occupancy & Lease Expiry Risk: If major IT or consulting tenants reduce leased office footprints due to remote work policies or corporate restructuring, occupancy rates can drop below 85%, reducing the Net Distributable Cash Flow.
  3. Secondary Market Price Volatility: Unlike bank fixed deposits whose principal is guaranteed, REIT units trade on the stock market. You may experience capital gains or mark-to-market losses depending on market sentiment and institutional flows.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum investment required to buy REIT units in India?

Under SEBI revised trading regulations, the minimum trading lot size for publicly listed REITs on NSE and BSE is exactly 1 unit. Retail investors can buy a single unit of Embassy, Mindspace, Brookfield, or Nexus Select Trust for approximately ₹300 to ₹400 directly through any standard demat account.

How is income distributed by Indian REITs taxed in FY 2026-27?

REIT quarterly distributions are split into three components: (1) Interest income, which is taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate; (2) Dividend income, which is tax-free if the underlying SPV chose the old corporate tax regime or taxable at slab rates if the SPV opted for Section 115BAA concessional tax; and (3) Repayment of SPV debt / capital reduction, which is taxed as income from other sources under Section 56(2)(xii) with cost-basis adjustments.

What is the capital gains tax rate on selling REIT units on the stock exchange?

For listed REIT units sold on NSE or BSE with STT paid: Long-Term Capital Gains (holding period > 12 months) are taxed at 12.5% for gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year. Short-Term Capital Gains (holding period ≤ 12 months) are taxed at a flat rate of 20%.

How do REIT cash yields compare to physical residential real estate rental yields in India?

Indian publicly listed commercial REITs historically generate pre-tax distribution yields between 6.5% and 8.0% per annum, compared to residential physical property rental yields in major metro cities (Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi-NCR) which average between 2.0% and 3.5% gross before property taxes and maintenance costs.
