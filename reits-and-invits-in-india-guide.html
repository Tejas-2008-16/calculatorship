REITs and InvITs in India: Complete Investor's Guide 2026

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) and Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) allow retail investors to own institutional commercial real estate and utility assets with a trading lot size of just 1 unit. Governed by SEBI's mandate to distribute at least 90% of Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF), unitholders receive quarterly payouts comprising interest, dividends, rental income, and capital reduction, each subject to distinct statutory tax rules under the Income-tax Act.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) and InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) allow retail investors to own fractional shares in commercial Grade-A office parks, malls, power grids, and highways with minimum ticket sizes under ₹400 on NSE/BSE.
  • By SEBI mandate, REITs and InvITs must distribute at least 90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unitholders bi-annually or quarterly, creating predictable cash flow yielding 6.5% to 8.5% pre-tax.
  • Cash distributions are a blend of Dividend, Interest, Return of Capital (ROC), and Other Income: each component carries distinct taxation under the Income Tax Act.
  • Since Finance Act 2023, distributions categorized as "Repayment of Debt / Return of Capital" exceeding the unit issue price are taxed as income from other sources at slab rates, slightly moderating post-tax yields for HNIs.
  • REITs solve the illiquidity, substantial ticket size (₹1 to 5 Cr), tenant default risk, and property tax headaches of physical real estate, trading transparently like equities.

For generations, real estate has been the holy grail of Indian wealth creation. Yet physical property ownership comes bundled with crippling flaws: illiquidity, tenant management nightmares, high maintenance costs, registration stamp duties of 5% to 8%, and rental yields on residential property that rarely exceed 2.5% to 3.0% per annum.

REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) and InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) have transformed commercial property investing. Regulated strictly by SEBI, these trusts permit salaried professionals to invest ₹5,000 or ₹50,000 into institutional-grade assets leased to Fortune 500 multinationals, earning dividend yields double or triple residential rentals while maintaining instant liquidity on the stock exchange.

Demystifying REIT Payout Taxation (Post-2023 Budget)

Every quarter, your REIT pays cash directly into your linked bank account. But this payout is not a monolithic dividend. It is split into four distinct tax components detailed in the quarterly statement:

Payout Stream Underlying Source Taxability for Unitholder
Dividend Component Dividends passed through from SPVs 100% Tax-Free if SPV has not opted for Section 115BAA concessional tax; otherwise taxable at slab rate
Interest Component Interest paid by SPVs to the Trust Fully Taxable at your personal income tax slab rate
Return of Capital (ROC) Loan repayment from SPVs Taxable at slab rate only to the extent cumulative payouts exceed initial issue price (Section 56(2)(xii))
Capital Gains on Unit Sale Sale of REIT units on stock exchange LTCG (>12 months): 12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh; STCG (≤12 months): 20%

Who Should Allocate to REITs and InvITs?

REITs are hybrid instruments combining fixed-income characteristics (regular quarterly payouts) with equity growth (rental escalations and property appreciation). They are best suited for:

  • Retirees seeking passive cash flow: A 7% to 8% distribution yield provides healthy quarterly income without needing to sell capital assets.
  • Salaried investors diversifying away from pure equity: Adding a 10% to 15% allocation to REITs dampens overall portfolio volatility while keeping returns ahead of bank fixed deposits.
  • Investors who want real estate exposure without property management: No dealing with brokers, municipal property tax, stamp duty registration, or defaulting tenants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can REIT distributions drop during economic slowdowns?

Yes. Unlike bank FDs which guarantee interest, REIT distributions depend on underlying rental collections and physical occupancy rates. If IT or tech multinationals downsize office space or surrender leases en masse, distributions can moderate. However, top Indian REITs maintain 85% to 92% committed occupancy with weighted average lease expiry (WALE) terms of 6 to 8 years, providing substantial cash flow stability.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the minimum investment amount and lot size for Indian REITs?

SEBI reduced the trading lot size of listed REITs and InvITs to just 1 unit on the stock exchange. Investors can purchase a single unit for approximately ₹300 to ₹400 directly through any demat trading account.

How are quarterly distributions from REITs taxed in the hands of unitholders?

REIT distributions are split into distinct components: Interest and Rental income are fully taxable at your income tax slab rate; Dividend income is tax-free if the underlying SPV chose the Old Corporate Tax Regime (or taxable at slab rate if it chose Section 115BAA); and Repayment of Debt / Capital Reduction is taxed under Section 56(2)(xii) as other income beyond issue cost adjustments.

What is the capital gains tax on selling listed REIT units in India?

Following Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, if listed REIT units are held for more than 12 months, Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) are taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year. Units held for 12 months or less are subject to Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) at 20% under Section 111A.

How much of their cash flows are REITs legally required to pay out to investors?

Under SEBI REIT Regulations, 2014, all registered REITs and InvITs must distribute at least 90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unitholders at least semi-annually, though in practice, most listed Indian REITs pay out quarterly.

What are the main listed REITs currently available on Indian stock exchanges?

The prominent SEBI-registered listed REITs in India include Embassy Office Parks REIT, Mindspace Business Parks REIT, Brookfield India Real Estate Trust, and Nexus Select Trust (India's first retail mall REIT).
