REITs and InvITs in India: Complete Investor's Guide 2026

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) and Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) provide retail investors in India with fractional ownership in Grade-A commercial office parks, shopping malls, power grids, and highways. Learn how these exchange-traded trusts function, analyze their 3-tier distribution taxation, and review worked income models.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) and InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) allow retail investors to own fractional shares in commercial Grade-A office parks, malls, power grids, and highways with minimum ticket sizes under ₹400 on NSE/BSE.
  • By SEBI mandate, REITs and InvITs must distribute at least 90% of their Net Distributable Cash Flows (NDCF) to unitholders bi-annually or quarterly — creating predictable cash flow yielding 6.5%–8.5% pre-tax.
  • Cash distributions are a blend of Dividend, Interest, Return of Capital (ROC), and Other Income — each component carries distinct taxation under the Income Tax Act.
  • Since Finance Act 2023, distributions categorized as "Repayment of Debt / Return of Capital" exceeding the unit issue price are taxed as income from other sources at slab rates, slightly moderating post-tax yields for HNIs.
  • REITs solve the illiquidity, massive ticket size (₹1–5 Cr), tenant default risk, and property tax headaches of physical real estate, trading transparently like equities.

For generations, real estate has been the holy grail of Indian wealth creation. Yet physical property ownership comes bundled with crippling flaws: illiquidity, tenant management nightmares, high maintenance costs, registration stamp duties of 5%–8%, and rental yields on residential property that rarely exceed 2.5% to 3.0% per annum.

REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) and InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) have revolutionized this landscape. Regulated strictly by SEBI, these trusts permit salaried professionals to invest ₹5,000 or ₹50,000 into institutional-grade assets leased to Fortune 500 multinationals — earning dividend yields double or triple residential rentals while maintaining instant liquidity on the stock exchange.

Demystifying REIT Payout Taxation (Post-2023 Budget)

Every quarter, your REIT pays cash directly into your linked bank account. But this payout is not a monolithic dividend. It is split into four distinct tax components detailed in the quarterly statement:

Payout Stream Underlying Source Taxability for Unitholder
Dividend Component Dividends passed through from SPVs 100% Tax-Free if SPV has not opted for Section 115BAA concessional tax; otherwise taxable at slab rate
Interest Component Interest paid by SPVs to the Trust Fully Taxable at your personal income tax slab rate
Return of Capital (ROC) Loan repayment from SPVs Taxable at slab rate only to the extent cumulative payouts exceed initial issue price (Section 56(2)(xii))
Capital Gains on Unit Sale Sale of REIT units on stock exchange LTCG (>12 months): 12.5% above ₹1.25 Lakh; STCG (≤12 months): 20%

Who Should Allocate to REITs and InvITs?

REITs are hybrid instruments combining fixed-income characteristics (regular quarterly payouts) with equity growth (rental escalations and property appreciation). They are best suited for:

  • Retirees seeking passive cash flow: A 7%–8% distribution yield provides healthy quarterly income without needing to sell capital assets.
  • Salaried investors diversifying away from pure equity: Adding a 10%–15% allocation to REITs dampens overall portfolio volatility while keeping returns ahead of bank fixed deposits.
  • Investors who want real estate exposure without property management: No dealing with brokers, municipal property tax, stamp duty registration, or defaulting tenants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can REIT distributions drop during economic slowdowns?

Yes. Unlike bank FDs which guarantee interest, REIT distributions depend on underlying rental collections and physical occupancy rates. If IT or tech multinationals downsize office space or surrender leases en masse, distributions can moderate. However, top Indian REITs maintain 85%–92% committed occupancy with weighted average lease expiry (WALE) terms of 6–8 years, providing substantial cash flow stability.
