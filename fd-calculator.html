How Fixed Deposit (FD) Compounding Works

A Bank Fixed Deposit (FD) is one of the most popular fixed-income investment products in India. When you deposit a lump sum amount for a fixed tenure, the bank pays you interest based on a pre-decided interest rate.

In most Indian commercial banks, compounding is calculated on a quarterly basis (4 times a year). This means every 3 months, the interest earned is added back to your principal, and future interest is calculated on this enlarged sum.

The FD Compound Interest Formula

Compound Interest Mathematical Formula
A = P × [ 1 + (r / n) ] ^ (n × t)
  • A : Final Maturity Amount
  • P : Principal Deposit (Starting capital)
  • r : Annual Interest Rate in decimal (7% = 0.07)
  • n : Compounding intervals per year (Quarterly = 4)
  • t : Number of years invested

TDS Rules on Bank Fixed Deposits

Under Section 194A of the Income Tax Act, banks deduct 10% TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) if your cumulative annual FD interest across all branches of a bank exceeds ₹40,000 in a financial year for regular individuals (or ₹50,000 for senior citizens aged 60+). If your total taxable income is below the exemption threshold, you can submit Form 15G or Form 15H to prevent TDS deductions.
