How to Read Your Salary Slip in India: Every Component Explained (2026)

Key Takeaways

  • CTC ≠ your take-home salary — employer PF, gratuity, and your own PF deductions reduce your monthly bank credit significantly
  • Basic Salary is the foundation — PF, HRA, and gratuity are all calculated as a percentage of Basic
  • HRA is partially tax-exempt if you live in a rented home and actually pay rent
  • TDS on salary is deducted based on your chosen tax regime — New Regime is now default from FY 2026-27
  • PF deducted from your salary is your money being saved, not lost — it earns 8.25% and builds retirement wealth
  • Professional Tax varies by state — Maharashtra deducts ₹200/month, other states differ

Why Most Indians Misread Their Salary Slip

You've been receiving a salary slip every month for years. And every month, most people do the same thing: scroll straight to the bottom, check the "Net Pay" figure, and close the document.

That habit is costing people money. Errors in salary slips — wrong PF deductions, incorrect HRA calculations, missing allowances from your offer letter, wrong Professional Tax slab — are surprisingly common in Indian payroll, especially at mid-sized companies without robust payroll software. The only person who can catch these errors is you, and only if you know what each line is supposed to say.

Beyond error-checking, understanding your salary slip helps you file your ITR correctly, negotiate better at your next appraisal, and make smarter tax-saving decisions throughout the year. This guide explains every single line on a typical Indian salary slip in plain language, with the actual formulas so you can verify your numbers.

The Big Picture: CTC vs Gross Salary vs Net (Take-Home) Salary

Before diving into individual lines, understand this hierarchy — it's the source of more confusion than anything else in Indian compensation.

CTC (Cost to Company) is what your employer spends on you annually. It includes your gross salary plus the employer's contribution to your PF, gratuity provision, health insurance, and any other benefits. When a company says "we're offering you ₹10 LPA," that ₹10 lakh includes these employer-side costs that never come to you directly.

Gross Salary is your total monthly earnings before any deductions — Basic + HRA + all allowances. This is the figure you'll see on the left side of your salary slip under "Earnings."

Net Salary (Take-Home) is Gross Salary minus all deductions (your PF contribution, Professional Tax, TDS, and any other cuts). This is what hits your bank account.

A ₹10 LPA CTC doesn't mean ₹83,333/month in hand. After employer PF (which is part of CTC but doesn't come to you), your own PF deduction, Professional Tax, and TDS — your actual take-home for a ₹10 LPA CTC in Pune is typically ₹68,000–₹73,000/month depending on your tax regime choice and rent situation.

The Earnings Side: Every Component Explained

Basic Salary

Basic Salary is the foundation of your entire salary structure. Typically set at 40–50% of your CTC (though the Code on Wages 2019 requires it to be at least 50% of total remuneration for many categories of employees — a rule being phased in).

It matters because almost everything else is calculated from it:
— Your Employee PF contribution = 12% of Basic Salary
— Your HRA exemption calculation uses your Basic as a reference
— Your gratuity (on exit after 5 years) = 15 days of Basic per year of service

A higher Basic Salary means higher PF deductions (your money, saved for retirement) and higher gratuity accrual (your money, paid on exit). Some companies artificially keep Basic low to reduce their own PF liability — the Code on Wages is slowly fixing this, but check your offer letter to verify.

HRA — House Rent Allowance

HRA is typically 40–50% of your Basic Salary. If you live in Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, or Chennai — historically classified as metro cities — your HRA is 50% of Basic. For all other cities, it's 40%. From April 2026, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, and Ahmedabad have been included in the 50% metro category — if you're in one of these cities and your payslip still shows 40%, flag it immediately with HR.

HRA is partially tax-exempt if you live in a rented accommodation and actually pay rent. The exempt amount is the lowest of these three figures:
— Actual HRA received from employer
— 40% or 50% of Basic Salary (depending on city)
— Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic Salary

If you don't live in a rented house (own your home or live with parents), the entire HRA is taxable. You cannot claim HRA exemption and simultaneously claim deduction on home loan interest under Section 24(b) for the same property.

Note: HRA exemption is available only under the Old Tax Regime. If you've opted for the New Regime (which is now the default), you cannot claim HRA exemption — your entire HRA is taxed as income.

Special Allowance

Special Allowance is the "balancing figure" in your salary structure — it's whatever is left after Basic, HRA, and other specific allowances are allocated from your CTC. It has no specific statutory definition, carries no tax exemption, and is fully taxable as income.

If you see a very large Special Allowance relative to your Basic, it may indicate that your salary structure is optimised to minimise PF contributions (since PF is calculated only on Basic, not Special Allowance). This isn't necessarily bad for take-home pay, but it does mean lower PF accumulation over time.

Other Allowances (LTA, Conveyance, Medical)

LTA (Leave Travel Allowance) — Covers travel costs for domestic travel within India. Tax-exempt up to actual travel cost, claimable for two journeys in a 4-year block. Can only be claimed under the Old Tax Regime.

Conveyance Allowance — ₹1,600/month was historically the standard tax-free conveyance allowance. Under the New Tax Regime, this is subsumed into the ₹75,000 standard deduction.

Children's Education Allowance — ₹100/month per child (maximum 2 children) is exempt from tax. Recently updated under Income Tax Rules 2026.

Medical Allowance — Separate from health insurance. Up to ₹15,000/year was historically exempt when submitted with actual bills; under the New Regime, this too is absorbed into standard deduction.

The Deductions Side: Every Cut Explained

Employee PF (Provident Fund)

Your PF deduction is 12% of your Basic Salary every month. This is not money being taken from you — it's your own savings going into your EPFO account, earning a government-declared interest rate (8.25% for FY 2024-25, one of the best risk-free rates available).

Your employer also contributes 12% of your Basic — but this is separate from your CTC in most structured salary packages, or included in CTC if mentioned. Of your employer's 12%, 8.33% goes to Employee Pension Scheme (EPS) and 3.67% goes to your EPF account.

The PF deduction is capped based on a statutory wage ceiling of ₹15,000/month — meaning the mandatory PF is ₹1,800/month (12% × ₹15,000) even if your Basic is higher. However, if your actual Basic is, say, ₹40,000, many companies deduct 12% of actual Basic = ₹4,800. Your offer letter will specify which method applies.

To verify your PF is actually being deposited and not just deducted, log in to the EPFO member portal at epfindia.gov.in using your UAN (Universal Account Number shown on your salary slip). Check the passbook every quarter.

TDS — Tax Deducted at Source

TDS is the income tax deducted from your salary monthly by your employer on behalf of the Income Tax Department. Your employer estimates your total annual tax liability at the start of the financial year based on your declared investments and tax regime choice, then divides it by 12 to arrive at a monthly TDS figure.

From FY 2026-27, the New Tax Regime is the default for all salaried employees. If you want to opt for the Old Tax Regime (to claim HRA, 80C deductions, LTA, etc.), you must submit Form 12BB to your employer at the start of the financial year and update it if your investment declarations change.

If your TDS amount changes significantly from month to month, it usually means: you submitted new investment declarations that reduced your liability, you received a bonus which increased your projected annual income, or your employer recalculated mid-year after a declaration update.

The annual TDS certificate is Form 16 (also referred to as Form 130 under the new Income Tax Code updates for 2026-27). The TDS figure on your salary slips across all 12 months should add up exactly to Part A of your Form 16. If they don't match, flag it with HR before filing your ITR.

Professional Tax

Professional Tax is a state government levy on salaried employees and self-employed professionals. It is not a central government tax. States have different slabs and amounts — Maharashtra deducts ₹200/month (₹2,400/year, with February reduced to ₹300 in some interpretations), Karnataka deducts up to ₹200/month, Tamil Nadu charges ₹208/month for salaries above ₹15,000, and several states including Delhi, Haryana, and Rajasthan do not levy Professional Tax at all.

Professional Tax is deductible from your income tax — so if you pay ₹2,400/year in PT, that reduces your taxable income by ₹2,400 under both Old and New Regimes.

Note that the CTC for this employee would be higher — the employer also contributes ₹2,400/month to PF (their share), plus gratuity provision and any health insurance premium, making the total CTC roughly ₹50,000–₹53,000/month despite a take-home of ₹43,000. This is why CTC and take-home are never the same number.

5 Things to Check Every Time You Get Your Salary Slip

Verify PF deduction = 12% of your Basic — divide your Basic by 100 and multiply by 12; if the number on your slip doesn't match, raise it with payroll immediately
Check your HRA rate is correct for your city — from April 2026, Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad, and Ahmedabad employees should see 50%, not 40%
Confirm all allowances from your offer letter appear — missing conveyance, meal coupons, or internet allowances mean you're being paid less than contracted
Cross-check TDS against your tax regime choice — if you opted out of New Regime but TDS looks too high, your Form 12BB may not have been processed
Log into EPFO quarterly to verify PF is actually deposited — deducted but not deposited is a serious payroll crime

Use Your Salary Slip to Make Smarter Financial Decisions

Your salary slip is not just a payment confirmation — it's a financial planning tool hiding in plain sight. The exact gross salary figure helps you calculate how much you can invest monthly. The TDS figure tells you whether you've submitted enough investment declarations. The PF balance (accessible via EPFO portal) shows you how much retirement wealth you've already built without realising it.

Once you understand every line on your payslip, you're also better equipped for salary negotiations — because you can structure your demand in a way that maximises take-home (requesting more internet allowance, NPS contribution via Section 80CCD(2), or meal coupons instead of the same amount as taxable special allowance).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my take-home salary so much less than my CTC?

CTC includes employer-side costs that never reach you — your employer's PF contribution (12% of Basic), gratuity provision (4.81% of Basic approximately), and health insurance premium. Your own PF deduction (12% of Basic), Professional Tax, and TDS further reduce what hits your bank account. For a ₹10 LPA CTC, the typical take-home is ₹65,000–₹73,000/month depending on your tax regime and HRA situation.

Is PF deduction bad for my take-home salary?

PF deduction reduces take-home, but it is not money you lose. It goes into your EPFO account earning 8.25% tax-free interest (under EEE status) — better than most FDs. When you retire or change jobs (and transfer PF), this corpus is yours. Think of PF not as a deduction but as forced savings at a very good interest rate.

Can I avoid TDS on my salary legally?

You cannot avoid paying income tax legally if your income is above the threshold. But you can reduce TDS by submitting accurate investment declarations via Form 12BB at the start of the year — including 80C investments (ELSS, PPF), health insurance premiums (80D), and HRA claims. These declarations reduce your estimated tax liability and therefore your monthly TDS deduction. Under the New Regime, deduction options are limited — mainly standard deduction of ₹75,000 and NPS Section 80CCD(2) employer contribution.

What should I do if I spot an error in my salary slip?

Email your HR or payroll team in writing (not just verbally) citing the specific line item, the amount shown, and the correct amount based on your offer letter or the applicable formula. Keep a record of the communication. Most payroll errors are genuine mistakes that companies correct in the next month's payroll. If unresolved after two months, escalate to your manager and if necessary to the Labour Commissioner — payroll errors are a compliance matter under the Payment of Wages Act.
