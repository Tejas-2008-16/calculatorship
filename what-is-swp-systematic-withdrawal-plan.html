SWP Explained: How to Turn Your Mutual Fund Investment Into a Monthly Income

SIP gets you to the mountain top. SWP is how you live there. A Systematic Withdrawal Plan lets you pull a regular monthly income from your mutual fund corpus while keeping the remaining money invested and — if done right — still growing. Here is everything you need to know to use it correctly.

SIP Reverse
SWP is the exact mirror image of how SIP works

Meet Priya, a 58-year-old retired schoolteacher in Nagpur. She has built a mutual fund corpus of ₹50 lakh over 20 years of disciplined SIP investing. She does not want to withdraw everything at once — that would leave it sitting in a savings account earning 3%. She does not want to depend on unpredictable dividends either. She wants ₹25,000 in her bank account every month, reliably, while the rest of her money keeps growing. That is exactly what a Systematic Withdrawal Plan is designed to do.

But before you set one up, there are things to understand: how the math works, how taxes apply, and the mistakes that quietly erode a corpus. All of that below.

What is SWP and How Does It Work Mechanically?

A Systematic Withdrawal Plan is the reverse of a SIP. Instead of putting a fixed amount into a mutual fund every month, you pull a fixed amount out of it every month (or quarter). The AMC automatically redeems however many units are needed to generate that withdrawal amount, based on the fund’s NAV on that date.

Say Priya has ₹50 lakh in a balanced advantage fund with a current NAV of ₹100 — giving her 50,000 units. She sets up an SWP of ₹25,000/month. On the SWP date, the fund checks the NAV (say it’s ₹102), redeems 245.09 units (₹25,000 ÷ ₹102), and credits ₹25,000 to her bank. Her remaining units: 49,754.91. This happens automatically, month after month, without her doing anything.

The Worked Example: Does the Corpus Actually Last?

The key question with SWP: will the corpus grow faster than you withdraw from it, or shrink over time?

Priya’s numbers:

  • Corpus: ₹50,00,000
  • Monthly withdrawal: ₹25,000 = ₹3,00,000/year
  • Withdrawal rate: 3,00,000 ÷ 50,00,000 = 6% per year
  • Fund’s expected CAGR: ~10–11% (balanced advantage fund, approximate historical range)

The fund earns roughly ₹5,00,000–₹5,50,000 per year in appreciation, while Priya withdraws ₹3,00,000. The gap means her corpus continues to grow even as she withdraws. Over 10 years, her ₹50 lakh corpus could potentially grow to ₹70–80 lakh (before taxes), while she has also received ₹30 lakh in total withdrawals.

The Central Arithmetic: If your annual withdrawal rate is less than your fund’s CAGR, the corpus grows. If it equals the CAGR, the corpus stays flat. If it exceeds the CAGR, the corpus shrinks. This is the math every SWP user must understand.

Flip the scenario: if Priya withdraws ₹60,000/month (₹7.2 lakh/year = 14.4% rate on a ₹50 lakh corpus) but the fund earns only 10%, she is withdrawing more than the fund earns. The corpus shrinks every year, and the rate of shrinkage accelerates. This is the scenario to actively avoid.

How Are SWP Withdrawals Taxed?

SWP withdrawals are not salary or interest — they are capital gains. Each month, when the AMC redeems units for your SWP, those units generate a capital gain (the difference between the redemption price and the original purchase cost of those specific units).

For Equity and Equity-Oriented Hybrid Funds:

  • Units held more than 12 months (LTCG): 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year (as per FY 2025-26 rules; verify current rates at the time of filing).
  • Units held less than 12 months (STCG): 20% on gains.

For Debt Mutual Funds (purchased after April 1, 2023):

Gains are added to your total income and taxed at your applicable slab rate — no separate LTCG/STCG treatment. This makes debt fund SWPs relatively less tax-efficient for people in higher slabs.

The Silver Lining: Each monthly SWP redemption only taxes the gain portion, not the full withdrawal. If Priya withdraws ₹25,000 but only ₹8,000 of that is gain (the rest is return of her original principal), only ₹8,000 is subject to capital gains tax. Efficient SWP planning uses this principal-first structure to its tax advantage.

What Is a Safe Withdrawal Rate?

For Indian equity mutual fund portfolios, a broadly accepted safe withdrawal range is 6–8% per year of the corpus. Below 6% and your corpus grows meaningfully. At 6–8%, the corpus remains roughly stable. Above 10% consistently, you risk depletion within 15–20 years.

Why not withdraw at the fund’s average return rate (say 12%)? Because returns are not linear. A fund averaging 12% CAGR over 20 years might fall 30% in year 2 and rise 40% in year 3. If you withdraw 12% during the bad years, you are selling units at low prices and permanently destroying capital. The safe withdrawal rate has a buffer built in for exactly this market volatility risk.

4 Common SWP Mistakes That Quietly Destroy a Corpus

Mistake 1: Starting SWP Too Soon After Investing

Some investors make a lumpsum investment and immediately start an SWP within the first year. Equity mutual fund units redeemed within 12 months attract 20% STCG tax. Wait at least 12–13 months after a lumpsum investment before starting an SWP on an equity fund. Our Lumpsum Calculator can show you how much those first 12 months of growth contribute to your total corpus before withdrawals begin.

Mistake 2: Choosing a Volatile Fund for SWP

Small-cap and mid-cap funds might return 15–18% on average, but they can also drop 40–50% in a bad year. If you are running SWP from a fund that crashes 45% the year you retire, you are redeeming units at rock-bottom prices. Use balanced advantage funds, hybrid equity funds, or large-cap funds with lower volatility as the base for your SWP corpus. Pure small-cap or sectoral funds are not suitable SWP vehicles.

Mistake 3: Withdrawing More Than the Fund Earns

The most common and most damaging error. If your corpus is ₹30 lakh and your fund earns ~10% (~₹3 lakh/year), but you withdraw ₹40,000/month (₹4.8 lakh/year), you are drawing down ₹1.8 lakh/year more than the fund earns. The corpus shrinks every year, and the rate of shrinkage accelerates as the corpus gets smaller. Run the numbers before setting the withdrawal amount.

Mistake 4: Not Accounting for Inflation

A ₹25,000/month withdrawal feels comfortable today. In 10 years at 6% annual inflation, the real purchasing power of ₹25,000 will be closer to ₹14,000 in today’s money. Either step up your SWP amount periodically (most AMCs allow this) or invest in a fund that historically beats inflation by a wide margin, so your growing corpus compensates for declining purchasing power.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is SWP different from a dividend plan?
In a dividend plan, the AMC decides when and how much to declare — you have no control, and the NAV drops by the dividend amount. In SWP, you decide the amount and timing of every withdrawal. SWP is more predictable, gives you full control, and is generally more tax-efficient than the dividend option.
Is SWP income taxable in India?
Yes. Each SWP withdrawal redeems mutual fund units and generates a capital gain. For equity funds, units held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% LTCG on gains above ₹1.25 lakh/year; units held under 12 months at 20% STCG. For debt funds purchased after April 2023, gains are taxed at your slab rate. Only the gain portion of each redemption is taxed, not the full withdrawal amount.
What is a safe withdrawal rate for SWP in India?
A broadly accepted safe withdrawal range is 6–8% per year of the corpus. On a ₹50 lakh corpus, that is ₹3–₹4 lakh/year, or roughly ₹25,000–₹33,000/month. Withdrawing consistently above 10% per year risks gradually depleting the corpus.
Can I set up SWP on any mutual fund?
Yes, most open-ended mutual funds allow SWP. Set it up monthly, quarterly, or weekly through your AMC app, broker platform (Groww, Zerodha Coin, Kuvera), or directly on MF Central. Minimum corpus and withdrawal amounts vary by AMC — check the Scheme Information Document for your specific fund.

How to Actually Set Up an SWP

Setting up an SWP takes under five minutes on most platforms. Log into your AMC app or broker platform, navigate to your fund holding, look for the “SWP” option, set the monthly amount, the SWP date (choose a date 3–5 days after your typical monthly expense cycle), and the bank account for credit. The first withdrawal usually happens within 1–2 business days of the set date.

One important first step: calculate how large your corpus needs to be to support your desired monthly withdrawal at a safe withdrawal rate. If you want ₹30,000/month (₹3.6 lakh/year) at a 6% withdrawal rate, you need a corpus of at least ₹60 lakh.
