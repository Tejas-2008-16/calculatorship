GST Calculator India (Inclusive & Exclusive)

Written by Tejas Waghmare • Last updated: November 2026
Educational content only: This calculator provides mathematical GST simulations under the Central Goods and Services Tax (CGST) and State GST (SGST) Acts. Consult a Chartered Accountant for complex input tax credit (ITC) reconciliation.

Direct Answer: The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is an indirect, destination-based tax levied across India. For intra-state transactions, GST is split equally into CGST and SGST (e.g. 18% = 9% CGST + 9% SGST). For inter-state transactions, the entire 18% is billed as Integrated GST (IGST).

Transaction & Tax Parameters

Invoice Breakdown

Net Pre-Tax Value ₹10,000
CGST Portion ₹900
SGST Portion ₹900
Total GST Tax Amount ₹1,800
Total Invoice Amount ₹11,800
Intra-state split: 50% CGST + 50% SGST. Inter-state: 100% IGST.

The Core GST Mathematical Formulas

1. GST Exclusive (Adding GST to Base Cost):

GST Amount = Base Value * (GST Rate / 100)
Total Invoice Value = Base Value + GST Amount

2. GST Inclusive (Extracting GST from Gross Price):

GST Amount = Total Value - [Total Value / (1 + GST Rate / 100)]
Net Base Value = Total Value - GST Amount

2 Worked Mathematical Examples in Rupees

Example 1: Software Consultancy Billing (Exclusive 18%)

A web developer in Maharashtra invoices a Mumbai client for ₹1,00,000 plus 18% GST (Intra-state transaction).

  • Net Base Value = ₹1,00,000
  • CGST (9%) = ₹9,000
  • SGST (9%) = ₹9,000
  • Total GST = ₹18,000
  • Gross Invoice Amount = ₹1,18,000

Example 2: Consumer Electronics Purchase (Inclusive 18%)

A consumer purchases a smartphone at an MRP of ₹23,600 including 18% GST.

  • Net Base Value = ₹23,600 / 1.18 = ₹20,000
  • Total Embedded GST = ₹23,600 - ₹20,000 = ₹3,600
  • CGST Component = ₹1,800; SGST Component = ₹1,800.

3 Common Mistakes When Calculating GST

1. Applying Exclusive Rates to MRP

In India, Maximum Retail Price (MRP) is statutorily inclusive of all taxes. Adding 18% on top of an MRP invoice is illegal under the Legal Metrology Act.

2. Confusing CGST/SGST with IGST

If the supplier and place of supply are in different states, charging CGST and SGST is incorrect. You must charge IGST; otherwise, your client cannot claim Input Tax Credit.

3. Overlooking Composition Scheme Limits

Small businesses enrolled in the Composition Scheme pay a flat 1% to 6% tax on turnover and are legally prohibited from collecting GST from customers or issuing tax invoices.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the threshold for mandatory GST registration?

The threshold turnover is ₹40 Lakh for businesses dealing strictly in goods (₹20 Lakh in special category states) and ₹20 Lakh for service providers (₹10 Lakh in special category states).

How does Input Tax Credit (ITC) work?

When you sell goods or services, you can offset the GST you paid on business purchases (input tax) against the GST you collect from customers (output tax), remitting only the net difference to the government.

What is the difference between GST exemption and Zero-rated supplies?

Exempt supplies (like fresh milk) carry 0% GST and businesses cannot claim ITC on inputs. Zero-rated supplies (exports and SEZ deliveries) carry 0% GST but allow full refund of input taxes.
