How to Save Tax in India: A Simple Guide for Salaried Employees (2026)

Paying taxes is part of life, but paying more tax than you legally have to is not. Here is an actionable roadmap to legitimately keep more of your hard-earned salary in your bank account.

August 18, 2026 By Tejas
← Back to Articles

Paying taxes is part of life. But paying more tax than you legally have to is not. Every year, millions of salaried employees in India end up paying thousands of rupees in extra tax simply because they did not plan their investments properly or did not know what deductions they were entitled to.

This guide is for you if you get a salary, file your taxes, and want to keep more money in your pocket without doing anything complicated or risky.

First: Old Tax Regime or New Tax Regime?

Before we talk about saving tax, you need to decide which regime to use — because the answer changes everything.

  • The Old Tax Regime has higher tax slabs but allows you to claim multiple deductions (like Section 80C, HRA, and health insurance). This is better if you have significant eligible investments or pay high rent.
  • The New Tax Regime has lower tax rates but almost no deductions allowed. It is simpler and often better for people with lower incomes or fewer eligible deductions.
Quick Rule of Thumb

If your deductions under the old regime add up to more than &8377;3.75 lakhs per year, the old regime likely saves you more tax. Otherwise, the new regime may be better. Use our free Income Tax Calculator to compare both regimes side by side in seconds.

The Biggest Tax Saver: Section 80C (Up to &8377;1.5 Lakh Deduction)

Section 80C is the most popular tax-saving provision in India. It lets you reduce your taxable income by up to &8377;1.5 lakh per year by investing in certain approved products:

  • ELSS Mutual Funds (Best for wealth building): Equity Linked Savings Scheme is a diversified equity mutual fund with the shortest lock-in (3 years) among all 80C options. Historically, ELSS funds have delivered 12%+ long-term compounding.
  • PPF — Public Provident Fund (Best for safety): Government-backed savings scheme currently yielding ~7.1% tax-free interest with a 15-year maturity.
  • EPF — Employee Provident Fund: Automatic 12% deduction from your basic salary for corporate employees. Counts directly toward your &8377;1.5L limit.
  • Life Insurance Premiums: Term insurance premiums for yourself, spouse, or dependent children qualify.
  • 5-Year Tax-Saving Bank FDs: 5-year lock-in bank deposits qualify, though interest earned remains taxable.

Section 80D: Save Tax on Health Insurance (Up to &8377;75,000)

Section 80D allows you to deduct medical insurance premiums from your taxable income over and above 80C:

  • For yourself, spouse, and dependent children: up to &8377;25,000/year
  • For parents below age 60: up to &8377;25,000/year additional
  • For senior citizen parents (age 60+): up to &8377;50,000/year additional

A salaried individual paying medical insurance for their family plus senior citizen parents can claim up to &8377;75,000 in deductions under Section 80D.

Section 80CCD(1B): Extra &8377;50,000 Deduction Through NPS

The National Pension System (NPS) provides an exclusive additional deduction of &8377;50,000 beyond the &8377;1.5 lakh 80C ceiling. For someone in the 30% tax bracket, claiming this saves an extra &8377;15,000 in cash taxes while establishing a retirement nest egg.

HRA: House Rent Allowance (If You Pay Rent)

If you live in rented accommodation and receive HRA as part of your salary breakdown, a substantial portion of that allowance is exempt from income tax under the Old Regime. The exempt amount is the lowest of:

  1. Actual HRA received from your employer.
  2. 50% of Basic Salary (in Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai) or 40% (other cities).
  3. Actual rent paid minus 10% of Basic Salary.

Note: If your employer does not provide HRA, you can claim rent deductions under Section 80GG (up to &8377;60,000/year).

Standard Deduction: &8377;75,000 (Automatic)

Every salaried individual in India gets an automatic standard deduction of &8377;75,000 under the New Tax Regime (and &8377;50,000 under the Old Regime). This deduction is applied automatically by your employer payroll without requiring receipts or bills.

Home Loan: Double Tax Benefit

If you are servicing a home loan, you can claim dual deductions:

  • Principal Repayment (Section 80C): Up to &8377;1.5 lakh per financial year.
  • Interest Repayment (Section 24b): Up to &8377;2.0 lakh per financial year on self-occupied properties.

Putting It All Together: A Real Example

Consider Meera, a 28-year-old software engineer in Pune earning a gross salary of &8377;10,00,000:

Deduction / Exemption Amount
Standard Deduction &8377;75,000
EPF Contribution (12% of Basic) &8377;48,000
ELSS Mutual Fund SIPs (completing 80C) &8377;1,02,000
NPS Contribution (Sec 80CCD 1B) &8377;50,000
Health Insurance Premium (Sec 80D) &8377;25,000
HRA Exemption &8377;1,20,000
Total Deductions Claimed &8377;4,20,000

Her taxable income drops from &8377;10,00,000 down to &8377;5,80,000, slashing her total tax liability to nearly zero under applicable rebate brackets.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Don't buy costly traditional endowment or ULIP insurance: They mix low insurance coverage with subpar 4% to 5% returns. Purchase pure term insurance for safety and ELSS/Index mutual funds for wealth creation.
  • Don't wait until March: Lump-sum panic investing in February or March strains cash flow. Automate monthly ELSS SIPs from April onwards.
  • Don't stick to Old Regime blindly: Always run your exact salary numbers through a calculator to compare before making tax declarations.
