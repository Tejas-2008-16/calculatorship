Leave Encashment Tax Exemption Guide: Section 10(10AA) Rules & Limits

When salaried employees in India resign, retire, or reach superannuation, accumulated earned leaves are converted into cash. Understanding the tax treatment under Section 10(10AA) is crucial: while central and state government employees enjoy 100% tax exemption, private sector and PSU employees operate under an enhanced statutory ceiling of ₹25 Lakh. Master the 4-way statutory calculation formula, 30-day leave entitlement rules, and Old vs New Tax Regime treatment for FY 2026-27.

Statutory Compliance Notice: Leave encashment exemptions are governed by Section 10(10AA) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, and CBDT Notification S.O. 2276(E). The enhanced statutory monetary limit of ₹25,00,000 applies to non-government employees retiring or resigning on or after April 1, 2023. Exemption rules apply under both Old and New Tax Regimes.

Key Takeaways & Executive Summary

  • Govt Employees: 100% tax-exempt on retirement or superannuation without any monetary limit under Section 10(10AA)(i).
  • Private & PSU Employees: Tax-exempt up to the least of 4 statutory calculations, capped at ₹25,00,000 lifetime limit under Section 10(10AA)(ii).
  • During Active Service: Any leave salary received while continuing in employment is 100% taxable as salary income for all employees.
  • The 30-Day Entitlement Rule: For tax exemption math, leave entitlement cannot exceed 30 days per completed year of actual service, regardless of employer company policy.
  • Available in New Tax Regime: Section 10(10AA) is an exclusion from gross salary and remains fully claimable under Section 115BAC (New Tax Regime).

1. What is Leave Encashment & How Does It Work?

Under corporate HR policies and statutory employment frameworks such as the Factories Act and State Shops and Commercial Establishments Acts, every salaried professional is entitled to various categories of leave, including casual leave, sick leave, and earned (privilege) leave. While casual and sick leaves typically lapse at the conclusion of each calendar or financial year, unutilized earned leave (EL) can often be carried forward.

When an employee accumulates surplus earned leave, employment contracts allow that balance to be monetized—either periodically during service or as a terminal lump sum at the end of employment. This monetization is legally termed leave encashment or leave salary. Because it represents compensation from an employer, the Income Tax Act categorizes leave encashment under the head “Income from Salaries”, subjecting it to specific statutory exemption provisions under Section 10(10AA).

2. Active Service vs Retirement/Resignation: Taxability Split

The timing of leave encashment is the primary determinant of its taxability under Indian law. Taxpayers frequently make the costly mistake of encashing leave balances while continuing in service, assuming it carries tax relief.

Timing of Encashment Government Employees Non-Government / Private Sector
During Active Service (Mid-Career) Fully Taxable Fully Taxable
At Retirement / Superannuation 100% Tax-Exempt (No Limit) Exempt up to ₹25 Lakh ceiling
Upon Voluntary Resignation 100% Tax-Exempt Exempt up to ₹25 Lakh ceiling
Paid to Legal Heirs on Death 100% Tax-Free 100% Tax-Free (CBDT Circular Letter)

Any amount encashed during ongoing service is classified under Section 17(1) as profits in lieu of salary and is taxed at your regular marginal income tax slab. However, employees can explore relief under Section 89 read with Rule 21A by submitting Form 10E if the receipt causes higher tax bracket spiking.

3. Central & State Govt vs Private/PSU Employees

The Income Tax Act bifurcates terminal leave salary into two distinct legal sub-clauses under Section 10(10AA):

Sub-clause (i): Central & State Government Employees

Any amount received as leave salary at the time of retirement or superannuation by an employee of the Central Government or a State Government is wholly exempt from income tax without any monetary restriction.

Sub-clause (ii): Non-Government Employees

Employees of private corporations, Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), nationalized banks, local authorities, and multinational enterprises are covered under Section 10(10AA)(ii). Exemption is limited to the minimum of four statutory computations, governed by the CBDT ceiling of ₹25 Lakh.

4. The 4-Way Statutory Exemption Formula Explained

For non-government employees, Section 10(10AA)(ii) mandates that the tax-exempt portion is strictly the lowest of the following four amounts:

Exempt Leave Encashment = Minimum of:

  1. Statutory Maximum Monetary Limit: ₹25,00,000 (specified by the Central Government).
  2. Actual Leave Encashment Received: The gross amount paid by the employer upon termination.
  3. 10 Months' Average Salary: 10 × (Average monthly salary drawn in the preceding 10 months).
  4. Cash Equivalent of Unutilized Leave: Value of unavailed earned leave calculated strictly at 30 days per completed year of service, valued at the 10-month average salary rate.

Any excess amount received over and above the exempt figure is added to Gross Salary and taxed at your applicable slab rate.

5. Defining Salary & The 30-Day Annual Leave Cap

Precise statutory definitions govern the variables used in the 4-way formula. Miscalculating these components leads to inaccurate tax reporting:

What Counts as “Salary” for Section 10(10AA)?

Similar to gratuity under the non-Gratuity Act framework and HRA math, “salary” for leave encashment includes:

  • Basic Salary: Actual basic pay earned.
  • Dearness Allowance (DA): Only to the extent it enters into the calculation of retirement benefits (forming part of salary).
  • Turnover-Based Commission: Commission paid as a fixed percentage on sales turnover achieved by the employee.

All other allowances—such as HRA, Special Allowance, Conveyance, Medical, and Performance Bonuses—are strictly excluded from the salary base.

The 30-Day Entitlement Statutory Restriction

Many private corporate firms provide generous leave policies granting 36, 40, or 45 days of earned leave per completed year of service. However, for Income Tax exemption under Section 10(10AA)(ii), statutory leave entitlement is capped at a maximum of 30 days per completed year of continuous service.

If your employer credited 40 days per year, the tax computation must recalculate your eligible earned leave using 30 days per year, subtract leaves actually availed and encashed during service, and determine the admissible unutilized balance.

6. Comprehensive Worked Numerical Case Study

Let us review an end-to-end mathematical example illustrating the application of the four-way statutory formula.

Assumptions & Employee Profile

  • Tenure of Completed Service: 24 years and 8 months (Fractional months are ignored; completed years = 24).
  • Company Leave Policy: 40 days of earned leave per year of service.
  • Total Leaves Availed/Encashed in Service: 200 days.
  • Leave Balance at Retirement: (24 × 40) − 200 = 760 days.
  • Last Drawn Basic Pay: ₹95,000 per month (unchanged over the preceding 10 months).
  • DA (Eligible for Retirement Benefits): ₹15,000 per month.
  • Special Allowance: ₹40,000 per month (Excluded).
  • Actual Leave Encashment Received: (760 / 30) × ₹1,10,000 = ₹27,86,667.

Step 1: Determine 10-Month Average Salary

Monthly Salary for Section 10(10AA) = Basic (₹95,000) + Eligible DA (₹15,000) = ₹1,10,000 per month.

Daily Salary = ₹1,10,000 / 30 days = ₹3,666.67 per day.

Step 2: Recalculate Eligible Leave Balance Under Tax Law

  • Statutory Leave Entitlement (Max 30 days/yr): 24 years × 30 days = 720 days.
  • Less Leaves Availed: 200 days.
  • Statutory Unavailed Leave Balance: 720 − 200 = 520 days (or 17.33 months).

Step 3: Execute the 4-Way Formula Comparison

Test Statutory Computation Amount
1. Statutory Cap CBDT Notification Limit ₹25,00,000
2. Actual Received Terminal payout from employer ₹27,86,667
3. 10 Months' Salary 10 × ₹1,10,000 ₹11,00,000
4. Cash Equivalent (520 / 30) × ₹1,10,000 ₹19,06,667

Result: The least of the four amounts is Test 3: ₹11,00,000.

  • Tax-Exempt Leave Encashment: ₹11,00,000
  • Taxable Portion: ₹27,86,667 − ₹11,00,000 = ₹16,86,667

The taxable portion of ₹16,86,667 is added to the employee's salary income for the financial year and subjected to TDS under Section 192.

7. Resignation vs Superannuation & Legal Precedents

For decades, assessing officers disputed whether employees who voluntarily resign are entitled to Section 10(10AA)(ii) exemptions, arguing that “resignation” does not constitute “retirement”. However, multiple high-ranking judicial authorities have firmly established that voluntary resignation qualifies:

  • Madras High Court in CIT v. R.J. Shahney (159 ITR 160): Held that the word “retirement” in Section 10(10AA) is of wide amplitude and includes resignation, as employment ceases upon acceptance.
  • Bombay High Court & Delhi ITAT Rulings: Confirmed that retirement contemplates every form of cessation of service, including voluntary resignation. Consequently, employer payroll teams routinely apply Section 10(10AA) exemptions in full-and-final settlement (FnF) statements upon resignation.

8. Section 10(10AA) in Old vs New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC)

A crucial consideration for salaried taxpayers is how terminal benefits interact with the revised tax regimes. While Chapter VI-A deductions (Section 80C, 80D, 80E, 80TTA) are eliminated under the default New Tax Regime, Section 10(10AA) leave encashment exemption remains 100% available under both regimes.

Parameter Old Tax Regime New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC)
Section 10(10AA) Exemption Available (up to ₹25L) Available (up to ₹25L)
Standard Deduction ₹50,000 ₹75,000
Slab Rate on Taxable Surplus Up to 30% (+ 4% cess) Progressive slabs up to 30%

9. Lifetime Limit Across Multiple Employers & ITR Reporting

The ₹25,00,000 statutory exemption under Section 10(10AA)(ii) is a cumulative lifetime ceiling across all employers throughout an individual's career. If you claim an exemption upon resigning from Employer A, your eligible limit with subsequent employers is reduced accordingly:

Subsequent Exemption Cap = ₹25,00,000 − (Exemptions Claimed Under Sec 10(10AA) in Prior Years)

In your Income Tax Return (ITR-1 or ITR-2), leave encashment is reported under Salary Components. Enter the gross leave salary received in Section 17(1) and claim the exempt portion under the dropdown for Section 10(10AA) — Earned leave encashment.

10. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

When did the ₹25 Lakh leave encashment exemption take effect?

The Ministry of Finance, via Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) Notification S.O. 2276(E) dated May 24, 2023, exercised powers under Section 10(10AA)(ii) to raise the limit from ₹3,00,000 to ₹25,00,000 with retrospective effect from April 1, 2023.

Are PSU bank and autonomous body employees treated as government employees?

No. Employees of Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs), state road transport corporations, and nationalized public sector banks are non-government employees for the purpose of Section 10(10AA). They are subject to the ₹25 Lakh limit under Section 10(10AA)(ii).

Can leave encashment received by legal heirs of a deceased employee be taxed?

No. Under official CBDT clarifications, leave salary paid to the legal heirs or nominees of an employee who dies in service is not taxable as salary or income from other sources in the hands of the recipient.

What if my employer deducts TDS on the entire leave encashment?

If your payroll department fails to apply Section 10(10AA) exemption in Form 16, you can substantiate and claim the exemption directly while filing your Income Tax Return, claiming a refund for the excess TDS withheld.
