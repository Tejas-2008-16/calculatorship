What Is a Recurring Deposit (RD)? Complete Banking Guide India

A Recurring Deposit (RD) is a guaranteed-return fixed-income banking product offered by commercial banks (such as SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Punjab National Bank) and India Post. It allows salaried professionals and conservative savers to deposit a fixed sum of money each month across a chosen tenure ranging from 6 months up to 10 years.

RDs instill disciplined savings while providing complete capital safety backed by the RBI's Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank.

The Exact Mathematical Formula (RBI Quarterly Compounding)

Unlike simple interest, Indian banks compound RD interest quarterly. The maturity sum is computed by aggregating each monthly deposit compounded for its remaining duration:

Maturity Value = Σ [ P × (1 + r / 4) ^ (4 × t_i) ]

Where:
• P: Monthly installment deposited in Rupees.
• r: Annual nominal interest rate (in decimal format, e.g., 0.07 for 7.0%).
• t_i: Remaining fraction of the year for installment i = (N - i + 1) / 12.
• 4: Compounding frequency (Quarterly).

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Example in INR

Suppose you open a 12-month RD of ₹5,000 per month at 7.0% per annum with State Bank of India:

  • Step 1: Total principal deposited = 12 × ₹5,000 = ₹60,000.
  • Step 2: Installment 1 (deposited on Day 1) compounds for all 4 quarters: ₹5,000 × (1 + 0.07/4)^4 = ₹5,359.
  • Step 3: Installment 12 (deposited in Month 12) compounds for just 1 month: ₹5,000 × (1 + 0.07/4)^(1/3) = ₹5,029.
  • Step 4: Sum of all 12 compounded installments = ₹62,317. Total interest earned = ₹2,317.

Frequently Asked Questions About Recurring Deposits

How is TDS applied on recurring deposit interest?

Under Section 194A of the Income-tax Act, banks deduct 10% TDS if the cumulative interest earned on all your deposits (FD + RD) across the bank exceeds ₹40,000 in a financial year (₹50,000 for senior citizens). If your total income is below the taxable threshold, you can submit Form 15G (or 15H for seniors) to prevent TDS.

What happens if I miss an RD installment?

Most banks allow a grace period of 5 to 10 days. If the installment remains unpaid, a small late penalty (typically ₹1.50 to ₹2.00 per ₹100 of deposit per month) is charged. If consecutive installments (usually 3 to 6 months) are missed, the bank reserves the right to prematurely close the RD account.

Can I take a loan against my recurring deposit?

Yes. Most Indian commercial banks allow an overdraft or demand loan of up to 90% of the accumulated RD balance at an interest rate typically 1.0% to 2.0% above the deposit rate, avoiding the need to break your RD prematurely.

When Does an RD Make Sense in Your Portfolio?

An RD is not designed for long-term wealth creation because post-tax returns barely match 6% inflation. However, it is the best tool for:

  1. Fixed Short-Term Milestones (6 to 24 Months): Building up cash for annual car insurance premiums, school admission fees, or a planned domestic vacation.
  2. Building an Emergency Fund: Channeling 10% of monthly pay until 6 months of living expenses are accumulated in risk-free bank deposits.
  3. Absolute Capital Preservation: Preventing capital market drawdown risk when your goal maturity is less than 2 years away.
