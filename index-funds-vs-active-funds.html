Index Funds vs Active Mutual Funds in India (2026): SPIVA Data & TER Analysis

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • SPIVA India data (2024) shows that 84% of large-cap active funds underperformed the Nifty 100 TRI over the past 10 years , making a low-cost Nifty 50 or Nifty 100 index fund a more reliable choice for large-cap allocation than most active managers.
  • Portfolio managers add measurable value in mid-cap and small-cap categories where markets are less efficient: SPIVA 10-year data shows only 51% of mid-cap active funds underperformed their category index , meaning roughly half did beat the index.
  • The expense ratio difference between a Nifty 50 Direct Index Fund (~0.10% to 0.20% TER) and a large-cap active Direct Fund (~0.80% to 1.20% TER) compounds into ₹14 Lakh in lost returns on a ₹10,000 monthly SIP over 20 years.
  • ETF versions of index funds have lower TERs than index fund FOFs but require a demat account; FOF versions allow SIP without demat , choose FOF for convenience, ETF for maximum cost efficiency.
  • Implementing a core-and-satellite strategy (60% to 70% in passive index funds as core, plus 30% to 40% in active mid/small-caps as satellite) provides market-rate returns on the core while seeking alpha on the satellite portion.

The index versus active fund debate has dominated Western personal finance for two decades. In India, it arrived later but the data is converging to the same conclusion: in efficient, heavily-researched large-cap markets, most active managers cannot beat the index consistently after fees. In less-efficient small and mid-cap segments, active management has historically added value. Our core question is not which approach is universally superior, it is which approach belongs in which portion of your portfolio.

Core Evidence for Index Funds: SPIVA Findings

S&P's SPIVA (S&P Indices Versus Active) India Scorecard is the most detailed annual study of active manager performance. Key findings from the 2024 report covering the 10-year period ending December 2024:

  • 84% of large-cap active funds underperformed the S&P BSE 100 (comparable to Nifty 100 TRI) over 10 years.
  • 77% of ELSS funds underperformed the S&P BSE 200 over 10 years.
  • 51% of mid/small-cap funds underperformed. That means about half the active managers in this space added value over a decade.

Empirical data is not a blanket indictment of active management. It is a category-specific argument: in large-cap space, index funds win more often than not. In mid-cap and small-cap, skilled active management can meaningfully outperform.

Expense Ratio Math: ₹14 Lakh on a 20-Year SIP

Fund Type Typical TER (Direct) Net Return at 12% Gross Market 20-Year Corpus (₹10,000/mo)
Nifty 50 Index Fund (Direct) 0.10% to 0.20% 11.85% ₹98,60,000
Large-Cap Active Fund (Direct) 0.80% to 1.20% 11.00% ₹86,46,000
Large-Cap Active Fund (Regular) 1.70% to 2.20% 10.00% ₹75,60,000

The Nifty 50 Index Fund investor has ₹14.14 Lakh more after 20 years compared to the Direct large-cap active investor, purely from the lower expense ratio , and this assumes the active fund manager matches the market exactly (which 84% do not, according to SPIVA).

Where Active Management Still Wins: Mid-Cap and Small-Cap

The large-cap market in India (the top 100 NSE companies) is intensely researched by thousands of analysts, institutional investors, and algorithmic traders. Prices are efficient: new information is priced into the market within seconds. Mutual fund managers find it very difficult to consistently discover superior information and exploit mispricing.

Meanwhile, mid-cap companies (ranked 101 to 250) and small-cap segment (251+) are less efficiently researched. Fewer analysts cover each stock, quarterly results are less scrutinized, and undiscovered businesses with extraordinary growth prospects exist. Skilled active managers with deep research capabilities can generate 2% to 4% annual alpha over the mid-cap index in this space, a difference worth the higher TER of 0.50% to 0.90% for a Direct plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

If I invest in both a Nifty 50 index fund and a large-cap active fund, am I getting real diversification?

No. Holding identical securities represents portfolio overlap, not true diversification. Typical large-cap active fund top 20 holdings are typically 60% to 75% identical to the Nifty 50 constituents. You are paying a 0.90% TER for marginal differentiation. Instead, pair your Nifty 50 index fund with a mid-cap or small-cap fund for genuine diversification across the market-cap spectrum.

Does the Nifty 50 index fund provide any downside protection during market crashes?

No. An index fund replicates the benchmark directly, taking on the same downside during market crashes. During the COVID-19 crash (February to March 2020), Nifty 50 fell approximately 38%. Parallel Nifty 50 index funds fell the same 38%. Fund managers with cash allocation or defensive sector tilts can cushion a portion of the fall , but most don't, and those who do often hold cash during subsequent recoveries, missing early-bull-run gains.

Recommended Framework: Core-and-Satellite

Allocate 60% to 70% of your equity SIP to a direct Nifty 50 or Nifty 100 index fund. Such passive holdings form your stable, market-return-generating core. Allocate the remaining 30% to 40% to one well-researched, consistently alpha-generating active mid-cap or flexi-cap fund , this is your satellite seeking to outperform. Review the satellite allocation every 3 years against benchmark. If it stops generating alpha, fold it into the index core. Use Zerodha Coin or Groww to implement this split-SIP structure within a single account.
