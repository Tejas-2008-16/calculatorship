How to Read a Mutual Fund Factsheet: 10 Critical Metrics Explained

Every month, Indian Asset Management Companies (AMCs) publish detailed factsheets for all mutual fund schemes. Deciphering these documents helps you evaluate fund performance, risk parameters, portfolio concentration, and manager skill before investing.

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • Every AMC publishes monthly factsheets (required by AMFI) — these are 1–4 page PDFs available free on the AMC website containing the fund's NAV, AUM, expense ratio, portfolio holdings, and performance data.
  • The most important single metric on a factsheet is not 1-year return — it is the 5-year and 10-year rolling CAGR versus the fund's benchmark index (e.g., Nifty 50 TRI), which shows whether the manager consistently adds value.
  • A fund with AUM below ₹500 Crore in the large-cap or index category warrants caution — low AUM creates tracking error risk and the possibility of unfavorable fund closure or merger.
  • The Total Expense Ratio (TER) on a factsheet is the annual percentage charged — a difference of 1% TER compounds into lakhs of rupees lost over 20 years (₹38 Lakh on a ₹15,000 monthly SIP over 25 years).
  • Portfolio turnover ratio above 100% in an equity fund signals aggressive trading by the fund manager, generating higher transaction costs that silently erode returns without appearing in the TER.

Every AMC in India publishes a monthly factsheet — a condensed snapshot of each fund's essential metrics. Most investors never open these documents; those who do often scan the 1-year return number and stop reading. That is the wrong approach. A factsheet contains eight critical pieces of information that, read correctly, tell you whether a fund deserves your money.

How to Evaluate Returns: The Right Benchmarking Method

The 1-year return on a factsheet is almost useless for fund selection—it reflects a single market cycle snapshot. Instead, evaluate:

  1. 5-year rolling CAGR vs benchmark TRI (Total Returns Index): Compare the fund's 5-year CAGR to its benchmark's TRI (which includes dividend reinvestment). Most publicly available comparisons use the Price Return Index, which is 1.5%–2% lower than TRI—making a fund look better than it actually is.
  2. Category rank consistency: A fund that ranked top-quartile in its category for 3 consecutive 3-year periods is a far more reliable choice than one that was top-ranked in a single 1-year window due to sector concentration that happened to work.
  3. Alpha vs beta: Alpha measures excess return over benchmark; beta measures how much the fund moves relative to market. A fund with alpha of 2.5% and beta of 0.85 outperforms while taking less market risk—ideal.

Where to Find Factsheets

  • AMC websites: All fund houses (HDFC AMC, SBI MF, ICICI Pru AMC, Mirae, etc.) publish monthly factsheets on their Investor Relations or Downloads page.
  • Value Research Online (valueresearchonline.com): Aggregates factsheet data with rolling return analysis, risk metrics, and portfolio overlap calculators.
  • Morningstar India (morningstar.in): Provides star ratings, analyst notes, and risk-adjusted return comparisons across categories.

Frequently Asked Questions About Factsheets

How often should I review my fund's factsheet?

Once every 6 months is sufficient for a long-term SIP investor. Monthly factsheet-checking can trigger unnecessary trading based on short-term noise. Meaningful evaluation requires at least 3–5 years of rolling return data. Set calendar reminders in April and October (start and mid of financial year) to briefly review your funds' 3-year performance versus benchmark.

Should I switch funds if my fund's AUM has grown very large?

Large AUM is a concern primarily for small-cap and mid-cap funds where very large fund sizes make it difficult to build meaningful positions in smaller companies without moving the market price. For large-cap and index funds, AUM above ₹5,000 Crore is actually a sign of investor confidence and operational stability. If a small-cap fund's AUM exceeds ₹15,000–₹20,000 Crore, the manager may be forced into large-cap territory to deploy capital, defeating the fund's stated mandate.
