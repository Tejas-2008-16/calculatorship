Cumulative vs Non-Cumulative FD, Section 194A TDS & Form 15G/15H

Scheduled commercial banks in India calculate term deposit interest using quarterly compounding as standard practice. Over multi-year deposits, choosing Cumulative reinvestment earns compound interest on accrued quarterly interest—pushing effective annualized yield on a 7.00% quoted rate to 7.19% APY. On a ₹4,00,000 cumulative FD at 7.00% over 5 years, you receive ₹5,65,911 at maturity versus ₹5,40,000 total cash collected (principal plus ₹1,40,000 flat quarterly interest) under quarterly payouts.

FY 2026-27 Section 194A TDS & Form 15G/15H Rules

  • Updated TDS Ceilings (Effective April 1, 2025): Banks deduct 10% TDS under Section 194A (mapped to Section 393 of Income-tax Act, 2025) only if total FD/RD interest credited across all branches of one bank exceeds ₹50,000 for general citizens or ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens aged 60+ in a financial year.
  • Preventing Unnecessary Deductions: If total taxable income stays below taxable thresholds (e.g., within FY 2026-27 New Regime zero-tax limits), submit Form 15G (under age 60) or Form 15H (age 60+) at the start of every financial year.
  • DICGC Safety Ceiling: Remember that DICGC deposit insurance covers up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank (covering principal plus accrued cumulative interest combined). Large cumulative deposits benefit from splitting across banks via our FD Laddering Strategy Guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the monthly interest on a ₹4 lakh FD in India?

Using standard Indian bank quarterly-discounted monthly payouts, a ₹4,00,000 Fixed Deposit earns approximately ₹2,155/month at 6.50% p.a., ₹2,320/month at 7.00% p.a., and ₹2,485/month at 7.50% p.a. (or slightly more on simple r/12 calculation). In cumulative mode at 7.00% over 5 years, ₹4,00,000 grows to ₹5,65,911.

What is the difference between a Cumulative FD and a Non-Cumulative FD?

In a Cumulative FD, quarterly interest is reinvested into the deposit and compounds until maturity—maximizing wealth for savers who do not need monthly income. Non-Cumulative FDs pay interest monthly, quarterly, or half-yearly to your savings account while returning only principal at maturity.

What is the Bank FD TDS threshold under Section 194A in FY 2026-27?

Under revised Section 194A (and Section 393 of Income-tax Act 2025, effective April 1, 2025), banks deduct 10% TDS (20% without PAN) when aggregate deposit interest at a single bank exceeds ₹50,000 in a financial year for general individuals, or ₹1,00,000 for senior citizens aged 60 and above. Eligible savers below taxable income can submit Form 15G or 15H.

Are cumulative Fixed Deposits insured by the RBI?

Bank deposits in scheduled commercial banks are insured by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC)—a wholly owned RBI subsidiary—for up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank covering both principal and accrued cumulative interest.
