About Calculatorship

Educational content only. This page explains how a financial concept works. It is not investment, tax, legal or insurance advice, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. I am a student, not a SEBI-registered adviser or a Chartered Accountant. Tax rules, interest rates and limits change, so check the official source linked below before you act, and speak to a qualified professional about your own situation. Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks, and past performance does not guarantee future returns.

Who Built This Site

Tejas Waghmare runs this site from Pune. Dropping out of college pushed me to teach myself modern front-end coding by building utilities people actually open twice. Calculatorship started as a scratchpad where I wrote fast browser-based financial tools and worked out Indian salary, loan, and compounding math by hand.

This project is built by a student without formal finance degrees: Never cleared any SEBI exam, nor trained as a Chartered Accountant or licensed research analyst. Putting a concept into plain HTML and JavaScript forces you to check every decimal place, every day-count convention, and every tax slab cutoff until a salaried earner can read their own payslip or EMI schedule without squinting at fine print. If you want to read my full background and coding setup, check my Author Profile page.

Why Calculatorship Exists

Commercial aggregator portals wear people down. Before showing a simple SIP projection or car loan breakup, half of those sites freeze the screen behind a mandatory mobile OTP box and feed your contact number to five outbound sales desks pitching high-commission insurance plans or personal loans, even when all you wanted was five seconds of clear rupee arithmetic. Forcing lead-generation signups onto basic math tools hurts usability. Money math should stay fast, free, and quiet. Everything here executes inside your browser tab. These calculators provide straightforward client-side arithmetic without signups or lead capture.

How Each Guide Gets Built

Source rules come first. Before writing a single formula or guide, primary notifications get pulled straight from the Income Tax Department portal, SEBI circular archive, AMFI industry disclosures, and the Reserve Bank of India bulletin so nothing leans on recycled second-hand blog posts.

JavaScript logic goes down next. Every calculator gets hand-checked against edge cases on scrap paper, from ₹0 starting balances and marginal relief cliffs near the Section 87A rebate ceiling to 30-year home loan amortisation curves where early principal payments cut total interest dramatically. Clear Indian English ties the examples together. Each page dates its latest statutory check clearly.

Client-Side Calculations & Privacy

Static HTML, clean CSS, and vanilla JavaScript run every calculator locally inside your browser tab. None of the salary, loan, or SIP figures you type ever touch a remote database. Standard aggregate analytics (Google Analytics 4) and clearly separated display advertising (Google AdSense) help pay for domain and hosting costs, as detailed in our Privacy Policy and Cookie Policy.

Where This Site Draws the Line

Calculatorship operates strictly as an educational resource. Being a self-taught developer with no SEBI RIA licence, CA membership, AMC tie-up, bank partnership, or brokerage payout means this site never pushes a fund, policy, or stock pick. Real-money complaints belong with official regulators anyway, whether you need SEBI SCORES 2.0 for market intermediaries or the RBI Integrated Ombudsman for banks and NBFCs.

Spotting & Fixing Mistakes

Laws shift quickly, and bugs sneak in. Send any outdated slab, broken slider, or math error through the Contact page feedback form. Every note gets read directly, and confirmed fixes go live fast and get logged openly.
