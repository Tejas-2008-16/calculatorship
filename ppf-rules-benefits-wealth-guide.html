PPF (Public Provident Fund) Rules, Benefits & Compounding Guide (2026)

Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know First

  • PPF carries the gold-standard Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) tax status: contributions, annual interest, and final maturity corpus are 100% tax-free.
  • Always deposit funds on or before the 5th of every month (or before April 5th for lump sums) to avoid losing an entire month's compounding interest.
  • Maxing out ₹1.5 Lakh per year yields ₹40.68 Lakh in 15 years, ₹66.58 Lakh in 20 years, and ₹1.03 Crore in 25 years.
  • To continue contributing after 15 years, you must submit Form H within 1 year of maturity.
  • Under Section 15 of the Government Savings Banks Act, PPF balances are legally immune from attachment by any court decree or creditor debt.

Introduction: The Unmatched Pillar of Indian Debt Portfolios

Introduced by the Ministry of Finance in 1968, the Public Provident Fund (PPF) is India's most prestigious small savings scheme. Designed to mobilize long-term savings and provide retirement security to all Indian citizens—especially self-employed individuals, professionals, and unorganized sector workers who lack mandatory Employee Provident Fund (EPF) benefits—the PPF combines absolute sovereign safety with the rarest tax privileges available under Indian law.

In 2026, while market-linked equity mutual funds offer growth potential, the PPF serves as the ultimate risk-free anchor for asset allocation. Backed directly by the Government of India, your principal and accrued interest carry zero credit risk and zero default risk.

This guide provides a detailed breakdown of PPF operating provisions: interest compounding mechanics, the critical 5th-of-the-month rule, multi-decade ₹1 Crore accumulation schedules, Form H extension protocols, loan facilities, and a head-to-head comparison with EPF and Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY).

The Sovereign EEE Tax Advantage Explained

PPF is one of the few surviving instruments in India that enjoys full Exempt-Exempt-Exempt (EEE) status under the Income Tax Act:

  • Exemption 1 (Investment Stage): Deposits up to ₹1,50,000 per financial year are eligible for deduction under Section 80C (under the Old Tax Regime).
  • Exemption 2 (Accrual Stage): The interest compounded annually at 7.1% is 100% exempt from income tax under Section 10(11) under both Old and New Tax Regimes. (Unlike bank FD interest, which is fully taxable at slab rates).
  • Exemption 3 (Withdrawal Stage): The entire final maturity lump sum (principal plus all accumulated interest) is completely tax-free upon redemption.

The '5th of the Month' Interest Rule: Mathematical Proof

One of the most misunderstood rules in Indian personal finance is the PPF interest timing rule. According to the Public Provident Fund Scheme rules:

"Interest shall be calculated for each calendar month on the lowest balance at credit of an account between the close of the fifth day and the end of the month."

Let us analyze what happens when an investor deposits ₹1,50,000 in a lump sum on April 5th versus April 6th:

Golden Rule: Always deposit between April 1st and April 5th for annual lump sums, or on or before the 5th of every month for monthly deposits.

Extension Rules: How to Submit Form H Correctly

When your PPF account reaches its 15th financial year maturity, you must make an active decision:

  1. Extension with Fresh Contribution (Form H Required): You must download and submit Form H to your bank or post office within 1 year of maturity. This allows you to keep depositing up to ₹1.5L annually while enjoying full 80C and compounding benefits. During this 5-year block, you can withdraw up to 60% of the balance that existed at the start of the block.
  2. Extension without Fresh Contribution (Default): If no form is submitted within 1 year, the account automatically extends without contributions. You cannot make new deposits, but your existing corpus continues earning 7.1% Fixed tax-free interest indefinitely, and you are permitted to make one withdrawal per financial year of any amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the current PPF interest rate and how often is it revised?

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) interest rate is currently 7.10% per annum, compounded annually. The rate is reviewed and notified on a quarterly basis by the Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance, Government of India.

What is the '5th of the Month' deposit rule in PPF?

In a PPF account, interest is computed monthly on the lowest balance recorded between the close of the 5th day and the last day of each calendar month. If you deposit funds on or before the 5th of a month, you earn interest for that entire month. If you deposit on the 6th or later, you forfeit that entire month's interest.

How does Form H work for extending PPF beyond 15 years?

To extend your PPF account beyond 15 years with continuing fresh annual contributions, you must submit Form H to your bank or post office within 1 year from the date of maturity. Extensions are granted in 5-year blocks. If you fail to submit Form H, the account extends automatically without contribution, and fresh deposits will earn 0% interest.

Is the PPF balance legally protected from court attachment in India?

Yes. Under Section 15 of the Government Savings Banks Act (incorporating provisions of the PPF Act 1968), the balance in a PPF account cannot be attached by any decree or order of any court of law in respect of any debt or liability incurred by the account holder.

What are the partial withdrawal and loan rules for PPF accounts?

Loans against PPF can be availed from the 3rd to the 6th financial year (up to 25% of the balance at the end of the 2nd preceding financial year) at a concessional interest rate of 1% above the prevailing PPF rate. Partial withdrawals are permitted once per financial year starting from the 7th financial year, capped at 50% of the eligible balance.

What happens if I deposit more than ₹1,50,000 in a single financial year in PPF?

Under statutory rules, any deposit exceeding ₹1.50 Lakh across personal and minor accounts in a single financial year is treated as irregular. The excess sum earns 0% interest, does not qualify for Section 80C deductions, and will be refunded back to the account holder without interest.

Step-by-Step Strategic Framework: How to Maximize Your PPF Wealth in 2026

Public Provident Fund (PPF) is the gold standard of sovereign-Fixed, tax-free debt accumulation in India. Maximize your returns with these four rules:

  1. The 'April 1st to 5th' Lump-Sum Rule: Deposit your full annual ₹1,50,000 contribution between April 1st and April 5th at the start of each financial year. Because interest is computed on the minimum balance between the 5th and month-end, early depositors earn 12 full months of interest on their entire annual deposit.
  2. The 15-Year Block Extension Protocol (Form H): When your 15-year account reaches maturity, extend it in 5-year blocks by submitting Form H within 1 year of maturity. Extending with contributions allows a 25-year PPF account to compound to over ₹1.03 Crore 100% tax-free!
  3. use Sovereign Immunity from Court Attachments: Under Section 9 of the PPF Act, PPF balances are legally immune from attachment by any court in respect of any commercial debts or liabilities, providing a permanent financial firewall for entrepreneurs and business owners.

Common Mistakes Indian Savers Make with PPF

Avoid these operational errors that reduce returns:

  • Depositing Funds on the 6th of the Month or Later: Depositing on the 6th forfeits interest for that entire calendar month, costing thousands over 15 years.
  • Exceeding the ₹1.5 Lakh Annual Limit Across Minor Accounts: The ₹1.5 Lakh ceiling applies cumulatively across your personal account and any account opened for a minor child. Excess deposits earn 0% interest and complicate tax returns.
  • Holding Multiple PPF Accounts in Your Own Name: Opening two separate PPF accounts in different banks is illegal under PPF scheme rules; the secondary account will be treated as irregular and earn zero interest.

Editorial Perspective: Myth-Busting the Public Provident Fund

In my view, while the Public Provident Fund is universally celebrated as the crown jewel of Indian debt investing, many savers hold flawed assumptions about its role in overall wealth creation. The primary myth is that PPF alone can fund a comfortable retirement. While 7.1% Fixed tax-free return is attractive, inflation in long-term goals like higher education and healthcare outpaces 7%. PPF is an insurance-grade capital preservation anchor, not a standalone wealth multiplier.

Another persistent misconception involves the new tax regime. Many individuals stopped funding PPF after migrating to the new regime because Section 80C deductions are unavailable. In my opinion, abandoning PPF for this reason is a tactical mistake: even without an initial deduction, a 7.1% sovereign, completely tax-exempt annual compounding instrument beats a 7.5% bank fixed deposit taxed at your marginal 30% slab (where net post-tax yield drops to ~5.2%).

Edge case & caveat: An essential caveat applies to accounts opened for minor children. The statutory limit of ₹1.5 Lakh per financial year applies to the guardian and minor child combined. Any excess deposit across both accounts earns 0% interest and will not qualify for tax benefits. Furthermore, if you wish to continue your account beyond 15 years with contributions, Form H must be submitted within exactly one year from maturity; failing to submit Form H turns subsequent deposits into irregular non-interest-bearing transactions.

How to Think About This Yourself

  • Capital Preservation Anchor: PPF provides sovereign safety with zero credit risk. Even in the New Tax Regime where Section 80C is unavailable, its 100% tax-free interest (Section 10(11)) provides a strong net return relative to taxable bank FDs.
  • The 5th-of-the-Month Operational Discipline: Because interest is computed on the lowest balance between the 5th and the end of each month, depositing on or before the 5th preserves 30 days of compounding return.
  • Cumulative Minor Cap: The statutory ₹1.5 Lakh ceiling per financial year applies across the parent and minor accounts combined. Avoid excess contributions that earn zero return.
  • Form H for Multi-Decade Compounding: To retain active deposit privileges past 15 years, submit Form H within 12 months of the maturity date in 5-year extension blocks.
